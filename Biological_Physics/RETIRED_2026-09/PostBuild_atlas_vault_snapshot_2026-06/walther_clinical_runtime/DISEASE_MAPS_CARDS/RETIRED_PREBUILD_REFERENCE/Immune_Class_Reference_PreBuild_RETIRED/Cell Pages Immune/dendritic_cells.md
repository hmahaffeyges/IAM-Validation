## What you should know about dendritic cells

- Dendritic cells are your immune system's professional teachers — they identify threats, capture pieces of them, and present those pieces to T cells to coordinate the rest of the immune response.
- Recent illness, recent vaccination, active infections, and chronic immune challenge mobilize them, and most elevations resolve as the underlying context resolves.
- Active autoimmune conditions, certain chronic infections, and some cardiovascular conditions can keep them elevated longer-term.
- In research on aortic disease and other cardiovascular conditions, EDEAR has observed dendritic cell signal as part of the broader immune involvement at affected vascular tissue.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

Dendritic cells are professional antigen-presenting cells. When your body encounters a threat, dendritic cells engulf pieces of the threat, process them into specific molecular fragments, and display those fragments on their surface. They then migrate to lymph nodes where they present the fragments to T cells, which coordinate the rest of the adaptive immune response.

Without dendritic cells, the adaptive immune system would not know what to respond to. They are the bridge between innate immunity (which detects threats generally) and adaptive immunity (which responds to specific threats). Dendritic cells come in several functional sub-types — conventional dendritic cells (cDC1 and cDC2) and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC) — each with somewhat different roles. EDEAR reads dendritic cells as a broader category.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: DCs, antigen-presenting cells (APCs), conventional DCs (cDCs), plasmacytoid DCs (pDCs).

## Where these cells live

Dendritic cells are produced from precursors in the bone marrow and circulate to peripheral tissues, where they patrol for threats. They concentrate at tissue sites likely to encounter threats — skin (Langerhans cells), gut, airways — and migrate to lymph nodes after encountering antigens. Dendritic cells are a relatively small fraction of circulating cells; most live in tissue.

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, dendritic cells are a moderate fraction of your circulating immune compartment. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history.

What moves dendritic cells around:

- **Recent illness.** Acute infections mobilize dendritic cells as your body identifies and presents the threat. Signal stays elevated for weeks during the response.
- **Recent vaccination.** Vaccinations specifically mobilize dendritic cells to present the vaccine antigens to T cells. The signal is one of the durable signatures of a recent vaccination response.
- **Active autoimmune conditions.** Autoimmune disease commonly elevates dendritic cells (the disease often involves misdirected antigen presentation).
- **Chronic infections.** Persistent infections keep dendritic cells engaged.
- **Cardiovascular conditions.** Active cardiovascular disease, particularly aortic disease, has been associated with dendritic cell involvement at vascular tissue.

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, ordinary stress, occasional alcohol use. The signal is sensitive to sustained antigen-presentation demand.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your dendritic cell reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — recent illness, recent vaccination, active autoimmune disease, chronic infection — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves over weeks to months once the underlying context is addressed.

If you cannot identify a recent context and the elevation is sustained, trajectory monitoring is the most useful next step. EDEAR tracks how this signal changes over time.

## Research observations on dendritic cells

In research on aortic disease cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed dendritic cell signal in aortic tissue from patients with bicuspid aortic valve plus aortic dilation (BAV+dilation) and from patients with aortic dissection. Dendritic cell involvement reflected immune cell infiltration of the affected vascular tissue, alongside macrophage and other immune cell infiltrate. For peripheral plasma readings, the dendritic cell signal is more subtle than the tissue-level reading; customers with cardiovascular family history or known cardiovascular risk factors should mention dendritic cell elevation alongside any stromal-class signals to their clinician.

In research on broader cardiovascular and inflammatory contexts, dendritic cells participate in the immune response to active disease activity in many cases. Specific cancer-cohort research on dendritic cells in EDEAR's cookbook is more limited than for T cells, B cells, and standard myeloid cells; this is an area of continuing research as additional cohort access becomes available.

## Frequently asked questions about dendritic cells

**What's the difference between dendritic cells and macrophages? They both engulf threats.**

Both engulf threats, but they have different primary jobs. Macrophages clean up damaged cells, debris, and pathogens — their primary role is destruction. Dendritic cells specialize in antigen presentation — they take pieces of threats to T cells to coordinate the adaptive immune response. Macrophages are general-purpose tissue cleanup; dendritic cells are specialized teachers.

**My dendritic cells are elevated and I just got vaccinated. Is that expected?**

Yes. Vaccinations specifically mobilize dendritic cells to present the vaccine antigens to T cells. The signal is one of the durable signatures of a recent vaccination response and resolves over months as your body completes the response.

**My dendritic cells are elevated and I have lupus. Is that expected?**

Many autoimmune diseases involve dendritic cell activity, and lupus in particular is associated with elevated plasmacytoid dendritic cell activity in research. If you have known lupus and your dendritic cell reading is elevated, that's consistent with research expectations. Your rheumatologist can put the reading in the context of your specific disease activity.

**Why are dendritic cells associated with cardiovascular conditions?**

Research has documented dendritic cell involvement in atherosclerotic plaque formation and in aortic disease. Dendritic cells contribute to vascular inflammation in some conditions. The signal in research tracks with disease activity in some cardiovascular contexts.
