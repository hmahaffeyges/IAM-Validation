## What you should know about NK cells

- Natural killer (NK) cells are your immune system's first-line cell-killing response — they kill infected cells, stressed cells, and abnormal cells without needing prior exposure or coordination from the rest of the immune system.
- Recent illness, recent vaccination, intense exercise, and chronic immune challenge mobilize them, and most elevations resolve within weeks to months.
- Persistent viral infections, autoimmune conditions, and active immune surveillance states can keep them elevated longer-term.
- In research on bladder cancer tumor microenvironment, EDEAR has observed substantial NK cell expansion alongside other immune cells. NK cells also participate in broader immune-class patterns the framework reads at the class level.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

NK cells are the rapid-response killers of your innate immune system. Unlike CD8 T cells (which need to be activated against specific targets), NK cells can kill on their first encounter with a threatening cell. They detect cells that are infected (often with viruses), cells that have lost normal protein-display markers (which can include some early cancer cells), or cells under significant stress.

NK cells release toxic granules directly into target cells to trigger their death, and they also release signaling molecules that recruit and coordinate the broader immune response. They are part of innate immunity rather than adaptive immunity — they do not "remember" specific threats the way T cells and B cells do, but they are always ready for action.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: natural killer cells, NK lymphocytes, CD56+ cells, large granular lymphocytes (LGLs).

## Where these cells live

NK cells are produced in the bone marrow and circulate through blood and lymphoid tissue. They concentrate at sites of viral infection, in the spleen and liver (where they survey for infected or abnormal cells), and at tumor microenvironments where research has documented active NK involvement. About 5-15% of your peripheral blood lymphocytes are NK cells at any given time, which is what EDEAR reads.

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, NK cells are a moderate fraction of your circulating lymphocyte compartment. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history. NK cells decline somewhat with age but generally remain a stable fraction of the immune compartment.

What moves NK cells around:

- **Recent viral illness.** Acute viral infections rapidly mobilize NK cells. They are typically among the first immune cells to expand during viral response.
- **Recent vaccination.** Vaccinations mobilize NK cells alongside the broader immune response.
- **Intense exercise periods.** Sustained high-intensity training mobilizes NK cells as part of the body's immune surveillance during physical stress.
- **Chronic viral infections.** Persistent viral infections (chronic HBV, HIV, CMV, EBV reactivation) keep NK cells engaged long-term.
- **Pregnancy.** Pregnancy substantially shifts NK cell activity to allow tolerance of the developing fetus.
- **Active autoimmune conditions.** Some autoimmune diseases elevate NK cell activity.
- **Chronic stress.** Sustained stress through prolonged cortisol elevation can shift NK cell methylation patterns.

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, occasional alcohol use, normal seasonal variation. The signal is sensitive to sustained immune-surveillance demand.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your NK cell reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — recent viral illness, vaccination, intensive training period, autoimmune flare, persistent infection — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves within weeks to a few months once the underlying context is addressed or runs its course.

If your NK reading is depressed (SUPPRESSED tier) without obvious explanation, that's worth conversation with your clinician. NK cell depression has been associated in research with sustained chronic stress, certain immunodeficiency conditions, and active viral suppression states. Most often a SUPPRESSED NK reading is explained by declared context at intake; when no declared context explains it, clinical evaluation is the right next step.

## Trajectory patterns

For subscribers with multiple tests, the NK cell trajectory tells a story that no single reading can. The signal responds within weeks to acute resolutions and over months to sustained changes. The trajectory is informative when it doesn't move — an NK signal that stays elevated across multiple tests despite addressed lifestyle drivers is the kind of pattern that makes a conversation with your clinician more useful than any single reading would be.

## Research observations on NK cells

In research on bladder cancer tumor microenvironment, EDEAR's framework observed substantial NK cell expansion in tumor tissue alongside expansion of nearly every other immune cell type tested — the broad immune infiltration pattern characteristic of bladder cancer in research. NK cells participated meaningfully in this multi-cell expansion pattern. For customers with plasma cfDNA samples (rather than tumor tissue), the peripheral NK cell signal is a different reading than the tumor-microenvironment signal.

In research on active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts, NK cells participated in the broader T-cell-up / myeloid-down population-fraction-shift pattern, with NK cell expansion alongside the T cell expansion.

In research on Alzheimer's disease blood cohorts, NK cells participated in the broader bidirectional cancellation pattern at the class level — some immune cell types elevating while others depressed — that the framework reads through its directional sub-panel rather than through any single cell.

NK cell research in EDEAR's cookbook is more limited than research on T cells and myeloid cells — this is an area of EDEAR's continuing research as additional cohort access becomes available.

### If your NK cell reading is SUPPRESSED

## Frequently asked questions about NK cells

**What's the difference between NK cells and CD8 T cells? They both kill threats, right?**

Both are cytotoxic, but they work differently. CD8 T cells need to be activated against specific targets and recognize them through the immune system's adaptive memory. NK cells are part of innate immunity — they can kill on first contact, without prior exposure, and they recognize cells that are stressed or that have lost normal protein-display markers. Together they form complementary parts of your cell-killing immune response.

**My NK cells are elevated and I had a recent viral illness. Is this expected?**

Yes. NK cells are typically among the first immune cells to expand during viral response. The signal stays elevated for weeks as your body completes the response.

**I do high-intensity training. Does that affect my NK cells?**

Yes. Sustained vigorous training mobilizes NK cells as part of the body's immune surveillance during physical stress. Athletes and high-intensity training participants often show elevated NK readings. Trajectory monitoring across training and recovery periods is informative.

**Can chronic stress depress my NK cells?**

Yes, sustained chronic stress through prolonged cortisol elevation has been associated in research with reduced NK cell activity. Stress management interventions typically restore NK function over weeks to months.
