\title{\textbf{Physics of Methylation: Landauer Metrology}\\[4pt]
\large Thermal Noise as the Unit of Measure --- a Fixed Physical Reference for Cytosine DNA Methylation, Tested Across Four Laboratories}
\author{Heath W. Mahaffey\\ \small IAMPerformance, Entiat, Washington, USA\\ \small \texttt{github.com/hmahaffeyges/IAM-Validation}}
\date{September 2026 --- draft for bioRxiv}

\begin{abstract}
Sanchez and Mackenzie established that cytosine DNA methylation (CDM) obeys Landauer's principle: the methylation machinery must dissipate at least $k_B T\ln 2$ per irreversible logic operation, and the statistical background of methylation change induced by thermal fluctuations follows a statistical-mechanical (Weibull/generalized-gamma) distribution from which regulatory signal can be discriminated \citep{sanchez2016,sanchez2019}. That work measures methylation \emph{divergence} from a reference sample or control pool --- ``the same origin of coordinates'' for control and treatment. Here we ask what follows if the origin of coordinates is instead fixed by the physics. We define a per-cell-class entropy reference $H_{\min}$, calibrated once on healthy reference methylomes and never re-fitted, and read a single sample's methylation entropy on class-identity loci as a dimensionless ratio $A = H(\bar\beta)/H_{\min}$. Thermal noise enters not as a background to be filtered but as the unit: the cellular operating regime is characterised by the dimensionless ratio $\mathcal{M} = \Delta G_{\rm ATP}/k_B T = 20.9$, alongside $\sim$117 for a CMOS logic transistor and 1 for an aluminium transmon at its gap temperature. We then test the metrology, not the biology, on 1,273 healthy whole-blood Illumina 450K arrays from four laboratories in three countries, processed from raw IDATs through one pipeline. A single affine map from the array pipeline onto the reference scale, fitted on one laboratory, transfers to the other three; healthy blood reads at the reference ($A = 0.99$--$1.01$) once mapped. A pooled healthy band does not transfer: each laboratory carries a constant offset in $A$ ($0$, $+0.024$, $-0.021$, $-0.046$), flat across age, that the array's own control probes predict in sign but not in magnitude. The healthy reference therefore has three layers --- a physical floor, a per-pipeline map, and a per-laboratory zero measured on a small healthy panel --- which is the structure clinical chemistry already prescribes for any absolute assay (CLSI EP28). We report the negative results (pooling; control-probe prediction; a production gauge found reading the wrong locus set by end-to-end simulation) alongside the positive ones, and make no disease or diagnostic claim. Code, sealed pre-registrations, outcomes and reference files are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Premise: the methylome obeys Landauer's bound}
\label{sec:premise}

Cytosine DNA methylation is a stable, enzymatically written and erased modification whose genome-wide patterning carries information about cell identity and state \citep{sanchez2016}. Sanchez and Mackenzie \citep{sanchez2016} modelled the change in methylation status at a genomic region as an information-processing operation: with $p_i$ the methylation level at cytosine $i$, the per-site Shannon entropy
\begin{equation}
H(C_i) = -p_i\log_2 p_i - (1-p_i)\log_2(1-p_i)
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
measures the uncertainty of methylation status at tissue level, the information processed in a region $R$ is the change in $\sum_{i\in R} H(C_i)$, and by Landauer's principle the minimum energy dissipated to process it is $E_R = I_R\,k_B T\ln 2$. They showed that the distribution of $E_R$ across genomic regions --- the \emph{methylation background noise} induced by thermal fluctuations --- is well described by a Weibull distribution whose scale parameter recovers the DNA persistence length (39--67 nm) from methylome data alone, and that regulatory signal can be discriminated from that background by signal-detection theory. The 2019 clinical paper \citep{sanchez2019} operationalises this in the MethylIT package: an information divergence (Hellinger) of each sample from a reference built from control individuals, a per-sample Weibull or gamma fit yielding a critical value, and a classifier-derived optimal cutoff separating disease-induced from background differentially methylated positions.

We take three things from this work as established and build on them: (i) $k_B T\ln 2$ is the correct physical anchor for methylation as an information-writing process; (ii) thermal fluctuations produce a genuine statistical background in methylation level that any analysis must account for; (iii) as Sanchez \emph{et al.}\ put it, proper measurement of the methylation signal requires a reference from which divergence is measured, so that background and signal are measured with the same origin of coordinates \citep{sanchez2019}.

The present work differs at exactly that point. In MethylIT the origin of coordinates is the centroid of a control group in the study at hand --- a reference that is re-established for every cohort. We ask whether the origin can instead be fixed by the physics, so that one sample can be read with no other sample in the room. We reached this question independently, from the thermodynamics of bound systems rather than from epigenetics; the author first read \citep{sanchez2016} after every reference value below had been frozen. We cite it because it is peer-reviewed physics that the methylome obeys Landauer's bound, and because a reader who knows MethylIT should see precisely where the two approaches agree and where they part.

\section{A fixed origin: the class entropy reference $H_{\min}$}
\label{sec:hmin}

Cells are grouped into eight architecture classes by lineage commitment (pluripotent stem, adult stem, progenitor, cycling epithelial, secretory, terminal post-mitotic, immune/haematopoietic, stromal). For each class $c$ a set of \emph{identity loci} is defined --- CpGs at which a healthy cell of that class sits at a characteristic, unimodal methylation level --- and a reference entropy $H_{\min}(c)$ is set as the binary entropy (eq.~\ref{eq:H}) of the class's healthy mean methylation level on those loci. The eight values were calibrated once, in April 2026, by Markov-chain Monte Carlo against published Roadmap Epigenomics and ENCODE reference methylomes (37 reference cell types; $\hat R < 1.001$), and have not been re-fitted since. No disease sample and no cohort of the present study entered that calibration.

A sample's reading on class $c$ is then
\begin{equation}
A_c = \frac{H(\bar\beta_c)}{H_{\min}(c)}, \qquad \bar\beta_c = \text{mean methylation level over the identity loci of } c,
\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
a dimensionless ratio in which $A_c = 1$ is where a healthy cell of that class sits and departures are read as distance from that fixed point. Because the identity loci are unimodal, $H(\bar\beta)$ is well defined; on bimodal marker panels the same statistic is dominated by Jensen's inequality and is not used (Section~\ref{sec:sim}).

Two clarifications for readers of \citep{sanchez2016}. First, $A$ is a ratio of entropies to a fixed reference, not a divergence between two samples; it carries no information about \emph{which} loci moved, only how far the class as a whole sits from its healthy entropy. Second, whole blood and tissue are mixtures: before any class is read, the sample is deconvolved by non-negative least squares against a 115-cell-type, 483,092-CpG reference atlas (the ``IAM Atlas'', posterior means and standard deviations per cell type), and a class gauge is reported only for classes the deconvolution finds present and determined (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{The dimensionless ratio of the cellular operating regime}
\label{sec:mahaffey}

In \citep{sanchez2016} the thermal energy $k_B T$ enters as the scale of the background to be modelled and, in downstream analysis, filtered. Here it enters as the unit. Define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M} = \frac{E_{\rm drive}}{k_B T},
\label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
the energy a substrate spends per irreversible write operation in units of the thermal noise quantum at its operating temperature. For the cell the drive energy is the free energy of ATP hydrolysis, $\Delta G_{\rm ATP}\approx 54$ kJ/mol, at $T = 310$ K:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{\rm cell} = \frac{\Delta G_{\rm ATP}}{RT} = \frac{54{,}000}{8.314\times 310.15} = 20.9 .
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:M} places this beside two engineered substrates with fully measured inputs.

$\mathcal{M}$ and $H_{\min}$ are different quantities: $\mathcal{M}$ is one number for the substrate, fixed by biochemistry; $H_{\min}$ is one number per cell class, in bits, fixed by the healthy reference methylomes. The claim of this section is modest and arithmetical: the same ratio that engineers use to place a transistor or a qubit relative to thermal noise places the cell at $\sim$21, and the healthy entropy reference of Section~\ref{sec:hmin} is a statement about how a cell holds its written state at that ratio. The choice of $k_B T$ as the unit follows from a broader argument the author develops elsewhere \citep{mahaffey2026physicists}; nothing in the results below depends on it.

\section{Methods: one pipeline, four laboratories, nothing fitted to the test data}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Cohorts.} Four public healthy whole-blood Illumina HumanMethylation450 cohorts with raw IDATs (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}): GSE87571 (Uppsala; 732 donors, ages 14--94), the 335 rheumatoid-arthritis-study controls of GSE42861 (Karolinska), the 210 depression-study controls of GSE125105 (Munich), and the 237 Parkinson's-study controls of GSE111629 (UCLA). Disease arrays in the three case--control studies were not downloaded. Only the IDATs of selected samples were fetched (per-sample, from GEO's sample directories), not the study RAW archives.

\paragraph{Processing.} Every array went through one pipeline: methylprep noob normalisation from raw IDATs (Stage 1), NNLS deconvolution against the IAM Atlas (Stage 2), and eq.~\ref{eq:A} on the immune-class identity loci (42,024 CpGs present on 450K). A presence gate (immune plus haematopoietic-progenitor fraction $\ge 0.85$, epithelial $\le 0.02$) excludes samples the deconvolution does not read as whole blood.

\paragraph{Scale map.} $H_{\min}$ was calibrated on Roadmap/ENCODE processed methylation levels; raw-IDAT noob $\beta$ sits on a different scale. An affine map $\beta_{\rm S1} = a\,\beta_{\rm ref} + b$ was fitted \emph{once}, on GSE87571 healthy blood, on the identity loci ($a = 1.0127$, $b = 0.0662$), then frozen. It was applied unchanged to the other three cohorts.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Each test below was written and checksum-sealed, with its pass conditions, before the cohort it tests was downloaded; outcomes are recorded against the seal, and post-seal analyses are labelled as such. Seals, outcomes and per-sample results are in the repository.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The pipeline map is a constant that transfers}
Unmapped, healthy blood from every cohort reads well below the reference ($A = 0.76$--$0.86$): the +0.066 offset between noob $\beta$ and the reference scale on the identity loci is the single largest term in the problem, and it is invisible to any within-cohort comparison because it cancels in a difference. The map fitted on Uppsala puts Karolinska's healthy blood at a median $A = 1.010$ (pre-registered bar $\pm 0.02$ of 1.000; pass), Munich at $0.965$ and UCLA at $0.940$. The map is a property of the pipeline, not of the cohort.

\subsection{A pooled healthy band does not transfer}
A per-decade percentile band of $A$ built on one laboratory contains 53\% of a second laboratory's healthy donors; a band pooled from two laboratories contains 55\% of a third's. The reason is in Table~\ref{tab:labs}: each laboratory sits at its own constant offset in $A$, the same in every age decade, of the order of the band's half-width. Pooling more laboratories widens the band; it does not remove a constant that is different for the next one.

\subsection{The array's control probes predict the sign of a laboratory's offset, not its size}
Illumina arrays carry 850 control probes (staining, extension, hybridisation, bisulfite conversion, specificity, normalisation). Summarising their log-intensities per control type and channel (33 features per array) and fitting a ridge model of $A$ on them, leave-one-laboratory-out: the held-out offset is predicted with the correct sign in all four cases, and within the pre-registered $\pm 0.010$ only when the held-out laboratory has a close neighbour in training (Karolinska from the other three: error 0.004; Uppsala 0.018; UCLA 0.026; Munich 0.052). The cohort-separating features are the normalisation and bisulfite-conversion-II controls in the red channel --- the laboratory's chemistry is on the array --- but the part of the offset that is pre-analytical (DNA input, conversion batch, storage) evidently is not. Within a single laboratory the same features explain a modest share of donor-to-donor variance in $A$ (a 13\% reduction in standard deviation on an unseen laboratory; pre-registered bar 20\%). We record this as a negative result.

\subsection{Consequence: the healthy reference has three layers}
A single sample's absolute reading is therefore made against
\[
\underbrace{H_{\min}}_{\text{physics; universal}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{pipeline map}}_{\text{one per array pipeline; transfers}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{laboratory zero}}_{\text{measured once per laboratory}},
\]
then placed in a per-decade healthy band. The laboratory zero is set the way clinical chemistry sets it for any absolute assay: a small panel of healthy samples run once through the same pipeline, with the resulting offset disclosed on every report (CLSI EP28). We arrived at this structure from the physics side and regard the convergence with existing clinical practice as confirmation rather than coincidence.

\subsection{What the calibration discipline found that a cohort comparison could not}
\label{sec:sim}
Two results from the same programme bear directly on the reliability of any absolute methylation reading, and neither could have been obtained by comparing groups.

\emph{End-to-end simulation with known truth.} Synthetic healthy patients were generated as mixtures of the Atlas's own healthy class posteriors and passed through the production chain. Every one read as a large departure. Decomposing the chain term by term located the cause in one function: the deployed gauge computed $H(\bar\beta)$ over a bimodal marker-panel union rather than the unimodal identity loci, and its healthy band had been compiled with the same statistic, so real cohorts had agreed with the band for eleven weeks without measuring anything. On the identity loci the same synthetic patients read $A = 0.99$. A statistic that agrees with its own reference band is not thereby measuring anything; only an input with known truth can show this.

\emph{Cross-method disagreement as diagnostic.} A second, unconstrained deconvolution method had been removed from the chain because it disagreed with the constrained one on every blood sample. Re-run as designed, the disagreement is explained by the conditioning of the reference: the atlas columns for the immune, progenitor and adult-stem classes are nearly parallel in blood (pairwise correlation up to $+0.99$; condition number 31), so the split among them is chosen by the constraint, not the data. The class whose fraction is undetermined is also the class that produced false alarms on healthy donors. The second method had diagnosed this months earlier; it was cut for delivering the message. Both checks now run on every release and cannot be disabled individually.

\section{Relation to information thermodynamics of CDM}
\label{sec:relation}

Both approaches rest on $k_B T\ln 2$. Sanchez and Mackenzie use it to model the thermal background of methylation change and, in MethylIT, to filter that background away so that regulatory sites can be found and classified against a control reference. We use it as the unit of measure and ask how far above the thermal floor a cell class holds its written state, against a reference fixed once by physics. One filters, one calibrates. The two are complementary rather than competing: their statistical-mechanical background model is a principled way to ask which of our identity loci carry regulatory rather than thermal signal --- a question we currently answer empirically by locus selection --- and is the first extension we intend to test. Conversely, the transfer and calibration discipline reported here applies to any absolute methylation measurement, including one built on their divergences, since a divergence from a control pool inherits that pool's laboratory offset.

Three limitations of the present comparison should be stated. Their data are whole-genome bisulfite sequencing read counts; ours are array $\beta$ values, and the scale-map result of Section~\ref{sec:results} is a statement about arrays. Their signal is a \emph{site} that moved; our reading is a \emph{class} against a floor, and carries no locus-level information. And their 2016 proposal of a binary methylation ``language'' is not used or evaluated here.

\section{What is not claimed}
This paper reports a metrology, tested on healthy blood. It does not claim detection of any disease, does not report any disease cohort, and does not propose a diagnostic. The instrument is at a research stage; any clinical use would require prospective validation, regulatory review and qualified clinical oversight. The reference values, the map and the band are published so that they can be checked, and the negative results are reported because they fixed the design.
