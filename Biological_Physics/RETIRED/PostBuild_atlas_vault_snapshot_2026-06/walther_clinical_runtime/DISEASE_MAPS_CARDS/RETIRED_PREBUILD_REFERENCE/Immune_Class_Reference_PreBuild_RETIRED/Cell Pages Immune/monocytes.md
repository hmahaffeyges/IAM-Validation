## What you should know about monocytes

- Monocytes are your immune system's first responders — they arrive at sites of infection or tissue damage to clean up debris, signal for backup, and direct the broader immune response.
- Recent illness, recent vaccination, sustained sleep deprivation, and chronic low-grade inflammation can all elevate them, and most elevations resolve within weeks to a few months.
- Persistent infections (dental, sinus, gut), metabolic conditions (diabetes, fatty liver disease), and obesity can keep them mildly elevated long-term, and addressing the underlying driver typically lowers the signal.
- In research on cancer cohorts, EDEAR has observed monocyte signal in multiple contexts — elevated in some prostate and bladder tumor microenvironment studies, and notably attenuating in long pre-diagnostic windows of breast cancer research as the disease window approached.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

Monocytes are circulating phagocytes — cells that engulf and digest debris, dead cells, microbes, and other material the immune system needs to clear. They live in the bloodstream for a few days, then migrate into tissue where they differentiate into macrophages or dendritic cells depending on what the tissue needs.

When your body has an active infection, tissue damage, or chronic inflammation, monocytes are produced in larger numbers in the bone marrow and shipped to the bloodstream to deploy into the affected tissue. The expansion shows up clearly in cellular methylation patterns. Monocytes also coordinate the immune response — they release signaling molecules that recruit other immune cells, activate T and B cells, and shape whether the response stays acute or transitions to chronic.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: classical monocytes, intermediate monocytes, non-classical monocytes (the three subsets), CD14+ monocytes, blood-derived monocytes.

## Where these cells live

Monocytes are produced in the bone marrow from myeloid precursor cells. They circulate in the blood for a few days, then exit into tissues where they differentiate into tissue-resident macrophages or dendritic cells. About 5-10% of your peripheral white blood cells are monocytes at any given time, which is what EDEAR reads in plasma cfDNA.

## How EDEAR reads them

EDEAR's framework reads monocytes through both the Stage 3 immune cell-type composition lens (using the production Salas IDOL 6-cell reference atlas, which includes a dedicated monocyte category) and through higher-resolution Stage 3 readings using 19-cell and TIM atlases that resolve monocyte subsets. Monocytes are well-represented in standard immune-cell reference atlases, so the framework reads them with high confidence at routine deconvolution resolution.

When monocyte signal is examined alongside the broader cell-type composition, EDEAR can distinguish patterns where monocytes elevate alone (suggesting one type of immune context) from patterns where monocytes elevate alongside neutrophils (a myeloid expansion pattern) or where monocytes drift down while T cells expand (a T-up / myeloid-down pattern that research associates with specific conditions).

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, monocytes are a modest fraction of your circulating immune compartment. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history. Smoking measurably elevates monocyte activity because the persistent low-grade tissue damage from tobacco recruits monocytes continuously.

What moves monocytes around:

- **Recent illness.** Acute infections elevate monocytes within days and the signal resolves over weeks as the infection clears.
- **Recent vaccination.** Vaccinations mobilize monocytes as part of the broader immune response. The signal typically resolves within a few weeks.
- **Sustained sleep deprivation.** Chronic poor sleep elevates monocyte activity through low-grade inflammatory pathways.
- **Persistent low-grade infections.** Dental disease, chronic sinusitis, urinary tract infections, gut infections — anything keeping the immune system mildly engaged keeps monocytes mildly elevated.
- **Metabolic conditions.** Type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and fatty liver disease are associated with persistent low-grade monocyte elevation in research, reflecting chronic metabolic inflammation.
- **Obesity.** Adipose tissue produces inflammatory signals that recruit monocytes; sustained elevation is common in obesity.
- **Smoking.** Persistent monocyte elevation is one of the more durable methylation signals associated with tobacco use.
- **Cardiovascular conditions.** Active cardiovascular disease (atherosclerosis, peripheral artery disease) is associated with monocyte involvement at the vessel wall.

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, occasional alcohol use, normal seasonal variation. The signal is sensitive to sustained immune activation and persistent inflammatory contexts, not to one-off events.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your monocyte reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — a recent illness, a vaccination, a period of sustained poor sleep, an active dental issue, an unmanaged metabolic condition — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves within weeks to a few months once the underlying context is addressed.

Some monocyte elevations are tied to chronic context that takes longer to address — metabolic health, smoking cessation, persistent dental or sinus issues. These signals respond to sustained lifestyle changes rather than to short-term interventions, and trajectory monitoring across months to years tracks the recovery as the underlying context improves.

If you cannot identify a recent or ongoing context and the elevation is sustained, trajectory monitoring is the most useful next step. EDEAR tracks how this signal changes over time. A monocyte elevation that resolves over your next test was your body recovering from something it handled. A monocyte elevation that stays stable points toward chronic context worth addressing in conversation with your clinician. A monocyte elevation that intensifies over time is the pattern that warrants a more direct conversation with your clinician.

## Trajectory patterns

For subscribers with multiple tests, the monocyte trajectory tells a story that no single reading can. The signal responds within weeks to acute resolutions (illness clearing, vaccination response running its course) and over months to years to sustained lifestyle changes (smoking cessation, weight management, metabolic improvements, dental health, sustained reductions in chronic stress and sleep deprivation).

The trajectory is also informative when it doesn't move. A monocyte signal that stays elevated across multiple tests despite addressed lifestyle drivers is the kind of pattern that makes a conversation with your clinician more useful than any single reading would be.

## Research observations on monocytes

In research on prostate cancer cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed monocyte elevation in Stage 3 cell-fraction readings of prostate cancer patients compared to matched controls — one of the cleanest single-cell-type findings in the prostate research stack. The monocyte signal was observable in routine plasma cfDNA samples and tracked alongside the broader immune-class signal in the same cohort.

In research on bladder cancer tumor microenvironment, EDEAR's framework observed substantial monocyte expansion in tumor tissue alongside expansion of nearly every other immune cell type tested. This "broad immune infiltration" pattern is characteristic of bladder cancer in research — every immune subset tested showed elevation rather than a selective shift. For customers with plasma cfDNA samples (rather than tumor tissue), the peripheral monocyte signal is a different reading than the tumor-microenvironment signal — research on plasma cfDNA in bladder cancer is ongoing.

Notably, in research on long-term breast cancer pre-diagnostic cohorts (Sister Study, EPIC-Italy), EDEAR's framework observed monocyte signal that participated in the broader long-pre-dx attenuation pattern: elevation in long pre-diagnostic windows that ATTENUATED as clinical diagnosis approached. The 0-2 year pre-diagnostic monocyte signal was notably weaker than the >10 year pre-diagnostic signal.

In research on glioma blood cohorts, monocyte signal moved as part of the broader cell-fraction shift toward neutrophil dominance and lymphocyte depletion.

In research on various cardiovascular cohorts, monocyte signal has been observed in association with active cardiovascular disease — atherosclerosis, peripheral artery disease, aortic conditions. Monocytes participate directly in vascular inflammation, and the signal in research tracks with disease activity.

## Vigilance and recommended actions

Your report's recommended actions section is calibrated to what flagged. Below is what each tier means for monocytes specifically.

### If your monocyte reading is NORMAL

Routine monitoring. Continue the lifestyle habits that work for you. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has discussed with you. Retest with EDEAR at your standard subscription interval.

### If your monocyte reading is ELEVATED

Review whether any of the common context drivers apply to you over the past few weeks or months — recent illness, recent vaccination, sustained sleep deprivation, active dental or sinus issue, active metabolic condition (especially diabetes or fatty liver disease), recent intensive period. If something fits, addressing it is usually enough. If you have known cardiovascular disease, smoking history, or metabolic conditions, share this reading with your clinician at your next visit. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you on the schedule they recommended. Retest with EDEAR in 6 months instead of waiting a full year, so trajectory becomes informative.

### If your monocyte reading is SIGNIFICANTLY ELEVATED

We recommend you discuss this finding with your clinician. Bring this report. The conversation is more useful with the result in hand than without. Particularly relevant contexts to mention: family or personal history of cardiovascular disease; family history of cancer; metabolic conditions; sustained smoking history; chronic infections that haven't been addressed. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you, on the schedule they recommend, and ask whether they want to adjust anything in light of the EDEAR finding. Retest with EDEAR every 6 months going forward to track how the signal moves.

We do not recommend specific clinical follow-up tests or specific screening schedules — those are decisions for you and your clinician, based on your full health history, family history, and clinical context that your clinician has and we do not.

### If your monocyte reading is SUPPRESSED

A SUPPRESSED reading on monocytes is uncommon and is most commonly explained by declared context at intake — current immunosuppression, active chemotherapy, transplant status, certain biologic medications, or rare bone marrow conditions. If you've declared any of these contexts, the SUPPRESSED reading reflects what your body is doing in response to your treatment or condition. If you cannot identify a declared context that explains it, discuss the reading with your clinician on your next visit.

## Frequently asked questions about monocytes

**My monocyte reading is elevated and I have a recent dental cleaning that found inflammation. Does that explain it?**

Persistent low-grade dental inflammation is one of the more reliable drivers of sustained monocyte elevation. Addressing the dental issue typically brings the signal back down over weeks to a few months. If the elevation persists after addressing the dental context, that's worth tracking with trajectory monitoring.

**I have type 2 diabetes and my monocyte reading is elevated. What does this mean?**

Type 2 diabetes is associated with persistent low-grade monocyte elevation in research, reflecting chronic metabolic inflammation. The signal often improves with sustained metabolic management (blood glucose control, weight management, exercise). Your endocrinologist or primary care clinician can put the reading in the context of your overall metabolic management.

**I'm a smoker (or recent quitter) and my monocytes are elevated. Will this resolve?**

Tobacco-driven monocyte elevation is one of the more durable methylation signals associated with smoking. Methylation marks at smoking-related sites have a half-life of roughly 10 years post-cessation in research, with substantial improvement in the first 5 years. Trajectory monitoring across the years after cessation tracks the recovery.

**My monocytes are elevated but my other immune cells are normal. What does that mean?**

Isolated monocyte elevation suggests a specific kind of immune context — often metabolic, cardiovascular, or persistent low-grade tissue inflammation rather than active infection or autoimmune activity. Trajectory monitoring is informative; pairing the reading with conversation about your overall health context with your clinician is the right next step.

**My monocytes were elevated and now they're back to normal — what happened?**

Monocyte signal responds to context. If a recent illness, vaccination response, dental issue, sleep disruption, or other temporary driver was responsible, the signal resolves as the context resolves. Resolution is the expected response when the underlying context is addressed.
