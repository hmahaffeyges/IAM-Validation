## What you should know about CD8 T cells

- CD8 T cells are your immune system's cytotoxic effectors — they directly kill infected cells, cancerous cells, and other targets the immune system identifies as threats.
- Recent illness, recent vaccination, intense exercise, and chronic immune challenge mobilize them, and most elevations resolve within weeks to months.
- Persistent viral infections, autoimmune conditions, and active inflammatory states can keep them elevated longer-term.
- In research on multiple conditions including active inflammatory bowel disease and certain pre-diagnostic cancer windows, EDEAR has observed CD8 T cell expansion as part of broader patterns the immune system shows under stress; in advanced cancer research, CD8 cells in some contexts show depression rather than elevation as the immune compartment becomes exhausted.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

CD8 T cells are the killers of your adaptive immune response. When your body identifies an infected cell, a cell with abnormal protein expression (which can include some early cancer cells), or any cell flagged by the rest of the immune system as a threat, CD8 T cells are the ones that destroy it. They release toxic granules directly into the target cell to trigger its death, then move on to find more targets.

CD8 T cells are kept in two functional states: naive CD8 cells (covered separately on the naive CD8 T cells page) that haven't yet been deployed against any specific target, and memory CD8 cells that have fought specific threats before and are kept in reserve in case those threats return. The total CD8 count includes both. EDEAR reads CD8 T cells at the broader category level on this page, and resolves naive CD8 separately at higher resolution.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: CD8+ T cells, cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), killer T cells, CD8 effectors.

## Where these cells live

CD8 T cells are produced from precursors in the bone marrow, mature in the thymus, and circulate throughout the body in blood and lymphatic tissue. They concentrate at sites of active immune response — virus-infected tissue, tumor environments, sites of chronic inflammation. About 15-30% of your peripheral blood T cells are CD8 T cells at any given time, which is what EDEAR reads.

EDEAR's framework reads CD8 T cells through both the Stage 3 immune cell-type composition lens (using the production Salas IDOL 6-cell reference atlas, which includes a dedicated CD8 T cell category) and through higher-resolution Stage 3 readings using a 19-cell reference atlas (UniLIFE) that resolves naive vs memory CD8 sub-types separately.

The total CD8 reading on this page integrates both sub-types.

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, CD8 T cells are a substantial fraction of your circulating T cell compartment. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history. CD8 reference ranges shift with age — the proportion of memory CD8 cells increases as life exposures accumulate.

What moves CD8 T cells around:

- **Recent viral illness.** Acute viral infections rapidly mobilize CD8 cells. The signal stays elevated for weeks to months as your body finishes the response and converts some cells into memory.
- **Recent vaccination.** Vaccinations mobilize CD8 cells, particularly for vaccines targeting viral pathogens. Signal typically resolves over weeks.
- **Intense exercise periods.** Sustained high-intensity training mobilizes CD8 cells as part of the body's broader immune surveillance during physical stress.
- **Chronic viral infections.** Persistent viral infections (chronic HBV, HIV, CMV, EBV reactivation) keep CD8 cells engaged long-term.
- **Active autoimmune conditions.** Some autoimmune diseases (particularly those with viral or stress triggers) elevate CD8 cells.
- **Active inflammatory conditions.** Active IBD, certain forms of arthritis, and other inflammatory states can elevate CD8 cells.
- **Cancer immune surveillance.** Some cancers in research show CD8 expansion (immune system actively surveying); some show depression (immune exhaustion in advanced disease).

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, a one-time stressful event, occasional alcohol use, normal seasonal variation. The signal is sensitive to sustained immune activation.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your CD8 T cell reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — a recent viral illness, a vaccination in the past few weeks, an intensive training block, an autoimmune flare, a chronic infection — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves within weeks to a few months once the underlying context is addressed or runs its course.

If your CD8 reading is depressed (SUPPRESSED tier) without obvious explanation, that's a different kind of finding worth conversation with your clinician. CD8 depression in research has been associated with immune exhaustion in advanced cancer, immune compromise in HIV+ contexts, and certain bone marrow conditions. Most often a SUPPRESSED CD8 reading is explained by declared context at intake (active chemotherapy, immunosuppression, etc.); when no declared context explains it, clinical evaluation is the right next step.

## Trajectory patterns

For subscribers with multiple tests, the CD8 T cell trajectory tells a story that no single reading can. The signal responds within weeks to acute resolutions and over months to sustained changes. The trajectory is informative when it doesn't move — a CD8 signal that stays elevated across multiple tests despite addressed lifestyle drivers is the kind of pattern that makes a conversation with your clinician more useful than any single reading would be.

CD8 trajectory across cancer treatment is particularly informative for customers receiving treatment for known cancer — research has observed that CD8 cells respond to immunotherapy and chemotherapy in patterns that track with treatment response. Customers in active treatment should retest at regular intervals (typically every 3-6 months) to build a trajectory the oncologist can use.

## Research observations on CD8 T cells

In research on active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed substantial CD8 T cell expansion in participants with active Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. CD8 expansion was one of the consistent T-cell expansion findings in IBD research, alongside CD4 and naive CD8 T cell expansion.

In research on advanced gastric cancer cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed an opposite pattern: CD8 T cell depression alongside broader T-cell depletion in the immune microenvironment, while B cells were relatively preserved. This is a different kind of immune signature than either pre-diagnostic cancer (which often shows immune expansion) or active inflammation (which shows uniform T-cell expansion). Advanced cancer immune-microenvironment depression is interpreted in research as immune exhaustion — the immune system has been engaging the cancer for a long time and is depleted.

In research on long-term cancer pre-diagnostic cohorts, CD8 T cell signal participated in the broader immune-class patterns the framework reads at the class level, including the long pre-diagnostic immune-class drift across multiple cancer types.

In research on Alzheimer's disease blood cohorts, CD8 T cells participated in the broader bidirectional cancellation pattern at the class level — some immune cell types elevating while others depressed — that the framework reads through its directional sub-panel rather than through any single cell.

## Vigilance and recommended actions

Your report's recommended actions section is calibrated to what flagged. Below is what each tier means for CD8 T cells specifically.

### If your CD8 T cell reading is NORMAL

Routine monitoring. Continue the lifestyle habits that work for you. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has discussed with you. Retest with EDEAR at your standard subscription interval.

### If your CD8 T cell reading is ELEVATED

Review whether any of the common context drivers apply to you over the past few weeks or months — recent viral illness, recent vaccination, intensive training period, autoimmune flare, persistent infection (especially chronic viral). If something fits, addressing it is usually enough. If you have a known chronic viral infection or autoimmune condition, share this reading at your next specialist visit. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you on the schedule they recommended. Retest with EDEAR in 6 months instead of waiting a full year.

### If your CD8 T cell reading is SIGNIFICANTLY ELEVATED

We recommend you discuss this finding with your clinician. Bring this report. The conversation is more useful with the result in hand than without. If you have known chronic viral infection, autoimmune disease, or IBD, share the reading with the relevant specialist. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you, on the schedule they recommend, and ask whether they want to adjust anything in light of the EDEAR finding. Retest with EDEAR every 6 months going forward to track how the signal moves.

We do not recommend specific clinical follow-up tests or specific screening schedules.

### If your CD8 T cell reading is SUPPRESSED

A SUPPRESSED reading on CD8 T cells is most commonly explained by declared context at intake — current immunosuppression, active chemotherapy, certain biologic medications, advanced HIV+ status with low T cell counts, or other immune-compromising contexts. If you've declared one of these contexts, the SUPPRESSED reading reflects what your body is doing in response. **If you cannot identify a declared context that explains a SUPPRESSED CD8 reading, this is the kind of finding worth discussing with your clinician sooner rather than waiting for your next routine visit** — CD8 depression in research has been associated with immune exhaustion in advanced cancer cohorts and other clinically significant contexts.

## Frequently asked questions about CD8 T cells

**What's the difference between CD8 T cells and naive CD8 T cells?**

Naive CD8 cells are CD8 cells that haven't yet encountered their specific target. Memory CD8 cells have. Total CD8 includes both. EDEAR reads them separately because they behave differently. The naive CD8 T cells page covers naive specifically.

**My CD8 is elevated and I had a recent viral illness. Is this expected?**

Yes. Acute viral infections rapidly mobilize CD8 cells. The signal stays elevated for weeks as your body completes the response. Trajectory monitoring across the recovery is informative.

**I have a chronic viral infection (like chronic HBV or HIV). What should I expect?**

Chronic viral infections keep CD8 cells engaged long-term. Customers with known chronic viral conditions often show sustained CD8 elevation as a baseline. Trajectory monitoring tracks how the signal responds to treatment and disease activity.

**My CD8 is depressed and I'm in active cancer treatment. What does that mean?**

Active chemotherapy substantially affects rapidly-dividing cells including CD8 cells. SUPPRESSED CD8 readings are common during treatment and resolve as treatment ends. Trajectory monitoring across treatment cycles is informative for tracking immune recovery between cycles.

**My CD8 is elevated but my CD4 is normal. What does that mean?**

T cell subsets can move independently. CD8 elevation without CD4 movement suggests something is engaging the cytotoxic function specifically — viral infections and certain cancer immune surveillance patterns often produce this asymmetric finding. Trajectory monitoring is informative.
