## What you should know about macrophages

- Macrophages are your immune system's tissue cleanup and repair coordinators — they live IN your tissues (not just in blood) and clear damaged cells, debris, and pathogens while orchestrating tissue repair.
- Recent infections, injuries, surgical recovery, and chronic tissue inflammation all elevate them, and most elevations resolve as the underlying tissue context heals.
- Persistent metabolic conditions (diabetes, fatty liver disease), cardiovascular disease, obesity, and chronic inflammation can keep macrophages mildly elevated long-term.
- In research on cardiovascular conditions including aortic disease, EDEAR has observed macrophage signal as part of the immune infiltrate associated with vascular tissue damage.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

Macrophages are tissue-resident phagocytes — large cells that engulf and digest damaged cells, dead cells, microbes, and other material the immune system needs to clear. Unlike monocytes (which circulate in blood), macrophages live primarily IN tissues, where they are positioned for rapid local response.

There are two broad origins for tissue macrophages: tissue-resident macrophages that develop early during fetal life and self-renew in place (microglia in brain, Kupffer cells in liver, alveolar macrophages in lung, etc.), and circulating monocytes that migrate into tissue and differentiate into macrophages on demand.

Beyond their cleanup role, macrophages orchestrate tissue repair after injury, present antigens to other immune cells (helping to coordinate adaptive immune responses), and play important roles in regulating chronic inflammation and tissue homeostasis. Macrophage dysfunction is implicated in many chronic disease processes — atherosclerosis, fatty liver disease, certain cancers, chronic inflammatory conditions.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: tissue-resident macrophages, M1 macrophages (inflammatory subtype), M2 macrophages (repair/anti-inflammatory subtype), tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs in cancer research), CD68+ cells.

## Where these cells live

Macrophages are produced from precursors in the bone marrow (as monocytes) that circulate to tissue and differentiate, or from tissue-resident populations that self-renew in place. They are found throughout the body — every major organ has its own resident macrophage population. EDEAR reads macrophage signal in plasma cfDNA primarily through fragments shed from tissue-resident macrophages and from monocytes that have differentiated into tissue macrophages.

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, macrophage signal in plasma cfDNA is a small but measurable fraction. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history. Smoking measurably elevates macrophage activity through persistent low-grade tissue damage, particularly in the airways and vascular system.

What moves macrophages around:

- **Recent illness with tissue involvement.** Infections that damage tissue (pneumonia, severe bronchitis, urinary tract infections, gut infections) elevate macrophage activity for weeks during the cleanup and repair phase.
- **Recent injury or surgery.** Wound healing involves substantial macrophage recruitment for weeks to months.
- **Chronic inflammatory tissue conditions.** Inflammatory bowel disease, chronic respiratory conditions, chronic kidney disease, and other chronic conditions involving tissue inflammation keep macrophages elevated.
- **Metabolic conditions.** Type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, and metabolic syndrome are associated with persistent low-grade macrophage activation in research.
- **Obesity.** Adipose tissue produces inflammatory signals that recruit macrophages; sustained elevation is common in obesity.
- **Smoking.** Persistent macrophage elevation is one of the durable signals of tobacco use, particularly in the airways.
- **Cardiovascular disease.** Active atherosclerosis, peripheral artery disease, and aortic conditions involve macrophage infiltration of vascular tissue.
- **Some cancers (tumor-associated macrophages).** Some cancers attract macrophages into the tumor microenvironment where they can have complex roles in disease progression.

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, occasional alcohol use, normal seasonal variation. The signal is sensitive to sustained tissue-level immune activity and chronic inflammatory contexts.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your macrophage reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — recent illness with tissue involvement, recent injury or surgery, an active metabolic condition, persistent chronic inflammation — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves over weeks to months once the underlying tissue context heals or improves.

Some macrophage elevations are tied to chronic context that takes longer to address — metabolic health, smoking cessation, weight management, control of chronic inflammatory conditions. These signals respond to sustained lifestyle changes rather than short-term interventions, and trajectory monitoring across months to years tracks the recovery as the underlying context improves.

If you cannot identify a recent or ongoing context and the elevation is sustained, trajectory monitoring is the most useful next step.

## Trajectory patterns

For subscribers with multiple tests, the macrophage trajectory tells a story that no single reading can. The signal responds within weeks to months to acute resolutions (illness clearing, wound healing) and over months to years to sustained lifestyle changes (smoking cessation, weight management, metabolic improvements, cardiovascular risk reduction).

The trajectory is also informative when it doesn't move. A macrophage signal that stays elevated across multiple tests despite addressed lifestyle drivers is the kind of pattern that makes a conversation with your clinician more useful than any single reading would be.

## Research observations on macrophages

In research on aortic disease cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed substantial macrophage signal in aortic tissue from patients with bicuspid aortic valve plus aortic dilation (BAV+dilation) and from patients with aortic dissection. The macrophage signal in research reflected immune cell infiltration of the affected vascular tissue — a documented feature of aortic disease pathology where macrophages contribute to both vascular inflammation and tissue remodeling. For peripheral plasma readings, the macrophage signal is more subtle than the tissue-level reading, but customers with cardiovascular family history or known cardiovascular risk factors should mention macrophage elevation alongside any stromal-class signals to their clinician.

In research on broader cardiovascular contexts, macrophages participate in atherosclerotic plaque formation and progression. Research has documented macrophage involvement at vessel walls during active atherosclerosis, with the signal tracking with disease activity.

In research on tumor microenvironments across multiple cancer types, macrophages contribute to tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) populations that can have complex roles in cancer progression. EDEAR's primary reading for cancer signal uses other markers (Stage 1 panel, Stage 2 cell-of-origin, Stage 3 immune cell-fraction shifts). Macrophage signal in plasma cfDNA in advanced cancer cohorts is an area of EDEAR's continuing research.

## Frequently asked questions about macrophages

**What's the difference between monocytes and macrophages?**

Monocytes circulate in blood. When they migrate into tissue and stay, they differentiate into macrophages. So macrophages are essentially the tissue version of monocytes (with some tissue-specific exceptions like microglia and Kupffer cells that develop differently). EDEAR reads them as separate cell types because their methylation signatures differ enough to resolve them.

**My macrophages are elevated and I have type 2 diabetes. Is that expected?**

Type 2 diabetes is associated with persistent low-grade macrophage activation in research, reflecting chronic metabolic inflammation. The signal often improves with sustained metabolic management.

**Can macrophage readings track healing from surgery or injury?**

Yes. Wound healing involves substantial macrophage recruitment over weeks to months. Customers tracking recovery from surgery or significant injury can see macrophage trajectory reflect the healing process — typically elevation in the weeks after the event, with gradual return toward baseline over months as tissue repair completes.

**My macrophages are elevated and I had recent dental work or other tissue procedure. Could that explain it?**

Yes. Tissue procedures of any kind (dental, surgical, biopsy) recruit macrophages to the site for cleanup and repair. The signal resolves as healing completes.

**Why was macrophage signal mentioned alongside cardiovascular conditions on this page?**

Research has documented macrophage involvement in atherosclerosis, aortic disease, and other cardiovascular conditions. Macrophages contribute to vascular inflammation and tissue remodeling. Customers with cardiovascular risk factors or family history can use macrophage readings as one signal among many that their clinician can put in clinical context.
