## What you should know about naive CD8 T cells

- Naive CD8 T cells are your immune system's "ready reserves" — fresh cytotoxic T cells that have not yet been deployed against any specific target.
- Recent illness, recent vaccination, and active immune challenge mobilize them, and most elevations resolve within weeks as your body finishes responding.
- Vigorous exercise, high-intensity training periods, and chronic immune activation (autoimmune disease, persistent low-grade infection) can keep them elevated longer.
- In research on active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts, EDEAR has observed substantial naive CD8 T cell expansion in blood — one of the strongest signals associated with active IBD activity in research.

## What this cell does (the longer-form version)

CD8 T cells are the cytotoxic effectors of your adaptive immune response. When your body identifies a threat — a virus-infected cell, a cancer cell, a foreign tissue — CD8 T cells are the ones that go in and kill it. The "naive" subset of CD8 T cells are the ones that haven't been deployed yet. They've matured in the thymus, they're circulating, and they're waiting to be activated when a specific threat shows up.

When your body needs more cytotoxic firepower — fighting a virus, mounting a response to vaccination, responding to chronic immune challenge — naive CD8 T cells get mobilized and start dividing rapidly. The rapid expansion shows up clearly in cellular methylation patterns, which is what EDEAR reads.

Other names you may see for these cells in research literature: CD8+ naive T cells, CD8+ Tnv, naive cytotoxic T cells, T-naive cells.

## Where these cells live

Naive CD8 T cells are produced from precursor cells in the bone marrow, mature in the thymus, and circulate throughout the body in blood and lymphatic tissue. They concentrate in lymph nodes, where they are presented with antigens by dendritic cells. A meaningful fraction of your peripheral blood T cells are naive CD8 T cells at any given time, which is what EDEAR reads.

## How EDEAR reads them

EDEAR's framework reads naive CD8 T cells through the Stage 3 immune cell-type composition lens, using a 19-cell reference atlas (UniLIFE) that includes a dedicated naive CD8 T cell category. The 19-cell resolution separates naive CD8 cells specifically from the broader CD8 T cell population — at lower-resolution 6-cell deconvolution, naive and memory CD8 cells aggregate into a single CD8 category and the naive-specific signal is invisible.

This higher-resolution reading matters because naive and memory CD8 cells move differently in some research patterns. A customer whose total CD8 reading looks normal might still have a naive CD8 signal that the framework picks up specifically.

## Healthy reference range and what affects it

At healthy baseline, naive CD8 T cells are a substantial fraction of your total T cell compartment — generally larger in younger adults and gradually declining with age as more of your T cells convert to memory subsets through cumulative exposures. Your specific reference range is calibrated for your age, sex, and smoking history.

What moves naive CD8 T cells around:

- **Recent vaccination.** Vaccination mobilizes naive CD8 T cells as your body builds new immune memory. The signal typically peaks within a few weeks after vaccination and resolves over the following months.
- **Recent viral illness.** Acute viral infections rapidly mobilize naive CD8 T cells. The signal stays elevated for weeks to months as your body finishes the response and converts some naive cells into memory cells.
- **Vigorous exercise periods.** Sustained high-intensity training mobilizes naive T cells as part of the body's broader immune surveillance during physical stress.
- **Active autoimmune conditions.** Active autoimmune disease keeps naive T cells mobilized as your body manages persistent immune activation.
- **Chronic infections.** Persistent infections (chronic viral, dental, sinus) keep the naive T cell compartment activated.
- **Active inflammatory bowel disease.** Active Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis flares are particularly associated with substantial naive CD8 T cell expansion in research.

What does NOT typically affect them: a single bad night of sleep, occasional alcohol use, normal seasonal variation in activity, ordinary day-to-day stress. The signal is sensitive to sustained immune activation, not to one-off events.

## Lifestyle interactions

If your naive CD8 T cell reading is elevated and you can identify a recent context — a vaccination in the past few weeks, a recent viral illness, an intensive training block, a flare of a known autoimmune condition — that context is usually the explanation. The signal typically resolves within weeks to a few months once the underlying context is addressed or runs its course.

If you cannot identify a recent context and the elevation is sustained, trajectory monitoring is the most useful next step. EDEAR tracks how this signal changes over time. A naive CD8 elevation that resolves over your next test was your body finishing a response. A naive CD8 elevation that stays stable points toward chronic immune activation worth addressing in conversation with your clinician. A naive CD8 elevation that intensifies over time is the pattern that warrants a more direct conversation with your clinician.

## Trajectory patterns

For subscribers with multiple tests, the naive CD8 T cell trajectory tells a story that no single reading can. The signal responds within weeks to the resolution of acute immune challenges — a vaccination response runs its course, an illness clears, a training block ends — so subscribers tracking these contexts often see the signal trend back toward their baseline within one or two retest intervals.

The trajectory is also informative when it doesn't move. A naive CD8 signal that stays elevated across multiple tests despite no clear active immune context is the kind of pattern that makes a conversation with your clinician more useful than any single reading would be.

## Research observations on naive CD8 T cells

In research on active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts, EDEAR's framework observed substantial naive CD8 T cell expansion in participants with active Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. The naive CD8 signal was one of the strongest expansion signals in the research, alongside expansion of total CD4 and CD8 T cell populations.

Standard clinical detection of inflammatory bowel disease relies on symptoms (abdominal pain, diarrhea, weight loss, fatigue) and confirmatory testing (colonoscopy, imaging, biopsy). The naive CD8 T cell signal in research is observable in routine blood draw and tracks active disease activity.

**The integrated population-fraction-shift signature across active IBD cohorts — T-cell expansion (CD4, CD8, naive CD8, regulatory T, NK) alongside concurrent monocyte and neutrophil depression — lives on the immune class page in Section 4 (Pattern B — Population-fraction-shift). The class page section also covers other conditions predicted to produce similar patterns (chronic viral infections, active autoimmune disease flares, sepsis, GVHD post-transplant) and what trajectory monitoring tells you for each.** Customers whose reports show naive CD8 T cell expansion alongside T-up / myeloid-down patterns should read both this page (for naive-CD8-specific context) and the immune class page Pattern B section (for the integrated multi-cell story).

In the same research cohorts, naive CD8 T cell signals were also observed in other chronic inflammatory contexts — autoimmune disease activity, chronic viral infections, periods of sustained immune challenge. The framework cannot distinguish active IBD from these other contexts from a single sample. Trajectory across multiple samples adds the temporal context that makes the signature interpretable.

## Vigilance and recommended actions

Your report's recommended actions section is calibrated to what flagged. Below is what each tier means for naive CD8 T cells specifically.

### If your naive CD8 T cell reading is NORMAL

Routine monitoring. Continue the lifestyle habits that work for you. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has discussed with you. Retest with EDEAR at your standard subscription interval.

### If your naive CD8 T cell reading is ELEVATED

Review whether any of the common context drivers apply to you over the past few weeks or months — recent vaccination, recent viral illness, intensive training period, autoimmune flare, persistent infection (dental, sinus, gut). If something fits, the signal will likely resolve as the context resolves. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you on the schedule they recommended. Retest with EDEAR in 6 months instead of waiting a full year, so trajectory becomes informative — a signal that resolves was your body completing a response; a signal that stays or intensifies is more useful information for a clinician conversation. If you have a known autoimmune or inflammatory condition, share this reading at your next check-in with your specialist.

### If your naive CD8 T cell reading is SIGNIFICANTLY ELEVATED

We recommend you discuss this finding with your clinician. Bring this report. The conversation is more useful with the result in hand than without. If you have known IBD, autoimmune disease, or chronic infection, share the reading with the relevant specialist as well. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has given you, on the schedule they recommend, and ask whether they want to adjust anything in light of the EDEAR finding. Retest with EDEAR every 6 months going forward to track how the signal moves.

We do not recommend specific clinical follow-up tests or specific screening schedules — those are decisions for you and your clinician, based on your full health history, family history, and clinical context that your clinician has and we do not.

### If your naive CD8 T cell reading is SUPPRESSED

A SUPPRESSED reading on naive CD8 T cells is uncommon and is most commonly explained by declared context at intake — current immunosuppression, active chemotherapy (which affects rapidly-dividing cells preferentially), HIV+ status with low CD4 counts, age-related thymic involution in older adults, or certain biologic medications. If you've declared any of these contexts, the SUPPRESSED reading reflects what your body is doing in response to your treatment, condition, or age. If you cannot identify a declared context that explains it, discuss the reading with your clinician on your next visit.

## Frequently asked questions about naive CD8 T cells

**What's the difference between naive and memory CD8 T cells?**

Naive CD8 T cells haven't yet encountered their specific target. Memory CD8 T cells have — they've fought a specific pathogen before and are kept in reserve in case it comes back. The two subsets behave differently, and EDEAR reads them separately. As you age, more of your CD8 T cells convert from naive to memory through cumulative life exposures, which is why naive CD8 reference ranges decline with age.

**I just got a vaccination — should I expect my naive CD8 T cells to be elevated?**

Yes, often for several weeks after vaccination. The body mobilizes naive T cells to build memory against whatever the vaccination targets. The signal resolves over the following weeks to months as your body completes the response. Customers should report recent vaccinations at intake so the report acknowledges the context.

**I have IBD and my reading is elevated. Does this match what research shows?**

Research has consistently observed substantial naive CD8 T cell expansion in active IBD. Customers with known IBD whose readings are elevated are seeing the same pattern research cohorts show during active disease activity. The reading does not tell you anything new about whether you have IBD (you already know); it can be informative about activity level and trajectory across treatment, which is something your gastroenterologist can use.

**I exercise intensely and my reading is elevated. Should I be concerned?**

Sustained vigorous training mobilizes naive T cells as part of the immune system's response to physical stress. Athletes and high-intensity training participants often show elevated naive T cell readings. Trajectory monitoring across training and recovery periods is informative; a signal that responds to your training schedule is what you'd expect.

**My naive CD8 is elevated but I can't identify a recent context. What now?**

Trajectory monitoring is the most useful next step. Many subtle immune contexts — sub-clinical infections, early autoimmune activity, chronic low-grade inflammation from the gut microbiome or dental health — may not be obvious to the customer but show up in the cellular reading. Retest in 6 months. If the signal resolves, your body handled whatever it was. If the signal stays or intensifies, that pattern in conversation with your clinician is more useful than guessing.
