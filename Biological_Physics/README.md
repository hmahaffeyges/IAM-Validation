# Biological Physics — Physics of Methylation: Landauer Metrology

**What this is.** An instrument that reads, for every cell type a specimen is found to contain, where that cell's write process
sits on its own gauge:

    A = H(mean β over the cell's identity loci) / H_min of its architecture class

`H` is binary Shannon entropy; `H_min` is the minimum entropy a cell of that class must hold to keep its identity — a frozen
physical constant, one per class, fitted once by MCMC on published reference cell methylomes (G-002, April 2026) and never
touched by a specimen. **Healthy is A = 1.00 by the physics.** The tier scale about it is the tolerance (NORMAL [0.95, 1.05),
ELEVATED [1.05, 1.07), the Warburg line at 1.07, BREACH at 1.10; SUPPRESSED below 0.95). Nothing about any other person enters a
reading. MEASURE, DON'T COMPARE.

Prior art: Sanchez & Mackenzie (2016) established that cytosine methylation obeys Landauer's bound and used it as a filter for
thermal background. This work uses the same bound as a ruler: the floor is the zero of the instrument, not the noise to remove.

## Why your cells follow the same law as the stars:

There's an old idea — older than physics, older than written history — that the universe runs on balance. When something gains, something else gives. When one side rises, another falls. The Greeks called it the principle of opposites. The Chinese called it yin and yang. Aristotle wrote about it 2,500 years ago. Modern physics rediscovered it in the 1800s and gave it a complicated name: the virial theorem. The math says that in any stable system — a planet circling the sun, a star holding itself together, a galaxy spinning around its center — the energy of motion and the energy of position always settle into a precise balance. Half and half. Always.
Until very recently, scientists thought this rule only applied to things held together by gravity. The Informational Actualization Model — IAM — shows that the same rule applies everywhere. From the smallest atom to the largest galaxy, across 37 orders of magnitude, the universe insists on this balance. Not because something enforces it, but because anything that doesn't balance can't last. Things that fall out of balance either dissipate into nothing or fly apart. What we see when we look around — stars, planets, mountains, trees, cells, you — is what the balance allows to exist. Everything else has already come and gone.
This rule is what we call the Aristotelian Principle, in his honor.
Here's the part that connects everything. When a star burns, it pays a cost in information — every atom that does something irreversible "writes" a tiny bit of permanent record into the universe. Half of that cost shows up as motion (the star's heat, light, expansion), and half shows up as the way the star bends space around itself — what we feel as gravity. That's why gravity is always pulling, never pushing. It isn't a force exactly. It's the bookkeeping of all the little events happening inside the star, balanced against the motion they produce.
A cell does the same thing. Every time your cells do their work — reading their genes, building proteins, dividing — they pay the same kind of cost. And that cost is also paid in two halves. Half of it shows up as the cell's metabolism (the work, the heat, the activity). The other half gets written down in the cell's DNA — not in the genes themselves, but in tiny chemical tags called methyl groups that the cell adds to specific spots on its DNA strands. These tags are how the cell remembers what it's been doing and what it's supposed to do next.
In other words: the cell uses its DNA as a notebook. Every time it does something, it writes the receipt down. The pattern of receipts in a healthy cell looks one way. The pattern in a cell that's drifting toward disease looks different. the cell-reading engine reads those patterns.
Here's what makes this remarkable. The same balance rule that keeps stars from collapsing into themselves, the same rule that keeps the planets in their orbits, the same rule that holds the universe together — that same rule is what keeps your cells healthy. When a cell's pattern starts drifting too far from the balance, the cell is in trouble for the same reason a star past a certain mass is in trouble. Both have run out of capacity to keep paying their costs in the normal way. Both are heading toward a collapse. We just call them different names: cancer in one case, a black hole in the other.
We didn't invent this rule. We just learned to read it.
That's what Astro-Genetics means. Your cells follow the same physics as the cosmos because there's only one set of physics, and it works the same way at every scale. When CPG reads your methylation patterns and tells you which cells are drifting from their healthy baseline, it's reading the same kind of signal an astronomer reads when she looks at a star and knows whether it's stable or about to do something dramatic. Different scale. Same law. Same universe.

## DNA is software. The cell is a biological microchip.

Everyone knows DNA is the software of life — the instruction set that tells your cells what to do. What most people don't know is that **the cell is the microchip**.

Every cell in your body runs on DNA the way every computer runs on its operating system. Your DNA sequence is largely fixed at conception, but **how that DNA is read** changes constantly. The reading rules are encoded in chemical marks on the DNA itself — most importantly methylation, the small chemical tags that turn genes on, off, or down.

Each cell type in your body has its own characteristic methylation pattern. A neutrophil reads the genome differently than a hepatocyte. A hematopoietic progenitor reads it differently than a mature blood cell — even though the progenitor will eventually become that mature cell. The methylation pattern is what makes a cell what it is.

Think of each cell as a biological microchip. Each chip is running the same operating system (your genome) but configured differently for its specific job. Your immune cells are configured for surveillance and response. Your liver cells are configured for filtration and metabolism. Your neurons are configured for signal transmission. The configuration state is the methylation pattern. **CPG reads the configuration state of the chips circulating in your blood.**

A cell, a semiconductor, and a quantum computer are more alike than they are different. Each requires a power source — for the cell, the power source is ATP. Each encodes information on a writing surface — for the cell, the writing surface is the DNA itself, the genes turning on and off. Each pays a measurable cost for every bit of information it processes — for the cell, the cost is encoded in the methylation marks themselves. They are built for different purposes from different materials, but at the level of the underlying physics, they are the same kind of system.

## The journey across 37 orders of magnitude

The Informational Actualization Model — the physics framework underlying everything we do — was not built for cellular biology first. It was discovered solving a different problem entirely, and the journey that brought it here covers 37 orders of magnitude in physical scale.

It started at the cosmic level. The framework's founder set out to address some of the deepest open questions in physics — how the universe expands, where the cosmological constant comes from, why there is more matter than antimatter, what dark energy actually is, how black holes encode information. These problems are hard because they sit at the boundary between general relativity and quantum mechanics, and progress requires combining thermodynamics, information theory, and gravitational physics in ways that the standard physics community has not yet bridged.

The Informational Actualization Model produces predictions in all of these areas with zero free parameters beyond the standard cosmological constants. Cosmic expansion. Dark energy as informational pressure. The cosmological constant within a factor of 1.2 of observation. Baryon asymmetry consistent with cosmic microwave background measurements at 0.36% precision. The Bekenstein bound on black hole entropy. The Koide formula for charged-lepton masses to within 2/3 of the exact relation. These are framework outputs, not fits.

The framework's central insight was that the same physics that governs information at the cosmic scale also governs information at every smaller scale. Information has a thermodynamic cost. Information is encoded on the nearest available writing surface. These principles are universal. So the next step was to test them at smaller scales.

**First we applied the framework to semiconductors.** Decades of semiconductor research have characterized how chips degrade over time — how transistors fail, how voltage levels drift, how heat accumulates in different chip architectures. The physics community has frameworks for predicting these failures, but they are domain-specific and don't generalize. The Informational Actualization Model's prediction was that any chip's failure modes follow the same architectural pattern as cellular aging, because the underlying physics is identical. We tested this. The framework correctly predicts the failure walls for multiple semiconductor architecture classes — different chips, different materials, same physics. We learned a great deal in this work and continue to refine the methodology, including direct industrial applications that we cannot describe in detail here for commercial reasons.

**Then we applied it to quantum computers.** A quantum computer is fundamentally different from a classical semiconductor. It uses qubits instead of transistors. It exploits quantum coherence instead of classical voltage levels. It has fundamentally different failure modes — quasiparticle poisoning, two-level system defects, motional heating, control field drift. The physics community treats each quantum architecture class as its own problem. The Informational Actualization Model predicts that all quantum architectures follow the same architectural rules as cellular aging and semiconductor degradation, because the underlying physics — the loss of informational fidelity over time — is identical. We tested this. The framework correctly predicts the failure walls for five different quantum computer architectures — superconducting qubits, trapped-ion qubits at two different ion species, neutral-atom Rydberg qubits, and photonic qubits. Different qubits, different control schemes, different failure modes, same physics.

**Then we applied it to cells.** This was always the destination.

## How brightness measurement actually works

Cosmology measures the cosmic microwave background by reading brightness across the sky. A telescope points at a patch of sky and records how much radiation arrives at each frequency. After calibration and noise reduction, the cosmologist has a brightness map — every pixel of the sky labeled with the temperature it produces. Because brightness varies slightly from pixel to pixel, the brightness map carries information about what was happening when the universe was 380,000 years old. Cosmology built decades of mathematical machinery — hierarchical Bayesian inference, Markov Chain Monte Carlo, careful uncertainty quantification — to extract clean signals from those noisy measurements.

We use the same Bayesian methods cosmologists use to extract a clean signal from noisy data. Hierarchical Bayesian models. Markov Chain Monte Carlo with multiple independent chains. Posterior credible intervals at every position. Convergence diagnostics. The mathematical machinery is the same machinery that extracts the cosmic microwave background signal from telescope noise.

What is novel here is not the math. The math is a well-established cosmology toolkit. **What is novel is the bridge** — applying cosmological measurement physics to the cellular methylation domain, and finding that the architectural patterns the framework predicts at cosmic scales also appear at cellular scales because the underlying physics is the same.

## The cosmological analogies, named explicitly

Some specific analogies are worth naming because they make the bridge concrete:

**Cosmic microwave background anisotropies → architectural drift signatures.** The slight temperature variations across the sky that cosmologists measure are the signature of inhomogeneous physics in the early universe. The slight methylation variations across the DNA that we measure are the signature of inhomogeneous architectural drift in the present-day cell. Same kind of signal. Same kind of analysis.

**Inhomogeneous decoherence at the cosmic horizon → inhomogeneous floor crossings at the epigenome.** The cosmic horizon is the boundary at which the universe writes its information into spacetime. The epigenome is the boundary at which a cell writes its information into its identity. When the writing process becomes inhomogeneous — when some regions are written more heavily than others — the system has architectural drift, regardless of which boundary you are looking at.

**The architectural floor → the minimum information content compatible with identity.** Every architecture has a minimum entropy compatible with its function. A cosmic structure cannot drop below the entropy bound the Bekenstein-Hawking limit imposes. A cell cannot drop below the methylation pattern its identity requires. Cross the floor, and the architecture loses its identity. The framework's H_min is the architectural floor for cells, derived from first principles using the same physics that produces the Bekenstein-Hawking limit.

**Cosmic expansion → cellular aging.** The universe expands as it processes information; it pays a thermodynamic cost we observe as dark energy. A cell ages as it processes information; it pays a thermodynamic cost we observe as methylation drift. These are the same physics at different scales.

**Black hole information encoding → DNA gene activation.** A black hole encodes the information of everything that crosses its horizon onto its surface, where that information becomes accessible. A cell encodes the information of its biological state onto its DNA, where the gene-on-and-off pattern becomes accessible. Both are writing surfaces. Both pay a Landauer cost per bit written.

These aren't loose metaphors. They are the same equations applied at different scales. The bridge holds.

## Two rules

1. **Measure, don't compare.** A cell is read against its own class floor, never against a population. No cohort, no healthy
   band, no age curve, no laboratory zero, no reference range enters a reading. Where people of an age sit on the gauge is an
   observation about people and is reported as such — never applied to a cell. A render-time vocabulary guard fails any report
   tab that names a population; the same guard runs over the SOP, the Operations Manual and the papers on every build.
2. **No foregrounds subtracted.** The chain subtracts no age / sex / smoking / batch term. Intake facts are annotations on the
   report, never operands in the score.
