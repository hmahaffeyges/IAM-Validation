## Section 1 — What the immune class is (the headline read)

A short opening that frames why the immune system is what EDEAR measures first and most carefully. Three or four paragraphs.

The immune class is the body's surveillance system. Roughly 70 percent of the cell-free DNA in a typical blood sample comes from immune cells. When you give EDEAR a blood sample, most of what we read is your immune system's current state. That is not a limitation — it is a window. The immune system is in constant conversation with the rest of the body. When something is happening anywhere — tissue repair, infection, sustained stress, early disease — the immune system shifts in characteristic ways. Reading that shift carefully is the foundation of cellular health monitoring.

The immune class is also the class that responds fastest to lifestyle change. Sleep, stress, exercise, alcohol, nutrition, and recent illness all show up in immune class methylation within weeks to months. This makes the immune class the primary trajectory signal for most subscribers — what you do to your body shows up here first.

The page explains that EDEAR reports immune health at three levels: the overall immune class score, individual immune cell type scores, and (for subscribers with multiple tests) the trajectory across time. Each level is useful in its own way.

## Section 2 — Your immune class score (interpreting your specific number)

A section that takes the customer's actual reported score and helps them understand what range they are in.

Visual element: a graph showing where the customer's number falls against age-matched and smoking-history-matched normal ranges. Their dot, the normal range distribution behind it, the labels NORMAL / ELEVATED / SIGNIFICANTLY ELEVATED / SUPPRESSED annotated on the curve. This is the picture worth a thousand words for the customer.

Interpretive paragraphs based on which range they are in:

**If NORMAL:** A short paragraph reassuring that this is the great majority of healthy adults. The customer's immune class is operating well within the typical range for someone their age and history. Brief discussion of why this is good news but should not be taken as permanent — the body changes, and the trajectory is the real signal. Encouragement to retest at the standard interval (probably every 3-6 months for engaged subscribers, every 12 months for casual subscribers — TBD by product team).

**If ELEVATED:** A longer, more careful paragraph. Mild elevation can have many causes. The page lists them in order of probability: recent illness within the past 1-3 months (very common), recent vaccination, sustained stress periods, allergies in active season, alcohol use, sleep deprivation over weeks, autoimmune conditions, chronic low-grade infection (sinusitis, gum disease, etc.), early stages of conditions that are years away from any clinical manifestation. The page is honest that EDEAR cannot tell you which of these is driving your specific result. The page explains what trajectory monitoring will tell you that no single test can: whether the elevation is stable (probably explained by ongoing context like chronic low-grade inflammation), resolving (something your body is recovering from), or intensifying (worth taking more seriously). Recommended actions: review whether any of the listed contexts apply to you, address what you can address in lifestyle (sleep, stress, alcohol, etc.), continue routine annual checkups with your primary care provider as you would anyway, retest in 3-6 months to see your trajectory.

**If SIGNIFICANTLY ELEVATED:** The most carefully written paragraph on the page. Significant elevation is uncommon in healthy adults. The page is honest: while many of the same causes as ELEVATED can drive significant elevation if they are particularly intense or sustained, significant elevation is the level at which we recommend you review the result with your primary care provider so they can put it in the context of your specific health history. EDEAR does not diagnose. EDEAR has measured your immune class and found it to be running noticeably above the typical range for someone of your demographic and history. Your doctor can take that information into consideration alongside everything else they know about you. The page walks through what to bring to the appointment: your EDEAR report, the date of testing, any recent illnesses, any medications, anything else you think might be relevant. Recommendation to retest in 3 months to track trajectory regardless of doctor's findings.

**If SUPPRESSED:** Lower than typical can mean active immunosuppression (medications, transplant), reduced immune compartment due to recent intensive treatment (chemotherapy, certain biologics), HIV or other immune-compromising conditions, age-related immune compartment changes, or in rare cases an underlying condition reducing immune cell turnover. The page is gentle and honest: this tier is uncommon, has multiple possible explanations, and is worth discussing with your primary care provider if you don't have an obvious explanation (current medications, recent treatment, declared transplant or HIV status). When the customer has declared one of those contexts at intake, the page acknowledges it explicitly: "You reported you are currently receiving chemotherapy. SUPPRESSED-tier readings are expected during active treatment and reflect your immune system's response to therapy, not a separate finding."

In all cases, the page emphasizes that a single number is one data point and the trajectory is what matters most.

## Section 3 — Your immune cell types in detail

A section that takes the customer's per-cell-type results and walks through each one. The customer sees the cell types where they have a clean reading; cell types without sufficient posterior resolution are aggregated up to the class score.

For each immune cell type, the page has a sub-section explaining:

- **What this cell type does.** Plain language: "CD4 T cells are the coordinators of your adaptive immune response. They identify specific threats and tell other immune cells what to do." Or: "Monocytes are the first responders that arrive at sites of infection or tissue damage and clean up debris while signaling for backup." One paragraph each, written for an engaged reader.

- **What the customer's score means for this cell type.** Their number, where it falls in the cell-type-specific normal range, and one sentence of plain-language interpretation.

- **What research has observed in this cell type.** A brief, citation-supported summary of what shifts in this cell type have been associated with in published research. Each cell type's content draws on specific anchored findings (the writer needs the source; the customer reads the descriptive framing without VAL IDs).

- **Lifestyle factors that affect this cell type.** Sleep, stress, alcohol, persistent infections, and other modifiable factors and the timeline customers typically see in their numbers.

The cell type list and per-cell-type research-anchored content, organized by immune lineage:

**B-cell lineage (4 pages — separate because B-lineage compartments diverge in different malignancies):**

**B cells** (umbrella) — antibody production and memory. Research has observed B cell expansion in bladder cancer tumor microenvironment research at d=+1.15 (consistent with mixed tumor-infiltrating-lymphocyte infiltration), and B cell preservation in advanced gastric cancer cohorts where T cells deplete strongly — a B-cell-rich pattern associated with certain cancer subtypes in research.

**Naive B cells** — B cells that have not yet encountered an antigen. Research has documented naive B cell elevation at d=+0.44 to +0.49 in the 0 to 2 year pre-diagnostic window of breast cancer (UniLIFE 19-cell sub-cell-type resolution on EPIC-Italy cohorts) — a near-diagnosis pattern distinct from the long-pre-dx regulatory T cell signal.

**Memory B cells** — long-lived B cells with prior antigen exposure. Memory B cells expand in chronic viral infections (CMV, EBV) and in autoimmune disease (lupus, rheumatoid arthritis); memory B can be expanded while naive B is depressed — an opposite-direction pattern.

**Plasma cells** — terminally-differentiated antibody-producing cells. Plasma cell elevation has been associated with multiple myeloma research cohorts, where plasma cells expand clonally while general B cells stay normal or DOWN. In B-cell lymphomas, the opposite pattern — general B-cell expansion while plasma cells stay normal.

**T-cell lineage (5 pages — naive CD4 and naive CD8 separated because of HIV opposite-direction risk):**

**CD4 T cells** (umbrella) — coordinator role. Research has observed sustained CD4 T cell elevation in active autoimmune conditions and in active inflammatory bowel disease where research found CD4 expansion at d=+1.64 to +1.66 in peripheral blood. Most people with elevated CD4 T cells do not have any of these conditions.

**CD8 T cells** (umbrella) — cytotoxic effector role. Research has observed CD8 expansion at d=+1.70 in active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts and CD8 depletion in advanced gastric cancer research where the immune microenvironment is suppressed.

**Naive CD4 T cells** — helper T cells that have not yet encountered antigen. Naive CD4 T cell depletion has been documented in chronic HIV-positive customers (the virus selectively targets the CD4 lineage) and in age-related immunosenescence.

**Naive CD8 T cells** — cytotoxic T cells that have not yet encountered antigen. Research has observed naive CD8 T cell expansion at d=+1.72 in active inflammatory bowel disease cohorts, consistent with rapid T cell mobilization in active immune response.

**Memory T cells** — T cells that have already encountered their antigen and are ready for rapid recall response. Memory T cell pool dynamics shift measurably with cumulative immune exposure, vaccination history, and chronic-viral-infection status (CMV-driven memory inflation is the canonical example).

**Regulatory T cells** — immune-suppression coordinators. The research finding most relevant to the trajectory product: regulatory T cell elevation has been observed at d=+1.26 in 10-or-more-years-before-breast-cancer-diagnosis cohorts in research, with the signal weakening as the diagnostic window approaches. This pattern is invisible at the broader CD4 aggregate level — it requires the regulatory T cell sub-resolution to see.

**NK and granulocyte lineages (4 pages):**

**NK cells** — innate cytotoxicity, the immune system's first line of cell-killing response. Research has observed NK elevation at d=+0.79 in bladder cancer tumor microenvironment cohorts. Sustained NK shifts in research have been associated with chronic viral infection and active immune surveillance.

**Neutrophils** — first-responder antimicrobial cells. Research has observed neutrophil expansion (+16% by cell-fraction in glioma blood research per Bracci 2022 NLR-shift signature; +1.24 by methylation deconvolution in bladder cancer microenvironment), neutrophil depression in active inflammatory bowel disease research at d=−1.34, and neutrophil attenuation near cancer diagnosis in some pre-diagnostic cohorts.

**Eosinophils** — allergy and parasite-response cells. Research has observed eosinophil elevation at d=+1.12 in long pre-diagnostic windows of breast cancer research cohorts. Customers with active allergies in season often see eosinophil shifts that resolve with their allergic context.

**Basophils** — small population, allergic and immune-modulation roles. Less commonly resolved at clean posterior; reported when IAMAtlas v1 confidence permits.

**Monocyte/macrophage lineage (4 pages — tissue-resident subtypes separated because organ-system patterns diverge):**

**Monocytes** — first-responder phagocytes circulating in blood. Research has observed monocyte elevation at d=+0.77 in prostate cancer cell-fraction readings and at d=+1.13 in bladder cancer tumor microenvironment. Research has also observed monocyte attenuation as cancer diagnosis approaches in some cohorts — a pre-diagnostic elevation pattern that flips toward depression near clinical detection.

**Macrophages** (peripheral / tumor-associated) — tissue-resident phagocytes outside the brain and liver. Research has observed macrophage elevation at d=+1.61 in aortic dissection research cohorts and macrophage shifts in tumor microenvironment research consistent with tumor-associated macrophage infiltration.

**Microglia** — central nervous system resident macrophages. Microglia signal elevation has been documented in research cohorts of multiple neurodegenerative conditions (Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, ALS) and in primary brain tumors including glioma. The blood-brain-barrier biology makes microglia diagnostically distinct from peripheral macrophages — microglia activate while peripheral macrophages stay normal in neurodegeneration, and the reverse can occur in solid tumors outside the CNS.

**Kupffer cells** — liver-resident macrophages lining hepatic sinusoids. Kupffer cell elevation has been documented in chronic liver inflammation (NAFLD/NASH, alcoholic liver disease, viral hepatitis). In advanced HCC, Kupffer cells can DEPLETE while peripheral tumor-associated macrophages within the tumor are elevated — an opposite-direction pattern within the same customer.

**Other immune (1 page):**

**Dendritic cells** — antigen-presenters that bridge innate and adaptive immunity. Research has observed dendritic cell shifts in cardiovascular cohorts (aortic dissection research showed dendritic cell elevation as part of the immune-infiltrate signature) and in cancer immune microenvironments.

The cell type list above (19 customer-facing pages from the production atlas) reflects the real cell types EDEAR resolves. Cells without sufficient posterior resolution at IAMAtlas v1 aggregate up to the immune class score. Customers see only the cell types where their measurement has clean enough posteriors for honest reporting.

## Section 3.5 — What this score does and does not measure (orientation)

A short orientation subsection that helps engaged customers understand the boundary of the immune class score before they read the cross-class scenarios in Section 4.

**What the immune class score measures.** The score primarily reflects peripheral immune cells — the white blood cells circulating in the customer's blood at the time of the draw. About 70% of the cell-free DNA in a typical blood sample comes from these peripheral immune cells, and the immune class score is computed against age-matched and history-matched healthy reference distributions for those peripheral cells. The score is sensitive to changes in the peripheral immune compartment, which is itself responsive to events anywhere in the body — so the score reads "the immune system's current state" through the peripheral lens.

**What the immune class score does not directly measure.** A few important boundaries worth understanding:

*Cells behind the blood-brain barrier.* The brain has its own immune cells — microglia are the brain's resident immune cells, derived from the same cellular ancestry as macrophages elsewhere in the body. Architecturally, microglia are immune-class cells. But microglia live behind the blood-brain barrier, which limits how much of their DNA crosses into peripheral blood. The immune class score reads only a small slice of microglial state; the score cannot reliably detect changes that are microglia-only. When a brain-related condition is involved, the immune class signal in peripheral blood reflects mostly the peripheral immune system's response to whatever is happening in the brain, not the brain-resident immune cells themselves.

*Brain tissue cells (neurons).* Neurons are NOT in the immune class — they are in the terminal class, a separate architectural category covered on the terminal class page. Neurons are also behind the blood-brain barrier. Research has shown that brain-derived DNA fragments DO reach plasma at low fraction at healthy baseline (about 0.3% in research cohorts) and at elevated fraction in some brain-related conditions — research on glioma cohorts has measured cortical-neuron cfDNA fractions of about 1.1% in glioma plasma compared to 0.3% in healthy reference. This signal is read at the tissue-of-origin step (the framework's Stage 2), not at the immune class score. If a customer's report shows an elevated cortical-neuron fraction alongside an elevated immune class score, that combination is what the framework's Stage 2 cross-reference module flags as warranting differential-diagnosis review by the customer's physician. The terminal class page covers cortical neurons in detail.

*Heart muscle cells (cardiomyocytes).* Like neurons, cardiomyocytes are terminal class, not immune class. Cardiomyocyte cfDNA fractions in plasma are relevant to cardiovascular conditions and are read at the tissue-of-origin step. The terminal class page covers them. Research on cardiovascular cohorts has documented cardiomyocyte and endothelial fragments at elevated fraction in certain conditions.

**Three measurement lenses, not one.** A useful framing for customers: the report shows three kinds of measurements computed from the same lab β-value input.

The first lens — **immune class architecture** — measures how disordered or coherent the peripheral immune cells' methylation patterns are compared to the healthy reference. This is the immune class score described above. Sensitive to overall immune state.

The second lens — **tissue-of-origin** — estimates which tissues your blood DNA fragments came from. The framework's Stage 2 deconvolution step uses a reference atlas of tissue-specific methylation patterns to back-solve which mixture of tissue contributions best explains your sample's β-values. The output is a fractional composition: this sample is consistent with X% liver-derived, Y% colon-derived, Z% cortical-neuron-derived, etc. Sensitive to which tissues are shedding more DNA than they normally do (which often signals tissue stress, damage, or pathology).

The third lens — **immune cell-type composition** — estimates the proportions of immune cell types in your sample. The same deconvolution logic applied to immune-cell reference atlases (Salas IDOL 6-cell, UniLIFE 19-cell). The output: this sample is consistent with X% neutrophils, Y% CD4 T cells, Z% B cells, etc. Sensitive to immune population shifts (neutrophil expansion, lymphocyte depletion, regulatory T cell elevation) that are themselves diagnostic of certain states in research.

All three lenses come from the same lab measurement (genome-wide methylation β-values from the customer's plasma cfDNA). The lab does not directly count cells — what arrives from the lab is methylation state at hundreds of thousands of genomic positions per sample. The cell-fraction and tissue-of-origin numbers are inferred from those β-values via the deconvolution step; they are estimates, not direct counts. The estimates are validated against research cohorts where ground-truth fractions are known and have been shown to track real biology cleanly when the reference atlas covers the cell types of interest.

**Why this matters for interpretation.** Sometimes a customer's report shows a finding that lives in one lens but not another — for example, an elevated cortical-neuron fraction at Stage 2 with the immune class still in NORMAL range, or an elevated immune class score with no specific tissue-of-origin elevation. These multi-lens patterns are interpreted through the cross-class scenarios in Section 4 and through the integrated interpretation page (when the customer has multiple findings across multiple lenses). The framework's run-everything architecture reads all three lenses for every sample so multi-lens patterns surface naturally rather than being missed.

## Section 4 — Conditions associated with immune-class signals in research

This is the section to read if your report flagged anything in your immune class. It is organized so you can quickly find the pattern that matches your own report.

**How to use this section.** Look at your report. Find the cells that flagged. Then scan the patterns below — each one starts with a quick at-a-glance summary of which cells go which direction. Find the pattern your report shows. Read the explanation. Take the explanation to your clinician.

We do not tell you which condition you have. We show you what we have observed in research, and you connect the dots together with your clinician.

A note before we begin: most flagged readings have everyday explanations — recent illness, sleep changes, stress periods, allergies, recovery from something. The patterns below are what research has documented across studied conditions, but most customers reading them will not have any of these conditions. The patterns are tools for the conversation, not labels for your situation.

### Pattern A — Some cells up, some cells down at the same time (the "scrambled" pattern)

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Your overall immune class score looks normal or only slightly elevated
- Specific immune cells are running higher than typical
- Other specific immune cells are running lower than typical
- Different cells moving in different directions all at once

The cancellation effect: when some cells go up and others go down, your aggregate score can read normal even though something is happening underneath. EDEAR detects this by looking at each cell type separately, not just the average.

**What conditions in research have shown this pattern:**

**Alzheimer's disease.** Research on Alzheimer's disease blood samples has consistently shown this pattern. The total immune score reads normal but the specific cells inside it are scrambled — some up, some down. Standard medical diagnosis of Alzheimer's relies on cognitive symptoms, by which point the underlying changes have been happening for many years. Methylation changes happen earlier than symptoms.

**Other conditions that may produce a similar pattern:** other forms of dementia, Parkinson's disease, ALS, and multiple sclerosis are predicted by research to show similar scrambled patterns, but EDEAR has less validation for these specifically. This is an active area of EDEAR's continuing research.

**Why trajectory matters more than any single reading for this pattern.** A single scrambled reading does not mean Alzheimer's. Most scrambled readings have everyday explanations — allergies, autoimmune activity, recovery from illness. **What matters for neurodegenerative monitoring is whether the scrambled pattern persists over years.** A pattern that resolves on retest was your body recovering from something temporary. A pattern that stays scrambled for years, in a customer with relevant family history, is what makes the framework useful for these conditions. Customers concerned about neurodegenerative risk should retest every 6 months to build a meaningful trajectory over time.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** Discuss the finding with your clinician at your next visit. Mention any family history of Alzheimer's, other dementia, ALS, MS, or Parkinson's. Continue the screening recommendations your clinician has discussed with you. Plan to retest with EDEAR in 6 months, not a year — trajectory is the tool for this pattern.

### Pattern B — T cells up while monocytes and neutrophils down (the "rebalancing" pattern)

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- CD4 T cells UP
- CD8 T cells UP
- Naive CD8 T cells UP
- Regulatory T cells UP
- NK cells UP
- Monocytes DOWN
- Neutrophils DOWN
- B cells: usually unchanged or slightly down

The whole immune compartment is rebalancing — fewer first-responder cells (monocytes, neutrophils), more coordinator and killer cells (T cells of all types).

**What conditions in research have shown this pattern:**

**Active inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis).** When IBD is actively flaring, research consistently shows this T-up / myeloid-down pattern. The pattern reflects how the immune system rebalances to manage active inflammation in the gut. Customers with known IBD whose readings show this pattern are seeing what research observed during active disease activity.

**Other conditions predicted to produce a similar pattern (research ongoing):** chronic viral infections (chronic HBV, HIV, EBV reactivation), active autoimmune disease flares (rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, MS, Type 1 diabetes during active autoimmune phase), sepsis, GVHD after transplant.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** If you have known IBD, autoimmune disease, or chronic viral infection, share this reading with the specialist who manages it — the pattern can help track activity level. If you do not have a known condition matching the pattern, discuss the reading with your clinician at your next visit. Continue your clinician's recommended screening. Retest with EDEAR in 6 months to track whether the pattern persists.

### Pattern C — All immune cells up dramatically with no specific tissue source (the "blood cancer" pattern)

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Your immune class score is significantly elevated
- Multiple immune cell types are dramatically elevated
- Your other class scores (organ-related) are NORMAL — no specific tissue source
- The immune signal is the LARGEST signal anywhere on your report

Some cancers do not start in solid organs — they start in the immune system itself. AML (a form of leukemia), lymphomas, and other blood cancers happen IN the immune cells, not in response to disease elsewhere.

**What research has shown:**

**Hematologic cancers (AML and related).** Research on AML cohorts has shown the strongest immune-class signal anywhere in EDEAR's research — far larger than any other condition. When the immune class is significantly elevated AND no organ-specific signal is firing, the pattern is consistent with what research has shown for hematologic cancers.

**This pattern is the one exception to "trajectory monitoring is the answer."** When the signal is this large, a single reading is enough to warrant a direct conversation with your clinician. You do not need to wait for trajectory.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** Discuss this finding with your clinician promptly — within days, not weeks. Bring this report. A complete blood count with differential and possibly a hematology consult are the kinds of clinical follow-up your clinician may consider. Continue your clinician's recommended screening on the schedule they recommend.

### Pattern D — Immune class drifting up many years before any cancer diagnosis (the "early signal" pattern)

This is the foundational pattern behind why EDEAR exists as a trajectory product. Research has shown that the immune system starts shifting MANY YEARS before solid cancers organize into something a clinical test can find.

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Your immune class score is moderately elevated
- Specific immune cells are elevated (which cells depends on the cancer type)
- Your organ-class scores are normal (no specific tissue source)
- You may have no symptoms at all

**What research has shown for specific cancers:**

**Breast cancer.** Research on women with blood drawn 10+ years before breast cancer diagnosis showed:
- Regulatory T cells UP
- Eosinophils UP
- Mild monocyte elevation
- Mild neutrophil elevation
- Slightly elevated breast tissue signal (in secretory class)

The signal was STRONGEST 10+ years before diagnosis and got SMALLER as diagnosis approached. Counter-intuitive, but consistently observed.

**Lung cancer.** Research on pre-diagnostic lung cancer cohorts showed similar long pre-dx immune patterns (specific cells under research). Customers with smoking history or family history of lung cancer should mention immune-class elevation to their clinician.

**Colorectal cancer.** Different direction from breast — research peripheral blood signal in pre-diagnostic CRC reads LOWER than typical. If your immune score is in the SUPPRESSED tier and you have family or personal history of colon cancer, mention it to your clinician.

**Pancreatic cancer.** Research showed pancreatic cancer produces a "scrambled" Pattern A type signature alongside slight pancreatic tissue elevation in long pre-dx windows. Pancreatic cancer is one of the hardest to detect early in standard medicine; trajectory monitoring is one of the few non-invasive tools available.

**Prostate cancer.** Research showed monocyte UP plus prostate tissue elevation in research samples.

**The capstone finding.** Across seven pre-diagnostic cancer cohorts spanning breast, lung, colorectal, pancreatic, and prostate cancers, research has documented architectural drift across multiple cell classes 2 to 5 years before clinical cancer diagnosis. The signal is real but subtle, and it is distributed across multiple types of cells rather than concentrated in any one place.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** This is exactly the pattern the trajectory product is designed for. The framework cannot tell you which (if any) organ will eventually develop a clinical condition. What it can do is track how the signal moves over time. Plan to retest with EDEAR every 6 months. Continue your clinician's recommended screening — including any additional vigilance your clinician recommends given your family history. If your trajectory shows the signal stable over years, that is informative. If your trajectory shows the signal intensifying, that is a different conversation with your clinician.

### Pattern E — Same condition reads opposite direction in different samples (the "compartment-flip" pattern)

This pattern is mostly relevant for understanding your report rather than something you would directly act on. Research has shown that some conditions read in OPPOSITE directions depending on whether the sample is plasma blood or actual tissue.

**At a glance — what this means for your report:**

Your EDEAR sample is plasma blood. So you see the BLOOD direction of any condition with this kind of split. For example, colorectal cancer in research:
- Plasma blood reading: immune class DOWN (suppressed)
- Tumor tissue reading: immune class UP

You would see the SUPPRESSED reading on your report. The framework is reading what is actually happening in your blood — your circulating immune compartment is acting suppressed, while research has shown that the same disease produces an OPPOSITE pattern in tumor tissue itself.

**What conditions in research show this:**

**Colorectal cancer.** Peripheral blood reads SUPPRESSED in research; tumor-infiltrating immune cells in research samples read elevated.

**What to do.** This pattern is included for context — most customers do not need to act on it directly. If your immune reading is in the SUPPRESSED tier and you have family history of colorectal cancer, mention it to your clinician.

### Pattern F — Immune class plus brain tissue signal together (the "glioma-pattern" pattern)

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Immune class moderately elevated
- Neutrophils UP
- T cells (CD4, CD8) DOWN
- B cells DOWN
- NK cells DOWN
- Cortical neurons signal showing in your report (this is on the TERMINAL class page, not the immune class page — visit the cortical neurons cell page for the full story)

This combination — immune system shifted toward neutrophils and away from lymphocytes, plus brain tissue cells showing up in your blood at higher than normal fraction — is what research has shown for glioma (a primary brain tumor).

**What research has shown.**

In research on glioma blood samples, three signals fire together at the same time:
- Stage 1 immune class score elevated (peripheral immune system responding to the brain tumor)
- Cortical neuron tissue showing in plasma at four times the healthy baseline (about 1.1% in glioma plasma versus about 0.3% in healthy plasma)
- Neutrophil expansion plus lymphocyte depression in cell-fraction estimates

**Why does brain tissue end up in your blood?** Brain cells are normally protected behind the blood-brain barrier — a tight cellular boundary that keeps brain DNA from crossing into peripheral blood at significant levels. When a tumor disrupts brain tissue, brain cells release fragments at higher rates, and some cross the barrier into the blood where EDEAR can read them.

**Why don't microglia (brain immune cells) show this signal directly?** Microglia ARE brain immune cells, but they live behind the blood-brain barrier just like neurons. Their DNA largely stays trapped behind the barrier. EDEAR cannot reliably read microglia-specific signal from plasma. This is why glioma's plasma signature comes through in cortical neurons (which DO cross the barrier when brain tissue is disrupted) and through peripheral immune response, NOT through microglia directly.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** Discuss this finding with your clinician promptly. If you have neurological symptoms (persistent unexplained headaches, seizures, vision changes, cognitive changes), neurological consultation is the kind of clinical follow-up your clinician may recommend. Bring this report. Visit the cortical neurons cell page in the terminal class for the brain-cell side of this story.

### Pattern G — Cardiovascular conditions with some immune involvement (the "heart and vessels" pattern)

Cardiovascular disease is detected primarily through OTHER class signals — vascular endothelial cells (in stromal class) and heart muscle cells (in terminal class). But several cardiovascular conditions also show some immune class involvement.

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Some immune class elevation
- Stromal class signal (vascular endothelial, fibroblasts) — see the stromal class page
- Some terminal class signal (heart muscle cells) — see the terminal class page

The primary cardiovascular signals live on the stromal and terminal class pages. The immune class involvement is the secondary component.

**What research has shown:**

**Pulmonary arterial hypertension** — research on cultured pulmonary endothelial cells showed concurrent immune class drift alongside the primary endothelial signal.

**Aortic disease (BAV with dilation, aortic dissection)** — research on aortic tissue showed immune cells (especially macrophages) infiltrating the affected vascular tissue. For peripheral plasma the signal is more subtle.

**Stroke** — research has shown that whole-blood signals do NOT distinguish different stroke subtypes well. The framework reads stroke patterns as relatively similar across subtypes from peripheral blood.

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** If you have cardiovascular family history or known risk factors and your report shows immune class elevation alongside stromal class involvement, share this finding with your clinician. Visit the stromal class page for the primary cardiovascular story.

### Pattern H — Immune class up with no clear tissue source (the "something is happening" pattern)

**At a glance — what your report would show:**

- Immune class moderately elevated
- Specific immune cells elevated (any combination)
- ALL organ-class scores normal — no tissue source has been localized

Your immune system is responding to something, and EDEAR cannot localize a specific tissue source from the data.

**What this can mean in research:**

- A condition involving a tissue EDEAR's atlas does not yet cover (some CNS tissue beyond cortical neurons, certain rare tissues)
- A pre-clinical state too early for organ-class signals to fire but already producing immune response
- An immune-only condition that does not show up at any organ tile
- Everyday context (recent illness, stress, sleep disruption, allergic season) that is driving the reading temporarily

**What to do if your report shows this pattern.** Trajectory monitoring is the right next step. If you have neurological or other unexplained symptoms, share the finding with your clinician at your next visit. Plan to retest with EDEAR in 6 months. ## Section 4.5 — What this class can and cannot detect through plasma

A short technical note for engaged readers. The immune class is read primarily through peripheral blood plasma. Several biological barriers and substrate considerations affect what the framework can and cannot detect.

**Cells behind the blood-brain barrier.** Microglia and other brain-resident immune cells live behind the blood-brain barrier. Microglia signal in plasma is normally very low because the cells don't substantially circulate; what reaches blood is the small fraction of brain-derived cfDNA that crosses the barrier under normal conditions, with elevation in conditions that disrupt the barrier (glioma) or drive substantial microglial turnover (active neurodegeneration). Microglia detection at v1 is exploratory at lower posterior precision than peripheral immune cells. The microglia cell page documents this in detail.

**Cells behind other organ-specific barriers.** Similar architecture limits some organ-resident immune cells from contributing to plasma cfDNA at high resolution. Kupffer cells (liver-resident macrophages) and other tissue-resident macrophage subtypes are detected through their tissue-turnover contribution to plasma cfDNA; their fractions read at lower magnitude than circulating immune cells.

**Substrate effects.** EDEAR's primary substrate is plasma cfDNA. Whole-blood substrate carries different signal characteristics. Tumor tissue substrate (research only) carries dramatically different signal characteristics. For customer plasma cfDNA reports, the peripheral blood pattern is what's being read.

**Reference atlas coverage.** The framework can only resolve cell types that are in its reference atlas. Cell types not in the atlas aggregate up into broader categories. EDEAR's atlas continues to expand as research provides additional cell-type references; customers' reports get richer over time as the atlas deepens.

The framework is honest about these limits because honesty is part of what makes it trustworthy. When a customer's pattern doesn't fit any documented signature, the framework reports that directly rather than fabricating a fit.

## Section 6 — Your trajectory (for subscribers with multiple tests)

This section appears with content only after a customer has at least two tests. The dashboard view of the customer's immune class score across time, with the framing that this is the product's actual value proposition.

### Why trajectory matters more than a single point — the science

Research on pre-diagnostic cancer cohorts has produced a counter-intuitive finding that is foundational to why trajectory monitoring is valuable. The immune-class signal in research cohorts is OFTEN STRONGEST at long pre-diagnostic windows — 10 or more years before clinical diagnosis — and ATTENUATES as diagnosis approaches. In one well-known breast cancer pre-diagnostic cohort, the research signal at >10 years before diagnosis read at d=+1.78 (a strong effect), while the same signal at 0-2 years before diagnosis read at d=+0.09 to +0.27 (much weaker). The immune system's response to pre-clinical drift is not a steady ramp toward disease — it is a long quiet shift that can ramp up early, plateau or attenuate, then ramp again at clinical manifestation.

This finding generalizes. Research across seven pre-diagnostic cohorts spanning breast, lung, colorectal, pancreatic, and prostate cancers has documented multi-class architectural elevation 2 to 5 years before clinical diagnosis at a mean shift of about ΔA = +0.014 above matched cancer-free controls. The pre-diagnostic signal is real; it is also subtle and distributed across multiple architecture classes rather than localized to the eventually-affected organ.

What this means for the customer: a single elevated reading at long pre-dx is informative. A trajectory across multiple readings is more informative. Trajectory is the tool that distinguishes "your body is responding to something it will resolve" from "your body is in a sustained shift" from "your body is intensifying along a particular pattern." No single time point can tell you which one.

The recovery side of trajectory matters too. Research on five clinical trial cohorts (in glioma, colorectal, breast, hematologic, and melanoma cancers) has shown that immune-class A-score trajectories distinguish treatment responders from non-responders, with complete-response cases approaching the NORMAL tier. Trajectory tracks both decline and recovery — both directions are valuable signals.

### What to look for in your trajectory

The section explains what to look for: stable (consistent context), trending up (something is sustained or worsening), trending down (recovering or improving from a prior state), oscillating (life is happening — illness, stress, lifestyle changes).

The section connects trajectory to the customer's logged covariates: "You reported a period of high stress in March; your immune class score rose 0.04 between February and April and resolved by August. This is the kind of trajectory pattern that gives meaning to single-point readings."

## Section 8 — The science behind your score

The optional drill-down for engaged customers who want to understand the technical foundation. Most customers will skip it. Customers who want it are exactly the customers who become long-term subscribers and brand advocates.

### DNA is the software of the body. The cell is a biological microchip.

Most people have heard DNA described as the software of the body — the instructions that tell each cell what to do. EDEAR takes that further. We treat the cell itself as a biological microchip running that software. Like any chip, it has an architecture — a structural design that determines how it operates, how it responds to inputs, how reliably it maintains its function over time. Different kinds of cells have different architectures because they do different jobs. A neuron in your brain runs at a different "intensity level" than an immune cell in your blood, the same way different parts of a city operate at different rhythms.

EDEAR has classified every human cell type into one of eight architectural categories using the physics of thermodynamics and information theory, applied with the calibration tools and statistical methods of astrophysics and cosmology. The eight architecture types are not a guess and they are not the result of clustering data until something fell out. They are derived from first-principles physics. Cells fall into these categories because they have to — because of how the underlying thermodynamics works. The classification holds across every species we have tested it on, from human to canine to broader mammalian cohorts in published research.

### Why cosmology

Decades ago cosmologists were looking at the cosmic microwave background — the faint radiation left over from the early universe — trying to extract real signals from extraordinarily noisy measurements. They couldn't repeat the experiment. They couldn't isolate variables. They had one universe to look at, and they had to figure out how to be honest about what they were seeing despite all that noise. To do it, they developed an unusually rigorous statistical toolkit: hierarchical Bayesian inference, Markov Chain Monte Carlo posterior calibration, careful uncertainty quantification, cross-validation across independent measurements that should agree if the underlying physics is right.

EDEAR's founder, working in cosmology, recognized something most people in either field had missed. The mathematics that lets cosmologists extract clean signals from CMB data is the same mathematics that describes how cells maintain their architectural identity. Cellular methylation — the chemical marks on DNA that tell each cell which parts of its genome to express — has the same underlying information structure as cosmological observations. The same calibration toolkit, applied to methylation data instead of CMB data, returns clean signal where existing methylation tools return noise.

We call this approach Astro-Genetics. EDEAR is the first product to apply it. Currently there are no other Astro-Geneticists, because no one else has yet recognized the bridge.

### Validated across 37 orders of magnitude

The same framework that classifies your cells has been independently tested at scales spanning 37 orders of magnitude. We first applied it to semiconductor chips and to quantum computing qubits — physical systems where the information-theoretic and thermodynamic principles are easier to measure directly. The eight-class structure that emerges in cellular biology is not an accident of biology; it is what the physics produces in any system where information is stored, processed, and degrades over time. Cells, semiconductors, and qubits all follow the same architectural rules because the underlying physics is the same.

This matters for trust. Most consumer methylation products are calibrated against a training dataset. If the dataset is biased, the product is biased. If the dataset is incomplete, the product has blind spots. EDEAR is calibrated against first-principles physics that doesn't change between species, between sample types, or between research populations. The framework works because the physics works.

### Built outside the institutional system

EDEAR exists because it had to. The standard path for new biomedical research is grant funding from federal agencies, institutional affiliation at a university or research center, and the slow climb through peer review and clinical validation that takes a decade and tens of millions of dollars. EDEAR's founder is an independent researcher without that institutional backing — and the gates were closed. Grant funding for unconventional cross-disciplinary research is hard to get. Institutional affiliation requires affiliation. Peer review of a framework that crosses cosmology, physics, and biology requires reviewers who understand all three.

So we bypassed the gates. EDEAR is a direct-to-consumer wellness product first because that's the only way to bring the science to the people who need it without waiting another decade for the institutional system to catch up. Subscription proceeds fund continued research, additional cohort access, and the methodology development that the institutional system would not fund directly.

This positioning is not posturing. The framework's MCMC calibration runs on the founder's son's gaming PC because that's what's available. The validation cohorts come from publicly accessible data because biobank access requires institutional applications we don't yet have. The framework's research output is on a public GitHub repository because peer-review journals don't know what to do with a paper that crosses three disciplines.

If the framework is right, the institutional system will catch up. In the meantime, EDEAR brings cellular health monitoring to people who could not otherwise access it.

### The three measurement lenses

A useful framing for customers: the report shows three kinds of measurements computed from the same lab β-value input.

The first lens — **immune class architecture** — measures how disordered or coherent the peripheral immune cells' methylation patterns are compared to the healthy reference. This is the immune class score described in Section 2. Sensitive to overall immune state.

The second lens — **tissue-of-origin** — estimates which tissues your blood DNA fragments came from. The framework's Stage 2 deconvolution step uses a reference atlas of tissue-specific methylation patterns to back-solve which mixture of tissue contributions best explains your sample's β-values. The output is a fractional composition: this sample is consistent with X% liver-derived, Y% colon-derived, Z% cortical-neuron-derived, etc. Sensitive to which tissues are shedding more DNA than they normally do (which often signals tissue stress, damage, or pathology). Research on a large set of cancer cohorts has shown the framework correctly localizing the cancer's primary tissue 10 out of 10 times in the published cohorts tested.

The third lens — **immune cell-type composition** — estimates the proportions of immune cell types in your sample. The same deconvolution logic applied to immune-cell reference atlases. The output: this sample is consistent with X% neutrophils, Y% CD4 T cells, Z% B cells, etc. Sensitive to immune population shifts that are themselves diagnostic of certain states in research.

All three lenses come from the same lab measurement (genome-wide methylation β-values from the customer's plasma cfDNA). The lab does not directly count cells — what arrives from the lab is methylation state at hundreds of thousands of genomic positions per sample. The cell-fraction and tissue-of-origin numbers are inferred from those β-values via the deconvolution step; they are estimates, not direct counts. The estimates are validated against research cohorts where ground-truth fractions are known and have been shown to track real biology cleanly when the reference atlas covers the cell types of interest.

### The field-effect concept

Research on cancer biology has found that tissue adjacent to a developing tumor is itself architecturally altered — sometimes years before any visible disease. Across many cancer types in research cohorts, tissue immediately next to a tumor reads about 20% of the way toward the tumor's signal. The architectural drift extends past surgical resection margins; research has measured it at five to ten centimeters from the tumor still being elevated above true-healthy reference. This is why EDEAR's pre-diagnostic detection capability is plausible: cellular architecture is shifting in the affected organ AND in the surrounding tissue AND in the immune system's response to that drift, all measurable in blood, well before any symptom or clinical detection. The framework reads multiple facets of this same underlying phenomenon simultaneously.

### Cross-species universality

The thermodynamic principles that produce the framework's class-level architectural floor are not human-specific. Research has validated the same patterns in canine cohorts and across pan-mammalian methylation studies covering hundreds of species. The framework reads cellular thermodynamics that is universal across mammals, which is one of the reasons the framework is trustworthy — it is anchored in physics that does not change between species, not in pattern-matching that might generalize poorly.

### Where to read more

For the deeply technical reader, the framework's mathematical foundations and validation record are publicly available on the EDEAR research GitHub repository. For the everyday subscriber, the per-cell pages within each class are the right destination — each cell page covers what that cell does, how EDEAR reads it, and what research has observed about it in language that doesn't require physics or biology training.

## Section 10 — How your report links to this page

A short technical-orientation subsection describing the report-to-website architecture so engaged customers understand how their report is structured and so the page maintains its design contract over time.

**Per-finding deep links.** When a customer's runtime report contains an immune class finding, that finding includes a "Learn more about this" link that takes the customer directly to the relevant subsection of this page. The link uses a stable anchor structure: per-cell-type anchors (one anchor per cell type covered in Section 3 with a link out to that cell's dedicated page), per-scenario anchors (one anchor per general or disease-specific scenario in Section 4), and per-tier anchors (NORMAL, ELEVATED, SIGNIFICANTLY_ELEVATED, SUPPRESSED). When a customer has multiple findings on their report, each finding has its own link to its own anchor.

**Two-level navigation: class pages and cell pages.** The website's structure is one page per architectural class (eight class pages total) and one page per cell type within each class (around 55 cell pages across the site once the framework is fully built out). The customer's report links to class pages for class-level findings and links directly to cell pages for cell-level findings. From any class page the customer can navigate to the cell pages for cells in that class via the cell list. From any cell page the customer can navigate back to its parent class page. There is no separate integrated-interpretation page that synthesizes multi-class findings — synthesis would be a diagnostic claim and we do not make those. Customers with multi-class findings receive multiple per-finding links in their report, one per relevant class or cell page, and read each page's substantive content and research observations to build their own picture in conversation with their physician.

**Return-to-report link.** Every page on the EDEAR site includes a "Return to your report" link in the page header that takes the customer back to their most recent report. The customer reads website context, then goes back to their numbers — the loop is what keeps the customer engaged.

**Anchor stability and versioned URLs.** Customer reports are generated as durable artifacts; a customer who downloaded a PDF report in 2026 may reread it in 2030 and follow its links. To prevent broken links over time, the website commits to two stability rules. First, anchor names are stable: a given cell-type anchor (e.g., the anchor for CD4 T cells) keeps the same identifier across page revisions; new content is added under new anchors rather than displacing old ones. Second, page URLs are versioned at the path level (e.g., `/v1/cellular-health/immune-system`) so when the website undergoes a major content revision, old reports link to the v1 page (which is preserved) and new reports link to the new version. The page footer of every versioned page includes a link to the current version so customers reading old content can navigate forward.

**The runtime engineering side.** The runtime engine produces the report; the report builder appends per-finding deep links based on the engine's output. The cross-reference module determines which findings warrant which links. The architectural separation is: engine produces findings + flags; report builder produces customer-readable text + deep links + the report's vigilance content; website serves the destination content. The three components are independent — the engine can be updated without touching the website, the website can be revised without touching the engine, and the report builder coordinates between them.
