## Results later that day

*What came of the session, in the order it happened. Each item links to the record that holds its numbers. Some answers are still to come.*

### What worked

1. **Fully held DNA reads at the writer's own limit.** On the 26,800 sites every healthy cell keeps fully methylated, the held error is 0.0136 to 0.0226, inside the copying enzyme's own limit, 1/(1 + D) = 0.0123 to 0.0207. That limit is a property of the enzyme, the same for every person: a reference that needs no atlas.
2. **The island side is held at the enzyme's limit, and ignores how the cell is built.** Across 56 healthy cell types, a mark gained between two unmethylated neighbors reads 0.0094 to 0.0162 (29 inside the writer's band, 27 just below it), with no relation at all to how much of the genome the cell holds loosely. The side that loses marks does follow it (rank correlation +0.87). So the island side is the clean place to watch for the overdrive writer.
3. **One reading per instrument removes a hidden artifact.** Reading a whole cell as one pool overstates its disorder, by a median 3.8 to 10.5 percent depending on the loose level, because disorder is not an average. Reading each territory first and then combining them removes it, and still gives one number.
4. **The four-domain table gained three rows:** gate error (the firm end), leakage (the loose end) and one reading per instrument, each translated across qubits, chips and cells.
5. **The atlas v2.1 fit launched,** after the morning's two catches: all 79 cell types distinct, agreement with the earlier atlas r = 0.996.

### What did not work

1. **The eraser test: NOT MET.** Removing the erasing enzyme never halved the held error (0.79 to 0.96 of control, five sets). The half-and-half per site is not where the middle line comes from. The middle line itself is measured and stands.
2. **Cell position from RNA: NOT MET.** How much writing and erasing enzyme a cell makes does not predict its held error.
3. **Our own bars.** Two round-number bars failed good work on the atlas; both verdicts stand in the record, and every bar now carries its reason.

### What it tells us about the loose territory that we did not know before

- The loose territory is what moves the loss side from cell to cell; the island side does not move with it.
- The top of the measured range, 0.042, is where the meter stops being able to read (few molecules qualify there), not the ceiling.
- So deriving the loose end means deriving what the substrate does where the writer is not holding, not tuning a number to the average. That is still open, by decision, until the first tests are in.
