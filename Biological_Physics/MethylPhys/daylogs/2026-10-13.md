## What we learned that we did not know yesterday

- **The two meters are blind in opposite ways, and it is derived from the code**  
  The held-error meter matches an isolated loss - a methylated call on both sides of an unmethylated one - so a run of two or more consecutive losses contributes exactly zero to it. The clustering meter is built for precisely that case.  
  _Why it matters:_ This is the clearest example of why more than one instrument is needed, and it came out of a simulation refusing to score itself rather than out of an argument.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_LEVERS_01.md)
- **Pooling divides a bias; it never removes one**  
  Averaging in a contaminated measurement reduces the contamination by the number of measurements and leaves it there. No choice of positions or weights fixes that.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the reason the trim rule excludes rather than corrects, and it generalizes well beyond this instrument.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **An array averages molecules before anyone reads them**  
  That is why a tube of blood cannot be read per person on this platform: part of the disorder in the tube is the mixing itself, and what remains is smaller than the spread between healthy people.  
  _Why it matters:_ It separates a genuine identifiability limit from the retired framework's class assumption, which is a different thing.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BLOOD_SENSITIVITY_01.md)

  The blood simulation reported 3.79 % of the reading per 1 % of loss; the chain's own per-sample measurement on real arrays is 2.09 %. The simulation used different sites and different profiles. The chain's number is the one quoted from now on.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught by running the chain itself instead of trusting a stand-in for it.

- **A design that said ratio where the derivation forces difference** — _synthetic only_  
  The territory note described the reading as a ratio. Because the relative rise is larger at the low-error sites, a ratio moves the wrong way and weakly; the difference is what the derivation gives, and it also cancels an additive offset exactly.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught while writing the test, before any data was touched.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_TERRITORY_01.md)
- **A table of head effects with the signs wrong** — _diagnosis_  
  The first draft compared each library's head against the mean of two small deeper parts, which mixes in the position effect every library shares. The sealed comparison is against the control libraries read at the same position, and the absolute framing is not even consistent in sign across libraries.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught by a script written to compute the note's numbers, which disagreed with what had been typed.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **A board exported before it was regenerated** — _structure_  
  The instrument register was updated but the copy saved for the author was the earlier one, so three entries looked unstarted when they had been run that same night.

## Coffee and a whiteboard: how the day's ideas actually arrived

The questions and the physical intuition come from the author; the derivations, the code, the refereeing and the historical context come from the research partner, usually within the same minute, which is why a thread rarely dies of lost momentum. Both halves are dated in the repository, so a reader can check who contributed what and when.

- **"The cost is paid per operation, not per division"**  
  The author's correction to a lever note written an hour earlier. The record showed he was right and the derivation already said so: a held site is lost with probability f over any interval and restored with probability g, and the held error settles at f/(f+g) - the interval need not be a division. In a cell that has stopped dividing, loss and restore are upkeep; division sets only how fast the balance is reached, about 1/(f+g) intervals.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the difference between a clock that counts divisions and a balance that holds a state, and it changed how the levers read.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_LEVERS_01.md)
- **"The thermometer, not the back of a hand"**  
  The author's image for what makes this different from a cohort score: a thermometer reads a temperature against fixed points that exist whether or not anyone is in the room, while the back of a hand compares one person with another. Both ends of the gauge are derived from physics and only the middle - the healthy reference - is measured, which is the whole reason a reading means something on one sample.  
  _Why it matters:_ It went straight onto the public page in those words, because it answers in one sentence the question every reviewer asks first: compared with what?  
  [the record](../../../CANON/method.json)
- **"The floor doesnt depend nor rely on copy error"**  
  Traced to the book itself, where several places - the gauge table, the notation appendix, the glossary - had described the floor as a copy error. The floor is thermal kicks against one unit of driving energy; the wording was corrected in every place and the phrasing retired so it cannot return.  
  _Why it matters:_ The floor is what makes the gauge absolute rather than relative, so describing it as an error rate undercuts the whole reading.  
  [the record](../../../CANON/foundation.json)
- **"Order vs disorder and not the hardware issues"**  
  The author's insistence that the reading is informational fidelity - errors in individual molecules - and never a statement about enzymes. A foundation entry now says the gauge never sees an enzyme, and that naming a mechanism for a pattern of disorder is an inference made outside the physics. Every lever must state what the reading actually sees.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the discipline that keeps a physics claim from quietly becoming a biology claim.  
  [the record](../../../CANON/foundation.json)
- **Core and medium: "a lever moves fidelity but it is just the medium"**  
  Every foundation entry is now tagged core or medium. The second instrument was retagged as an instrument rather than a law, while the balance stayed core. The levers are how a handle reaches the law's terms in this particular substrate - change substrate and the levers change completely while the law does not move, which is why the same construction reads a qubit and a chip where there is no enzyme in sight.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the answer to the obvious attack: that the framework is biology dressed as physics.  
  [the record](../../../CANON/foundation.json)
- **The sphere, carried forward with its weak version named as weak**  
  Three claims were separated rather than blurred: the DNA address wrapped onto a ball, named weak because its curvature is a filing system and not biology; the information sphere, which is established mathematics - for any site the pair of square roots lies on a unit circle, the distance between two states is an angle, and a whole methylome lands on a high-dimensional sphere where the entropy both instruments read is defined; and the nucleus sphere, real curvature that other people have already measured, where the stretches pressed against the nuclear envelope are the ones cancers lose in large blocks. What carries over from the microwave sky is splitting a map by scale and equal-area pixels; the acoustic peaks do not.  
  _Why it matters:_ The author's correction is the reason it is now a record rather than a conversation: it is not a strand wrapped on a ball, it is the order a species has to hold, and the map measures the output rather than the hardware.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_SPHERE_01.md)
- **"Doesnt physics move the levers as much as the levers move the physics?"**  
  That question turned a list of planned experiments into a register of candidate INSTRUMENTS. If a handle moves fidelity, the same handle can be read backwards as a measurement - so each lever is also a possible reading, with its own blind spot.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the reason today ended with six instruments rather than one long queue of tests.  
  [the record](../INSTRUMENTS.md)
- **"Can the atlas not help us sort the whole blood enough... or is that old IAM's class h_min illusion?"**  
  The answer split: it is not the retired framework's assumption, it is an identifiability limit - fractions can be estimated, individual molecules cannot be separated on an array. That distinction is what made the measurement worth running, and the measurement then gave a limit and an opening in the same table.  
  _Why it matters:_ A question that could have been answered with an opinion was answered with a number instead.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BLOOD_SENSITIVITY_01.md)
- **"The lever chapter will be the most attacked part of the book"**  
  That judgement is why every lever now carries a falsifier written before any data is opened, and why each falsifier states whether it would break the law itself or only our account of the biology. Two are law-scope; three are medium-scope.  
  _Why it matters:_ A chapter that states what would break it is harder to attack than one that argues it is right.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_LEVERS_01.md)
- **The sphere, carried forward**  
  The question of how the whole genome could be represented and measured as one object stays open with three versions on record: an address-on-a-ball version, named weak; an information version, named as the one to build; and a nucleus version, kept as a separate question about the medium rather than the information.  
  _Why it matters:_ The reading is of order against disorder in the information, not of the strand, so the map has to be of the information - which is also what decides how the cosmology methods would transfer.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_SPHERE_01.md)
- **What this section is for**  
  A reader can see that the ideas arrived in conversation rather than from a plan. Several of the day's results exist because a question was asked in passing and could be turned into a derivation, a cost estimate and a committed test before the thread cooled - and several were discarded in the same conversation for reasons recorded here rather than forgotten.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the part of a research program that papers never show, and it is the part a potential collaborator is actually evaluating.
