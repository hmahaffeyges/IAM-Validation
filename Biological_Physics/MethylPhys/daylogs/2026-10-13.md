- **Territory contrast: a parameter-free prediction, met to 1 %** — _synthetic only_  
  The balance equation says a common shortfall in maintenance should move the hard-to-maintain parts of the genome more than the easy parts, by a factor fixed by healthy baselines alone. Predicted 4.158, measured 4.12 to 4.20 across shortfalls of 2 to 20 %, with nothing fitted. The declared blind spot was confirmed: a loss hitting both territories equally moves the reading by 0.1 % of the signal.  
  _Why it matters:_ This is the first lever-grade prediction on arrays rather than sequencing - a number calculated in advance, then matched.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_TERRITORY_01.md)
- **Territory contrast: the practical bar failed** — _failed_  
  The same test asked whether comparing two kinds of site inside one sample cancels the usual nuisances. It cancels an additive plate offset exactly, and cannot cancel a multiplicative one: a 10 % change in which cells are present moves the reading about five times as much as a 2 % maintenance shortfall. There is also a ceiling effect - these sites sit so close to fully switched on that noise can only push them one way.  
  _Why it matters:_ The quantity is real but the instrument is not usable yet. The next version reports two forms of the comparison, because one cancels each kind of nuisance, and measures the ceiling effect from the data instead of ignoring it.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_TERRITORY_01.md)
- **The ceiling audit: 7,681 readings, none past the ceiling** — _audit_  
  The gauge's top end is fixed by physics: past a certain damage the needle stops climbing and comes back down, so a very damaged sample can read deceptively low. Every reading we hold that records this was checked - 7,681 of them - and not one is past that point. The composition-aware path behind the tissue results never recorded the check, so those three cannot be audited from their saved files.  
  _Why it matters:_ The direction of that gap is the safe one: a past-ceiling tumor reads lower than its damage warrants, so it could only have made us miss tumors, never invent one. The check is now recorded for every future reading of that kind.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_CEILING_AUDIT_01.md)
- **Whole blood on arrays: per person no, per group yes** — _development_  
  Read with the chain's own stages on 24 healthy whole bloods - 23 read, one withheld by the chain for site coverage. Healthy people differ by 6.4 %, and the chain's own leverage is 2.09 % of the reading per 1 % of pattern loss, so one person would need about a 7.2 % loss to leave the healthy range. Tumor tissue shows 2 to 7 %. Comparing groups is a different story: with 50 people on each side, a mean loss under 1 % separates them.  
  _Why it matters:_ This is a real result and a real limit at once. Group studies in blood are within reach now; per-person blood detection needs either a tighter reading or the molecule-by-molecule route, which is sequencing.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BLOOD_SENSITIVITY_01.md)
- **The sequencing failure diagnosed: the reading had been taken from the worst place in the file** — _diagnosis_  
  At the head of each run the chemistry's conversion rate differs from the rest of the file by 34 times its own uncertainty, in every library including both controls. On top of that common effect, the three libraries that failed read 11 to 14 % below the controls read at the same position, recovering to within a few percent deeper in. And the original reading reproduces the head to 0.0 % - so the commissioning check had inherited exactly the place where those libraries are depressed.  
  _Why it matters:_ The sign of the head effect differs between libraries, which rules out estimating its bias and correcting for it. The head has to be excluded, not corrected.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **The first proposed fix failed its own planted test** — _failed_  
  Reading several places in each file and pooling the counts turns a 15 % error into a 5 % error against a 3 % tolerance - better and still failing. The reason is arithmetic rather than tuning: pooling still counts the bad block, so it divides a bias instead of removing it. Adding more data removes noise, never bias.  
  _Why it matters:_ It cost an afternoon of compute-free work and no data, which is what planting tests first is for.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **The trim rule: six planted bars, all met** — _synthetic only_  
  The replacement skips the unsettled head of a file, and how much to skip is decided by the raw reads' own chemistry - the fraction of C among C and T outside a CpG, which needs no alignment and knows nothing about our physics. On made-up files it recovered a planted 12 % error to within a fifth of a percent, left a clean file untouched, refused a file bad throughout, behaved identically whether the planted error pushed up or down, and did nothing at all when the chemistry looked fine.  
  _Why it matters:_ That last bar is the honest limit, declared rather than hidden: the rule may not act on a bias it has no independent evidence for. Trimming until the answer improves would be tuning, and a reviewer should refuse it.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_TRIM_01.md)

## What we learned that we did not know yesterday

- **The two meters are blind in opposite ways, and it is derived from the code**  
  The held-error meter matches an isolated loss - a methylated call on both sides of an unmethylated one - so a run of two or more consecutive losses contributes exactly zero to it. The clustering meter is built for precisely that case.  
  _Why it matters:_ This is the clearest example of why more than one instrument is needed, and it came out of a simulation refusing to score itself rather than out of an argument.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_LEVERS_01.md)
- **Pooling divides a bias; it never removes one**  
  Averaging in a contaminated measurement reduces the contamination by the number of measurements and leaves it there. No choice of positions or weights fixes that.  
  _Why it matters:_ It is the reason the trim rule excludes rather than corrects, and it generalizes well beyond this instrument.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **An array averages molecules before anyone reads them**  
  That is why a tube of blood cannot be read per person on this platform: part of the disorder in the tube is the mixing itself, and what remains is smaller than the spread between healthy people.  
  _Why it matters:_ It separates a genuine identifiability limit from the retired framework's class assumption, which is a different thing.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BLOOD_SENSITIVITY_01.md)

## Our own mistakes today, and what caught each

Four, and none was caught by re-reading our own work. This section exists because a record that only contains successes is not a record.

- **A simulated leverage 1.8 times too high** — _development_  
  The blood simulation reported 3.79 % of the reading per 1 % of loss; the chain's own per-sample measurement on real arrays is 2.09 %. The simulation used different sites and different profiles. The chain's number is the one quoted from now on.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught by running the chain itself instead of trusting a stand-in for it.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BLOOD_SENSITIVITY_01.md)
- **A design that said ratio where the derivation forces difference** — _synthetic only_  
  The territory note described the reading as a ratio. Because the relative rise is larger at the low-error sites, a ratio moves the wrong way and weakly; the difference is what the derivation gives, and it also cancels an additive offset exactly.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught while writing the test, before any data was touched.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_TERRITORY_01.md)
- **A table of head effects with the signs wrong** — _diagnosis_  
  The first draft compared each library's head against the mean of two small deeper parts, which mixes in the position effect every library shares. The sealed comparison is against the control libraries read at the same position, and the absolute framing is not even consistent in sign across libraries.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught by a script written to compute the note's numbers, which disagreed with what had been typed.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_IAMA_READACROSS_01.md)
- **A board exported before it was regenerated** — _structure_  
  The instrument register was updated but the copy saved for the author was the earlier one, so three entries looked unstarted when they had been run that same night.  
  _Why it matters:_ Caught by an independent review of the saved file. Fixed at the source - the register entry that had never been updated - rather than in the copy.  
  [the record](../INSTRUMENTS.md)
