## What we set out to do

Goal for the day: an instrument built from physics alone, so a sample can be read without a library of other people's cells (an atlas) and without lab-by-lab calibration. Every test ran on made-up data first, then on real data once.

## What ran, and how each came out

Each prediction was written down before its data was read, and every counting method was first checked on made-up data with a known answer.

- **Every healthy cell follows the same simple rule** — _development_  
  Cells tag their DNA to remember what kind of cell they are, and make a few tagging mistakes. Across 56 kinds of healthy human cells, the number of mistakes depends on one thing: how much of its DNA the cell keeps loosely tagged. All 56 fall on one straight line (53 within 0.003 of it). Whiteboard part 7 (the loose end read off the line); made reproducible as committed scripts with manifest rows PHY-045 and PHY-046.  
  _Why it matters:_ This is what lets an instrument know what a healthy cell should look like from that cell's own sample, instead of looking it up in an atlas. It is the central step toward a test that does not depend on any one lab's reference data.  
  [the record](../../../development/loose_end_2026-10-15/loose_end_line.py)
- **The rule holds with a cut set by physics** — _development_  
  The line first used a borrowed cut (70 percent tagged) to call a stretch of DNA loose. Replaced with the physics value, 2/3, where a stretch is held by at least one bit's worth of energy: the line holds (r 0.85) and the cells keep the same order (0.996 agreement). Box job eef8d756, manifest PHY-048.  
  _Why it matters:_ The result does not hinge on a number someone chose; it comes from the physics.  
  [the record](../../../development/loose_share_2026-10-15/arch_cut.py)
- **The rule from each sample's own DNA pieces, with no arrays** — _development_  
  Read straight from each sample's own sequenced pieces of DNA, with nothing borrowed from other data, the same line comes back (r 0.81; odds of chance about 1 in 10 trillion). It is weaker and the firm rate moved, and young progenitor cells sit below it, and the whole-genome version (box job 2d3d817b) gave the same picture: r 0.79, the same firm rate, on all 56 cells once ten refused downloads were re-read. Manifest PHY-049.  
  _Why it matters:_ The first reading of the gauge with no atlas and no arrays at all.  
  [the record](../../../development/loose_share_2026-10-15/own_reads_loose.py)
- **Island side with the eraser removed: read once** — _development_  
  The DNA's bare 'islands' are kept clean 1.7 times better than the writer enzyme alone allows. Removing the eraser enzyme barely changed that (1.07 times), so a shield, not the eraser, keeps them clean. Manifest PHY-046.  
  _Why it matters:_ Narrows what holds a cell's identity in place, the next piece to derive.  
  [the record](../../../development/island_eraser_2026-10-15/island_eraser.py)

- **Sealed before the atlas exists: the Loyfer and Moss use tests** — _sealed_  
  Two jobs a reference atlas is built for: finding each cell's marker sites, and working out which cells are in a mixed sample. Atlas v2.1 is compared with Moss's published reference on the same held-out arrays, including mixtures with brain and lung cells Moss's reference does not hold. Each bar was first checked on made-up data to pass when it should and fail when it should; the first version of one bar could not see a planted worse atlas and was fixed before any real data. The bars are a showcase for the atlas's users, not a referee's gate.  
  _Why it matters:_ So the atlas is judged on the job its users actually need, by rules fixed before its first value exists.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_ATLAS_USE_01.md)
- **The clustering meter passes its last check** — _sealed_  
  A public study measured 14 healthy adults twice on different chips; nobody in this work had ever read it. The bar, set before the arrays were read, allowed a spread of 0.125 between a person's two readings; the meter's spread was 0.038. The same 173 arrays were a third laboratory for its healthy range: 172 read inside it.  
  _Why it matters:_ Every check in the meter's plan is met, and the plan's own rule says that commissions it. It is commissioned on whole blood and isolated neutrophils, our second commissioned instrument; its detection of clumped damage has been shown on changes planted into real arrays, not yet on real tumors, which is its next test.  
  [the record](../doors/COMMISSIONING_NOTE_CSCORE.md)
- **Tumors' bare islands: written over in some, not all** — _development_  
  All six early-onset colorectal cancer pairs counted on the island side. Four of six tumors read above the healthy island range; three of six read more than 1.2 times their own normal tissue. One pair runs the other way, and its tumor's sequencing error is 3.5 times its normal's.  
  _Why it matters:_ Reported, not scored. It says the islands are written over in some tumors and by less than double; the firm-territory test is next.  
  [the record](../doors/DEV_BACKBONE_01.md)

## What we learned that we did not know yesterday

- **A healthy cell carries its own reference** — _development_  
  Its expected tagging mistakes follow from how loosely it holds its DNA, one straight line for every cell type tested.  
  _Why it matters:_ The reason a future test may need no library of other people's cells.  
  [the record](../../../development/loose_end_2026-10-15/loose_end_line.py)
- **One loose level for every kind of cell** — _development_  
  Loosely held DNA makes the same rate of mistakes in every cell type.  
  [the record](../../../development/WHITEBOARD_2026-10-15.md)
- **The cell's bare islands are held by a shield, not the eraser** — _development_  
  Bare islands hold at 4.38 kT against 3.83 kT for the firm tags.  
  [the record](../../../development/WHITEBOARD_2026-10-15.md)
- **What the new instrument can read** — _structure_  
  Any organism that tags its DNA, from any lab, wherever single-piece sequencing exists: public nanopore genomes, plants grown at two temperatures, dogs, unread salmon data (PHY-058 to PHY-068).  
  _Why it matters:_ Opens far more data than the old reference-bound instrument could use.  
  [the record](../../../CANON/manifest.json)
