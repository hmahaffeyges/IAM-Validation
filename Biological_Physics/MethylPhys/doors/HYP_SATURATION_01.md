# HYP-SATURATION-01 — a saturated field, and a tumor as new surface (hypothesis, 2026-10-13; the author's; NOTHING TESTED)

**The author's picture, in his words (2026-10-13).** "I imagine a sick organ, a sick cavity, a sick environment, and the cells in that
particular organ are writing like crazy at their limit and something has to give if it continues. So the weakest point in that
particular system, or that particular group of informational components, all of a sudden can write no more, and something has to
give, so a tumor forms so the cells can continue writing - only this time they have created a new surface to do so. The black hole."

**Stated as physics, without purpose.** An organ under sustained stress keeps its cells writing near their limit, so the whole field
drifts right of the healthy middle toward the full surface (CANON/foundation.json, gauge_direction). The components with the least
headroom saturate first. A saturated region cannot hold more error on the surface it has; where clones that keep dividing take over,
their growth adds surface. As with a black hole, whose entropy grows with its area, more writing requires more surface. Cells do not
form a tumor in order to keep writing; a saturated field is the condition under which dividing clones win, and their expansion is new
surface. This sits beside, not against, the established account (driver mutations and clonal selection): it proposes the CONDITIONS.

**What already fits (descriptive, development use of spent data).** Adjacent normal tissue reads right of healthy: 28 of 28 cancer
types in the class-era test (VAL-003) and colorectal, liver and lung on the current gauge (DEV-CEILING-02 step 3), consistent with
field cancerization. Tumors carry sites at the full surface where healthy references carry none (DEV-CEILING-02 step 3).

**Falsifiers, written before any of them is run.** Each would break this picture where stated.
1. **The weakest point fails first.** In tissue on the way to cancer, fragile territory (least holding headroom) must reach the surface
   before stable territory. If stable territory saturates first, or both together, the picture is wrong. Reading: the territory
   contrast (DEV-TERRITORY-01/02), on an ordered lesion series.
2. **The field comes before the tumor.** Healthy-donor tissue < adjacent normal < tumor, read the same composition-aware way, with
   stronger fields where tumors are multiple. If adjacent normal reads like healthy-donor tissue once composition is accounted for, the
   field is composition and the picture loses its first step. Test: DEV-FIELD-01 (to be designed).
3. **Saturation per cell, growth in surface.** Past breach, the per-site surface share must level off while tumors keep growing; if it
   keeps climbing with stage without limit, or falls, the new-surface step is wrong. Data: TCGA stage, size and purity.
4. **Least headroom goes first.** Groups of components with the smallest headroom between their healthy middle and the full surface
   must breach first. If breach order is unrelated to headroom, the picture is wrong. (The author's class-min intuition, restated as
   headroom on the current gauge rather than as classes.)

5. **New surface needs new supply (author, 2026-10-13: the tumor's structure and blood vessels).** Every write costs energy, so a
   growing writing surface needs a growing supply: the angiogenic switch (a tumor past a millimeter or two recruits its own vessels)
   and the metabolic switch are that supply. Malignant tumors are complex but DISORDERED - chaotic, leaky vessels and broken
   architecture - while benign tumors stay organized and recognizably their tissue; on the gauge that is disorder on the right and a
   held pattern near the middle. Prediction: tumors with a larger share of sites at the full surface carry stronger angiogenesis and
   metabolic-switch expression signatures, and benign lesions read near 1 without them. If surface share and those signatures are
   unrelated, the energy-supply step is wrong. Data: TCGA expression for the same tumors already read.

6. **The moves are non-random (author, 2026-10-13: "it is intentional in that its not random erasure of entire blocks of genes or
   random tagging of entire blocks. I dont mean intentional in a philosophical sense, I just mean that its not random").** Right of
   Normal is departure from the healthy pattern, not chaos: a tumor removes marks in whole blocks and adds them at specific promoters,
   and both WHERE it removes and WHERE it adds are patterned. Prediction, on the 219 tumors already read: the sites that gain and the
   sites that lose recur across patients far more often than chance (a permutation of site labels within each tumor sets chance), and
   gains concentrate at gene promoters. If gains and losses scatter - no more recurrence than the permutation gives - the moves are
   drift, and the picture of a patterned, selected program is wrong. Wording rule: "non-random" and "selected", never "intentional".

7. **Two phases: the program, then the disorder (author, 2026-10-13: "The tumor and the not un-intentional gene writing and
   erasing are creating the extra writing surface but the divisions bring in the disorder").** Three culprits act on the loss side:
   writer mistakes and erasure (in every cell) and missed restores at copying (only where cells divide, growing with division rate).
   Phase 1, the PROGRAM: writer and eraser, non-random, switch specific genes; on single molecules it is CONCORDANT (whole molecules
   switched together), on arrays recurrent promoter gains. Phase 2, the DISORDER: division-driven missed restores, random per event and
   patterned only by replication timing; on single molecules it is DISCORDANT (mixed patterns within a molecule, molecules from one
   locus disagreeing), on arrays loss at isolated sites in late-replicating blocks. Predictions: the program appears first, already in
   precancer, and disorder grows later with how far the tumor has gone; across the 219 tumors the promoter program and the late-block
   loss are only weakly related; and the breach is where discordance starts to climb. If the program and the disorder rise together as
   one quantity, or disorder appears before the program, the two-phase picture is wrong. Prior result in the same direction (not ours):
   locally disordered methylation varies within tumors and tracks outcome in a leukemia (Landau et al. 2014, Cancer Cell).

8. **What spreads is instability, not only tumors (author, 2026-10-13: "I envision what is spreading is not tumors, but
   instability... informational instability, the opposite of fidelity. Once the local signal is corrupted the saturated areas need new
   writing surfaces... being they are already running at their max the tumor is quick to form").** The power-grid picture he brought:
   a cascade follows the network and only trips buses with thin margin, and voltage collapse shows as a sag across remote buses BEFORE
   the outage reaches them. In cancer the network is lymph and blood, and "seed and soil" says only receptive organs take a seed; the
   pre-metastatic niche says distant organs are prepared before any cell arrives. To leave and to settle, a cell switches its program
   and switches back, largely epigenetically - the program phase (falsifier 7) running twice. Predictions, each breakable:
   (a) metastases carry the primary's concordant program, plus more disorder (more discordance, more late-block loss) than the primary;
   (b) tissue in a common metastatic destination reads right of healthy before it is seeded (the sag);
   (c) the instability is systemic: in people with cancer, tissue away from the tumor and blood cells read right of healthy donors,
       and further in spread disease than in localized disease, compared within one series (DEV-GROUP-01 rule).
   If metastases share none of the primary's program, or distant tissue and blood read like healthy donors once series and composition
   are controlled, the picture of spreading instability is wrong. Data to find, metadata only: paired primary and metastasis methylation
   sets; distant-organ tissue before seeding; blood or cell-free DNA from localized against spread disease, in one series.
   **The hope, stated as a hope:** if fidelity can be read across the whole body, the signal that the system is running at its limit
   might one day guide how to turn it down. Nothing here supports a treatment claim.

**B1 and B2 met in every cancer: the moves recur across patients, and a shuffle gives none.** **B3 not met in any cancer:** by the
fixed definition (TSS200 or TSS1500), recurrent gains are no more promoter-heavy than the array itself. That part of the claim, as
written, does not hold on these data.

**Two weaknesses, stated so nobody has to find them for us.**
1. **The null is weak.** A within-tumor shuffle produces no site recurring in half the patients, so almost any shared structure beats
   it. Passing B1 and B2 shows the moves are shared, not that they are a program.
2. **Composition is recurrent by construction.** Each difference is tumor minus adjacent tissue, and every tumor is richer in its cell
   of origin than the tissue beside it. Sites that differ between cell types will recur in every patient for that reason alone. A
   hard test needs the recurrence measured after the composition is removed (the composition-aware reading), or on purified cells.

The next version, written before it is run, needs those two fixes and a fresh set: all four sets here are spent.
