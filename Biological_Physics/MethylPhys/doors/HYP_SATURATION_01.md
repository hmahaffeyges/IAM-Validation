# HYP-SATURATION-01 — a saturated field, and a tumor as new surface (hypothesis, 2026-10-13; the author's; NOTHING TESTED)

**The author's picture, in his words (2026-10-13).** "I imagine a sick organ, a sick cavity, a sick environment, and the cells in that
particular organ are writing like crazy at their limit and something has to give if it continues. So the weakest point in that
particular system, or that particular group of informational components, all of a sudden can write no more, and something has to
give, so a tumor forms so the cells can continue writing - only this time they have created a new surface to do so. The black hole."

**Stated as physics, without purpose.** An organ under sustained stress keeps its cells writing near their limit, so the whole field
drifts right of the healthy middle toward the full surface (CANON/foundation.json, gauge_direction). The components with the least
headroom saturate first. A saturated region cannot hold more error on the surface it has; where clones that keep dividing take over,
their growth adds surface. As with a black hole, whose entropy grows with its area, more writing requires more surface. Cells do not
form a tumor in order to keep writing; a saturated field is the condition under which dividing clones win, and their expansion is new
surface. This sits beside, not against, the established account (driver mutations and clonal selection): it proposes the CONDITIONS.

**What already fits (descriptive, development use of spent data).** Adjacent normal tissue reads right of healthy: 28 of 28 cancer
types in the class-era test (VAL-003) and colorectal, liver and lung on the current gauge (DEV-CEILING-02 step 3), consistent with
field cancerization. Tumors carry sites at the full surface where healthy references carry none (DEV-CEILING-02 step 3).

**Falsifiers, written before any of them is run.** Each would break this picture where stated.
1. **The weakest point fails first.** In tissue on the way to cancer, fragile territory (least holding headroom) must reach the surface
   before stable territory. If stable territory saturates first, or both together, the picture is wrong. Reading: the territory
   contrast (DEV-TERRITORY-01/02), on an ordered lesion series.
2. **The field comes before the tumor.** Healthy-donor tissue < adjacent normal < tumor, read the same composition-aware way, with
   stronger fields where tumors are multiple. If adjacent normal reads like healthy-donor tissue once composition is accounted for, the
   field is composition and the picture loses its first step. Test: DEV-FIELD-01 (to be designed).
3. **Saturation per cell, growth in surface.** Past breach, the per-site surface share must level off while tumors keep growing; if it
   keeps climbing with stage without limit, or falls, the new-surface step is wrong. Data: TCGA stage, size and purity.
4. **Least headroom goes first.** Groups of components with the smallest headroom between their healthy middle and the full surface
   must breach first. If breach order is unrelated to headroom, the picture is wrong. (The author's class-min intuition, restated as
   headroom on the current gauge rather than as classes.)

**In plain words.** The idea is that a whole organ can be under strain before any tumor exists, its cells writing at their limit, and
that the tumor appears at the weakest spot as new room to keep writing - the way a black hole grows its surface. It is an idea, not a
result. Four tests are written down that could each prove it wrong.

**What it would take to finish:** falsifier 2 first (DEV-FIELD-01), then 1 and 4 on an ordered lesion series, then 3 on TCGA stage.

**Not a milestone:** a hypothesis with no test run.
