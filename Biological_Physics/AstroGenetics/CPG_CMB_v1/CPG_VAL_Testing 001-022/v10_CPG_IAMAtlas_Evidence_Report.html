SECTION 1

What we've learned — plain language for clinicians

This section is intentionally jargon-light. If you are reading this to decide whether to take a sample, recommend a sample, or interpret a CPG report, this section is for you. Section 2 onward has the technical detail.

The instrument in one paragraph

CPG reads a single methylation array (Illumina EPIC or 450K) drawn from a blood buffy coat and reports where the patient sits, by architecture class and by cell type, relative to an age-matched healthy reference. It does this by measuring the methylation entropy of CpG sites known to mark specific cell architectures and dividing each by a per-class "information floor" derived from first principles. The output is a vector of 8 architecture-class A-scores, a finer vector of 115 cell-type A-scores, and a single universal-departure number (Mahalanobis hyper-volume distance) summarizing how far the patient's cellular state is from the healthy centroid.

1.1 Breast pre-diagnostic detection — what we found

Headline (plain English)

In blood drawn from women more than 10 years before their clinical breast cancer diagnosis, CPG sees a broad cellular-architectural disturbance that distinguishes them from age-matched healthy controls. The signal is not one biomarker — it is a coordinated shift across many cell types, with the strongest individual signals in basophils (a normally-rare immune cell) and in breast epithelial cells circulating in blood. The same single summary number that catches this also outperforms the existing published Xu-538 breast panel by a meaningful margin.

What this means for clinical use. A blood draw a decade before clinical disease could identify women in a higher-risk trajectory while there is still time for lifestyle, screening intensification, or chemo-prevention to matter. CPG does not diagnose breast cancer. It detects a methylation-trajectory pattern that, in two independent EPIC-Italy cohorts, was associated with clinical diagnosis years to a decade later.

Specific findings.

  • Universal departure-from-healthy: Mahalanobis hyper-volume distance d = +1.88 (GSE51057, n=11 cases / 177 HC) and d = +2.10 (GSE51032, n=36 cases / 424 HC). Both effect sizes are very large by the conventional Cohen's d interpretation (d > 0.8 = "large").
  • Strongest individual cell-type signal: basophils, d = +1.58 / +1.01 across the two cohorts. Basophils are normally about 1% of circulating leukocytes — invisible if you only score the immune class as a whole. The 115-cell A-score resolution surfaces them.
  • Tissue-of-origin signal in blood: breast epithelial cells in blood show d = +1.28 / +0.61 even more than a decade before diagnosis. This is the "field-effect cancerization" pattern — the tissue at risk is already shedding methylation-distinguishable cells into the circulation long before the tumor is clinically visible.
  • Immune surveillance suppression: the principal component capturing T-cell variation is suppressed (d = −0.67 / −0.58 case vs control). The immune system appears to be "looking away" from the future cancer.
  • Reproducibility: when the post-build pipeline was re-run on the same data in 2026-06-03, GSE51032 Mahalanobis came out at d = +2.088 vs the original anchor of +2.097 — within 0.4%, well within sampling variation. The instrument is internally consistent across pipeline revisions.
  • Cross-method validation: a second deconvolution method (NILC v2) run on the same data agrees with the primary Walther method on dominant blood compartments at Spearman ρ ≈ 0.74 (immune) and ρ ≈ 0.82 (progenitor). The findings are not artifacts of one deconvolution algorithm.

1.2 Alzheimer's and tauopathy differentiation — what we found

Headline (plain English)

In blood drawn at the time of clinical Alzheimer's diagnosis, CPG sees a targeted disturbance of immune-class cells — not the broad architectural shift seen in pre-diagnostic breast cancer. More importantly, the same blood-based metric distinguishes three neurodegenerative diseases that are clinically hard to tell apart: Alzheimer's (signal goes UP), progressive supranuclear palsy and corticobasal degeneration (signal goes DOWN — the opposite direction), and frontotemporal dementia (intermediate). This direction-of-departure discrimination is the strongest evidence yet that the underlying physics of the metric is biologically meaningful.

What this means for clinical use. Differential diagnosis among AD, FTD, and PSP/CBD can take years and multiple specialist visits today. CPG offers a single blood draw that places a patient on a three-way continuum, potentially shortening the diagnostic workup. As with breast, it is not a stand-alone diagnostic — it is a flag that changes downstream workup.

Specific findings.

  • AD has a targeted not universal signature. AIBL cohort (n=161 AD / 471 HC EPIC): top per-cell-type effect Eosino A-score d = −0.43 (Bonferroni-significant, p = 2.3 × 10⁻⁵); 20 of 115 cell types Bonferroni-significant in the negative direction; zero positive. Universal Mahalanobis only modest at d = +0.20 — confirming the targeted nature.
  • Cross-platform replication on AddNeuroMed (n=93 AD / 96 HC 450K): Eosino effect replicates at d = −0.46 (vs AIBL d = −0.43). Universal Mahalanobis attenuates to near-null on 450K due to coverage gap, but the per-cell biology is robust.
  • Three-way specificity discrimination (GSE53740 GIFT cohort): AD Mahalanobis d = +0.68 (p = 0.001); PSP/CBD d = −0.38 (p = 2 × 10⁻⁶, the BELOW-normal architectural compaction direction); FTD d = +0.28 (intermediate). Same metric, three distinct disease signatures by direction.
  • Disease-trained panel outperforms universal screen. The 7-CpG Rule A panel achieves AUC 0.84 for AD discrimination on AIBL; universal Mahalanobis achieves AUC 0.62. This is the opposite of breast pre-dx (where universal Mahalanobis BEAT the disease-trained Xu-538 panel by 0.75). Different diseases have different signature topologies; the operational scoring routes accordingly.
  • Cellular age signature (independent layer): in GSE53740 GIFT AD samples, immune-class cellular age comes back ~9 years younger than HC (case 55.4y vs HC 64.6y, d = −0.56 on OK-status samples). This is consistent with cell-cycle arrest / senescence in the immune compartment rather than chronological aging.
  • All 8 null tests pass. Every VAL in the AD series survived random label-permutation testing at p < 0.05. The signals are not artifacts of how cases were assigned.

1.3 What both cards reveal about the instrument

The two cards together establish that CPG can detect two qualitatively different signature topologies with the same instrument:

  • Broad architectural shift (breast pre-diagnostic): many cell types simultaneously disturbed, captured well by a universal-departure metric.
  • Targeted disruption (AD at diagnosis): one or a few cell types specifically disturbed, captured well by disease-trained panels but poorly by a universal screen.

The same Mahalanobis hyper-volume distance can also distinguish direction-of-departure: positive (outward, in AD), negative (inward / compaction, in PSP/CBD), and intermediate (FTD). This is direct evidence that the underlying architectural-information physics is doing biologically meaningful work — the instrument is not just detecting "different from healthy" in a generic sense, it is detecting WHICH kind of different.

1.4 What's still uncertain — be honest with your patient

  • Specificity for breast pre-dx. The breast signal at >10y pre-dx is captured by the universal Mahalanobis. We have NOT yet tested whether other cancer pre-dx cohorts (CRC, lung, etc.) would show the same universal signal. So the universal metric may be capturing "future cancer somewhere" rather than "future breast cancer specifically." The breast-epithelial tissue-of-origin signal is more specific, but only emerges with the second-stage analysis.
  • Single-patient interpretation. All effect sizes here are cohort-level. A single CPG report on a single woman tells you where she sits on the population distribution; it does NOT tell you "this woman will get breast cancer in 11 years." Serial sampling against her own baseline is the intended clinical use mode.
  • Cross-ethnicity validation. The breast cohort is EPIC-Italy (Mediterranean European). The AD cohorts are predominantly European-ancestry. Cross-ethnicity replication is outstanding.
  • Cross-platform attenuation. Universal Mahalanobis on 450K platform comes out attenuated relative to EPIC due to the 86–95% CpG coverage overlap. Per-cell biology replicates; universal metrics may need platform-specific normalization.
  • Disease-trained panel for AD has age confound. The 7-CpG Rule A panel's R² with age is 0.26 — meaningful age signal mixed in. Production reports for AD must explicitly subtract age before reporting an AD-A-score (the age-axis foreground subtraction module handles this). The 115-cell A-score layer is naturally age-orthogonal at the architectural level.
  • The matrix-based per-patient matching engine is not yet wired. The disease signature matrix v1.6 has documented signatures for AD, PSP/CBD, FTD, breast pre-dx, and 76 other conditions, but the per-patient "match my profile against the matrix" function (Stage 8 Path B) is specced and not yet implemented. Per-patient reporting currently goes through the card-driven Stage 8 Path A.
SECTION 2

What CPG does — current methodology

The CPG instrument is built on the IAMAtlas REBUILD reference posterior. Production scoring uses the modules documented here. External published panels and atlases that were used during the historical build phase are no longer in the production scoring chain.

2.1 The reference

IAMAtlas REBUILD — a hierarchical Bayesian posterior over 483,092 CpG sites, sliced into 8 architecture classes (terminal, immune, secretory, progenitor, cycling, stromal, stem_adult, stem_pluri) and 115 cell types. Each class has an information-theoretic minimum methylation entropy H_min(class), frozen on 2026-04-06, used as the divisor in the A-score normalization. The atlas lives at Biological_Physics/atlas_vault/iamatlas_v0_1/IAMAtlasREBUILD.csv.xz with SHA-256 tracking.

2.2 The runtime pipeline

For each patient methylation array, the runtime executes a 12-stage chain. Stages 2 through 7 produce the scoring outputs that populate the report; Stage 8 matches against the card and/or the matrix; Stages 9 and 10 assemble and deliver.

2.3 The Recipe boundary

The Recipe — the first-principles derivation chain from Jacobson's thermodynamic gravity through the virial theorem through Landauer's principle through the H_min calibration — is vault-only. It does not appear in this report, in any commercial code, in any repository, or in any public document. The published A-score formula A = H(β) / H_min(class) is the public-facing equation; the H_min values per class are quoted as constants without their derivation. This is the Coca-Cola model: the can is public, the recipe is vault. Recipe transfer occurs only under full institutional acquisition.

SECTION 5

Why the two cards look different — physics perspective

The clinical observation: breast pre-dx shows broad architectural disturbance, AD shows targeted immune-class disruption, PSP/CBD shows architectural compaction (the opposite direction). Why?

The IAM framework predicts that disease at the cellular level is not "a brake falling off a single gene" — it is a system-wide loss of pattern-maintenance margin across thousands of CpG addresses simultaneously, eventually converging on focal failure. Different diseases approach focal failure along different trajectories through the architecture-class state space.

Breast pre-diagnostic: at >10 years before clinical disease, the system has not yet localized. The methylation architecture is in a broadly perturbed state — many cell types simultaneously drifting from their H_min floors. The universal Mahalanobis metric is the natural readout because every dimension is contributing.

AD at diagnosis: by clinical onset, the disease has localized to immune-class architecture (consistent with the neuroinflammation literature). The architectural disturbance is concentrated in a few cell types — Eosino, Neutro, T-cells. The universal metric averages this concentrated signal against many quiet dimensions, attenuating the apparent effect. The disease-trained panel weights the affected dimensions and recovers the signal.

PSP/CBD architectural compaction: the 4R-tauopathy diseases show methylation patterns MORE compressed toward the H_min floors, not less. The Mahalanobis distance to the HC centroid goes negative because the patient cluster is INSIDE the HC distribution rather than outside it. Biologically, this is consistent with accelerated cellular quiescence / stress-induced compaction. It is the strongest single piece of evidence that the metric is measuring real architectural geometry — generic "different from healthy" would not produce direction-resolved discrimination across diseases.

The cellular-age findings carry the same theme: in GSE53740 GIFT AD samples, immune cellular age comes back ~9 years younger than HC — consistent with cell-cycle arrest in the immune compartment, not chronological aging. In breast pre-dx GSE51032, cycling-class cells come back ~5.5 years younger (d = −0.53) — proliferating compartments slowing down years before clinical disease.

The framing for any clinician trying to interpret a CPG report: "Information just means order. There is a minimum order required for a cell to remain a cell. CPG measures how close the patient's cells are to that floor, and in which direction they are drifting."

SECTION 6

Reproducibility

Every claim in this report is reproducible from the public repository github.com/hmahaffeyges/IAM-Validation. Each VAL block lives in its own folder with five files:

  • PREREG.md — what was tested, what direction was expected, what decision rules were declared
  • per_sample.csv — one row per patient with the signal column, arm, and covariates
  • null_results.json — outputs of the L9 null suite (HC-label permutation minimum; age-strata permutation where age-relevant)
  • cohort_manifest.json — link to the primary cohort folder, observed effect size, primary null p-value
  • CPG_VAL_NNN_OUTCOME.md — substantive narrative explaining what the test showed and what it means

Cohort folders at validation_runs/<card>_cohorts/<GSE>_*/ carry the β matrix (SHA-256 tracked; large files stored gzipped to fit under GitHub size limits), clinical metadata, raw GEO metadata, the per-cohort Walther + NILC + Mahalanobis + cellular age + tier outputs, the per-cohort 115-cell A-score CSV in foundation-cohort format, and the cohort manifest.

Reproducer scripts for the extractor (GEO streaming) and the driver (running all SOP stages) are committed alongside the cohort folders. An independent investigator can clone the repo, run the extractor, run the driver, and reproduce every headline number in this report to within numerical tolerance.

Earlier pre-build-era evidence (the historical calibration phase using external panels and atlases) is preserved separately at RETIRED_Evidence_Report.html and does not bear on production CPG claims.

Chain-integrity test — L9 N7 end-to-end (2026-06-05)

N7 is the chain-level integrity check, run once per chain version. It is distinct from the per-VAL nulls (N1 HC permutation, N2 age-strata, N3 sex-strata, N4 cohort-split, N6 injection-recovery, N8 look-elsewhere), which are disease-specific and reported per VAL above. N7 instead generates synthetic patients with known truth, feeds the synthetic β matrices through the entire production chain — Walther IAM Deconvolver → IAMAtlas A-scoring → Mahalanobis healthy hull — and checks whether the chain recovers the truth at each link. Reference: SOP v1.2 §87 / SOP v1.2 §89 (Synthetic Patient Generator).

Why a chain-integrity test is necessary. Every per-VAL null tests whether a specific finding could have arisen by chance. A chain-integrity test answers a different question: does the chain, as a system of cascaded measurements, recover known truth when given a synthetic input whose true cell-type composition and disease signature have been declared in advance? Without N7, a per-VAL null result is only as trustworthy as the unverified assumption that the chain itself is producing correct readings. With N7, the chain itself is calibrated. This is the same discipline Planck applies via FFP10 simulated skies — generate a known truth, pass it through the pipeline, verify recovery.

Test scope (first run, 2026-06-05). Two recovery criteria implemented end-to-end on three synthetic cohorts (n_case=50, n_hc=200 per cohort; 22,542-CpG atlas subset for speed):

  • R1 — Walther class-fraction recovery. PASS criterion: mean(|fraction_recovered − fraction_true|) ≤ 0.10 across the 8 architectural classes.
  • R3 — Mahalanobis case-vs-HC separation under known signal injection. Two reference modes tested: (a) the production Mahalanobis healthy hull (calibrated on n=601 real HC), and (b) a within-cohort hull built from the synthetic HC arm.

Findings.

  • Chain modules wire end-to-end. 750 synthetic patients (250 × 3 conditions) flowed through Walther → A-scoring → Mahalanobis without error. The chain produces all expected outputs in the expected formats at every stage.
  • Walther class-fraction recovery validated to MAE < 1%. All three conditions return overall MAE in the 0.0076–0.0093 range — an order of magnitude under the 0.10 PASS threshold. The deconvolver returns near-truth fractions; the dominant immune class is recovered at MAE = 0.006–0.012, and the rarer stem_pluri / stem_adult classes also within 0.004–0.014.
  • Signal recovery is substrate-specific by 3.5×. When the injected disease panel was placed on the cell-type marker substrate (the 6,802 CpGs that the chain actually scores), case-vs-HC Cohen's d reached +10.24 against the within-cohort centroid. When the same 500 CpGs were placed at uniformly random positions across the atlas, only 107/500 (21.4%) landed on the marker substrate and recovery dropped to +5.10. The chain measures where its markers are; diffuse signal away from the markers is appropriately attenuated by entropy averaging over the marker pool.
  • The production Mahalanobis healthy reference is not the appropriate oracle for synthetic recovery tests. Synthetic patients have systematically different β distributions than the n=601 real HC cohort that calibrated the reference (different cell-composition statistics, Gaussian σ=0.03 measurement noise vs real array noise, atlas-mean linear-mixture β). All synthetic patients sit far from the real-HC centroid, so the case-vs-HC subtraction against the production reference is dominated by reference-mismatch rather than injected signal. This motivates an N7 v0.2 design point: either generate synthetic patients whose composition statistics match the production HC cohort, or build a separate synthetic-baseline Mahalanobis reference for use in N7 only.
  • Within-cohort R3 with unequal arm sizes has a sampling-variance baseline of Cohen's d ≈ +3. The NULL condition (no signal injected) produced d = +3.07 against the within-cohort reference because n_case=50 vs n_hc=200 produces a 2× ratio in within-group SD (case sd=0.92, HC sd=0.45). The smaller group has wider spread around the centroid by sampling, not by signal. Signal above this floor is still cleanly recoverable (+2.03 off-substrate, +7.17 on-substrate above the null floor), but the absolute R3 criterion needs matched arm sizes or cross-validation to remove the baseline. This is an N7 v0.2 design point.

What N7 v0.1 establishes. The chain operates correctly: it recovers cell-type composition fractions to MAE < 1%, it measures injected signal that lands on its substrate, and it does not fabricate signal when none is present (the NULL false-positive d above zero is explained entirely by unequal arm sizes, not by the chain). The chain is calibrated for the kind of signal it is built to detect.

Critical calibration finding — Route A threshold

The v0_1 reference used a fixed d ≥ 2.0 Route A threshold which is mathematically inappropriate for 112-dimensional data. Under multivariate normality with 112 features, expected median Mahalanobis distance is √112 ≈ 10.58 — so d ≥ 2.0 fires on every sample regardless of clinical status. The threshold is corrected in v0_2/v0_3 to a percentile-of-pooled-HC value, derived per-version from the actual HC distance distribution under the new hull. Engine reads thresholds from the artifact's route_A_calibration_v0_N block at session startup. Cards never carry threshold values.

Cards-don't-carry-runtime-data discipline (formalized 2026-06-06)

Per-card runtime data (hull centroid, covariance, percentile thresholds, per-cohort distribution metadata, validation lineage) lives in the runtime artifact at Mahalanobis_healthy_reference/*.json — NOT in disease cards. Each disease card references the artifact path; engine consumes the artifact directly. This separation ensures hull expansion does not require touching any card. The same discipline applies to all runtime references (cellular age reference matrix, celltype markers artifact, tier breakpoints, directional panels) — cards reference paths, runtime artifacts hold data. Discipline formalized in BUILD_SPEC v1.2 §Stage 5.1 + SOP v1.3 §48 + changelog patch.

The direct answer to "why did we spend three weeks on the new physics chain": Because comparison-studies methodology cannot tell you when it is wrong. A regression-fit clock produces r = 0.9999 on the cohort it was fit to and contains zero information about transferability. The physics chain on Hannum tells us exactly what it knows (A_immune declines with age, p < 10⁻⁶, real biology) AND what it doesn't (cellular age numeric readout is out of calibration for this population — saturation rate 93.1%). The astrophysics adaptations produce per-patient artifacts with quantifiable calibration boundaries instead of cohort averages with hidden assumptions. The hull versioning protocol is the methylome analog of Planck's data-release versioning (2013 → 2015 → 2018) — each release froze a calibration set for cosmological analysis; new releases expanded the data; prior releases retained for lineage.
