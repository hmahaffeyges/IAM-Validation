\chapter{From the virial theorem to the coupling}\label{ch:virial}

\section{The virial partition}

For any system bound by a potential homogeneous of degree $-1$, gravitational or Coulomb, Euler's theorem on homogeneous functions gives the
time-averaged balance
\begin{equation}
 2\langle K\rangle+\langle V\rangle=0,\qquad \langle K\rangle=\tfrac12|\langle V\rangle| .
 \label{eq:virial}
\end{equation}
The factor $\tfrac12$ is not a fit. It is fixed by the degree of the potential, and the potential's degree is fixed by Gauss's law in three
spatial dimensions. There is no free parameter.

\section{What $\langle K\rangle$ is}

Take a system of mass $M$ falling from rest at infinity ($E_i=0$) into a bound equilibrium with $E_f=\langle K\rangle+\langle V\rangle<0$.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item First law: the transition is irreversible and releases heat $Q=E_i-E_f=-(\langle K\rangle+\langle V\rangle)>0$.
 \item Second law: it produces entropy $\Delta S=Q/T$ at the bound system's temperature $T$.
 \item Landauer: the minimum cost of producing $\Delta S$ irreversibly is $E_{\mathrm L}=T\Delta S=Q$. The temperature cancels.
 \item The $1/r$ equilibrium, Eq.~\eqref{eq:virial}, gives $-E_f=\langle K\rangle$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\;\langle K\rangle=Q=T\Delta S=E_{\mathrm{L}}=\tfrac12|\langle V\rangle|\;}
 \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
\calc\ Each step is standard physics. Eq.~\eqref{eq:identity} does not derive the virial theorem from thermodynamics; it states what the kinetic half is:
the Landauer cost of the binding transition, written at $k_BT\ln2$ per bit. Because Landauer's principle holds at every scale and temperature,
the same identity holds for every $1/r$ bound system. This is the law the book calls IAM's law.

\section{The cosmological coupling}

Applied to the matter in the universe, the kinetic half of the gravitational binding is the part that writes records. The fraction of the critical
density that does so is
\begin{equation}
 \beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.1575\quad(\Omega_m=0.315).
 \label{eq:betam}
\end{equation}
\prediction\ $\beta_m$ is not fitted. It enters every chain fixed at this value.

\section{When the writing switches on}

The records accumulate on the cosmological horizon. The activation function, from horizon thermodynamics (Jacobson; Cai and Kim), is
\begin{equation}
 E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)=e^{-z}.
 \label{eq:Ea}
\end{equation}
\calc\ $E\to0$ as $a\to0$ ($E(z=10)=4.5\times10^{-5}$); $E(1)=1$; $dE/da=E/a^2>0$ always; $E\to e$ as $a\to\infty$, never reached.
Its rate of change $dE/d\ln a=E/a$ peaks exactly at $a=1$; $E$ itself has its inflection at $a=1/2$. The activation reaches $10\,\%$, $50\,\%$ and $90\,\%$
of today's value at $z=2.30$, $0.69$ and $0.11$.

\section{The matter coupling and its two consequences}

The writing acts as a friction on matter only, through
\begin{equation}
 \mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(a)+H_0^2\,\beta_m E(a)},\qquad \Sigma(a)=1 .
 \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
Photons on null worldlines accumulate no proper time and write nothing, so the lensing combination $\Sigma$ is unchanged.
\calc\ $\mu(1)=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.864$; $\mu(z=0.5)=0.948$; $\mu\to1$ above $z\approx2$.

\begin{checkbox}
Two predictions follow with no further input:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Growth: the matter coupling is weaker today by $1-\mu_0=13.6\,\%$; in the Planck chains $\sigma_8$ falls from $0.813$ to $0.800$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:chains}).
 \item Expansion seen by matter: $H_0^{\mathrm{matter}}=H_0\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=67.16\times\sqrt{1.1575}=72.26$ km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, against the
       photon-sector $67.16$. Distance-ladder probes, on timelike worldlines, read the first; the CMB reads the second.
\end{itemize}
\end{checkbox}
