\chapter{The cosmological constant}\label{ch:lambda}

\section{The problem}
Quantum field theory with a Planck-scale cutoff gives a vacuum energy density $\rho_{\rm vac}\sim E_P^4/(\hbar c)^3=4.63\times10^{113}$\,J\,m$^{-3}$,
with $E_P=\sqrt{\hbar c^5/G}$. The measured dark-energy density is $\rho_\Lambda=\Omega_\Lambda\rho_c c^2=5.25\times10^{-10}$\,J\,m$^{-3}$
(Planck 2018, $H_0=67.4$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6846$). The ratio is
\begin{equation}\frac{\rho_\Lambda}{\rho_{\rm vac}}=1.133\times10^{-123}.\end{equation}
Supersymmetric cancellation, the string landscape, quintessence and modified gravity have been proposed; none explains the value. The paper's
starting point is that $\rho_{\rm vac}$ and $\rho_\Lambda$ may be different physical quantities: the first a property of the vacuum, the second a
property of the universe's history.

\paragraph{The frame.} IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}): every irreversible transition from quantum superposition to classical record pays $k_BT\ln2$ per bit
to the nearest encoding surface, at that surface's temperature, and the record is written on that two-dimensional surface. The law is the same at
every scale at which records are made --- the methylation record of a cell, a switching semiconductor, a measured qubit, the horizon of a black hole
($T_HS=\tfrac12Mc^2$, Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}) --- across some 37 orders of magnitude. At the cosmic horizon the decoherence is gravitational collapse of matter on timelike worldlines
and the surface is the horizon at $T_{GH}=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$. Nothing here modifies gravity. The Einstein equations are untouched; what is completed is the horizon entropy functional,
the place Jacobson showed the field equations come from. Jacobson's entropy $S=A/4\ell_P^2$ is purely geometric, and Einstein's $\Lambda$ was a
constant he could not identify. Both lack the same piece: $S_{\rm info}$, the classical information produced by matter decohering on timelike
worldlines, encoded on the horizon. The missing information was always information. Before structure forms $E(a)\to0$: no structure, no decoherence, no cost,
and $\Lambda$ is the vacuum baseline, so the early universe is general relativity with $\Lambda$, which is $\Lambda$CDM exactly. When matter
collapses into halos, galaxies and stars, decoherence accelerates, the horizon must accommodate the new bits, and the expansion felt by matter
speeds up; the structural contribution $S_{\rm info}$, carried by $\beta_mE(a)$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}), accumulates. The cosmological
constant is therefore the accumulated Landauer cost of baryonic decoherence: the baseline before structure, growing with it. Recombination, the acoustic peaks and nucleosynthesis are unchanged by construction.

\section{The horizon identity}
Writing the critical density in Planck units gives, exactly,
\begin{equation}\frac{\rho_\Lambda}{\rho_{\rm vac}}=\frac{3\Omega_\Lambda}{8\pi}\left(\frac{l_P}{l_H}\right)^2,\qquad l_H=c/H_0,\label{eq:ident}\end{equation}
which reproduces $1.133\times10^{-123}$ to all digits. The same statement in horizon thermodynamics: the Gibbons--Hawking temperature
$T_{GH}=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$ times the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy $S=k_BA/4l_P^2$ of the Hubble horizon equals the mass-energy inside it,
\begin{equation}T_{GH}\,S=M_Hc^2\qquad\text{(any }H),\end{equation}
the cosmological counterpart of the Smarr relation of Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}. So $\rho_\Lambda$ is $\Omega_\Lambda$ times the horizon's entropy
priced at its own temperature, per unit Hubble volume. The 123 orders of magnitude are this identity: any expression containing the measured
$H_0$ and the Planck length reproduces them. They are not evidence for a mechanism. What a mechanism must explain is the O(1) factor
$3\Omega_\Lambda/8\pi$.

\section{The cosmological-constant expression}
In the paper, $\rho_{\rm vac}$ is the energy of the complete Planck-scale substrate on the horizon and $\rho_\Lambda$ is the Landauer cost of the
classical information written on it by gravitational decoherence on timelike worldlines. Dark matter, in the virial reading of
Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}, does not decohere further, so only baryons write; the paper takes the writing fraction as $\Omega_b/\Omega_m$ and
a geometric factor $2/\pi$:
\begin{equation}\frac{\rho_\Lambda}{\rho_{\rm vac}}\Big|_{\rm IAM}=\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\frac{l_P}{l_H}\right)^2\frac{\Omega_b}{\Omega_m}
=1.380\times10^{-123}\quad(\times1.218),\label{eq:base}\end{equation}
and with the de Sitter temperature ratio $T_{dS}/T_H=H_\Lambda/H_0=\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}=0.8274$,
\begin{equation}\frac{\rho_\Lambda}{\rho_{\rm vac}}\Big|_{\rm corrected}=\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\frac{l_P}{l_H}\right)^2\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\,
\frac{\Omega_b}{\Omega_m}=1.142\times10^{-123},\label{eq:corr}\end{equation}
$0.79\,\%$ above the measured value. The exponent of $\Omega_\Lambda$ that would close Eq.~\eqref{eq:base} exactly is $0.521$.

\section{What the expression asserts}
Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} by the identity \eqref{eq:ident}, the $l_P$, $l_H$ and $\pi$ cancel:
\begin{equation}\boxed{\;\frac{\Omega_b}{\Omega_m}=\frac{3}{16}\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\;}\label{eq:rel}\end{equation}
Planck 2018: $0.1564$ against $0.1551$. On the CMB-only 18th chain (Chapter~\ref{ch:baryon}) the ratio of the two sides is
$1.0046\pm0.0068$ ($0.7\sigma$). On the $\Lambda$CDM chains it is $1.0056$--$1.0106$. That is expected: the informational term is off at recombination, so the
CMB returns the same $\Omega_b$, $\Omega_m$ and $\Omega_\Lambda$ with or without it. The relation is a property of the measured universe;
$\Lambda$CDM takes those values as given, and the informational term is what would have to explain them. It holds at the present epoch only, since $\Omega_\Lambda(a)$ changes and $\Omega_b/\Omega_m$ does not.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rel} ties the dark-energy fraction to the baryonic fraction of matter. If $3/16$ follows from physics with nothing adjusted, it
would explain the O(1) factor that the identity leaves open. Among O(1) combinations of $\Omega_b/\Omega_m$, $\Omega_\Lambda$, $\pi$ and small
integers, 2 of 540 fall within $1\,\%$ of the measured value, so the derivation, not the agreement, carries the weight.

\section{The factors}
\paragraph{Ultraviolet cutoff.} The paper takes the Planck cutoff because $l_P^2$ is the area per horizon bit in the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy.
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:rel} the cutoff cancels; it fixes only how the result is written.
\paragraph{$2/\pi$.} The paper argues that a de Sitter observer's static patch subtends $2\pi$ of the horizon's $4\pi$\,sr, so writing area is
$A_{\rm eff}=2\pi l_H^2$. The static patch is bounded by the whole horizon sphere and receives signals from all $4\pi$\,sr; and with
$A_{\rm eff}=2\pi l_H^2$, $l_P^2/(A_{\rm eff}/4\pi)=2(l_P/l_H)^2$, not $(2/\pi)(l_P/l_H)^2$. The $2/\pi$ is not derived.
\paragraph{$\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$.} $T_{dS}=T_H\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$ is correct for the horizon set by $\Lambda$ alone. It was applied to
close the 1.22 residual of Eq.~\eqref{eq:base}; a derivation must fix where it enters before the comparison.
\paragraph{$\Omega_b/\Omega_m$.} The physical argument is that only baryons decohere. The formula record shows the factor entered when the
residual stood at 12, and the $2/\pi$ when it stood at 1.9; both are given here as the paper's reasons, with that order.

\section{Accumulation over cosmic history}
The accumulation must follow from the history of baryonic decoherence. The paper's integral
\begin{equation}\frac{\Lambda(a)}{\rho_{\rm vac}}\propto\int_{a_{\rm EW}}^{a}\frac{\Omega_b(a')}{\Omega_{\rm tot}(a')}
\frac{l_H(a')^2}{l_P^2}\frac{da'}{a'^2H(a')/H_0}\end{equation}
grows as $a'^{-3}$ toward early times; from $a_{\rm EW}=2.3\times10^{-15}$ its coefficient is $3\times10^{30}$ against the required $0.523$.
Weighting by the activation $d\mathcal E(a)$ instead gives $0.47$ to $5.8$ depending on the form chosen; none is selected by a derivation.

IAM's Law prices each bit at $k_BT_{GH}\ln2$ on the horizon; the virial partition sets how much bound matter writes, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (the
kinetic half), activated by $E(a)$. The result is a horizon-entropy term in the first law, not heat radiated by baryons. For scale, the heat radiated by baryonic virialisation
summed to today (Sheth--Tormen halos above $10^8\,M_\odot$, $\sigma_8=0.811$; 58\,\% of baryons in halos, mean dispersion 213\,km\,s$^{-1}$) is
$3.2\times10^{-8}\,\rho_\Lambda c^2$, so radiated heat is not the measure of the cost; the history integral must be written in the horizon-entropy
variable that $\beta_mE(a)$ carries.

\section{The cosmological constant is not constant}
An accumulated $\Lambda$ grows with time. A dark-energy density that increases as the universe expands has $w<-1$, not $w>-1$ as the paper
states; this agrees with the structural term's $w_{\rm info}=-1-1/(3a)$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). DESI DR2 prefers $w_0>-1$ today with $w_a<0$;
the two are compared in the observations chapter.
