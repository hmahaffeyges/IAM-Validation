\chapter{IAM's Law: the thermodynamic cost of a classical record}\label{ch:iams_law}

\section{The law in plain language}
Matter in the universe is found in two conditions. Some of it holds definite classical states: a position, an energy level, an identity that any number
of observers can read and agree on. The rest is in quantum superposition, carrying amplitudes for outcomes that have not yet been recorded anywhere.
The passage from the second condition to the first is decoherence. It happens constantly: every atom that binds, every molecule that forms, every star
that contracts, every halo that settles into equilibrium leaves a record in its surroundings that was not there before.

The claim of this chapter is that this passage has a price. Every irreversible transition from quantum superposition to classical record costs at least
$k_BT\ln2$ per bit of record produced, and the temperature $T$ is that of the nearest encoding surface --- the horizon that bounds the region in which the
record is written. Once paid, the cost is not recovered. Classical records are bought one bit at a time.

Two consequences follow, at two scales. Locally, the kinetic half of the virial theorem --- the energy a bound system keeps as motion --- equals the heat it
released in forming, which is the minimum cost of the record its formation wrote (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}). Globally, the accumulated cost of every
record written since stable matter existed is a term missing from the entropy of the cosmic horizon. Jacobson (1995) obtained the Einstein equations from
the thermodynamics of local horizons, and Cai and Kim (2005) obtained the Friedmann equations from the thermodynamics of the cosmic horizon; both wrote
the horizon entropy with geometric terms only. Adding the record term gives the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$, a weaker effective coupling for
the growth of matter structure, $\mu(a)<1$, unchanged light propagation, $\Sigma=1$, and a coupling fixed by the virial half, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$.

\section{Statement of the law}
\begin{quote}\textbf{IAM's Law.} Every irreversible transition from quantum superposition to classical record pays $k_BT\ln2$ per bit to the nearest
encoding surface at the local temperature,
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{\rm rec}=k_BT_H\ln2\quad\text{per bit}. \label{eq:law}
\end{equation}
\end{quote}
Landauer's principle (1961) bounds the cost of an irreversible bit at the temperature of the reservoir that receives the heat; it leaves that temperature
to be supplied. IAM's Law names the reservoir: the nearest horizon, whose temperature is fixed by its geometry --- the Gibbons--Hawking temperature
$T_H=\hbar H/(2\pi k_B)$ at the cosmic horizon, the Hawking temperature $\hbar c^3/(8\pi GMk_B)$ at a black hole. In the laboratory the nearest bath is the
apparatus itself and the law reduces to Landauer's bound as measured. Identifying the horizon as the reservoir is the one physical assumption the law adds
(Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}, Assumption~2).

\section{The decoherence event}
A system $S$ coupled to an environment $E$ evolves from a product state into an entangled one (Zurek 2003),
\begin{equation}
|\psi_S\rangle\otimes|E_0\rangle\;\longrightarrow\;\sum_ic_i\,|s_i\rangle\otimes|E_i\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\{|s_i\rangle\}$ are the pointer states selected by the interaction. When the environmental states become orthogonal, $\langle E_i|E_j\rangle\to\delta_{ij}$,
the reduced density matrix of the system becomes diagonal,
\begin{equation}
\rho_S={\rm Tr}_E|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\;\longrightarrow\;\sum_i|c_i|^2|s_i\rangle\langle s_i|.
\end{equation}
Interference between the outcomes is gone, and the environment now holds a record of which pointer state was selected. For $N$ distinguishable pointer
states the record is $I=\log_2N$ bits. Undoing it would require gathering every environmental correlation and erasing it, which dissipates at least
$k_BT\ln2$ per bit (Landauer 1961; measured by B\'erut et al.\ 2012). The record is therefore irreversible at any finite temperature.

\section{Where the cost is paid}
For a concentrated collapse the nearest encoding surface is the horizon of the black hole that forms, at its Hawking temperature
(Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}). For structure distributed through the universe it is the cosmic horizon, at
\begin{equation}
\Delta E_{\rm rec}(a)=k_BT_H(a)\ln2=\frac{\hbar H(a)}{2\pi}\ln2 . \label{eq:epochcost}
\end{equation}
Bits are expensive when $H$ is large and cheap when it is small. The total paid over cosmic history depends on both the rate at which records are written and
the price per bit, and that combination is what $E(a)$ tracks.

\section{The local expression: the kinetic half}
Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law} sets out the virial theorem and its thermodynamic reading in full. In brief: a system bound by $1/r$ that forms from rest
releases heat $Q=-E_f$ (first law); Euler's theorem for a potential homogeneous of degree $-1$ gives $2\langle K\rangle+\langle V\rangle=0$, so
$Q=\langle K\rangle=\tfrac12|\langle V\rangle|$; and Landauer's principle makes $Q$ the minimum cost of the irreversible formation. Half of the binding energy
is kept as motion, and an equal amount leaves as the heat that pays for the record of the bound state. The equality $Q=\langle K\rangle$ is mechanics; the
law identifies what that heat buys. The same half appears on the horizon of a Schwarzschild black hole, $T_HS=\tfrac12Mc^2$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}).

\section{The global expression: completing the horizon entropy}
\subsection{Jacobson's construction}
At any point $p$ a small spacelike two-surface defines a local Rindler horizon with null generator $k^a$, approximate boost Killing vector
$\chi^a=-\kappa\lambda k^a$ and Unruh temperature $T=\hbar\kappa/(2\pi k_Bc)$. The heat crossing the horizon and the change of its area are
\begin{equation}
\delta Q=-\kappa\int_{\mathcal H}\lambda\,T_{ab}k^ak^b\,d\lambda\,dA,\qquad
\delta S=-\eta\int_{\mathcal H}\lambda\,R_{ab}k^ak^b\,d\lambda\,dA,
\end{equation}
the second from the Raychaudhuri equation with $S=\eta A$. Requiring $\delta Q=T\,\delta S$ for every null $k^a$ gives the Einstein equations with
$G=c^3/(4\hbar\eta)$. Written as
\begin{equation}
\frac{\hbar\eta}{2\pi}=\frac{c^3}{8\pi G},\qquad \eta=\frac{c^3}{4\hbar G}=\frac{1}{4\ell_P^2},
\end{equation}
the $1/4$ is the $2\pi$ period of the Euclidean horizon divided by the $8\pi$ of the field equations; given $G$, nothing else enters, and one unit of entropy, $k_B$,
corresponds to $4\ell_P^2$ of horizon ($4\ln2\,\ell_P^2$ per bit) (Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}). Jacobson's construction is a machine: choose the entropy, obtain the gravity.

\subsection{Cai and Kim, and the missing term}
Cai and Kim applied the first law $-dE=T_H\,dS$ at the apparent horizon of a Friedmann universe, $\tilde r_A=c/H$, with area $A_H=4\pi c^2/H^2$, entropy
$S_{\rm geo}=k_Bc^3A_H/(4G\hbar)$ and the Gibbons--Hawking temperature, and recovered the Friedmann equations. Their entropy, like Jacobson's, is geometric
only. IAM's Law adds the entropy of the records written in the bulk:
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm total}=S_{\rm geo}+S_{\rm info},\qquad -dE=T_H\,d\!\left(S_{\rm geo}+S_{\rm info}\right). \label{eq:firstlaw}
\end{equation}
The geometric part returns standard cosmology. The record part adds an effective density in the dynamics of matter,
\begin{equation}
H_m^2=H_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}^2+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2 , \label{eq:Hm}
\end{equation}
which acts only where records are written --- in collapsing, inhomogeneous matter. In an exactly homogeneous universe there is no structure, the Weyl tensor
vanishes, and the term vanishes with it. The background expansion and everything carried by light are unchanged; the term enters the growth of matter
perturbations (Section~\ref{sec:perturbations}).

\subsection{Two sources of records}
The vacuum supplies a constant baseline of irreversible records, independent of structure; through Jacobson's construction it enters as the cosmological
constant. Gravitational collapse, binding and decoherence of matter supply a part that grows with structure: zero before structure forms, growing as halos
and the cosmic web assemble, and levelling off as expansion slows collapse. The second part is $\beta_mE(a)$.

\subsection{The rate at which records reach the horizon}
The rate of irreversible record production from structure formation is taken as
\begin{equation}
\dot I\propto\rho_m(a)\,D(a)^n\,f(a)\,H(a), \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
with $D$ the linear growth factor and $f=d\ln D/d\ln a$. Each bit costs $k_BT_H\ln2$, and the horizon stores records per unit area, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{dS_{\rm info}}{dt}=\frac{\dot I}{T_HA_H}. \label{eq:Sdot}
\end{equation}
In matter domination ($H\propto a^{-3/2}$, $A_H\propto a^3$, $T_H\propto a^{-3/2}$, $D\propto a$, $f\approx1$),
\begin{equation}
\frac{dS_{\rm info}}{d\ln a}\propto\frac{\rho_mD^nf}{T_HA_H}\propto a^{\,n-9/2},\qquad S_{\rm info}\propto\frac{a^{\,n-9/2}}{n-9/2}.
\end{equation}
The form $S_{\rm info}\propto-1/a+{\rm const}$ that produces $\exp(1-1/a)$ requires $n-9/2=-1$, so
\begin{equation}
n=\tfrac72 .
\end{equation}
Integrating Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rate}--\eqref{eq:Sdot} with the full $\Lambda$CDM growth factor gives $dS/d\ln a\propto a^{-1.02}$ for $n=7/2$ in the matter era;
the $\Lambda$ era raises the effective exponent (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}).

\subsection{The activation function}
The record density obeys
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho_{\rm info}=\rho_{\rm info}\frac{H}{a}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{d\ln\rho_{\rm info}}{da}=\frac{1}{a^2},
\end{equation}
which integrates, normalised to today, to
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(\int_1^a\frac{da'}{a'^2}\right)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)=e^{-z}. \label{eq:Ea}
\end{equation}
Its properties are the properties of an irreversible record: $E\to0$ as $a\to0$; $E(1)=1$; $dE/da>0$ always, because no payment is returned;
$E\to e\approx2.718$ as $a\to\infty$, so the accumulated term approaches a finite value. The $1/a$ in the exponent is the record surface density on the
horizon: what enters is not how many records the universe has written but how densely they cover the horizon.

\subsection{Variational form}
Define $\phi\equiv\ln E=1-1/a$, which obeys the constraint $\dot\phi=H/a$. It does not propagate; it follows the record history. The action
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm info}=\int\left[-\frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G}\beta_me^{\phi}+\lambda\left(\dot\phi-\frac{H}{a}\right)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x,
\end{equation}
with Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$, returns Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hm} on variation with respect to $g_{ab}$. The continuity equation gives the equation of state
\begin{equation}
w_{\rm info}(a)=-1-\frac{1}{3a}:\qquad -1.67\ (a=0.5),\quad -1.33\ (a=1),\quad -1.17\ (a=2),
\end{equation}
below $-1$ at all finite $a$ and tending to $-1$. Because $\phi$ is a constrained thermodynamic variable rather than a propagating field, this is a
parametrisation of the entropy completion, not a dark-energy fluid; energy--momentum is conserved, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm total}=0$, and the
construction is consistent with the contracted Bianchi identity.

\subsection{The coupling from the virial half}
In virial equilibrium half the binding energy of a self-gravitating system stays in the gravitational field and half leaves as the heat that pays for its
record. The record density today is therefore half the matter density:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\rm info}(a{=}1)=\beta_m\rho_{\rm crit}=\tfrac12\rho_m\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.1575\quad(\Omega_m=0.315).
\end{equation}
The coupling is a prediction. It enters every chain at this value and is never sampled. Applying a population-level equilibrium to non-equilibrium
structure formation is Assumption~3.

\subsection{Matter and light}
\textbf{Which degrees of freedom write records.} Decoherence is a process in proper time and needs $\Delta\tau>0$. Matter moves on timelike worldlines and
writes records; light moves on null geodesics, $ds^2=0$, $\Delta\tau=0$, and writes none. Hence, for matter,
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2}<1, \label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
and for light $\Sigma(a)=1$. Today $\mu=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.864$, so $\mu-1=-0.136$; $\mu(z{=}0.5)=0.948$, $\mu(z{=}1)=0.982$, and $\mu\to1$ at early times.

\section{Perturbations: why growth is weaker and light is not}\label{sec:perturbations}
The field $\phi=1-1/a$ is defined by the background apparent horizon $c/H(t)$. Local overdensities source the metric potentials but do not move the
background horizon at first order, so
\begin{equation}
\delta\phi=0
\end{equation}
in linear theory --- as for the cosmological constant, $\delta\Lambda=0$. Perturbing the constrained action, variation with respect to $\lambda$ gives
$\delta\dot\phi=0$, and with $\delta\phi(a_i)=0$ the field stays unperturbed. In conformal Newtonian gauge the perturbation equations are those of general
relativity,
\begin{equation}
k^2\Psi=-4\pi Ga^2\rho_m\delta_m,\qquad \Psi=\Phi,\qquad \ddot\delta_m+2H_m\dot\delta_m-4\pi G\rho_m\delta_m=0,
\end{equation}
with the single change that matter perturbations feel the matter-sector rate $H_m$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hm} as friction. Growth is suppressed; the potentials
obey the unmodified Poisson equation with no anisotropic stress, so light, which responds to $(\Psi+\Phi)/2$, is unaffected: $\Sigma=1$. CMB spectra, CMB
lensing, BAO angles and supernova distances are those of $\Lambda$CDM; the CMB TT spectra differ by less than $0.13\,\%$ at $\ell>30$.

\textbf{Where this sits among modified-growth models.} Most extensions of gravity strengthen growth: $f(R)$ gives $\mu>1$ with $\Sigma=1$, the normal branch
of DGP gives $\mu>1$ with $\Sigma=1$, and Horndeski theories move $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ together. The self-accelerating branch of DGP gives $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$,
but it carries a ghost and is excluded by data. Weaker growth with unchanged lensing, from friction on matter alone and no new propagating field, is
the signature of the informational term.

\textbf{Numerical record.} Level~1 used MGCAMB with Cobaya in the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ form: twelve chains, Planck alone and with redshift-space distortions,
BAO or Pantheon+. Level~2 modified CAMB itself, putting $H_m$ into the matter equations (three chains). Two Level~2b runs applied the term to the
background expansion and drove $H_0$ to $61.5$, which BAO and the distance ladder exclude: the term belongs in the perturbations. Every chain ends at
$R-1\le0.010$ (Part~2).

\section{Assumptions and how the law can fail}\label{sec:assumptions}
\textbf{Assumption 1 --- gravity is thermodynamic.} The Einstein equations are an equation of state obtainable from $\delta Q=T\,dS$ on causal horizons
with entropy proportional to area. Supported by the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy, the Unruh effect and the Cai--Kim derivation; not universally accepted.

\textbf{Assumption 2 --- records contribute to horizon entropy.} The cost of every irreversible transition to a classical record in the bulk is held on the
nearest encoding surface and enters its entropy. This is the one identification: the record was always there, and counting it reveals what the established results share. It is consistent with the holographic principle and
Landauer's principle and has not been derived from a complete quantum theory of gravity --- the same gap every thermodynamic approach to gravity has.

\textbf{Assumption 3 --- the virial coupling is exact at the population level.} The virial theorem holds strictly for systems in stable equilibrium;
structure formation is not. $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ applies the half to the ensemble of bound structures. The chains show the consequence is consistent with
Planck; they do not show the coupling is exact.

\textbf{Derived, given these.} $E(a)$ from the record constraint; $\mu<1$ and $\Sigma=1$ from timelike versus null propagation and $\delta\phi=0$; $n=7/2$ from
the horizon accounting; $\mu-1=-0.136$ today.

\textbf{Three ways to fail, separately.} (1) The core law fails if records do not enter horizon entropy as identified --- shown by a complete theory that
excludes the channel, or by an internal inconsistency. (2) The cosmological implementation fails if growth data disagree with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ ---
Euclid and DESI will tell --- without necessarily failing the core law. (3) The broader extensions fail without touching (1) or (2).

\section{The derivation in three steps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Jacobson: $\delta Q=T\,dS$ on local Rindler horizons with $S=\eta A$ gives $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi GT_{ab}/c^4$.
\item Cai--Kim: $-dE=T\,dS_{\rm geo}$ on the apparent horizon gives $H^2=(8\pi G/3)\rho+\Lambda/3$.
\item IAM: $-dE=T\,d(S_{\rm geo}+S_{\rm info})$ gives, for matter, $H_m^2=H^2+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2$.
\end{enumerate}
Step 3 is the only new input: gravitational decoherence writes classical records irreversibly, their cost is held on the cosmic horizon, and the term
grows monotonically. Everything else is established physics.

\section{The Mahaffey number}
Dividing the drive that powers irreversible records in a system by the thermal quantum of its surroundings gives the dimensionless number that sets its
operating regime,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M=\frac{E_{\rm drive}}{k_BT},
\end{equation}
in the tradition of Reynolds, Mach and Prandtl. Expressed in Landauer units it is $\mathcal M/\ln2$. A living cell drives its records with ATP hydrolysis at
fixed body temperature: $\mathcal M=20.94$ ($30.2$ in Landauer units) --- far above the thermal floor, which is how biology keeps high-fidelity records at
$310$\,K. For a black hole the drive is its rest energy and the temperature its own: $Mc^2/(k_BT_{\rm BH})=8\pi GM^2/(\hbar c)=2S_{\rm BH}/k_B$, the Smarr
relation again. With the architecture of the system, $\mathcal M$ sets the IAM floor (Chapter~\ref{ch:floor}).
