\chapter{Open problems, and how to prove the framework wrong}\label{ch:open}

\section{The central problem}

The floors are calibrated. A derivation of $\Hmin(\text{class})$ from the Landauer cost, the class's governing inversion and nothing fitted to cells would turn the cellular
rung of the framework from a calibrated instrument into a prediction. The companion volume shows how far first-principles derivation goes on horizons and in hardware; on the cell
it has not started. The most promising route the numbers of Part~I suggest is the balance of maintenance fidelity: a floor as the steady-state entropy of a locus under a writing
process of finite discrimination and a class-specific rate of copying and removal (Eq.~\ref{eq:steady}). Each class's inversion would then name which rate governs its floor.

\section{Open problems, in order of consequence}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Provenance of the 37 reference values.} Trace each to its paper, figure and region set, and recompute it from the primary data where they are deposited.
\item \textbf{Held-out transfer of identity loci.} 87.7~\% and 73.1~\% against a bar of 95~\%. More samples per cell on one platform separate selection noise from donor spread.
\item \textbf{The count of classes.} Run CLASS-COUNT-01 on cells held out of the fit.
\item \textbf{Breach and the cancer region on the gauge.} Place them from per-person data on this scale: tumour/normal pairs, the DNMT1-inhibitor series and blood cancers.
\item \textbf{A physical zero for each laboratory.} A nonlinear response model from the control probes, or a reference material of known methylation run through each laboratory.
\item \textbf{Minor cells.} Readings of cells at a few per cent on bulk specimens, and the pre-registered minor-cell procedure.
\item \textbf{The sky's offset off the identity loci,} to be re-scored on atlas v2.
\item \textbf{Temperature scaling.} Measure $\alpha$ on sorted cells of another species before reading any ectotherm.
\item \textbf{The four fragmentomic substrates.} Identity loci and a reference on each.
\item \textbf{Trace detection thresholds.} The chain's trace-class detector sets its thresholds from a panel of healthy arrays. That is a population defining a threshold, and it has to be
      replaced by a threshold from constructed specimens and the array's own noise.
\end{enumerate}

\section{How to falsify the framework at cellular scale}

\begin{keybox}[title=Falsifiers]
Any one of the following, established by a pre-registered procedure on the present instrument, would show that the gauge does not measure what this book says it measures.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sorted cells of a class, from donors and sources not used in the calibration or the atlas, read systematically outside Normal on their own identity loci, with composition
      verified.
\item A ninth floor, fitted by CLASS-COUNT-01 with its own inversion, reads held-out cells closer to 1.00 than the eight do, beyond tolerance: the class structure is wrong.
\item In a leukaemia, the affected lineage reads no higher than the unaffected lineages in the same specimen, across a cohort of specimens.
\item Two technical replicates of the same DNA on the same chip differ by more than the tier half-width in a major cell.
\item The out-of-span map fails to separate a held-out cell spiked in at 5~\% from a $\pm50$~\% error in the known cells' fractions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{keybox}

\section{A closing note}

The instrument in this book was built quickly and corrected often. Most corrections came from the same source: a number that a population supplied, entering through the field's usual
tools, and removed once seen. What remains is a reading that depends on a cell, its floor and the array's own measurements of itself. That is a narrow thing to measure, and its
narrowness is its strength: a reading that no cohort shaped cannot be selectively right, so wherever it is wrong it will be seen to be wrong. The author's aim is that the work be
checked and replicated. Everything needed to do so is in the repository, and every number in this book can be recomputed from Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
