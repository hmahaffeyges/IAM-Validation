\chapter{The sky: what the atlas cannot explain}\label{ch:sky}

\section{The idea}

After component separation, a CMB map is what the foreground model cannot explain. The methylome's counterpart is the \emph{sky}: at every
address on the array, the specimen's methylation minus what its solved composition predicts,
\begin{equation}
  r_i=\beta_i-\sum_c f_c\,\mu_{ic},\qquad z_i=\frac{r_i}{\sigma_i},
  \label{eq:sky}
\end{equation}
drawn over the whole array. A specimen whose cells are all in the atlas and at their reference states reads a quiet sky: $z$ centred on zero
with unit spread. A cell the atlas does not contain, or a known cell far from its reference state, leaves structure. The sky is the chain's view
of everything the per-cell readings do not look at.

\section{The noise term is the whole question}

The sky is only as honest as $\sigma_i$. The first chain took $\sigma_i$ from the scatter of forty healthy arrays per laboratory. That is a
population defining the noise, and it was retired. What replaced it uses nothing but the atlas and the array:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i^2=\sum_c f_c^2\,s_{ic}^2+\sigma^2_{\mathrm{array}}(\beta_i),\qquad
  \sigma^2_{\mathrm{array}}(\beta)=a+b\,\beta(1-\beta),
  \label{eq:skysigma}
\end{equation}
where $s_{ic}$ is the atlas's posterior uncertainty for cell $c$ at locus $i$ and $a$, $b$ are fitted on this array's own 65 SNP probes, whose true
values are known to be 0, $\tfrac12$ or 1. \derived{} (the construction); the SNP-probe noise model is \calibrated{} per array.

\section{What the sky still shows}

The admitted skies are not centred: median $z$ is $-0.42$ to $-0.80$, and 7--24~\% of addresses have $|z|>2$. A diagnostic written after the bars
localised the cause. On the identity loci, where $A$ is read, the median residual is about zero. Off them, the array sits 0.03--0.04 below the
composition expectation. \measured{} The residual does not follow $\beta$ as a compression would (it is negative at both ends, not opposite in sign),
so the hypothesis that the array squeezes $\beta$ toward $\tfrac12$ is falsified. What fits is that the first chain's pipeline map was fitted on identity
loci and does not transfer to the rest of the array, or that the first atlas carried a source offset at those addresses, or both. Atlas v2 estimates a
source term at every locus and sits on the array's own scale, which addresses both routes; the sky on atlas v2 has not yet been scored. \openprob{}
The offset does not touch $A$.

\section{The cell-count floor}

At multipole $\ell$ the CMB offers $2\ell+1$ independent modes, and the fractional uncertainty on the power there can never fall below
$\sqrt{2/(2\ell+1)}$ however good the telescope. A specimen has the same kind of limit. At a locus with true methylation $\beta$ in a specimen of $N$
genome copies, the measured fraction has binomial standard deviation
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{copies}}=\sqrt{\frac{\beta(1-\beta)}{2N}}
  \label{eq:cellcount}
\end{equation}
for diploid DNA. \derived{} Figure~\ref{fig:cellcount} sets it against array noise. For a whole-blood draw with millions of cells the copy-number floor is
negligible. For plasma cell-free DNA, which carries of order $10^4$ genome equivalents per millilitre-scale draw, it is $0.003$ at $\beta=0.7$, below array
noise at a single locus but not negligible for a cell present at one per cent, whose contribution comes from about a hundred genomes. \calc

\section{A cleaner sky: the out-of-span map}

The sky of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sky} still mixes two things: error in the solved fractions of known cells, and signal from cells the atlas does not hold. They can
be separated exactly. Any mixture of the atlas's cells, at any fractions, lies in the span of the cells' profiles. Projecting the residual onto that span and
onto its complement,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf r=\underbrace{P_{\mathrm{span}}\mathbf r}_{\text{composition error}}+\underbrace{(I-P_{\mathrm{span}})\mathbf r}_{\text{what no known mixture can make}},
  \label{eq:outspan}
\end{equation}
leaves an out-of-span map that a composition error cannot move at all. \derived{} It is the direct counterpart of template cleaning on the sky, and the
answer to the question of how to quieten the noise from cells whose fractions are uncertain. It is pre-registered (PROC-OUTSPAN-01) with four tests written
before any specimen is projected: a constructed specimen of known cells must read quiet; the map must stay quiet when the known cells' fractions are thrown off
by $\pm50$~\%; cells held out of the atlas, spiked in at 1--10~\%, must be detected, 12 of 16 at 5~\%; and between two draws of one person a change of
composition must read quiet while a newly added unknown cell appears. \prediction
