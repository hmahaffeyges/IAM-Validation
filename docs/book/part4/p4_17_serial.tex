\chapter{Serial mode: one person, many draws}\label{ch:serial}

\section{The strongest measurement is a difference}

The instrument's value lies in the sequence of tests for one person: a trajectory from draw to draw that shows change or its absence,
and the cleanest sky map that one person's draws can give. The reason is in the measurement. Everything that is constant in a person between two draws, their genotype, their laboratory's pipeline if it is
held fixed, the reference's errors at their composition, cancels in a difference. In CMB language, two draws of one person are two maps of one sky,
and their difference is the split-map null test turned into a signal.

\section{What serial mode will compute}

Serial mode is not yet part of chain v3. \openprob{} Its design is pre-registered, and a module of
pure functions exists that is not wired into the run. Given a draw and an earlier draw of the same person (same
identifier hash, same array type, same pipeline; otherwise the pair is refused), it computes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\Delta A$} for the cell read in both draws, with its fraction in each;
\item \textbf{the difference sky}, $\Delta\beta_i=\beta_i^{\mathrm{now}}-\beta_i^{\mathrm{prior}}$ at every address. No expectation, no $\sigma$ from any
      group of people and no composition enter: two measurements of one person and their difference;
\item \textbf{a trajectory} when there are three or more draws: $A$ by date, the sign of the last step, and whether any step exceeded the change floor.
      No fit is made to a trajectory and no group of people defines its expected shape.
\end{enumerate}
Composition drops out of a difference only in part: if a person's fractions change between draws, the difference sky carries that change. The
out-of-span projection of Chapter~\ref{ch:sky} would remove it exactly, which is why one of its tests is written on serial pairs.

\section{The change floor}

A difference means nothing without the size of difference that noise alone produces. That is set by technical replicates: the same DNA measured twice,
read through the same chain. For neutrophil Met-A it is measured from same-chip technical replicates through the commissioned chain. \openprob
The floor that matters for a laboratory running serial draws is the tared one, on one platform, with references on the same slide. A set of same-chip
replicates through the chain would measure it.

\section{Which serial data the chain can read}

The longest public serial set, individuals drawn up to five times over two decades (E-MTAB-7309)~\cite{Wang2018SATSA}, has not been read through
chain v3. Its input quality is the first obstacle: of 1,056 arrays calibrated by Stage~1, 738 fall below the intake line, median call rate 0.894,
and the call rate falls with the draw's age decade. \measured{} A trajectory built from such draws must carry the intake status of
every draw, and a quarantined draw is not a point on it. \openprob

\section{Why serial mode also makes the reference better}

A person measured repeatedly is their own reference at every address. With enough draws, the per-address spread of one person between draws is a noise map made
with no group of people at all, and differences from it are that person's changes. The same draws, pooled over many people, would say which addresses are stable in
every person and which move: information the purified-cell reference does not carry. Neither use requires comparing one person with another.
