\chapter{Identity loci: where a cell is read}\label{ch:identity}

\section{What an identity locus is}

A cell is read on loci where its own methylation sits at its floor point. If a cell of class $k$ has mean methylation near $\bstar(k)$ at a
set of loci, then $H(\bbar)\approx\Hmin(k)$ there and the cell reads $A\approx1$ in its reference state. Departures from that state then move
$A$ in the direction and by the amount of Chapter~\ref{ch:gauge}. Such loci are the cell's \emph{identity loci}. They are not markers: a marker
separates a cell from others and usually sits near 0 or 1; an identity locus sits where the cell's entropy equals its floor, and is chosen per
cell, never per class.

\section{The rule}

For each atlas cell, from the atlas v2 posterior and nothing from the first atlas (author's ruling: v2 is not built to depend on v1):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the value is measured: $n_{\mathrm{obs}}\ge2$ (one, flagged, for the four pooled brain cells), and converged, $\hat R<1.01$;
\item the posterior mean lies within $\bstar(k)\pm0.05$ on the upper branch;
\item at least 100 loci qualify.
\end{enumerate}
Where a cell has array samples, the array samples choose the loci. Where it has only sequencing samples, their values are first put on the array
scale by a per-locus regression learned on the cells that both platforms measured (Chapter~\ref{ch:atlas}), and the cell carries the flag.

\section{How the rule was reached}

The rule was reached through two failures, both recorded before anything was changed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The first rule required the whole 95~\% posterior interval of a locus to lie inside the window. Only 30 of 74 cells could build 100 loci: cells
      measured on two to five sequencing samples have intervals of 0.105--0.174, wider than the 0.10 window itself. \measured{} The rule could not be met
      by the cells it was meant to protect. A replacement using the posterior mean, with the atlas's uncertainty carried onto each reading instead, was
      written before rebuilding. All 74 cells then built sets.
\item The replacement was tested the way a new sample would test it: choose each cell's loci from half its samples, read the other half. Only 71~\% of
      310 held-out readings fell in Normal (bar 95~\%), and six cells' medians fell below Normal. \measured{} A diagnostic then showed that the scatter
      tracked the \emph{source} of the samples, not how many samples chose the loci, and that choosing loci stable across donors did not help.
\end{enumerate}
The array-first rule with a per-locus sequencing correction answers the second failure. On the same held-out construction, array samples reading
array-chosen loci fell in Normal 87.7~\% of the time, with 28 of 29 cell medians in Normal; sequencing samples corrected to the array scale fell in Normal
73.1~\% of the time, with 14 of 17 medians in Normal. \measured

\begin{keybox}[title=Status]
Every one of the 74 cells has a production set of identity loci (Figure~\ref{fig:idloci}): 434 to 58,938 loci, median 11,050. Twenty-nine were chosen on
array samples, 44 on sequencing samples corrected to the array scale, and one (cortical neurons) on one array sample with corrected sequencing. Each cell's
atlas mean reads 0.987--0.999 on its own set, which is circular and printed only for comparison. \measured

The held-out transfer, the test that matters, does not yet meet 95~\%: 87.7~\% for array-chosen cells and 73.1~\% for sequencing-only cells. Readings on
sequencing-only cells carry that flag. \openprob
\end{keybox}

\section{Why held-out readings are the only test}

The floors were fitted so that 37 reference cells read 1.00, and identity loci are chosen so that a cell's own atlas mean reads 1.00. Neither is evidence
by itself. The evidence is a sample of a cell that played no part in choosing its loci, read on them, landing at 1.00 within tolerance. That is how every
identity set is tested, and it is the number the book reports.

\section{Two explanations for what remains}

A held-out sample reading off 1.00 has two explanations. One is selection noise: when a cell with few samples chooses loci, some enter the window by that
sample's noise, and because methylation is skewed high, more enter from above than below; a new sample then sits above $\bstar$ and reads below 1. All six
failures in the second test were below Normal, which fits. The other is real donor-to-donor spread of a sorted cell at its floor loci, larger than
$\pm5$~\%. The source-dependence found by the diagnostic says that, for sequencing-only cells, much of the spread is the platform transfer. More samples per
cell on one platform would separate the two.
