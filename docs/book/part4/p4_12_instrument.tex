\chapter{The instrument layer: intake, calibration, tare}\label{ch:instrument}

\section{From intensities to $\beta$}

A bisulfite array does not report $\beta$. It reports two fluorescence intensities per probe, methylated $M$ and unmethylated
$U$, in two colour channels, stored in IDAT files. $\beta=M/(M+U+\alpha)$ with a small offset $\alpha$ is computed only after
background correction, dye-bias correction and probe-type handling~\cite{Triche2013,Fortin2017}. This is the methylome's
map-making step, and like map-making it is where most of the instrument's errors enter. The chain does it itself, from the IDATs,
and records every number the array reports about its own quality (Stage~1).

\section{Intake: the array's own numbers}

Before any cell is read, Stage~0 asks whether the array is a measurement at all. The quantities are the array's own: the fraction of
probes distinguishable from background (the detection call rate) and the control probes' intensities. None refers to a person.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}\toprule
call rate & decision \\\midrule
$\ge0.98$ & proceed \\
$0.93$--$0.98$ & proceed with penalty, flagged \\
$<0.93$ & quarantine: no report is written \\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The 0.93 line was set by the author from a 48-array measurement across four laboratories: the Uppsala panel read a median call rate of
0.985 (minimum 0.979), Karolinska 0.975 (minimum 0.891), UCLA 0.953 (minimum 0.932), and Munich 0.878 (maximum 0.928). \measured{} The
line sits in the gap between Munich's best array and UCLA's worst.

\paragraph{The low-signal laboratory.} The Munich arrays had been read for months before this gate existed. Their non-polymorphic
control intensities were one-sixth of Uppsala's (green 1,291 against 8,582; red 2,268 against 14,427), and about one address in eight
could not be distinguished from background. \measured{} Three separate failures of earlier procedures traced back to that one laboratory's
input, including the construction of a ``laboratory zero''. The lesson was recorded as a rule: a check that is deferred never advances a
specimen. A deferred detection or call-rate check is now a hard quarantine.

\paragraph{A bias found on the way.} A probe at background reads both channels near noise, so its $\beta$ sits near 0.33--0.42 whatever
the true methylation. Identity loci sit near 0.74. Leaving such probes in pulls the mean $\beta$ down and $H(\bbar)$ up: about 0.002 in
$A$ for every per cent of a cell's identity loci at background. \measured{} Every reading before 27 September 2026 carried that upward bias.
Probes at background are now removed before any stage reads a $\beta$.

\section{The pipeline map}

A cell's floor point $\bstar$ is defined on the scale of the reference data. An array processed by the chain's Stage~1 reads a given
methylation state at a slightly different $\beta$ from the reference sources. The first atlas sat on the Roadmap sequencing scale, so the
first chain mapped every specimen onto that scale with a linear map, $\beta_{\mathrm{ref}}=(\beta-0.0662)/1.0127$ for the Stage~1 450K
pipeline, fitted on 32,688 identity loci.

\begin{keybox}[title=The last population in the first chain]
That map was fitted as the per-locus Stage~1 mean of 560 healthy whole-blood arrays (GSE87571) against the atlas's immune mean, and was
accepted when healthy immune $A$ read 0.99 after it; it transferred to 315 controls of a second laboratory at a median of 1.0097.
\record{} By the rule in the front matter, a map fitted on 560 people is a population defining a constant: in effect it set the healthy
median to the fixed point. It is the last such constant in the first chain.

The second atlas removes it by construction. Atlas v2 is built on the chain's own Stage~1 array scale (author's decision, 28 September
2026); sequencing sources enter it through source terms fitted on purified reference cells measured on both platforms
(Chapter~\ref{ch:atlas}). A specimen processed by Stage~1 is then already on the atlas scale, and no map from people is needed. \measured
\end{keybox}

\section{A tare from the array itself}

A physical instrument is zeroed against a known input. Every 450K array carries one: 65 probes that sit on common single-nucleotide
polymorphisms and read $\beta=0$, $\tfrac12$ or $1$ by genotype. PROC-TARE-01 asked whether those three known points could zero the array,
by fitting $\beta'=(\beta-T_{\mathrm{off}})/T_{\mathrm{scale}}$ per array so the three clusters land back on $0$, $\tfrac12$, $1$.

It failed every bar written for it. \measured{} The array does compress $\beta$ by about 6~\% at the extremes on every laboratory
($T_{\mathrm{scale}}$ 0.89--0.94), and that is real. But the linear tare moved $A$ by a near-constant $-0.044$ on 94~\% of arrays
regardless of where they sat, which is an offset and not a tare; $T_{\mathrm{scale}}$ varied more within a chip than between chips; and neither
parameter predicted $A$ ($r=-0.14$ and $-0.07$ on 732 arrays). Compression at $\beta=0$ and 1 says little about the response at $\beta\approx0.7$,
where identity loci sit.

\section{What remains open}

Figure~\ref{fig:tare} shows the instrument's own spread: four laboratories' healthy whole blood reads immune $A$ medians from 0.961 to 1.016,
all inside Normal but 0.055 apart. A laboratory zero computed from healthy panels would remove the spread, and was built, and is kept on record
unapplied, because it is a population defining a correction. The two routes that would zero the instrument physically are recorded and not yet
started: a nonlinear response model using the control probes as well as the SNP clusters, and a physical reference material of known methylation
run through each laboratory's own pipeline. \openprob
