\chapter{The gate error over its floor: $A$ for quantum processors}\label{ch:ascoreqc}

\section{From gate error to information cost}

A two-qubit gate with error probability $p$ preserves the intended state with probability
$1-p$. The information lost per gate, in nats, is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon=-\ln(1-p)=p+\tfrac12p^2+\dots
 \label{eq:Agate}
\end{equation}
For every platform of current interest $p<10^{-2}$, so $\varepsilon$ equals $p$ to better
than 0.5\%. The gate error in nats, $\varepsilon$, is therefore a re-expression of the published error, and the ranking
it produces is the ranking by two-qubit error. Its value lies in the \emph{decomposition} and in
the \emph{floor} it is compared with, not in the transformation itself.

randomized and cross-entropy benchmarking, and median and mean over the device, so rankings at

\section{Floors and walls}

For each architecture class the quantum-processor report assigns a \emph{material floor} $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{floor}}$, the error
below which the dominant physical mechanism of that class does not permit a gate to go, and
the reading on the one gauge is $A=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{floor}}$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:onegauge}). The floors quoted in the source are:
Yb$^+$ laser gates $3\times10^{-4}$; Ba$^+$ laser gates $1\times10^{-4}$; Ca$^+$ electronic
$3\times10^{-5}$; Al/AlO$_x$ transmon $\sim1\times10^{-3}$ at the present participation ratio;
Ta-based transmons $\sim3\times10^{-4}$; neutral-atom Rydberg $5\times10^{-3}$; photonic $\sim0.05$.
The source states that the floors come from oxide stoichiometry and defect physics, not from a
line fitted through hardware data. The derivations are not reproduced in the source papers,
so this edition labels the floors \calibrated.

\begin{remark}[IonQ, a floor in the data]
IonQ reported the same $p_{2Q}=0.40\%$ for Harmony (2020), Aria (2022) and Forte (2024). the quantum-processor report
reads this as the Yb$^+$ laser-motional class reaching its floor $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{floor}}=3\times10^{-4}$. A
factor of 13 above that floor, however, it is a plateau and not a floor. A stall at
$4\times10^{-3}$ for three generations is equally consistent with an engineering limit in
laser delivery. The data show that the error stopped improving; they do not show that it
stopped at a physical minimum.
\end{remark}

\section{The threshold picture}

Surface-code error correction needs physical error below about $10^{-2}$, and useful
fault-tolerant overhead needs about $10^{-3}$ or better~\cite{Fowler2012,GoogleWillow2025}.
the quantum-processor report places each platform on a gauge of floor, error-correction threshold and fault-tolerance
target. The book keeps the physical content, $A=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{floor}}$, and does not
reproduce the scoring scale.
