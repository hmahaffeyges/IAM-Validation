\chapter{The cost of holding a qubit}\label{ch:xqp}

\section*{The place}
IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}) prices every irreversible record at $k_BT\ln2$ per bit at the nearest encoding surface. A superconducting qubit
is a device built to \emph{avoid} records: it holds a superposition for as long as it can. This chapter asks what the law says that holding costs, and
where the cost should appear in a measurement.

\section{The anomaly}

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/part3/fig_xqp_thermal.pdf}
\caption{Equilibrium quasiparticle fraction in aluminium, $x_{\rm qp}=\sqrt{2\pi k_BT/\Delta}\,e^{-\Delta/k_BT}$ with $\Delta=182\,\mu$eV \calc: $1.5\times10^{-62}$ at 15\,mK ($\Delta/k_BT=141$), reaching $10^{-7}$ only near 135\,mK. Shaded: the background commonly measured in transmons, $10^{-8}$--$10^{-6}$, and up to $10^{-5}$ in less protected devices \observed; the best reported device reaches $\lesssim10^{-9}$ (text).}\label{fig:xqp_thermal}
\end{figure}
In Al/AlO$_x$/Al transmons the measured density of broken Cooper pairs (quasiparticles) is commonly
\begin{equation}x_{\rm qp}=n_{\rm qp}/n_{\rm cp}\sim10^{-8}\text{--}10^{-6},\qquad n_{\rm cp}=2\nu_0\Delta\approx4\times10^6\,\mu{\rm m}^{-3},\end{equation}
with $x_{\rm qp}\approx10^{-7}$ in many devices~\cite{Serniak2018}, that is $n_{\rm qp}\approx0.4$ per $\mu$m$^3$ \observed. The lowest reported value is
$x_{\rm qp}\lesssim10^{-9}$~\cite{Connolly2024}. In a transmon measured at a refrigerator temperature of 20\,mK, $n_{\rm qp}=0.04\pm0.01\,\mu$m$^{-3}$~\cite{Riste2013}
\observed, which is $x_{\rm qp}=1\times10^{-8}$ with the $n_{\rm cp}$ above \calc. In thermal equilibrium at 15--20\,mK there should be fewer than one quasiparticle in a volume the size
of the Earth \calc. Known external sources have been reduced one by one --- stray infrared light~\cite{Barends2011}, environmental radioactivity~\cite{Vepsalainen2020,Cardani2021}, cosmic rays~\cite{McEwen2022} --- and an excess over equilibrium
remains. What the measurements show, read carefully:
\begin{itemize}
\item A transmon measured above ground and deep underground at Gran Sasso, where muon interactions are six orders of magnitude rarer, shows a
similar average $T_1$ of about 80\,$\mu$s at both sites~\cite{DeDominicis2026}, and
$T_1$ is insensitive to added shielding while the quasiparticle tunnelling rate is very sensitive to it~\cite{Gordon2022} \observed. Together these say that at present
lifetimes $T_1$ is not set by radiation-generated quasiparticles, and that a large part of the measured tunnelling is of external origin. They do not
by themselves show an internal source \interp.
\item The excess quasiparticles have an energy distribution in equilibrium with the phonon bath~\cite{Connolly2024}. In aluminium, quasiparticles relax to the gap edge by phonon
emission far faster than they recombine at these densities~\cite{Kaplan1976} \observed, so any source gives this distribution; it is consistent with an internal source but does
not single one out \interp.
\item Background tunnelling in two co-housed charge-sensitive transmons runs at the single-hertz level, and individual events are
uncorrelated between the two qubits, while bursts, about once per minute, are correlated across devices~\cite{Sundelin2026,McEwen2022};
each burst has a characteristic lifetime of about 7\,ms and raises the tunnelling rate a thousand-fold in both devices~\cite{Sundelin2026}
\observed.
\item The tunnelling rate depends on the shunting-capacitor material and geometry, and an anomalous temperature dependence in some devices has been
modelled as high-transmission sites with a reduced gap in the junction barrier~\cite{Kurter2022}; most detectable surface TLS sit on the junction
leads~\cite{Lisenfeld2025}. Across 30 to 60 qubits per design, the aluminium gap $\Delta_0$ has median values between 183 and 193\,$\mu$eV, while the reduced gap
$\Delta_1$ of the high-transmission sites has medians from 5 to 30\,$\mu$eV, a large junction-to-junction variation~\cite{Kurter2022}
\observed.
\item A subset of TLS are themselves quasiparticles trapped in shallow subgap states formed by spatial fluctuations of the
superconducting gap: they are highly coherent, some with linewidths down to the 70\,kHz resolution of the resonator, and the landscape
reshuffles irreversibly on warming to about 300\,mK~\cite{deGraaf2020} \observed. The TLS and the quasiparticles are therefore
coupled, not independent \interp.
\item In 3D gap-engineered transmons, gap engineering reduced the burst detection rate by a factor of five, four orders of magnitude
short of what fast thermalisation of the quasiparticles to the cryostat temperature would give; the authors associate the shortfall with
slow thermalisation of the chip's phonons after a burst, which raises the effective chip temperature to about 90\,mK~\cite{Nho2026}
\observed.
\end{itemize}
These leave room for a source inside the device, at the junction; they do not establish it. The proposal below is that source, stated so that a
measurement can rule it in or out.

\section{The proposal}
\conjecture\ A qubit holds its state against a bath of two-level fluctuators (TLS) in the oxide and at the metal--oxide interfaces~\cite{Phillips1987,Muller2019}. A TLS flip kicks the condensate
phase by $\delta\phi\sim g/\omega_q\approx2\times10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-3}$ (for $g/2\pi=1$--10~MHz at 5~GHz); the condensate restores its phase in $\hbar/\Delta\approx3.6$\,ps, five to seven orders of magnitude faster than
the next flip ($\tau_{\rm TLS}\sim1$--$100\,\mu$s). After restoration the condensate is the same whichever way the TLS flipped: the information about
the flip has been discarded. That is an erasure, and by IAM's Law it is paid in heat. Each active site therefore produces an irreversible event at rate
$1/\tau_{\rm TLS}$, whether or not the qubit is being used. The separation of time scales makes the events independent, as the uncorrelated background
events require.
\paragraph{Which temperature.} In conventional thermodynamics the Landauer cost of resetting a degree of freedom is paid to the reservoir that
absorbs the heat, here the phonons at $\sim20$\,mK, where $k_BT\ln2$ is about $1\,\mu$eV. Charging the erasure at the condensate's own scale,
$\Delta/k_B\approx2.1$\,K, is IAM's Law applied to the nearest encoding surface \conjecture. It is the one place in this chapter where IAM departs from standard
thermodynamics, and therefore the place a clean experiment decides.

\section{From events to quasiparticles}
Three facts of superconductor physics fix how events become a density.
\paragraph{Pair breaking is quantised.} A quasiparticle exists only once a Cooper pair is broken, which costs $2\Delta$ ($364\,\mu$eV in Al) and yields
two quasiparticles. Landauer's principle sets the \emph{minimum} heat of an erasure~\cite{Landauer1961}, $\Delta\ln2=126\,\mu$eV if the bath sits at the gap scale;
heat below $2\Delta$ goes into phonons that cannot break pairs. An erasure event that produces quasiparticles therefore breaks a pair: yield $2$ per
event \derived\ (within the proposal).
\paragraph{Quasiparticles spread.} In aluminium they diffuse $\sqrt{D\tau_{\rm qp}}\approx250$--$800\,\mu$m within a lifetime $\tau_{\rm qp}\sim100\,\mu$s for a diffusion constant
$D\approx0.6$--$6.4\,\mu$m$^2$\,ns$^{-1}$, across the whole island \calc. What a qubit measures is the island average. Injected quasiparticles in a phase qubit were observed to recombine over about 300\,$\mu$s~\cite{Lenander2011} \observed. Trapping
shortens the effective lifetime: a single vortex traps quasiparticles measurably, and trapping by vortices can lower the background
density enough to improve coherence~\cite{Wang2014} \observed. Trapping at vortices or at interfaces enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp} through
$\tau_{\rm qp}$, which is why $\tau_{\rm qp}$ is measured on the device itself \derived.
\paragraph{The density is per pair near the gap.} $n_{\rm cp}=2\nu_0\Delta$ counts the electrons within $\Delta$ of the Fermi surface, not all
conduction electrons ($n_e/2=9\times10^{10}\,\mu$m$^{-3}$, which is $22{,}600$ times larger) \calc.
With $N$ active sites on an island of volume $V$, the steady state is
\begin{equation}x_{\rm qp}=\frac{2N\,\tau_{\rm qp}}{\tau_{\rm TLS}\,n_{\rm cp}\,V}.\label{eq:xqp}\end{equation}
\derived\ (from the proposal and the three facts above).

\paragraph{The volume of one erasure.} Equation~\eqref{eq:xqp} counts quasiparticles over the island, because that is where they
spread and what the qubit measures. The erasure itself is local. A phase disturbance at a site is screened by the condensate over the
London penetration depth $\lamL$; weighting the volume around the site by the squared envelope of that screening, $e^{-2r/\lamL}$,
gives
\begin{equation}V_{\rm eff}=4\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-2r/\lamL}\,r^2\,dr=\pi\lamL^3\label{eq:xqp_veff}\end{equation}
\derived\ (for that weight). The weight is a choice. It keeps the exponential envelope of the screened Green's function and drops its
$1/r$ factor, which would need a cutoff at the core of the site; a different weight changes $V_{\rm eff}$ by an order-unity factor or
more \interp. With the effective penetration depth of about 50\,nm measured for high-purity aluminium~\cite{Ganjam2024},
$V_{\rm eff}=3.9\times10^{-4}\,\mu$m$^3$, which holds about $1.6\times10^{3}$ pairs at $n_{\rm cp}=4\times10^{6}\,\mu$m$^{-3}$ \calc. The
London length is a phase-screening length. The condensate's coherence length, which sets the distance over which the gap amplitude
recovers after pair breaking, is a different length, and in thin films both depend on the electron mean free path, so the film values,
not the bulk ones, are the inputs a referee would ask for \interp. The London volume is the scale of the erasure and the island volume the
scale of the density it feeds; a measurement that resolves the quasiparticles near one site from the island average separates the two
\openprob.

\paragraph{A feedback between fluctuators and quasiparticles.} \conjecture\ If some quasiparticles are trapped in subgap states and act as
fluctuators themselves, as some TLS do~\cite{deGraaf2020}, part of the active-site count is fed by the density: $N=N_0+\phi\,N_{\rm qp}$,
with $N_{\rm qp}=x_{\rm qp}n_{\rm cp}V$ the quasiparticles on the island and $\phi$ the fraction of them active as fluctuators at the
same $\tau_{\rm TLS}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:xqp} then closes on itself,
\begin{equation}x_{\rm qp}=\frac{x_0}{1-g},\qquad g=\frac{2\phi\,\tau_{\rm qp}}{\tau_{\rm TLS}},\label{eq:xqp_feedback}\end{equation}
with $x_0$ the density at $\phi=0$ \derived\ (within the conjecture). The loop settles at a finite density only for $g<1$; at
$\tau_{\rm qp}=100\,\mu$s and $\tau_{\rm TLS}=30\,\mu$s that requires $\phi<0.15$ \calc. The fraction $\phi$ has not been measured on any
device \openprob; TLS spectroscopy that counts the quasiparticle-like fluctuators, set beside the measured density on the same device,
bounds it \interp.

($\tau_{\rm TLS}=30\,\mu$s, $\tau_{\rm qp}=100\,\mu$s; \calc.) A few pinholes cannot hold the background. Hundreds to thousands of active fluctuators can, and
TLS spectroscopy maps them mostly on the junction leads~\cite{Lisenfeld2019,Lisenfeld2025}, which is where the source must be. The number $N$ is a property of each device and has to be
measured; nothing else in Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp} is free. An alternative source must be excluded by the same measurement: slow relaxation of mechanical stress
produces phonon bursts and excess quasiparticles with several of the same signatures~\cite{Anthony2022}. The two are separated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp}: only the erasure source
scales with the active-fluctuator count $N$ and with $1/\tau_{\rm TLS}$. Two times of a fluctuator must be kept apart. One is its own energy relaxation time: for 56 TLS strongly coupled to seven transmons on
two chips with an acoustic bandgap structure, it spans 0.25\,$\mu$s to 5.4\,ms~\cite{ChenTLS2024} \observed. The other is its switching
time $\tau_{\rm TLS}$, the one that enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp}; it is read from the low-frequency noise of the TLS bath, which in a
superconducting resonator has been measured as $1/f$ frequency noise at Fourier frequencies near 0.1\,Hz~\cite{Burnett2014} \observed.

\section{The test}
On one device, measure the five quantities $x_{\rm qp}$ (parity switching~\cite{Riste2013,Serniak2018}), $\tau_{\rm qp}$, $\tau_{\rm TLS}$ (1/$f$ noise), the active fluctuator count
$N$ (TLS spectroscopy) and the island volume $V$. The law predicts \prediction
\begin{equation}\frac{x_{\rm qp}\,n_{\rm cp}\,V\,\tau_{\rm TLS}}{N\,\tau_{\rm qp}}=2.\end{equation}
Across devices, the background should scale as $N/(\tau_{\rm TLS}V)$: better barriers (longer $\tau_{\rm TLS}$, fewer fluctuators) lower it; better
shielding does not. The proposal fails if the background does not follow $N/V$, or if it keeps falling with shielding after external sources are gone.

\prediction\ Charging the erasure at the gap fixes two further results. Below about 100\,mK, where the thermal fraction is below
$10^{-9}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:xqp_thermal}), the background should not change with refrigerator temperature; and the same device moved
underground should lose its cosmic-ray part and keep its internal part. The proposal fails if the background tracks the refrigerator
temperature in that range, if it does not scale with $1/\tau_{\rm TLS}$, or if it falls underground far below its value at the surface.
The two measurements are a temperature sweep of parity switching on one device, as in Ref.~\cite{Riste2013}, and a same-device
comparison above and below ground, as in Ref.~\cite{DeDominicis2026}.

\prediction\ Equation~\eqref{eq:xqp} contains no qubit frequency: where the erasure is charged at the gap, a low-frequency design and a
transmon on the same barrier carry the same background. In a design whose erasure scale is the qubit's own quantum $hf$ rather than the
gap, the bath that receives the cost changes, and with it the dependence on frequency. The test is the background $x_{\rm qp}$, from
parity switching, on long-$T_1$ low-frequency devices and on transmons made in one junction process. A background that tracks the qubit
frequency shows that the erasure scale in those designs is $hf$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp} then needs its own derivation for them.

\section{The coherence ceiling}
Quasiparticle relaxation of a transmon is $\Gamma_1=(x_{\rm qp}/\pi)\sqrt{2\omega_q\Delta/\hbar}$~\cite{Catelani2011}. With
$\Delta_{\rm Al}=182\,\mu$eV at $5$\,GHz, $x_{\rm qp}=10^{-7}$ caps $T_1$ at $0.24$\,ms and $10^{-6}$ at $0.02$\,ms \calc; transmons that reach
$T_1\approx0.3$--$0.5$\,ms (Chapter~\ref{ch:scprimer}) carry less than $10^{-7}$, and there dielectric (TLS) loss and quasiparticles compete. If the
background is the cost of holding the state, it is lowered by changing the barrier and its fluctuators, not by shielding the device.

\section{Scope}
The proposal concerns the quiescent background in Al/AlO$_x$ devices once radiation and thermal sources have been reduced until
$x_{\rm qp}$ plateaus \interp. It says nothing about $T_2$, about the microscopic origin of the $1/f$ noise that sets $\tau_{\rm TLS}$,
which is taken from measurement, or about bursts, which are a separate, non-Poissonian channel \interp. Sufficiently strong microwave drives can
activate multiphoton pair-breaking processes that generate quasiparticles and cause qubit errors~\cite{Chowdhury2026} \derived\ (in the cited theory); that channel
depends on the drive, not on the quiescent TLS bath, and is separate as well. Other architectures (low-frequency fluxonium, $0$--$\pi$
and cat qubits, trapped ions, neutral atoms) have their own encoding surfaces and baths. IAM's Law applies to each of them, but the
numbers of Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp} do not transfer without a derivation of their own \interp.

\section{Status}
Stands: a proposal for an internal contribution to the background quasiparticle density, from erasures at the TLS rate, compatible with the measurements
above but not established by them; the same-device test decides it \conjecture. Derived from the proposal: the density in the field's units, with quantised pair breaking
and diffusion over the island (Eq.~\eqref{eq:xqp}) \derived. Open: the active-site count $N$ for a measured device, and whether each erasure event breaks a
pair (yield $2$) or only some do, which would lower the yield and raise the required $N$ \openprob.
