\chapter{Coherence walls and the substrate saturation point}\label{ch:walls}

\section{Three components of a gate error}

The qubit gauge (Chapter~\ref{ch:ascoreqc}) splits a superconducting two-qubit error into three parts:
\begin{equation}
 p_{2Q}=p_{\mathrm{coh}}+p_{\mathrm{mat}}+p_{\mathrm{ctrl}} .
 \label{eq:pdecomp}
\end{equation}
Here $p_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is the error from finite $T_1$ and $T_2$ over the gate time;
$p_{\mathrm{mat}}$ is the error from the substrate's TLS population, which grows as $T_1$
approaches the material ceiling; and $p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is the control and crosstalk overhead,
with an architecture-specific floor. The coherence term is standard. For a gate of duration
$t_g$, $p_{\mathrm{coh}}\approx t_g/T_1+t_g/T_\phi$ up to state-averaging factors~\cite{Abad2022}.
The split between $p_{\mathrm{mat}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is a model output \calibrated.

\section{The substrate saturation point}

\begin{definition}
For a given substrate and junction material, $T_{1,\mathrm{free}}$ is the coherence ceiling
set by its TLS population. The \emph{substrate saturation point} is
\begin{equation}
 T_1^*=0.65\,T_{1,\mathrm{free}},
\end{equation}
beyond which further increases in $T_1$ no longer reduce the two-qubit error, because the
material and crosstalk terms grow faster than the coherence term falls.
\end{definition}

\noindent In the model the two-qubit error is lowest at $T_1^*$ and rises beyond it. The values are
$T_{1,\mathrm{free}}\approx500\ \mu$s for Nb/AlO$_x$, so $T_1^*\approx325\ \mu$s; $T_{1,\mathrm{free}}\approx300\ \mu$s for Al/AlO$_x$,
so $T_1^*\approx195\ \mu$s; and $T_{1,\mathrm{free}}\approx2{,}000\ \mu$s for Ta-based transmons, so $T_1^*\approx1{,}300\ \mu$s. The ratio $0.65$
reflects the nonlinear form of the substrate term and is device dependent; it and the three ceilings are \calibrated, and no derivation of them is given here.

\section{What would test it}

The signature of the saturation point is an error that rises while $T_1$ rises past $T_1^*$, at fixed coupling graph and fixed
temperature. A transition between two processors with different coupling graphs cannot separate ``more couplers'' from ``past $T_1^*$'':
a denser graph adds control and crosstalk error on its own. No published same-topology series yet tests the point \openprob. The
discriminating test is a same-topology device with $T_1>T_1^*$; the falsifier is stated in Section~\ref{sec:walls_pred}.

\section{Willow and the materials escape}

Google's Willow processor has mean $T_1=68\ \mu$s~\cite{GoogleWillow2025} \observed. Its junctions are Al/AlO$_x$, so it sits at
$68/195=0.35\,T_1^*$ \calc, far below the saturation point; in the model its present error is set by control rather than by the substrate.
For Ta-based transmons, where the surface oxide is more crystalline and the TLS density lower~\cite{Place2021,Wang2022Ta},
$T_1^*\approx1{,}300\ \mu$s: a window four times that of Nb/AlO$_x$ \calc. When an Al/AlO$_x$ processor reaches $T_1^*$ is
\openprob: no rate of approach has been derived.
