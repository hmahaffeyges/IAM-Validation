\chapter{Coherence walls and the Substrate Inversion}\label{ch:walls}

\section{Three components of a gate error}

the quantum-processor report decomposes a superconducting two-qubit error into three parts~\cite{MahaffeyQAPE}:
\begin{equation}
 p_{2Q}=p_{\mathrm{coh}}+p_{\mathrm{mat}}+p_{\mathrm{ctrl}} .
 \label{eq:pdecomp}
\end{equation}
Here $p_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is the error from finite $T_1$ and $T_2$ over the gate time;
$p_{\mathrm{mat}}$ is the error from the substrate's TLS population, which grows as $T_1$
approaches the material ceiling; and $p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is the control and crosstalk overhead,
with an architecture-specific floor. The coherence term is standard. For a gate of duration
$t_g$, $p_{\mathrm{coh}}\approx t_g/T_1+t_g/T_\phi$ up to state-averaging factors~\cite{Abad2022}.
The split between $p_{\mathrm{mat}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is a model output of the quantum-processor report.

\section{The Substrate Inversion}

\begin{definition}
For a given substrate and junction material, $T_{1,\mathrm{free}}$ is the coherence ceiling
set by its TLS population. The \emph{Substrate Inversion} is the point
\begin{equation}
 T_1^*=0.65\,T_{1,\mathrm{free}}
\end{equation}
beyond which further increases in $T_1$ no longer reduce the two-qubit error, because the
material and crosstalk terms grow faster than the coherence term falls.
\end{definition}

\noindent the quantum-processor report sets $T_{1,\mathrm{free}}\approx500\ \mu$s for Nb/AlO$_x$, so $T_1^*\approx325\ \mu$s,
and $T_1^*\approx195\ \mu$s for Al/AlO$_x$. The factor 0.65 is described as reflecting the
nonlinear form of the substrate term. It is \calibrated\ on IBM's trajectory, and its derivation
is not given.

\section{Heron R2 and Nighthawk}

The case that motivated the definition is IBM's move from Heron~R2 (2024) to Nighthawk
(January~2026)~\cite{MahaffeyQAPE}. $T_1$ rose from 300 to 350~$\mu$s (+17\%), past $T_1^*$,
while the two-qubit error rose from $2.00\times10^{-3}$ to $2.15\times10^{-3}$ (+7.6\%). Nighthawk
also has 20\% more couplers per qubit (square lattice versus heavy-hex). the quantum-processor report's decomposition
is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:inversion}.

\begin{checkbox}
The components sum correctly: $910+340+750=2000$~ppm and $740+450+960=2150$~ppm. The source's
change table lists $p_{\mathrm{coh}}$ as ``$-190$~ppm''; the change is $-170$~ppm. The source
also lists Nighthawk's $\varepsilon$ as both $2.152\times10^{-3}$ and $2.156\times10^{-3}$.

\textbf{Is the inversion identified?} The observation is that error rose while $T_1$ rose,
together with a denser coupling graph. The decomposition attributes $+110$~ppm to the
substrate and $+210$~ppm to control overhead. The larger term is the one expected from
the topology change alone, independent of any substrate saturation. The single transition
cannot separate ``more couplers'' from ``past $T_1^*$''. The discriminating test is a same-topology
device with $T_1>T_1^*$. The source states the corresponding falsifier: \prediction\ an IBM
Nb/AlO$_x$ chip with $T_1>325\ \mu$s and $p_{2Q}<2\times10^{-3}$ at unchanged connectivity would
require the framework to be revised.
\end{checkbox}

\section{Willow and the materials escape}

the quantum-processor report places Google Willow~\cite{GoogleWillow2025} at $T_1=68\ \mu$s, $0.21\,T_1^*$, with a projected crossing near
2028. It states that the successor ``requires tantalum or equivalent''. For Ta-based transmons,
where the oxide is more crystalline and the TLS density lower~\cite{Place2021,Wang2022Ta}, it sets
$T_{1,\mathrm{free}}\approx2{,}000\ \mu$s and $T_1^*\approx1{,}300\ \mu$s, a fourfold wider window.
(The source gives the window as both 4$\times$ and 3.7$\times$.)

\begin{remark}
The 0.21 ratio uses the Nb/AlO$_x$ value $T_1^*=325\ \mu$s. Willow's junctions are Al/AlO$_x$,
for which the quantum-processor report's own $T_1^*$ is 195~$\mu$s, giving $0.35\,T_1^*$. The projected crossing year
should be recomputed on that basis.
\end{remark}

\section{Scaling with qubit count}

the quantum-processor report models the dependence of effective error on qubit count and connectivity with an
architecture-specific cost per added coupler, $\alpha$, which it states as
\begin{itemize}
 \item essentially free for SC heavy-hex before $T_1^*$;
 \item ``toxic'' for SC CZ after $T_1^*$, with an amplifier $g\approx16$;
 \item negligible for trapped-ion QCCD, $\alpha\approx0.02$, where connectivity grows as $\log N$;
 \item flat for Rydberg arrays.
\end{itemize}
These are \calibrated\ on nine IBM platforms and the published ion and atom trajectories.
