\chapter{The Information Sphere}\label{ch:infosphere}

\indevelopment{} This chapter describes a part of the chain that runs on every sample. Its readings are shown in every report beside the commissioned ones and are flagged as in development until each passes tests written before its data are read.

\section{Two ways to put a genome on a sphere}

The address globe of Chapter~\ref{ch:explorer} places every site by where it sits on the DNA: neighbors on the globe are neighbors on a
chromosome. The information sphere places every site by how it behaves: its methylation across the 79 healthy cell types of IAM-Atlas
v2.1. Neighbors on it are sites that do the same job in the same cells, wherever they sit in the genome. The two answer different
questions about the same sample, and each sees what the other cannot.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.33\linewidth}p{0.33\linewidth}}
 & \textbf{Address globe} & \textbf{Information sphere}\\\hline
A site's place & where it sits on the DNA & how it behaves across the 79 cell types\\
Neighbors are & sites next to each other on a chromosome & sites that act alike, wherever they are\\
It answers & where on the genome a change is & what kind of change it is, and in which cell's program\\
It cannot tell & that distant sites belong to one program & where on a chromosome a change is\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A loss along one stretch of a chromosome is one patch on the address globe and scattered points on the information sphere. A cell's
whole program failing at sites spread over every chromosome is scattered dots on the address globe and one territory on the information
sphere.

\section{Nobody places the sites; the mathematics does}

No person and no program chooses where any site, cell or tissue lands. Each of the 191{,}458 sites (those the atlas measured in all 79
cell types and that vary between them) is placed from its 79 measured levels alone, by four fixed steps applied to every site the same way.

\paragraph{1. Each site becomes a point on a sphere.} A site's level in cell type $c$ is a probability $b_c$. Its square-root vector
\begin{equation}
u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{79}}\left(\sqrt{b_1},\dots,\sqrt{b_{79}},\ \sqrt{1-b_1},\dots,\sqrt{1-b_{79}}\right)
\end{equation}
has length exactly one, because $b_c + (1-b_c) = 1$ in every cell type. Every site therefore lies on the surface of a sphere. The sphere
is not a drawing choice; it is where probabilities naturally live.

\paragraph{2. The angle between two sites is their true distance.} The angle $\theta = \arccos(u_1\cdot u_2)$ is the Fisher--Rao
distance of information geometry. For nearby states, the relative entropy between them is, to leading order, proportional to $\theta^2$.
Relative entropy is the quantity IAM's instruments measure as disorder against a physical floor, so the instruments and the sphere
measure in one currency.

\paragraph{3. Each site's own level is taken out.} Without this step a site's average level decides where it lands, and sites that are
mostly methylated simply crowd together. The level is removed by subtracting the square-root vector of a flat profile at the site's own
mean level and scaling the remainder to length one. A site is then placed only by how it departs from its own level across the cells.

\paragraph{4. The three leading directions are drawn.} They keep 39\,\% of the differences between sites. The positions are then frozen,
and every sample is read on the same sphere.

\paragraph{Only then are labels added.} Each site is labeled with the cell type where it departs most from its own level, and the 79
cell types are grouped into 15 families by tissue and lineage. That grouping is a stated labeling; nothing is computed from it, and the
positions were fixed before any label existed.

\section{What the geometry found by itself}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lineages fall together.} The blood lineages lie side by side: bone-marrow progenitors, B and NK cells, T cells and myeloid
cells. Gut, pancreas, lung and airway, kidney, brain and muscle each hold their own region. Cells that share a developmental origin share
regulatory programs, and the methylation alone put them next to each other.
\item \textbf{Each cell has two poles.} A cell's methylated sites and its unmethylated sites gather on opposite sides of the sphere. For T
cells, each side gathers tightly (a concentration of 0.88 and 0.86, where 1 is all in one place) while both together give 0.14. Taking
each site's level out turns ``higher in this cell'' and ``lower in this cell'' into opposite directions.
\item \textbf{Most sites carry no single identity.} Only 41{,}241 of the 191{,}458 sites stand apart in one cell type by at least 0.10
more than in any other (the median margin is 0.036). The picture colors those and draws the rest as faint dust.
\item \textbf{The sky is partly empty.} 45\,\% of the sphere holds no sites. It is masked, as the microwave-sky maps mask our galaxy,
rather than stretched to fill the sphere, because stretching would falsify the distances.
\end{itemize}

On the atlas's real healthy cells, 11 of 12 groups of cell-marking sites land together beyond what their methylation level alone would
give, and look-alike groups with the same levels do not (0 of 100).

\section{Reading one sample}

A sample is read against its own expected healthy self: the atlas mixed at the cell shares the IAM-Deconvolver found in that sample, with
no other person's sample involved. Each site's change is the one the address globe already reads (its difference from that expectation,
in the site's healthy width and in the sample's own spread). On the information sphere the change is averaged over each territory (one
family's clearly marking sites, methylated or unmethylated there) and judged against the sample's own other territories: a territory has
changed when it stands beyond the line pure chance crosses less than once in 20 samples. The count is fixed by the measurement; the
slider only fades the rest of the sphere to dark. The sample's distance from its own expected healthy self is the root-mean-square
Fisher--Rao angle.

The example healthy blood lights 0 of its 27 territories. The same blood with a clustered loss planted along stretches of DNA also
lights 0, while the address globe's Met-A layer lights 44 of its 597 blocks for it: a loss along the DNA belongs to the address globe,
as the two spheres' difference predicts.

\section{What it is for}

These are uses to test, synthetic first, not results.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Distance from one's own healthy state}, as an angle rather than a score we define.
\item \textbf{Tracking one person over time}: repeated draws trace a path, and a slow drift could show before any single reading leaves
Normal.
\item \textbf{Programs that fail together}, found with no list given in advance.
\item \textbf{Where cell-free DNA came from}, by the territory it lands in.
\item \textbf{Quality control}: an array or laboratory sitting off the healthy map stands out on its own.
\item \textbf{Gene function}: sites labeled by their genes' functions, and a sample's change stacked over every site of one function.
\end{itemize}

\section{The sky tools}

Because the information sphere is a masked sky, the methods built for the microwave background apply directly: the masked angular power
spectrum (at what scale the variation lives); the cross-spectrum of two independent readings, in which noise drops out; component
separation, with cell types in the role of frequencies, to separate ``the mix changed'' from ``the cells changed''; the dipole, as the net
direction a person's state has moved; spherical wavelets and matched filters; shape statistics; and stacking. Each is tested synthetic
first.

\section{What is old and what is new}

The square-root geometry is decades-old mathematics, used for distances between probability distributions and between populations. The
closest work in biology places single cells on a sphere with a learned model. What is new here is treating a whole methylome as a sky: a
fixed construction rather than a fitted one, placing sites rather than cells, read with the microwave-sky tools, and with one individual
compared against itself.
