\section{What a molecule shows that an array cannot}

An array reports, at each site, the fraction of copies that are methylated. A sequencing read reports one molecule: a run of
CpGs, each methylated or not, on one strand of one cell's DNA. On a molecule that is otherwise methylated, an unmethylated CpG
between two methylated neighbors is a held site that has been lost: the balance between writing (on SAM) and erasing (on oxygen and
2-oxoglutarate) left it out of its held state. That is the \emph{held error}, and it is the most direct reading of the writing and erasing
the book's physics is about. It is not a copying error: neurons, which do not divide, hold the same balance.

\section{The statistic}

A molecule qualifies when it carries at least six CpG calls and at least 80~\% of them are methylated. Each interior call is
an opportunity; an unmethylated interior call with both neighbors methylated is an isolated error. The held error is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon=\frac{\sum\text{isolated errors}}{\sum\text{opportunities}},
  \qquad E_{\rm hold}=\kB T\ln\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon},
  \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
computed on the methylated channel only. The unmethylated channel is not used: there, bisulfite conversion failure dominates
the isolated calls. The chain requires at least 100,000 opportunities and reports the reading separately on odd and even
molecules as a repeat check. \derived{} for the form; the selection rules are \calibrated.

\section{The floor, the healthy reference and the ceiling}

If the cell holds each site with an energy $E$ against thermal kicks, a two-state Boltzmann factor gives the error that energy allows,
$\varepsilon=1/(1+e^{E/\kB T})$. \derived{} Three energies mark the gauge:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{\min}=\frac{1}{1+e^{M}},\qquad \varepsilon_0=\frac{1}{1+e^{\phi M}},\qquad \Hmin=H(\varepsilon_{\min}),\qquad \Href=H(\varepsilon_0).
  \label{eq:eps0}
\end{equation}

The floor is where thermal kicks win against everything one ATP can buy, and no cell comes near it. The healthy reference is where healthy
cells actually hold their sites: the holding energy measured across 56 healthy cell types, $3.08\,\kB T$ (public single-molecule files counted by chain Stage Q2, rebuilt by one committed
script), gives $\phi=0.147$ of one ATP
(Chapter~\ref{ch:landauer}), $\varepsilon_0=0.044$ and $\Href=0.259$ bits. Healthy cell types spread around it (held error 0.033--0.055),
so about half hold their sites better than $\Href$; none approaches $\Hmin$. The distance between floor and reference is the cell's
efficiency, $\phi$. Thermodynamics bounds the holding energy by the free energy spent per renewal (one ATP sets the floor); the height
the cell actually holds is set by the kinetics of maintenance, the balance of restore and loss, $E_{\rm hold}=\kB T\ln(g/f)$
(Section~\ref{sec:balance}), whether the cell divides or not. Restore and loss rates measured in the same cells return this balance by construction, since at steady state $g/f=(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$;
deriving $\phi$ needs the writer's discrimination measured outside the cell, and is the central open problem of the cellular rung
(Chapter~\ref{ch:open}). \openprob{} A candidate that gives it from $M$ and the price of the methyl donor, by a different route, is set out next. \conjecture

\section{A candidate for the healthy reference from $M$ and the SAM price}\label{sec:middle_candidate}

The floor and the ceiling are derived; the healthy reference is measured. A candidate gives it from $M$ and the price of the methyl donor, with nothing fitted to
methylation data ($M$ comes from the free energy of ATP and body temperature, the SAM price from published biochemistry). \conjecture{} It holds the two marks apart by how
they are held. The floor is a site held behind a wall $M$ kicks high: Boltzmann's factor, an error that falls exponentially with the
energy, a record held by the landscape alone. The healthy reference is a site held by restoring that is paid for continuously against
thermal kicks: a driven passage, an error that falls only in proportion to the work. \interp

Take a site's state as one coordinate from held ($x=0$) to lost ($x=\ell$), with the restore pushing back with a constant work $W$ across
the mark against thermal diffusion. The restore-to-loss odds are the undriven mean first-passage time over the driven one (the held end
reflecting, the lost end absorbing; the Einstein relation ties the drift to the diffusion):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{g}{f}=\frac{w/2}{1-\left(1-e^{-w}\right)/w},\qquad w=\frac{W}{\kB T},\qquad \frac{g}{f}\to\frac{w}{2}\ \text{for}\ w\gg1 .
  \label{eq:middle_passage}
\end{equation}
\derived{} (first passage, borrowed physics) IAM's Law enters through $W$: every restore is paid at the encoding surface in coins of
$M\,\kB T$. The restore that sets the healthy level is one methyl transfer (below: half-lost pairs are restored far faster than lost
ones), and each transfer spends one SAM, whose synthesis spends ATP $\to$ AMP $+\,2$P$_i$. Adenylate kinase ($\mathrm{ATP}+\mathrm{AMP}\rightleftharpoons2\,\mathrm{ADP}$) links
the two bonds, so one SAM costs $2-\Delta G_{\rm AK}/\Delta G_{\rm ATP}$ coins, exactly two where it is at equilibrium. \derived{} With $W=2M\kB T=41.88\,\kB T$, $g/f=21.45$, $\varepsilon_0=0.04454$, $E_{\rm hold}=\kB T\ln(g/f)=3.066\,\kB T$ and $\phi=E_{\rm hold}/M\kB T=0.1464$, against the measured
$3.08\,\kB T$, $\phi=0.147$, and a pooled healthy lone-loss intercept of 0.04526 (95~\% 0.04375--0.04657, 197 samples). \conjecture{}
The simpler form $g/f=M$ gives 0.04558 and $3.042\,\kB T$; the two differ by about 2~\%, finer than the present resolution.

What it explains, and what has been measured beside it:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rate independence.} The odds depend on $w$ alone, not on how fast restore and loss act (checked by direct simulation at a
  fourfold change in speed). The balance therefore does not depend on division, as Section~\ref{sec:balance} requires. \derived
\item \textbf{Restores are single transfers.} On hairpin files, which read both strands of each molecule, the two strands of a fully held
  CpG are lost together 5.08--8.21 times as often as independent strands allow in maintenance-only clones, and 5.43--5.56 times in wild-type
  cells whose writers can rewrite a lost pair. \measured{} In a balance of three states per pair (both held, one lost, both lost) the
  coupling $4P_{UU}P_{MM}/P_{H}^2$ equals the ratio of the restore rate of a half-lost pair to that of a fully lost one \derived{}: the copier,
  brought to half-marked pairs, restores them far faster than a lost pair is rewritten, so the restore that sets the healthy level is one
  methyl transfer, not two. \interp
\item \textbf{The crew sets the push.} Removing UHRF1, which brings the maintenance methyltransferase to the site, raises the held error
  2.925 times (0.03216 to 0.09407); in Equation~\eqref{eq:middle_passage} the work delivered per restore falls from $59.2$ to $18.2\,\kB T$.
  \measured{} (rise) \interp{} (reading)
\end{itemize}

Two predictions were written before any of their data were read. \textbf{Energy or supply:} if the push is the coin's energy, a fall in
SAM/SAH by a factor $r$ lowers each methyl transfer by $\ln r$ of a kick, and the held error rises by only $\times1.057$--$1.077$ for a fall of
3.33--5.00; if it is the coin's supply, the held error rises by the full factor, $\times3.33$--$5.00$. \prediction

What is not settled: the factor of two was looked for after one coin gave odds of about $M/2$; its source, the price of one SAM, must be
set by how near equilibrium adenylate kinase sits in each cell: within a factor two the price is 1.967--2.033 coins and the held error
0.04386--0.04524 (the textbook tables give 2.09--2.14, because their standard values imply $K_{\rm AK}\approx0.22$ rather than the measured value near 1);
the recycling of the methyl group (SAH back to methionine) is not counted; the constant push across one mark is a modeling choice; the stem-cell control of the UHRF1 series holds at 0.03216,
which needs $59.2\,\kB T$ per restore, more than two coins ($41.88\,\kB T$); and removing the eraser lowers the held error only modestly, so
whether loss at held sites is undriven or partly paid erasing changes the factor of order one. \openprob

\section{The reading: each sample against its own reference}

The commissioned IAM-A needs no outside reference. A sample's own molecules carry two numbers. The first is its held error
$\varepsilon$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps}. The second is its \emph{loose share} $L$: the genome is cut into tiles of 2,000 consecutive CpGs,
and $L$ is the share of tiles (each with at least 200 calls) whose mean methylation is below 0.70. Across 56 healthy cell types
(153 samples of one public atlas~\cite{Loyfer2023}, counted by the chain), the held error follows the loose share on one line,
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{\rm pred}(L)=0.0403+0.0346\,L,\qquad
  \text{IAM-A}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{\rm pred}(L)},
  \label{eq:iama}
\end{equation}
fitted once (Theil--Sen, across cell types) and frozen in the chain (self-reference line v1). \measured{} A sample is read against what its
own loose share predicts: 1 is on the healthy line, above 1 is more held sites lost than its own state predicts (right on the gauge). So the middle mark of IAM-A's gauge is not an average of other people: it is computed from the sample itself. The form of the line follows from the cell's design, which holds firm the part of the genome it uses and leaves the share $L$ loose; its two constants are the measured part, counted once. An attempt to derive the firm rate from the copying enzyme's own error, $1/(1+D)$, did not hold on the commissioned counter (DEV-WRITER-01 and -02), so it stays measured. \measured{}
Before reading, a sample-shape check compares the sample's tile distribution with the healthy samples of its cell type and refuses
a sample whose shape lies outside them; a refused sample is listed with its reason and not read. The healthy band, v1, is
$0.779$--$1.055$, set on healthy samples only.

\textbf{Commissioned} on healthy blood CD8 T cells, whole-genome bisulfite: on a rule sealed before any of the data was
downloaded, 7 of the 8 samples it read, from a laboratory it had never seen, read Normal; the shape check refused five (all four naive
samples and one central-memory sample). The counts, the line and the shape reference were rebuilt identically from public data on a clean
machine (158 of 158). Other cell types and chemistries are development.

\subsection*{The earlier form: a frozen position (retired to a development flag)}

Its numbers are kept as they were measured, on the earlier counter (superseded). Before the self-reference line, each healthy cell type was given its own position $P$ relative to the healthy reference, measured once
per cell type and per read pipeline, and frozen. This form is kept in the chain behind a development flag only:
\begin{equation}
  \text{IAM-A}_{\rm position}=\frac{H(\varepsilon)}{P_{\rm cell}\,H(\varepsilon_0)},\qquad P_{\rm neutrophil}=1.149 .
  \label{eq:iamapos}
\end{equation}
\measured{} $P$ was measured on three healthy granulocyte donors (range 1.140--1.155, coefficient of variation 0.74~\%), on
whole read-level files of one public atlas~\cite{Loyfer2023}, with one named read-level pipeline. The average healthy cell type, $\Href$, then
sits at $A=1/P=0.870$ on the neutrophil's gauge, and the floor $\Hmin$ at $1\times10^{-7}$. \calc

\begin{rulebox}
The floor and the reading must be defined by the same statistic on the same selection of molecules. A floor taken from a
different statistic (all-site held error, with no filter on methylated molecules) misplaces healthy molecules several-fold.
\measured{} The chain enforces the rule.
\end{rulebox}

\section{One energy, many cells}

Read for 153 samples of 56 healthy cell types by chain Stage Q2 on whole files (the third item on 399 genomic windows, PROC-CHANNEL-01), with no labels and no floor:
\begin{itemize}
\item the held error ranges 0.033--0.055 and the holding energy $3.08\pm0.12\,\kB T$: close to one energy in every cell type;
\item on one physics floor (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps0}) healthy cell types read 0.81--1.18, median 1.02; 27 of 56 inside Normal. One
      floor is not yet precise enough for an individual reading, which is why each cell type carries its own $P$;
\item on the 26,800 CpGs that all 56 cell types keep methylated, the same bits in every cell, the held error still differs
      1.66-fold from the lowest (naive CD8 T cells, lung macrophages, B cells) to the highest (smooth muscle, heart
      fibroblasts, erythroid progenitors), and the difference is stable across donors (intraclass correlation 0.80);
\item cells do not sort by turnover: colon epithelium (a 3.4-day lifespan) and cardiomyocytes (56,000 days)~\cite{Sender2021} carry about the same
      held error, 0.042 and 0.043.
\end{itemize}
\measured{} for all four. The architecture of each cell's maintenance, not how often it is replaced, sets its position.

\section{Tumors}

The same statistic reads tumors against the same person's normal tissue with no reference group: in six early-onset
colorectal pairs the tumor's held error is higher in all six, by 7--33~\%, and in oral squamous carcinoma in four of four, and in
three of the four when 5-hydroxymethylcytosine is removed (Chapter~\ref{part4:ch:reach}). \measured
