\chapter{The mechanism in the Boltzmann code: a matter-sector expansion rate}\label{ch:level2}

\section*{Summary}
Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime} tested the dual-sector prediction through MGCAMB's parameterised $\mu$~\cite{Zhao2009MGCAMB,Wang2023MGCAMB}. This chapter
puts the mechanism itself into the CAMB Boltzmann solver~\cite{Lewis2000CAMB}: cold dark matter and baryons experience a modified effective
expansion rate, while the photon perturbation equations, neutrinos, recombination and the background Friedmann equation remain standard
$\Lambda$CDM. The modification is governed by a single quantity, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$, obtained from the virial theorem applied to the Planck
2018 matter density $\Omega_m=0.3153$~\cite{Planck2018VI}, combined with the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$. The implementation requires
three changes to the CAMB Fortran source (\texttt{equations.f90}); no new free parameter is introduced beyond standard $\Lambda$CDM.

Three converged Markov chains against the full Planck likelihood (NPIPE CamSpec TTTEEE~\cite{Rosenberg2022}, low-$\ell$ TT and EE, and lensing) give
$\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ between the lowest points of the two chains (the lowest $\chi^2$ after the $30\,\%$ burn-in) and $-0.01$ between their chain averages, relative to $\Lambda$CDM: for the same number
of parameters, a likelihood ratio of $0.76$. Every standard cosmological parameter shifts by less than $0.1\sigma$. The matter clustering amplitude
shifts from $\sigma_8=0.8087\pm0.0059$ ($\Lambda$CDM) to $\sigma_8=0.7998\pm0.0058$ (dual sector), a $1.1\,\%$ reduction in the direction reported by
KiDS-1000, DES Y3 and HSC Y3 \measured.

The sector-dependent expansion rate yields a Hubble-parameter split directly from the MCMC posterior: $H_0^{(\gamma)}=67.16\pm0.47$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$
for photons ($0.37\sigma$ from Planck $\Lambda$CDM) and $H_0^{(m)}=72.26\pm0.50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ for matter ($0.75\sigma$ from SH0ES) \measured\ \calc. Both lie
within $1\sigma$ of the observations, with the background Friedmann equation unmodified. Two exploratory chains put the same term into the background Friedmann equation instead: $H_0$ moves to $61.5$, $10.9\sigma$ from Planck, the large distortion expected when the term is placed where IAM says it does not belong. In IAM the background does not change; the term lives in the entropy functional and enters through the perturbations. All chains, configuration files, the modified Fortran source and the analysis scripts are in the repository
(\texttt{camb\_validation/}).

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:l2_intro}
The $\Lambda$CDM model provides an excellent fit to the CMB anisotropies~\cite{Planck2018VI} and to large-scale structure. Two persistent
discrepancies have motivated controlled late-time modifications.

The first is the Hubble tension. The CMB-inferred value $H_0=67.4\pm0.5$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ (Planck 2018~\cite{Planck2018VI}) differs from the
local distance-ladder measurement $H_0=73.04\pm1.04$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ (Riess et al.\ 2022~\cite{Riess2022}) by $4.9\sigma$,
$(73.04-67.4)/\sqrt{0.5^2+1.04^2}$ \calc. The difference has been examined with independent local methods, including the tip of the red giant
branch~\cite{Freedman2021} and strong-lensing time delays~\cite{Wong2020} \observed. The tip of the red giant branch, calibrated with the Hubble and
James Webb telescopes and 24 calibrating type Ia supernovae, gives $H_0=70.39\pm1.22\,({\rm stat})\pm1.33\,({\rm sys})\pm0.70\,(\sigma_{\rm SN})$,
$\pm1.94$ in quadrature (Freedman et al.\ 2025~\cite{Freedman2025}) \observed. It lies $0.96\sigma$ below the matter-sector rate $72.26$ derived below
and $1.66\sigma$ above the photon-sector rate $67.16$ \calc: at its present precision the red-giant ladder does not separate the two rates.

The second is the $\sigma_8$/$S_8$ tension. Weak-lensing surveys have reported $S_8\equiv\sigma_8(\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5}$ 2--3$\sigma$ below the Planck
$\Lambda$CDM prediction: KiDS-1000 cosmic shear $0.759^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$ (Asgari et al.\ 2021~\cite{Asgari2021}) and multi-probe
$0.766^{+0.020}_{-0.014}$ (Heymans et al.\ 2021~\cite{Heymans2021}), DES Y3 $3\times2$pt $0.776\pm0.017$~\cite{DESY3}, and HSC Y3 cosmic shear
$0.769^{+0.031}_{-0.034}$~\cite{HSCY3} \observed. The completed KiDS survey gives $0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$, in agreement with
Planck~\cite{Wright2025} \observed.

Both differences set early-universe measurements made with photons against late-universe measurements made with matter. This motivates a question:
could matter and photons experience different effective expansion rates at late times while sharing a common expansion history in the early
universe?

Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime} showed that a scale-independent suppression $\mu(a)<1$ with $\Sigma(a)=1$ in the $\mu$--$\Sigma$
framework~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016} is compatible with the full Planck likelihood in four data combinations (twelve chains, $\Delta\chi^2$ from
$+0.56$ to $+1.73$). That analysis used the MGCAMB parameterisation~\cite{Wang2023MGCAMB}, which imposes $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ as effective functions
inside the Boltzmann solver without specifying the physical mechanism.

This chapter goes beyond the parametric level. The physical mechanism is coded directly into the CAMB Fortran source: the cold dark matter and baryon
perturbation equations use a modified effective expansion rate (\texttt{adotoa\_matter}), while the photon perturbation equations use the standard
$\Lambda$CDM rate. The global background Friedmann equation is unmodified. This produces the same $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ signature as the MGCAMB
parameterisation, but through an explicit sector-dependent coupling rather than an imposed functional form.

The dual-sector mechanism predicts that the expansion rate experienced by matter-sector observables differs from that experienced by photon-sector
observables. At the present epoch this gives the Hubble-parameter split $H_0^{(m)}/H_0^{(\gamma)}=\sqrt{1+\beta_m}$, with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ the
coupling from the virial theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}) \derived.

Section~\ref{sec:l2_model} defines the modification and its implementation in CAMB; Section~\ref{sec:l2_data} describes the data and the chains;
Section~\ref{sec:l2_accounting} lists the parameter accounting; Section~\ref{sec:l2_results} gives the parameter constraints; Section~\ref{sec:l2_hubble}
the sector-dependent Hubble parameter; Section~\ref{sec:l2_background} the background test; Section~\ref{sec:l2_discussion} the discussion; and
Section~\ref{sec:l2_conclusions} the conclusions.

\section{Model and implementation}\label{sec:l2_model}
\subsection{The dual-sector perturbation mechanism}
The modification applies only to the perturbation equations that govern cold dark matter and baryons. The global background expansion rate $H(a)$,
fixed by the Friedmann equation, remains standard $\Lambda$CDM:
\begin{equation}
H^2(a)=H_0^2\left[\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_ra^{-4}+\Omega_\Lambda\right].
\label{eq:l2_friedmann}\end{equation}
Inside the perturbation equations, the evolution of CDM and baryons uses a matter-sector expansion rate,
\begin{equation}
H_m^2(a)=H^2(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2,\qquad E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right),
\label{eq:l2_Hm}\end{equation}
with $E(a)$ the activation function of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_activation}. The coupling is
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=\frac{0.3153}{2}=0.15765,
\label{eq:l2_beta}\end{equation}
where $\Omega_m=0.3153$ is the Planck 2018 best fit (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing; Planck Collaboration 2020~\cite{Planck2018VI}, Table~2) \measured, and the
factor $1/2$ follows from the virial theorem applied to the partition of gravitational energy between the geometric and informational channels
(Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}) \derived.

Photon perturbation equations use the unmodified rate $H(a)$. This is the complete specification: no additional field, parameter or modification of
the gravitational action is introduced.

\subsection{Effective $\mu(a)$ and $\Sigma(a)$}
The mechanism maps onto the standard $\mu$--$\Sigma$ framework. Write the linear growth of the matter contrast in $\ln a$ with the rate the matter
equations see. In matter domination and later, $\delta_m''+(2+d\ln H/d\ln a)\delta_m'=4\pi G\bar\rho_m\delta_m/H^2$; if the source of the matter
equation is measured per matter-sector Hubble time, the right-hand side becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{4\pi G\bar\rho_m}{H_m^2}\,\delta_m=\frac32\,\Omega_m(a)\,\frac{H^2}{H_m^2}\,\delta_m\equiv\frac32\,\Omega_m(a)\,\mu(a)\,\delta_m ,
\end{equation}
which identifies the effective coupling, while the lensing equation, which involves photons only, is unchanged:
\begin{align}
\mu(a)&=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\label{eq:l2_mu}\\
\Sigma(a)&=1\quad\text{(exact)}.\label{eq:l2_sigma}
\end{align}
\derived\ (for a Newton constant scaled by $H^2/H_m^2$; how the code realises it is given below). At the present epoch,
\begin{equation}
\mu(z{=}0)=\frac{1}{1+\beta_m}=\frac{1}{1.15765}=0.864,
\label{eq:l2_mu0}\end{equation}
a $13.6\,\%$ suppression of the effective gravitational coupling for matter perturbations \calc. The suppression vanishes at high redshift: $E(a)\to0$
as $a\to0$, so $\mu\to1$ during radiation domination, at recombination and in the early matter era. Table~\ref{tab:l2_mu} gives $\mu(a)$ and the
matter-sector enhancement $H_m/H$ at selected redshifts, and Fig.~\ref{fig:lt_mu} shows $\mu(z)$ and $E(a)$; the modification is confined to
$z\lesssim2$.

\subsection{The CAMB Fortran implementation}\label{sec:l2_code}
The implementation changes three places in the CAMB~1.5.8 source file \texttt{equations.f90} (\texttt{camb\_validation/equations\_iam\_level2.f90}).
No other file is changed. The code is printed as it is in the source.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Module-level parameters} (l.~55--56): the coupling and a switch,
\begin{Verbatim}[breaklines,breakanywhere,fontsize=\footnotesize]
real(dl) :: iam_beta = 0.15765_dl
logical  :: iam_dual_sector = .true.     ! Enable dual-sector mode
\end{Verbatim}
\item \emph{Matter-sector expansion rate} (l.~2245--2252). After the standard \texttt{adotoa} $=\mathcal H=aH$ is computed (unchanged), the
matter-sector rate is formed by adding to $\mathcal H^2=\texttt{grho}/3$ the extra term $\beta_mE(a)\,a^2$ times today's total density:
\begin{Verbatim}[breaklines,breakanywhere,fontsize=\footnotesize]
if (iam_dual_sector .and. iam_beta > 0.0_dl .and. a > 1.0d-6) then
    iam_Ea = exp(1.0_dl - 1.0_dl / a)
    iam_grho0 = State%grhob + State%grhoc + State%grhornomass + State%grhog + State%grhov
    iam_grho_extra = iam_beta * iam_Ea * a2 * iam_grho0
    adotoa_matter = sqrt((grho + iam_grho_extra) / 3._dl)
else
    adotoa_matter = adotoa
end if
\end{Verbatim}
Since \texttt{iam\_grho0} $=8\pi G\sum_i\rho_{i,0}=3H_0^2$ for a flat universe, $\mathcal H_m^2=\mathcal H^2+\beta_mE(a)a^2H_0^2$, which is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_Hm} multiplied by $a^2$. The sum leaves out the massive-neutrino density ($\Omega_\nu=0.0014$ for the one $0.06$\,eV neutrino of
the runs), so the coupling as coded is $0.9986\,\beta_m$: a $0.14\,\%$ difference, small against the $2\,\%$ posterior error on $\Omega_m/2$. \calc
\item \emph{CDM and baryon density equations} (l.~2324--2337). CAMB works in synchronous gauge, in which the CDM is at rest and the matter density
contrasts are driven by the metric variable $z=h'/(2k)$, fixed by the $00$ Einstein equation as $z=(\tfrac12\,\texttt{dgrho}/k+\texttt{etak})/\mathcal H$.
In the CDM and baryon equations only, $\mathcal H$ (\texttt{adotoa}) is replaced by $\mathcal H_m$ (\texttt{adotoa\_matter}):
\begin{Verbatim}[breaklines,breakanywhere,fontsize=\footnotesize]
if (iam_dual_sector .and. adotoa_matter /= adotoa) then
    z_matter = (0.5_dl*dgrho/k + etak)/adotoa_matter
    clxcdot = -k*z_matter
else
    clxcdot = -k*z
end if
ayprime(ix_clxc)=clxcdot

!  Baryon equation of motion — IAM: use matter-sector expansion rate
if (iam_dual_sector .and. adotoa_matter /= adotoa) then
    clxbdot = -k*(z_matter + vb)
else
    clxbdot = -k*(z+vb)
end if
\end{Verbatim}
\end{enumerate}
The metric source that drives matter density growth is thus weakened by the factor $\mathcal H/\mathcal H_m=\mu^{1/2}$, while the metric itself, the
photon Boltzmann hierarchy, the neutrino equations, recombination, the background (\texttt{dtauda}), lensing and the $C_\ell$ calculation are
untouched. The global background expansion history is standard $\Lambda$CDM.

\paragraph{How the code realises the mechanism.} Built with the switch on and off at the Run~A posterior-mean parameters
(\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}verification/\allowbreak{}scripts/\allowbreak{}l2growth.sh}; outputs in
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}verification/\allowbreak{}chains/\allowbreak{}data/}), the coded change lowers $\sigma_8$ today by $1.2\,\%$, against $0.8\,\%$ for
Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_mu} applied as $G_{\rm eff}=\mu G$ at the same parameters, with the same redshift dependence (Table~\ref{tab:l2_growth}) \measured.
The coded mechanism is therefore somewhat stronger than Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_mu}, in the same direction. The three ways of writing the term in the
growth equation are compared in Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}.

\subsection{Parameter count}
The model introduces no free parameter beyond standard $\Lambda$CDM:
\begin{itemize}
\item the coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$: the factor $1/2$ from the virial theorem, $\Omega_m$ the independently measured matter density;
\item the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$, from horizon thermodynamics (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_lnE});
\item the normalisation $E(1)=1$, set by definition.
\end{itemize}
The six sampled parameters are identical to those of $\Lambda$CDM: $\{\Omega_bh^2,\Omega_ch^2,\theta_{\rm MC},\tau,\ln(10^{10}A_s),n_s\}$.

$\Delta\Omega_m=+0.05\sigma$, against $-1.51\sigma$ for $\sigma_8$ \measured: parameter rebalancing is not the source of the shift. It is slower growth.

\subsection{Planck + growth rate: Run D}\label{sec:l2_rund}
Run D adds the seven $f\sigma_8$ points to the Planck likelihood. Its standard parameters agree with Run A to within $0.06\sigma$, and the $\sigma_8$
suppression is stable: $\sigma_8=0.7995\pm0.0058$ (Run D) against $0.7998\pm0.0058$ (Run A). Relative to Run C, $\Delta(\ln A_s)=+0.08\sigma$ and
$\Delta\Omega_m=0.00\sigma$ \measured.

Run D is not, however, a test of the growth rate of the informational term. The coded change acts on the CDM and baryon density equations; CAMB
computes $f\sigma_8$ from the matter velocities, which the change does not touch. With the switch on, CAMB reports $f=0.542$ at $z=0$, while the
density field grows at $f=0.498$, $8.1\,\%$ less; with the switch off the two agree to the $1\,\%$ accuracy of the interpolation
(Table~\ref{tab:l2_growth}) \measured. Matter is conserved, so the velocity follows the density and the physical growth rate is
$f\sigma_8=d\sigma_8/d\ln a$. In that form the coded mechanism gives an $f\sigma_8$ today $6\,\%$ below $\Lambda$CDM at fixed parameters; CAMB's
velocity-based output says $1\,\%$ above. The growth likelihood of Run~D therefore did not see the suppression, and its $\chi^2$ cannot be read as a
test of it. A Planck + growth comparison with $f\sigma_8=-(1+z)\,d\sigma_8/dz$ taken from the density, against a matched $\Lambda$CDM + growth chain on
the same likelihoods, is the next run \openprob. For orientation only: the $z=0.85$ point ($f\sigma_8=0.315\pm0.095$) lies $1.39\sigma$ below the
$\Lambda$CDM value at the Run~A parameters ($0.448$), and no smooth growth history fits it well \calc.

The coupling is fixed at $\beta_m=0.15765$, i.e.\ $\Omega_m=0.3153$. In Run~A, Planck gives $\Omega_m=0.3166\pm0.0065$, so
$\Omega_m/2=0.1583\pm0.0032$: the fixed value is $0.2\sigma$ ($0.4\,\%$) from it \measured. The fixed coupling is consistent with the matter density the
same chain prefers.

\section{The sector-dependent Hubble parameter}\label{sec:l2_hubble}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_Hm}, the ratio of the matter-sector to the photon-sector expansion rate at any redshift is
\begin{equation}
\frac{H_m(a)}{H_\gamma(a)}=\sqrt{1+\frac{\beta_mE(a)H_0^2}{H^2(a)}},
\label{eq:l2_ratio}\end{equation}
and at the present epoch, where $E=1$ and $H=H_0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{H_m(z{=}0)}{H_\gamma(z{=}0)}=\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=\sqrt{1.15765}=1.0759 .
\label{eq:l2_ratio0}\end{equation}
\derived

\subsection{Numerical values from the posterior}
The matter-sector Hubble constant follows in four steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run~A gives $H_0=67.161\pm0.467$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ \measured. This is the photon-sector value: the Hubble constant of the unmodified
Friedmann equation that governs the global background geometry.
\item The coupling is fixed, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.3153/2=0.15765$; the posterior's $\Omega_m=0.3166\pm0.0065$ gives $\Omega_m/2=0.1583\pm0.0032$, $0.2\sigma$
($0.4\,\%$) from the fixed value.
\item The enhancement factor is $\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=\sqrt{1.15765}=1.0759$.
\item The matter-sector Hubble constant is
\begin{equation}
H_0^{(m)}=H_0\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=67.161\times1.0759=72.26\pm0.50\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},
\label{eq:l2_H0m}\end{equation}
with the uncertainty propagated linearly from the $H_0$ posterior \calc.
\end{enumerate}
Against the observations,
\begin{align}

\section{The background test}\label{sec:l2_background}
A natural question is whether the $\beta_mE(a)$ term should modify the background Friedmann equation directly, rather than entering through the
perturbation equations. We test this explicitly. Two exploratory MCMC runs were performed with the IAM term added to CAMB's \texttt{dtauda}
function (the background expansion rate), rather than to the perturbation equations. In this configuration the Friedmann equation becomes
\begin{equation}
H^2(a)=H_0^2\left[\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_ra^{-4}+\Omega_\Lambda+\beta_mE(a)\right].
\label{eq:l2_bg}\end{equation}
The result is $H_0=61.45\pm0.42$ and $61.52\pm0.43$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, $10.9\sigma$ from Planck: the CMB can absorb a background change only by
moving $H_0$ \measured. This large distortion is what the test was built to show. In IAM the background does not change; the term lives in the
entropy functional and enters through the perturbations.

\interp\ In the dual-sector picture the standard $\Lambda$CDM Friedmann equation describes the global spacetime geometry, while the matter-sector
enhancement enters through the effective expansion rate experienced by CDM and baryons in the perturbation equations. The photon sector, propagating
on null geodesics through the unmodified background, sees standard $\Lambda$CDM distances and angular scales.

\subsection{What the analysis shows}
The dual-sector perturbation mechanism, implemented as three Fortran changes to CAMB with no additional free parameter:
\begin{enumerate}
\item reproduces the full Planck likelihood as well as $\Lambda$CDM with no added parameter ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$, likelihood ratio $0.76$);
\item lowers $\sigma_8$ by $0.009$ ($1.1\,\%$, $1.51\sigma$) in the direction reported by weak-lensing surveys, without degrading any CMB observable;
\item gives, from a single posterior, a Hubble-parameter split consistent with both the CMB value ($67.16$, within $0.37\sigma$ of Planck) and the
local value ($72.26$, within $0.75\sigma$ of SH0ES);
\item operates at the perturbation level: placed in the background instead, the same term moves $H_0$ to $61.5$, $10.9\sigma$ from Planck.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the analysis does not show}
It does not show that the Hubble tension is resolved. The dual-sector framework gives a consistent description that accommodates both endpoints, but
IAM's own equations reproduce the Planck data with no added parameter ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$), so at present precision Planck does not yet separate the two
models. The measurement that can separate them is the growth rate at the predicted amplitude, measured from the density field
(Section~\ref{sec:l2_rund}).

\subsection{Relation to the MGCAMB analysis}
Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime} tested the same prediction ($\mu_0=-0.135$, $\Sigma_0=0$) with the MGCAMB parameterisation in twelve chains and four data
combinations. The present analysis extends it by implementing the mechanism directly in the Boltzmann solver. The results agree: $\sigma_8=0.800$--$0.802$
with the informational term in both analyses, from the same $\beta_m$ \measured. The fractional shifts differ, $1.6\,\%$ with MGCAMB's $\mu$ (from
$0.814$, \texttt{plik\_lite}) and $1.1\,\%$ here (from $0.809$, NPIPE CamSpec), because the $\Lambda$CDM baselines and the likelihoods differ. At the
present precision the MGCAMB approximation $\mu=1+\mu_0\,\Omega_{\rm DE}/\Omega_\Lambda$ is adequate \interp.

\subsection{Falsifiability}
The mechanism makes specific predictions that distinguish it from $\Lambda$CDM and from other modified-gravity scenarios \prediction:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mu(z)<1$ with the redshift dependence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_mu}, not a constant offset; the forecast is in
Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}.
\item $\Sigma(z)=1$ at all redshifts. Any detection of $\Sigma\neq1$ falsifies the photon exemption; the forecast with $\Sigma_0$ free is in
Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}.
\item The Hubble split $H_0^{(m)}/H_0^{(\gamma)}=\sqrt{1+\Omega_m/2}$ is a specific, parameter-free prediction, testable with gravitational-wave
standard sirens~\cite{Abbott2017Siren}, whose hosts are galaxies.
\item $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ depend on redshift only, not on wavenumber $k$; scale-dependent growth modifications are excluded.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:l2_conclusions}
A dual-sector perturbation mechanism, in which matter and photons experience different effective expansion rates at late times, has been coded into
the CAMB Boltzmann solver. It requires three changes to the Fortran source and introduces no free parameter beyond standard $\Lambda$CDM.

Against the full Planck likelihood the mechanism reproduces the CMB as well as $\Lambda$CDM with no added parameter ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ between the lowest points)
while lowering $\sigma_8$ from $0.809$ to $0.800$, in the direction favoured by weak-lensing surveys. Every standard cosmological parameter shifts by
less than $0.1\sigma$.

The sector-dependent expansion rate produces a Hubble-parameter split: $H_0^{(\gamma)}=67.16$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ ($0.37\sigma$ from Planck) and
$H_0^{(m)}=72.26$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ ($0.75\sigma$ from SH0ES). The mechanism operates at the perturbation level; the background Friedmann equation
is standard $\Lambda$CDM. The background alternative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_bg}, run as written, is the next test.

The predictions are specific and falsifiable. Euclid and DESI will measure $\mu(z)$ and $\Sigma(z)$ with a precision that bears directly on the
predicted amplitude (Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}); a growth comparison with $f\sigma_8$ from the density field is the next chain to run.

The mechanism is coded into a standard Boltzmann solver with no new parameter. A cosmologist can take the modified source, point it at the next growth data, and see directly whether the matter-sector expansion rate holds.
