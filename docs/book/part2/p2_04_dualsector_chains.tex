\chapter{The matter ledger of the horizon: the dual-sector model and its chains}\label{ch:dual}

\section{One term added to the horizon entropy}

In the Jacobson and Cai--Kim derivations~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005}, the apparent-horizon entropy is geometric, $S_{\mathrm{geo}}=\pi c^5/(G\hbar H^2)$.
IAM adds the accumulated Landauer cost of every irreversible record written on a timelike worldline:
\begin{equation}
 -dE=T_H\,d\!\left(S_{\mathrm{geo}}+S_{\mathrm{info}}\right).
\end{equation}
The information density is taken to grow as $\dot\rho_{\mathrm{info}}=\rho_{\mathrm{info}}H/a$ \conjecture. With $\dot a=aH$ this is
$d\rho_{\mathrm{info}}/da=\rho_{\mathrm{info}}/a^2$, whose solution, normalised to one today, is the activation function \derived:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\;E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)=e^{-z}\;}
 \label{part2:eq:Ea_chains}
\end{equation}
$E\to0$ at early times, $E(1)=1$, and $E\to e$ as $a\to\infty$. Two properties fix where the effect lives in time:
the inflection of $E(a)$ is at $a=1/2$ ($z=1$), and the production per e-fold, $dE/d\ln a=E/a$, peaks exactly at $a=1$ \calc.
Entropy production per e-fold is at its maximum today.
The derivation is written out step by step, with the status of each premise, in Appendix~\ref{app:der:activation}.

\section{Two sectors}

Records are written only on timelike worldlines. A null worldline accumulates no proper time ($d\tau=0$), so it writes none.
Matter feels the information term; light does not. In the standard modified-growth form \derived:
\begin{equation}
 \mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(a)+\beta_m E(a)H_0^2},\qquad \Sigma(a)=1,
 \label{part2:eq:mu}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ multiplies Newton's constant in the growth equation
$\ddot\delta_m+2H\dot\delta_m-\tfrac32\Omega_mH_0^2a^{-3}\mu\,\delta_m=0$ and $\Sigma$ multiplies the lensing potential.

\begin{keybox}[title={The coupling is predicted, not fitted}]
The virial theorem gives the matter coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}). With Planck's $\Omega_m=0.3153$~\cite{Planck2018VI},
$\beta_m=0.15765$ \prediction. This value is \emph{fixed} in every chain below; nothing in $\beta_m$ is sampled.
Left free against the local distance-ladder values read as matter-sector rates (SH0ES $73.04\pm1.04$~\cite{Riess2022}; tip of the red giant branch $70.39\pm1.94$~\cite{Freedman2025}) and seven $f\sigma_8$ points, $\beta$ returns $0.155\pm0.029$ \calc. That interval is the local rate restated, $\beta=(72.45/67.4)^2-1$: the $f\sigma_8$ points alone allow $-0.28\le\beta\le0.47$ (68\,\%) and change by only $\Delta\chi^2=0.08$ between $\beta=0$ and $\Omega_m/2$ \calc. It is therefore not a separate measurement of the coupling; the test of $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ is the growth rate at the predicted amplitude.
\end{keybox}

\begin{checkbox}
\calc\ From Eq.~\eqref{part2:eq:mu} with $\beta_m=0.15765$: $\mu=0.864$ at $z=0$, $0.905$ at $z=0.2$, $0.982$ at $z=1$, $0.998$ at $z=2$.
The coupling deficit $1-\mu(z{=}0)=13.6\%$ is the change in the effective Newton constant for matter today. It is \emph{not} the change in growth:
growth integrates $\mu$ over time, so the growth factor today changes by about $0.8\%$ with the exact coupling and by about $1.7\%$ in the MGCAMB form the
Level~1 chains use, which is why $\sigma_8$ falls by about $1.6\%$ in those chains (next section; Fig.~\ref{fig:s8_inferred}).
The three ways of entering the growth equation and their growth factors are in Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}.
\calc\ The coupling today does not depend on how steeply the record is written. For $E_c(a)=\exp[c(1-1/a)]$, $E_c(1)=1$ for every $c$, so
$\mu(z{=}0)=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.8638$ for any $c$ \derived; over $0.8\le c\le1.2$ the amplitude today moves by $0.0017$ in $\sigma_8$
($0.21\,\%$, same early amplitude) \calc.
\end{checkbox}

\paragraph{Model.} This fit takes Planck's published $H_0=67.4$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ as its fixed photon-sector input; it is a fit to ten
published numbers, not to the chains. The matter-sector rate today is
\begin{equation}
H_0^{\rm (m)}=H_0\sqrt{1+\beta},\label{eq:dual_free_H0m}
\end{equation}
the value at $a=1$ of $H_m^2=H^2+\beta E(a)H_0^2$ with $E(1)=1$ \derived. Growth follows Eq.~\eqref{part2:eq:mu} with $\beta$ in place of $\beta_m$,
on the $\Lambda$CDM background ($\Omega_m=0.3153$), with the same early amplitude as $\Lambda$CDM ($\sigma_8=0.811$ for $\Lambda$CDM); then
$f\sigma_8(z)=f(z)\,\sigma_8(z)$ with $f=d\ln D/d\ln a$. The statistic is $\chi^2=\sum_i[(O_i-P_i)/\sigma_i]^2$.

\paragraph{Result.} \fitted\ Minimising over $\beta$ gives $\beta=0.155^{+0.029}_{-0.029}$ (68\,\%) and ${}^{+0.059}_{-0.058}$ (95\,\%). The entries
split as follows (Fig.~\ref{fig:dual_freecoupling}):

$\beta=0$ ($\Lambda$CDM) & 31.79 & 6.35 & 38.14\\
$\beta=0.155$ (best) & 1.45 & 6.43 & 7.88\\
$\beta=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$ (fixed) & 1.46 & 6.43 & 7.89\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\calc\ The whole difference, $\Delta\chi^2=30.26$, is in the three $H_0$ entries ($30.34$); the seven growth points prefer $\beta=0$ by $0.08$.
SH0ES alone contributes $(73.04-67.4)^2/1.04^2=29.41$ at $\beta=0$; the Planck and SH0ES values differ by $4.89\sigma$. The inverse-variance mean
of the two local values is $72.45\pm0.92$, and $(72.45/67.4)^2-1=0.155$: the best-fit coupling is the local rate restated through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual_free_H0m}. With the coupling fixed at $\Omega_m/2$ and this fixed input, the local rate of the fit is $67.4\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=72.52$, $0.50\sigma$
from SH0ES \calc. The sector rates of this book come from the Level~2 chain: photon sector $67.16\pm0.47$, matter sector
$67.16\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=72.26$, $0.75\sigma$ from SH0ES (Section~\ref{sec:chains}) \measured; with those two values the three $H_0$ entries give
$\chi^2_{H_0}=1.72$ \calc.

\paragraph{What the fit measures.} The penalised criteria $\mathrm{AIC}=\chi^2+2k$~\cite{Akaike1974} and $\mathrm{BIC}=\chi^2+k\ln n$~\cite{Schwarz1978}
($n=10$) charge for added parameters. Counting two (the matter and photon couplings) gives $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=26.26$ and $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=25.65$,
and the Akaike ratio $e^{-\Delta\mathrm{AIC}/2}=2.0\times10^{-6}$; with the coupling fixed at $\Omega_m/2$ nothing is added and
$\Delta\chi^2=30.25$ \calc. These numbers are the distance-ladder tension expressed in the two-sector model. In this data set the Planck value is
held fixed while the local values are free to move with $\beta$, so any term that raises the local rate by $7.5\,\%$ removes the same $\chi^2$;
on the criteria of Kass and Raftery~\cite{KassRaftery1995} the size of the number says how large the tension is, not that the coupling has been
detected \interp. The test of the coupling is the growth rate at the predicted amplitude, and there the seven points do not distinguish $\beta=0$
from $\beta=\Omega_m/2$. On the full Planck likelihood with the coupling fixed, $\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ with no added parameter (Chapter~\ref{ch:level2})
\measured. The photon coupling is bounded separately, on the acoustic scale: $\beta_\gamma<0.0039$ (95\,\%), so photons couple at least
$40\times$ more weakly than matter (Chapter~\ref{ch:dsnote}) \calc.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Level 1, 12 chains (MGCAMB~\cite{Zhao2009MGCAMB,Wang2023MGCAMB}).} Four data sets (Planck; +RSD; +BAO; +Pantheon+), each run three ways: IAM with

\subsection{How the Level 1 chains are configured}\label{sec:chain_config}
\measured\ Every Level~1 chain uses the same Boltzmann setup, read from its Cobaya input file (\texttt{mgcamb\_validation/\allowbreak{}chains/\allowbreak{}*.input.yaml}).
MGCAMB runs in the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ mode (\texttt{MG\_flag}~$=1$, \texttt{pure\_MG\_flag}~$=2$) with the dark-energy-tracking form
(\texttt{musigma\_par}~$=1$), $\mu(a)=1+\mu_0\,\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)/\Omega_\Lambda$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_mgcamb}), with

\section{The test ahead}

\prediction\ The informational term predicts $\mu_0=-0.136$ with $\Sigma=1$: weaker growth of matter with no change in lensing by light. Euclid~\cite{Euclid2025}
