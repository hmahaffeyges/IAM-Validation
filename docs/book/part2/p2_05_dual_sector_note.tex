\chapter{Why matter and light are in different sectors}\label{ch:dsnote}

This chapter lays out the physical reasoning behind IAM's dual-sector structure and the empirical evidence that bears on that separation. No claims are
made here beyond what is established in the other chapters of this Part. The goal is to make the case as plainly as possible.

\section{The boundary Einstein built into the theory}\label{sec:dsn_boundary}
\interp\ Albert Einstein wrote two things that, taken together, define the physical boundary at the center of IAM's dual-sector structure. He did not
connect them. The tools to connect them did not exist in his lifetime. But the boundary was always there, encoded in his own mathematics.

\textbf{The first} is the geodesic equation of general relativity, which Einstein himself noted was an independent postulate introduced alongside the
field equations. He regarded this as one of the imperfections of the original theory: a complete field theory should derive the motion rather than
assume it, and with Infeld and Hoffmann he later derived the motion of bodies from the field equations themselves~\cite{Einstein1938} \observed. The
geodesic equation has two distinct forms, depending on the nature of the worldline:
\begin{align}
\text{Massive particles (matter):}\quad & g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau}=-1,\qquad d\tau>0, \label{eq:dsn_timelike}\\
\text{Massless particles (photons):}\quad & g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}=0,\qquad d\tau=0 \label{eq:dsn_null}
\end{align}
(signature $-+++$, $c=1$; $\lambda$ an affine parameter). This is not an approximation or a convention. It is a fundamental distinction in the causal
structure of spacetime. Matter propagates on timelike worldlines and accumulates proper time. Photons propagate on null worldlines and accumulate none.
The universe treats them differently at the level of the metric itself.

\textbf{The second} is $E=mc^2$~\cite{Einstein1905}. \interp\ Mass is not simply energy with a label attached. Mass is energy bound into a configuration
that moves on timelike worldlines. A massless photon carries energy but no rest mass---it lives on the null boundary. A massive particle carries the same
energy bound into a configuration that accumulates proper time. $E=mc^2$ is a statement relating energy on the two kinds of worldline, written in 1905,
ten years before general relativity formalized the geometric distinction between them.

Einstein found the same boundary twice from different directions without the framework to see that both findings described the same thing.

\begin{keybox}[title={The core claim of IAM's sector split, stated plainly}]
Any physical process that requires a finite proper time interval $\Delta\tau>0$ to complete is inaccessible to photons, for whom $\Delta\tau=0$ exactly.
Gravitational decoherence---the irreversible production of classical information during structure formation---is such a process. Therefore photons
cannot participate in it. Therefore the informational entropy $S_{\rm info}$ that drives IAM's modification is produced exclusively by the matter sector.
The sector split is not imposed on the physics. It follows from Einstein's own geodesic structure combined with the requirement that decoherence is an
irreversible thermodynamic process. \derived\ (given the premise that record production needs $\Delta\tau>0$; Part~\ref{part:1})
\end{keybox}

\section{What the cosmological constant was always missing}\label{sec:dsn_lambda}
Einstein introduced $\Lambda$ to hold the universe static and later abandoned it. But the deeper issue is structural, not merely numerical. $\Lambda$ is a
property of spacetime geometry---uniform, isotropic, time-independent, and sector-blind. It applies identically to null and timelike geodesics because it
lives in the metric, prior to any distinction between matter and radiation.

\interp\ This is the correct description of the vacuum baseline. Quantum vacuum fluctuations produce a constant, time-independent background that maps
directly to $\Lambda$. To that extent $\Lambda$ is not wrong (its size is a separate question, Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda}).

\conjecture\ What $\Lambda$ cannot describe is the thermodynamic consequence of the matter sector's existence. The moment massive particles populate the
universe---the moment there is energy moving on timelike worldlines---a new process begins that $\Lambda$ has no language for. Irreversible decoherence
events start running. Classical information accumulates. The cosmic horizon encodes it. The encoding drives expansion. The expansion dilutes matter
density and throttles further collapse. This entire feedback loop is invisible to a geometric constant written before the timelike sector existed.

\begin{keybox}[title={$\Lambda$CDM and the record term}]
$\Lambda$CDM is what you get when you apply the null geodesic description uniformly to a universe that contains matter. It works remarkably well because
the informational term $\beta_mE(a)$ is smooth and its integrated effect resembles a slowly varying constant over the redshift range where data has
historically been most precise. But it cannot explain why dark energy dominates at approximately the same epoch as structure formation peaks---because a
true constant has no mechanism connecting it to when matter does things. In IAM the informational pressure \emph{is driven by} structure formation, so it
dominates when matter is most active. The coincidence is causal, not accidental. \conjecture
\end{keybox}

\interp\ The $\Lambda$ problem was never that Einstein made an error. It is that the tools to complete the story of $E=mc^2$---to follow the timelike
sector's thermodynamic consequences all the way to their cosmological implications---did not arrive until Landauer (1961)~\cite{Landauer1961},
Bekenstein--Hawking (1973--1975)~\cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}, Jacobson (1995)~\cite{Jacobson1995}, and the decoherence framework of Zurek
(1980s)~\cite{Zurek1981,Zurek1982} had all been published. Each tool was developed independently, in separate fields, for separate purposes. IAM is
what happens when you follow all of them to the same conclusion simultaneously.

\section{The sector split in IAM: physics, not assumption}\label{sec:dsn_firstlaw}
IAM's modification of the Jacobson--Cai--Kim first law~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005} is
\begin{equation}
-dE=T_H\,d\!\left(S_{\rm geo}+S_{\rm info}\right), \label{eq:dsn_firstlaw}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\rm geo}=k_BA_H/(4\ell_P^2)$ is the standard Bekenstein--Hawking geometric entropy of the horizon area $A_H$ ($A_H/4G$ in units
$\hbar=c=k_B=1$; one nat per $4\ell_P^2$, one bit per $4\ln2\,\ell_P^2$) and $S_{\rm info}$ is the cumulative classical information produced by
gravitational decoherence, encoded on the horizon at Landauer cost $k_BT_H\ln2$ per bit.

The sector split enters through a single physical requirement:
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm info}>0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad d\tau>0 . \label{eq:dsn_iff}
\end{equation}
Photons have $d\tau=0$ exactly. For them, $S_{\rm info}=0$, the first law reduces to its standard form, and standard GR is recovered. This is not a
special case or an exception built into the framework. It is the direct consequence of requiring that decoherence---an irreversible thermodynamic
process---needs finite proper time to run \derived.

The mapping to the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ modified gravity parametrization follows directly:
\begin{align}
\mu(a)&=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2}<1\qquad\text{(matter sector: suppressed growth)}, \label{eq:dsn_mu}\\
\Sigma(a)&=1\qquad\text{(photon sector: standard GR, exact)}, \label{eq:dsn_sigma}
\end{align}
with the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ derived from integrating the decoherence constraint (Appendix~\ref{app:der:activation}), and the coupling
constant $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$ derived from the virial theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial})---zero free parameters beyond $\Lambda$CDM \derived. At
$z=0$, $\mu=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.864$.

\observed\ The combination $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$ exactly is not unique to IAM in the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ parameter space. In $f(R)$ gravity, in the
quasi-static regime, lensing is unmodified ($\Sigma=1$) and $\mu$ lies between $1$ and $4/3$~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2008}. In DGP gravity light deflection
is also unmodified; the self-accelerating branch has weaker effective gravity, $\mu<1$, but carries a ghost~\cite{Koyama2007}, and the normal branch has
$\mu>1$. Generic Horndeski theories predict correlated deviations in both~\cite{Amendola2018}. What is specific to IAM is the sign and redshift form of
$\mu(a)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsn_mu}, with its amplitude fixed in advance by $\Omega_m/2$, so that $\mu(0)=0.864$ is a prediction, not a fit. IAM occupies a
region of parameter space that current growth data do not disfavour (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}).

\section{Summary}\label{sec:dsn_summary}
\interp\ The dual-sector structure of IAM is not an assumption layered onto general relativity. It is the consequence of taking seriously two things
Einstein already wrote:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Massive particles and photons follow fundamentally different geodesics---timelike ($d\tau>0$) and null ($d\tau=0$) respectively. This distinction is
in the field equations of 1915--1916~\cite{Einstein1915,Einstein1916}.
\item $E=mc^2$ relates energy bound into matter, which moves on timelike worldlines, to energy carried on null worldlines by radiation. This relation was
written in 1905~\cite{Einstein1905}.
\end{enumerate}
Any thermodynamic process requiring finite proper time---including gravitational decoherence, the irreversible production of classical information, and
its Landauer encoding on the cosmic horizon---is accessible only to the timelike sector. Photons, for whom $d\tau=0$, cannot participate. The sector split
follows from the causal structure of spacetime itself.

$\Lambda$CDM applies the null geodesic description uniformly to a universe that contains matter. IAM follows $E=mc^2$ to its thermodynamic conclusion.
The empirical record---seventeen converged MCMC chains consistent with Planck at $\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$, 1590 Pantheon+ light curves (full covariance) whose distances follow
$\Lambda$CDM geometry as the split requires, the Planck posterior consistent with the fixed $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ at $0.2\sigma$, and the CMB bounding the
photon coupling at $\beta_\gamma<0.0052$---is consistent with the dual-sector description. The physical argument explains why the split is expected; the
numbers decide whether it holds, and Euclid, DESI Year~5 and CMB-S4 will test it further.

The split is stated sharply enough to be tested from both sides. A cosmologist with the public chains can rerun the bound on $\beta_\gamma$ today and set $\mu_0$ against the next survey release when it arrives.
