\chapter{Records at the quantum scale}\label{ch:quantumrecords}

\section*{The place}
IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}): every irreversible transition from a quantum superposition to a classical record pays at least $k_BT\ln2$ per bit,
at the temperature of the surface where the record is written. Earlier chapters followed the law outward to the cosmic horizon. This chapter follows it
down to where records begin --- the quantum-to-classical transition itself --- and back up again, to show that the cosmic ledger $E(a)$ is the sum of
those transitions.

The question that opens the chapter is whether quantum physics can be trusted at cosmological scales as the driver of a decoherence mechanism. It is
the foundational gap between the laboratory and the cosmology, and the chapter is written to answer it. The answer has three parts. Zurek's program
says how records form and become objective. IAM's Law says what each record costs and where it is written. Gravitational collapse, which cosmology
already calculates, sets how fast records are written. The quantum step turns out to be local and instantaneous; the cosmological step is classical and
slow.

\section{What decoherence explains, and what it leaves open}\label{sec:qr_zurek}
The question of how classical reality emerges from quantum mechanics has occupied physicists for a century. The measurement problem --- why quantum
superpositions give way to definite classical outcomes --- resists resolution within the standard formalism. Zurek's program of decoherence and quantum
Darwinism~\cite{Zurek1981,Zurek1991,Zurek2003,Zurek2009} is the most complete account of how classical reality emerges from quantum laws. Its central
insight is that classicality is not a property of systems in isolation but of systems entangled with their environments. Environmental monitoring
selects preferred pointer states~\cite{Zurek1981} by einselection, the environment-induced superselection that suppresses all but the most robust quantum
states. Classical information about the pointer states is then redundantly encoded across many independent environmental fragments, so that many
observers can confirm the same outcome without disturbing the system~\cite{Zurek2009}. This is quantum Darwinism: the survival of the fittest pointer
states through redundant environmental encoding. The framework is well supported by experiment and analysis~\cite{JoosZeh1985,Schlosshauer2005}. It
explains why definite outcomes are observed, why preferred bases exist, and why different observers agree on classical reality. It does not say what a
record costs, or where it is ultimately kept.

\paragraph{Einselection and pointer states.} For a quantum system $S$ interacting with an environment $\mathcal E$, the total state evolves unitarily:
\begin{equation}
|\Psi\rangle_{S\mathcal E}=\sum_i c_i\,|s_i\rangle_S\otimes|e_i\rangle_{\mathcal E}.\label{eq:qr_SE}
\end{equation}
\derived{} (unitary evolution of a system and its environment). Tracing over the environment suppresses the off-diagonal elements of the reduced density
matrix of $S$ on a decoherence time $\tau_D$. Not all states decohere equally. States that commute with the interaction Hamiltonian --- the pointer
states --- survive environmental monitoring while their superpositions are suppressed~\cite{Zurek1981}. Einselection promotes the pointer states to the
status of classical reality. For a system of mass $M$ in a spatial superposition of size $\Delta x$, coupled to a thermal environment,
\begin{equation}
\tau_D\sim\tau_R\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm th}}{\Delta x}\right)^2,\qquad\lambda_{\rm th}=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2Mk_BT}},\label{eq:qr_tauD}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_R$ is the relaxation time and $\lambda_{\rm th}$ the thermal de Broglie wavelength~\cite{Zurek2003,JoosZeh1985}. \derived{} For macroscopic
objects $\Delta x\gg\lambda_{\rm th}$ and $\tau_D\to0$: decoherence is essentially instantaneous. For one gram at 300\,K, $\lambda_{\rm th}=3.7\times
10^{-23}$\,m, and a superposition of 1\,cm gives $\tau_D/\tau_R\sim10^{-41}$. \calc

\paragraph{Redundant encoding.} Classical objectivity requires more than decoherence: many independent observers must agree on the outcome without
disturbing the system. In quantum Darwinism this objectivity arises because environmental fragments independently carry redundant copies of the
information about the pointer state~\cite{Zurek2009}. The redundancy $R_\delta$ --- the number of independent fragments each carrying all but a fraction
$\delta$ of the information about the pointer state --- is the quantitative measure of classicality. High redundancy means the pointer state is
objectively classical; low redundancy means it remains quantum. \derived{} (definition within quantum Darwinism).

\paragraph{The cosmic frontier.} The framework is local by construction: a system, its environment, observers reading fragments, complete within its
intended domain. Zurek's environment is always local --- a laboratory, a heat bath, a radiation field. Integrated over the whole universe across
13.8\,Gyr of gravitational structure formation, three questions follow. They are not gaps in that framework but extensions beyond its scope, and they
need a bridge to horizon thermodynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
\item When records are integrated over all structure formation, what is the ultimate environment, the last repository of all redundantly encoded
information?
\item What sets the rate of quantum-to-classical transitions across physical scales, from atoms to the cosmic horizon?
\item What are the observable consequences of the accumulated ledger, the universe's record of every pointer state selected?
\end{enumerate}
The questions become tractable once the local framework is joined to the Jacobson and Cai--Kim horizon thermodynamics~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). Figure~\ref{fig:qd_local_cosmic} shows the correspondence: every element of the local picture has a counterpart at cosmic
scale. Quantum Darwinism is the foundation; the cosmic horizon is the last boundary; IAM's Law, with the virial half setting how much bound energy writes,
is the rule that joins them.

\section{The answer IAM's Law gives}\label{sec:qr_answer}
IAM's Law answers both open points. Each irreversible record costs $k_BT\ln2$ per bit, paid at the nearest encoding surface. A record in a detector is
paid at the detector; a record made by a collapsing cloud of matter is paid at the largest surface that matter shares, the cosmic horizon at
$T_{GH}=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$~\cite{GibbonsHawking1977}. Every gravitational record --- every virialization of a dark matter halo, every stellar collapse,
every black hole formation --- is a quantum-to-classical transition written at Landauer cost~\cite{Landauer1961}. Summed over the history of structure
formation, the records written at the horizon are the accumulated entropy $S_{\rm info}$, and its ledger is the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). Zurek's environment, followed to its largest scale, ends at the horizon. \interp

\section{The virial half: how much bound energy writes}\label{sec:qr_virial}
IAM's Law is stated first; the virial theorem is one instance of it, the rule that fixes how much of a bound system's energy is available to write
(Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}). For any potential $V\propto r^{-n}$, the virial theorem for a bound system in equilibrium states
\begin{equation}
2\langle K\rangle=n\,\langle|V|\rangle .\label{eq:qr_virial_n}
\end{equation}
For the gravitational and Coulomb potentials, $n=1$:
\begin{equation}
2K=|V|,\qquad K=\tfrac12|V| .\label{eq:qr_virial_1}
\end{equation}
\derived{} (Clausius 1870~\cite{Clausius1870}; for a circular orbit in $V=-kr^{-n}$, $mv^2/r=nkr^{-n-1}$ gives $2K/|V|=n$ directly). This is exact for
all $1/r$ potentials in equilibrium. It holds for the hydrogen atom and for the cosmological coupling constant, because both are governed by $1/r$
potentials in three spatial dimensions. Table~\ref{tab:virial_domains} and Figure~\ref{fig:virial_domains} (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}) give the
one-half across the systems in which $1/r$ binding has been tested, from the hydrogen atom to the cosmic horizon. The cosmological entry is a prediction
fixed in every chain, not a measurement of the ratio.

In IAM the kinetic half of each bound structure is the share of its binding energy that is written as irreversible record, priced at $k_BT\ln2$ per bit
at the surface where it is written; the other half remains in the configuration. Dividing the kinetic half by the price of a bit counts the records a
virialization writes. Applied to the matter of the universe, this fixes the coupling:
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.15765 .\label{eq:qr_betam}
\end{equation}
\derived{} (given IAM's Law and the virial partition; $\Omega_m=0.3153$). $\beta_m$ is held fixed at this value in every chain (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial});
it is not a posterior.

\paragraph{From the hydrogen atom to the cosmic horizon, in four steps.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Atomic.} For the Coulomb $1/r$ potential in hydrogen the virial theorem gives $T=\tfrac12|U|$: half of the binding energy is kinetic
(electron motion, classical after einselection), half is the electron--nucleus configuration. \derived
\item \emph{Gravitational.} For the gravitational $1/r$ potential of a virialized dark matter halo the same theorem gives $K=\tfrac12|U|$: half of the
gravitational energy is in the motion of the matter, half in its configuration. \derived
\item \emph{Informational.} The kinetic half, divided by the price of a bit $k_BT_H\ln2$, counts the records each virialization writes at the surface
where they are written --- quantum superpositions converted to classical outcomes. \interp
\item \emph{Cosmological.} Applied to the matter density, the share is $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:qr_betam}). \derived{} (given steps 1--3)
\end{enumerate}
The same theorem, $2K=|V|$ for $1/r$ potentials, governs the kinetic energy of the electron in hydrogen and the coupling of matter to the cosmological
record. In Zurek's language: the share of bound energy available to the quantum-to-classical transitions is one half at every scale of $1/r$ binding.

\section{Collapse sets the rate}\label{sec:qr_rate}
Can the quantum-to-classical transition be trusted to drive anything at cosmological scale? The answer turns on time scales. For a virialized halo of
mass $M$ and virial radius $R_{\rm vir}$, the gravitational self-energy sets a decoherence time (the Di\'osi--Penrose criterion~\cite{Diosi1989,Penrose1996})
\begin{equation}
\tau_D\sim\frac{\hbar R_{\rm vir}}{GM^2}.\label{eq:qr_tauhalo}
\end{equation}
For a Milky Way halo ($10^{12}M_\odot$, $200$\,kpc) this is
\begin{equation}
\tau_D\simeq2.5\times10^{-87}\ {\rm s},\label{eq:qr_tauMW}
\end{equation}
about $10^{104}$ times shorter than the Hubble time. \calc{} Environmental decoherence by photons and particles~\cite{JoosZeh1985} is faster still;
either way the transition is instantaneous on any cosmological time scale. In Zurek's language: the pointer states of gravitationally collapsed
structures are selected essentially at the moment of virialization. The rate-limiting step is not the quantum decoherence itself, which is
instantaneous, but the rate at which new pointer states are created --- the rate of virialization, set by the gravitational collapse time, of order
$H^{-1}$. The quantum step is therefore local and immediate, and the rate at which records are written is set by something cosmology already calculates:
the rate of gravitational collapse. \derived{} (given Eq.~\ref{eq:qr_tauhalo})

\paragraph{One record per particle.} The simplest count takes the information written per virialization as proportional to the number of particles
selected into a classical configuration, $N_p\propto M/m_p$. The rate per unit volume is then
\begin{equation}
\dot I=\int\frac{dn}{dM}\,\frac{M}{m_p}\,H(a)\,\frac{d\ln F_{\rm coll}}{d\ln a}\,dM,\label{eq:qr_Idot_particle}
\end{equation}
with $dn/dM$ the Press--Schechter mass function~\cite{PressSchechter1974} and $F_{\rm coll}$ the collapsed fraction. In matter domination, with peak
height $\nu=\delta_c/[\sigma(M)D(a)]$, $\delta_c=1.686$, and a threshold mass $M_{\min}$, $F_{\rm coll}={\rm erfc}(\nu_{\min}/\sqrt2)$ and the count is
exact:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dF_{\rm coll}}{d\ln D}=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\nu_{\min}\,e^{-\nu_{\min}^2/2},\qquad
n_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{d\ln(dF_{\rm coll}/d\ln D)}{d\ln D}=\nu_{\min}^2-1 .\label{eq:qr_neff_nu}
\end{equation}
\derived{} (sympy, \texttt{verify\_records\_measurement\_time.py} A5). The exponent is set by the threshold: $n_{\rm eff}=7/2$ at $\nu_{\min}=2.12$ and
$5/2$ at $\nu_{\min}=1.87$ (Figure~\ref{fig:qd_exponent}b), and since $\nu\propto1/D$ it drifts with time. The integral
$\int_0^\infty\nu^2e^{-\nu^2/2}d\nu=\sqrt{\pi/2}$ is a pure number and carries no power of $D$. \calc{} The particle count needs an arbitrary threshold
and does not carry the Landauer factor $1/T_H$, which prices energy, not particles; the next section uses the energy instead.

\section{The cosmic horizon as the last environment}\label{sec:qr_horizon}
Quantum Darwinism establishes that classical information is redundantly encoded in environmental fragments. It assumes a local environment and does not
ask where all the redundantly encoded information finally goes. IAM's Law gives the answer: to the nearest encoding surface, and for records that matter
shares on cosmic scales, to the cosmological apparent horizon. Through the Cai--Kim first law~\cite{CaiKim2005} and the covariant entropy
bound~\cite{Bousso2002}, irreversible entropy production in the bulk is encoded at the horizon at a cost $k_BT_H\ln2$ per bit, $T_H=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$
the Gibbons--Hawking temperature~\cite{GibbonsHawking1977}. Every pointer state selected by gravitational einselection contributes one entry to the
cosmic ledger. \interp{} The accumulated ledger is the activation function
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)\label{eq:qr_Ea}
\end{equation}
(Eq.~\ref{part2:eq:Ea}). In Zurek's language: $E(a)$ is exponentially small at early times, before stable pointer states of collapsed structure exist;
it rises monotonically as records accumulate; $E(1)=1$ is the ledger today; and $E\to e$ as $a\to\infty$, an asymptote never reached. \derived{} (properties
of Eq.~\ref{eq:qr_Ea}). Because each record is irreversible, the ledger only grows: this is the arrow of time in Zurek's language, set out in
Chapter~\ref{ch:time}. \interp

\paragraph{The sector split from null geodesics.} In Zurek's framework pointer states are selected by the interaction Hamiltonian with the environment.
Massive particles on timelike worldlines ($d\tau>0$) accumulate proper time and take part in gravitational records, each paid at Landauer cost. Photons
on null geodesics ($d\tau=0$) accumulate no proper time and write no gravitational record in flight. This is the origin of the sector split:
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2}<1,\qquad\Sigma(a)=1\label{eq:qr_mu}
\end{equation}
(Eq.~\ref{part2:eq:mu}). \derived{} Matter on timelike worldlines feels a modified effective coupling because it writes records; light on null geodesics
does not, so $\Sigma=1$ exactly, with no free parameter. Here $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ keep their meaning as the cosmological modified-growth and lensing
functions; they are not labels for the state of a laboratory system.

\section{Fundamental particles as stable records}\label{sec:qr_particles}
Quantum Darwinism explains why some states survive environmental monitoring. It does not say what sets the masses of the most stable pointer states, the
fundamental particles. The proposal is a fixed-point condition. A particle of mass $m$ is assigned its reduced Compton sphere, $\bar\lambda_C=\hbar/mc$,
with the area count of a horizon of that size,
\begin{equation}
S=\frac{4\pi\bar\lambda_C^2}{4\lP^2}=\pi\left(\frac{\mP}{m}\right)^2 ,\label{eq:qr_Scompton}
\end{equation}
\conjecture{} (the area law applied to the Compton sphere; it is not saturation of Bekenstein's bound, which gives $S\le2\pi$ here,
Chapter~\ref{ch:electronmass}). The Landauer cost of keeping this record at the Gibbons--Hawking temperature $T_{GH}=\hbar H_0/2\pi k_B$ is
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm L}=S\,k_BT_{GH}\ln2=\pi\left(\frac{\mP}{m}\right)^2\frac{\hbar H_0\ln2}{2\pi}.\label{eq:qr_ELcompton}
\end{equation}
\calc{} For the electron, $S=1.79\times10^{45}$ and $k_BT_{GH}\ln2=2.54\times10^{-53}$\,J ($H_0=67.4$), so $E_{\rm L}=4.6\times10^{-8}$\,J, $5.6\times10^5$
times $m_ec^2$: the full area count, priced bit by bit, does not by itself equal the rest energy. \calc{} The fixed point that does close is set out in
Chapter~\ref{ch:electronmass}: a cell count $(\mP/m)^{3/2}$ and an electromagnetic factor $\alpha^{5/2}$, both assumed, give a mass that agrees with the
measured electron mass within the $0.3\,\%$ set by the uncertainty of $H_0$. \conjecture{} In Zurek's language the electron would then be the most stable
pointer state in nature: the mass at which the cost of keeping its record equals its rest energy. \interp

\section{Black holes as local encoding surfaces}\label{sec:qr_bh}
Black holes saturate the Bekenstein--Hawking bound~\cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}: they hold the most information per unit area of any surface. In the
language of quantum Darwinism they are the most efficient record-keepers in nature, and their Hawking radiation is the environment into which they
redundantly encode. \interp{} The black-body throughput of a horizon, the Hawking power divided by the price of a bit at the Hawking temperature, is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm BH}=\frac{P}{k_BT_{\rm BH}\ln2}=\frac{c^3}{1920\,GM\ln2}\ {\rm bits\,s^{-1}},\label{eq:qr_GammaBH}
\end{equation}
152.6\,bits\,s$^{-1}$ for one solar mass (Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}). \derived{} (Stefan--Boltzmann law for the Hawking spectrum; the radiation's own
entropy rate, $\tfrac43P/T$, is $4/3$ of this, 203.4\,bits\,s$^{-1}$ \calc).

\paragraph{Two horizons.} The black-hole horizon, hot and local, $T_{\rm BH}=\hbar c^3/8\pi GMk_B$, is the immediate encoding surface; the cosmic
horizon, cold and global, $T_H=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$, is the largest. The hot surface writes fast; the cold surface writes slowly. \interp{} The energy
identity of a Schwarzschild horizon is
\begin{equation}
E=T_{\rm BH}S_{\rm BH}=\tfrac12Mc^2 ,\label{eq:qr_smarr}
\end{equation}
the same whether $S$ is counted in nats with $k_BT$ per nat or in bits with $k_BT\ln2$ per bit: the Smarr relation $Mc^2=2T_{\rm BH}S_{\rm BH}$~\cite{Smarr1973}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}). \derived{} (sympy, A9: $T_{\rm BH}S_{\rm BH}/Mc^2=1/2$ for every $M$). The black hole carries the virial half.

\section{What quantum Darwinism and IAM's Law each leave open}\label{sec:qr_open}
Quantum Darwinism does not fully resolve the measurement problem --- why a particular outcome occurs in a single run. It explains why classical
alternatives are available and agreed on by many observers. IAM's Law leaves that an open problem too. What it adds is the cosmic accounting: the Landauer
cost of each quantum-to-classical transition is paid at the surface where it is written, for collapsed matter ultimately the cosmic horizon; the
accumulated cost is $E(a)$; and the macroscopic consequence is a modified growth history that surveys can measure. The measurement problem becomes, in
this limited sense, observationally accessible. \interp{} The definition of a measurement that IAM's Law does give, and how it meets the laboratory, is
the subject of Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement}. The universe has been paying the thermodynamic price of its own classicality, one record at a time; the
arrow of time is the accumulated receipt (Chapter~\ref{ch:time}). \interp

\section{Light writes nothing in flight}
A photon travels a null geodesic, $d\tau=0$. It accumulates no proper time and makes no gravitational record in transit; its record is written where it
is absorbed, in matter. This is the sector split seen from the quantum side: matter on timelike worldlines writes, light does not, and so $\mu<1$ for
matter while $\Sigma=1$ for light. \derived

\paragraph{Entanglement.} IAM's Law changes nothing in quantum mechanics. An entangled pair is one quantum state, and its correlations are those of that
state --- $|S|=2\sqrt2$ for an ideal source~\cite{Tsirelson1980} --- until an irreversible record is written. Photon pairs have been distributed over 1,200\,km by satellite
(Yin et al.\ 2017~\cite{Yin2017}); electron spins 1.3\,km apart have violated a Bell inequality without loopholes, entangled by interfering their spontaneously emitted
photons (Hensen et al.\ 2015~\cite{Hensen2015}). \observed\ Both are consistent with standard decoherence. The second shows a point the law makes precise: an emitted photon is not yet
a record, and its which-path information can still be erased by measuring it in a conjugate basis; the record is written, and the cost paid, where it is
absorbed. \interp

For a massive object held in a spatial superposition, the gravitational self-energy $E_G\simeq Gm^2/R$ sets the scale of any gravitational channel.

with the boundary's capacity taken as $k_BT/E_G$, gives
\begin{equation}\tau_{\rm IAM}=\frac{\hbar\,(k_BT)^2\ln2}{E_G^3}\;\propto\;T^2m^{-5}\quad(\text{fixed density}),\qquad\tau_{\rm PD}\propto m^{-5/3}.\label{eq:qr_tauIAM}\end{equation}

quadruples $\tau_{\rm IAM}$; the two times cross at $m\approx2.2\times10^{-10}$\,kg. The two proposals therefore differ by nearly eight orders of magnitude at
$10^{-12}$\,kg (a nanogram), and in their dependence on temperature. \calc{} A higher bath temperature lengthening a coherence time runs against every environmental channel,
which makes the $T^2$ law both the sharpest test and the most exposed claim.
