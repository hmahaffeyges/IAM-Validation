\chapter{Three cluster masses}\label{ch:threeway}

\section*{The place}
The same clusters as Chapter~\ref{ch:lensdyn}, read three ways: by the X-ray gas held in hydrostatic equilibrium, by the Sunyaev--Zeldovich (SZ)
distortion that the hot electrons imprint on the CMB, and by the weak lensing of background galaxies. The first two answer to the potential that
matter feels; the third to the potential that light feels.

\section*{Summary}
In the dual-sector picture the gravitational decoherence term renormalises the effective expansion rate that governs matter perturbations, while the
photon sector and the lensing relations are unchanged: $\mu(a)<1$ and $\Sigma(a)=1$ at linear order. This chapter asks what that implies for the three
cluster mass estimators. In the Level~1 form, where $\mu$ multiplies Newton's constant for matter, the hydrostatic and SZ masses are low by $\mu$ and
the lensing mass is the true mass, so the lensing-to-hydrostatic ratio is $R(z)=1/\mu(z)$, with a negative redshift slope, $dR/dz=-0.18$ at $z=0.3$,
opposite in sign to the slope of non-thermal pressure support. In the Level~2 form the Weyl potential and light geodesics are unmodified, the offset
between lensing peaks and gas follows the same collisionless dynamics as in $\Lambda$CDM, $R=1$, and clusters see the term only through the growth
history that sets their abundance and merger timing. No parameter beyond those of $\Lambda$CDM is introduced in either form.

\section{Three estimators}\label{sec:tw_estimators}
Galaxy clusters are the most massive gravitationally bound structures. Their masses can be estimated through several independent physical channels,
each sensitive to a different part of the gravitational field. In general relativity all of them converge on the same true mass once systematics are
accounted for. A systematic difference between estimators that depends on whether the estimator probes the matter sector or the photon sector would be a
direct signal of a departure from general relativity in the sector split. \interp
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{X-ray hydrostatic mass.} The temperature and density profiles of the intracluster medium (ICM) give the pressure gradient needed for
hydrostatic equilibrium, which balances the gravitational acceleration felt by the gas. It is a matter-sector estimator.
\item \emph{SZ mass.} Inverse Compton scattering of CMB photons off hot ICM electrons produces a spectral distortion proportional to
$\int n_eT_e\,dl$. SZ masses come from a $Y$--$M$ scaling relation calibrated on hydrostatic X-ray masses, so they probe the same matter-sector
acceleration.
\item \emph{Weak-lensing mass.} The coherent distortion of background galaxy shapes by the cluster measures the lensing potential $\Phi+\Psi$. It is a
photon-sector estimator.
\end{enumerate}
The hydrostatic mass bias, lensing masses above the hydrostatically calibrated Planck masses by about $20$--$45\,\%$ in the
calibrations of the Planck masses and above X-ray hydrostatic masses by about $5\,\%$ in the LoCuSS sample at $0.15<z<0.3$ (Table~\ref{tab:ld_published}), has been attributed mainly to non-thermal pressure from turbulence and bulk
motion~\cite{Nagai2007Xray,Nelson2014}. \observed\ The dual-sector model offers a complementary physical reading: part of the bias is gravitational,
from the weaker matter coupling $\mu<1$. The two parts differ in their redshift dependence. Non-thermal pressure support evolves weakly and positively
with redshift; the gravitational part follows $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$, which falls from its present value toward zero at high redshift. The sign of the
redshift slope is the discriminant. \conjecture

\section{The perturbation mechanism}\label{sec:tw_mechanism}
The dual-sector term enters the matter perturbation equations as a single change to the Hubble friction. In the modified CAMB Fortran implementation
(Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}), in the code's units ($8\pi G=1$, conformal time), the matter-sector expansion rate that enters the CDM and baryon equations
of motion is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dot a}{a}\bigg|_{\rm matter}=a\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\rm tot}+\beta_mE(a)\,\rho_0}{3}}\,,
\label{eq:tw_adot}\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\rm tot}$ is the total energy density and $\rho_0$ the present critical density; in physical units this is
$H_m^2=H^2+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:l2_Hm}). \derived\ (unit conversion) The activation function is
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right),
\label{eq:tw_E}\end{equation}
obtained from the gravitational decoherence rate integrated over the cosmic apparent horizon~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005} (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}),
and the coupling is
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.15765,
\label{eq:tw_beta}\end{equation}
set by the virial partition for $1/r$ potentials in three spatial dimensions (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}). \prediction\ The photon-sector equations are
untouched: $\Sigma=1$, because photons travel on null geodesics, along which no proper time elapses, and so undergo no gravitational
decoherence. \interp

The effective matter coupling at $a=1/(1+z)$ is the ratio of the $\Lambda$CDM Hubble rate squared to the matter-sector rate squared,
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_\Lambda(a)}{H^2_\Lambda(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\qquad H^2_\Lambda(a)=H_0^2(\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_\Lambda),
\label{eq:tw_mu}\end{equation}
a mapping onto the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ form (Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}). \interp\ The 18 converged chains fix $\beta_m$ and test this prediction
against the Planck 2018 likelihood (Chapter~\ref{ch:dual}); they do not fit it. At $a=1$, $\mu=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.8638$: the matter coupling is
$13.62\,\%$ below unity. \calc\ That number is the coupling, not the growth suppression, which is $0.78\,\%$ in the linear growth factor today
(Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}).

\section{The three-way ratio in the Level 1 form}\label{sec:tw_ratio}
\subsection{Mass estimators in modified gravity}
\textbf{Step 1 (hydrostatic mass).} In hydrostatic equilibrium the pressure gradient of the gas balances the gravitational acceleration it feels,
$dP/dr=-\rho_g\,g(r)$. An observer who assumes general relativity writes $g=GM_{\rm hydro}(<r)/r^2$. In the Level~1 form the gas feels
$g=\mu\,GM_{\rm true}(<r)/r^2$, so
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm hydro}=\mu\,M_{\rm true}.
\label{eq:tw_mhydro}\end{equation}
With $\mu<1$ hydrostatic masses systematically underestimate the true mass. \derived\ (Level~1 form)

\textbf{Step 2 (SZ mass).} The SZ mass is calibrated on hydrostatic masses through the $Y$--$M$ relation, so at leading order it inherits the same factor:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm SZ}\approx\mu\,M_{\rm true}.
\label{eq:tw_msz}\end{equation}
\derived\ (Level~1 form)

\textbf{Step 3 (lensing mass).} Because $\Sigma=1$, the lensing mass recovers the true mass:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm lens}=\Sigma\,M_{\rm true}=M_{\rm true}.
\label{eq:tw_mlens}\end{equation}
\derived\ (Level~1 form)

\subsection{The three-way prediction}
The ratio of lensing mass to hydrostatic mass is
\begin{equation}
R(z)\equiv\frac{M_{\rm lens}}{M_{\rm hydro}}=\frac{1}{\mu(z)},
\label{eq:tw_R}\end{equation}
the curve of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ld_ratio}. \derived\ (Level~1 form) In general relativity $R=1$; in the Level~1 form $R>1$, with a redshift dependence set by
$E(a)$ and $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ alone. The SZ-to-X-ray ratio is
\begin{equation}
\frac{M_{\rm SZ}}{M_{\rm hydro}}\approx\frac{\mu}{\mu}=1,
\label{eq:tw_szx}\end{equation}
and the ordering of the three estimators is
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm lens}>M_{\rm SZ}\approx M_{\rm hydro}.
\label{eq:tw_order}\end{equation}
\calc\ Of these, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tw_szx} holds by construction: the $Y$--$M$ relation is calibrated on hydrostatic masses, so the ratio is close to unity
in any theory of gravity and cannot test the sector split (Section~\ref{sec:tw_sz}). The first inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tw_order} is shared with
non-thermal pressure, which also lowers both matter-sector estimators. What non-thermal pressure cannot give is a lensing-to-hydrostatic ratio that
falls with redshift following $E(a)$. The test is therefore the redshift slope. \calc

\section{The redshift slope}\label{sec:tw_slope}
The discriminant between the gravitational part and non-thermal pressure support is the sign of $dR/dz$.

\emph{Level~1 form:} $dR/dz=-0.183$ at $z=0.3$ ($-0.31$ at $z=0$, $-0.12$ at $z=0.5$). As $z$ increases, $E(a)$ falls, $\mu\to1$ and $R\to1$: the
slope is negative. \calc

\emph{Non-thermal pressure:} higher-redshift clusters are less relaxed, so non-thermal pressure support is larger and hydrostatic masses are more
biased~\cite{Nelson2014,ShiKomatsu2014}: the slope is positive. \observed\ (simulations)

The two contributions have opposite signs. Writing $M_{\rm hydro}=(1-b)M_{\rm true}$, the two factors multiply,
\begin{equation}
1-b_{\rm hydro}(z)=\bigl[1-b_{\rm NT}(z)\bigr]\bigl[1-b_{\rm IAM}(z)\bigr],\qquad b_{\rm IAM}(z)=1-\mu(z),
\label{eq:tw_bias}\end{equation}
so that to first order the biases add, $b_{\rm hydro}\simeq b_{\rm NT}+b_{\rm IAM}$; at $z=0.3$ the cross term is $0.014$ for the factor used below.
\derived\ $b_{\rm NT}$ increases weakly with $z$ and $b_{\rm IAM}$ decreases with $z$ following $E(a)$, so the total flattens as the gravitational
part fades. \calc

For illustration take a non-thermal factor $C_{\rm NT}=1+0.20(1+z)^{0.2}$, multiplying $R$; its slope is $+0.027$ to $+0.036$ over $0.15<z<0.65$
\calc, inside the range $+0.02$ to $+0.04$ suggested by simulations~\cite{Nelson2014,ShiKomatsu2014}. The form and its normalisation are assumed here; fitting them to the simulations is the next step. \openprob\ With it the two effects combine as in Table~\ref{tab:tw_bins}. The product falls slowly with redshift, with slope
$-0.187$ at $z=0.3$ and $-0.025$ at $z=1$; it turns over at $z=1.40$, where the falling gravitational part and the rising non-thermal part balance.

\paragraph{Precision needed.} The observable is the slope of the total, $R\times C_{\rm NT}$, not of $R$ alone: a straight line through the four bin
centres has slope $-0.152$, and a $3\sigma$ detection of a non-zero slope needs a fractional error of $1.5\,\%$ per bin (equal errors). \calc\ The
errors $\sigma(dR/dz)\approx\pm0.03$ for a cross-matched eROSITA + Planck + DES sample of $\sim180$ clusters over $0.1<z<0.6$, and $\pm0.008$ for
$\sim5000$ clusters, have been put forward as the reach of current and coming samples; with them the slope $-0.18$ would stand at $0.18/0.03=6$ and
$0.18/0.008\approx22$ times the error, and its separation from a non-thermal slope of $+0.02$ to $+0.04$ at about 7 and 25--28 times. \calc\ Deriving either error from a sample calculation (selection, mass scatter, the non-thermal model) is the next step, so no significance is claimed here. \openprob

\section{The SZ-to-X-ray ratio}\label{sec:tw_sz}
In the Level~1 form both matter-sector estimators are suppressed by the same $\mu$, so $M_{\rm SZ}/M_{\rm hydro}\approx1$. The eROSITA cluster
catalogue~\cite{Bulbul2024} provides the X-ray side of the Planck cross-match. But Section~\ref{sec:tw_estimators} states the reason the ratio is close to
unity: SZ masses are calibrated on hydrostatic X-ray masses through the $Y$--$M$ relation. $M_{\rm SZ}/M_{\rm hydro}\approx1$ therefore holds in any
theory of gravity and is not a test of the sector split. \calibrated\ The three conditions read:
\begin{align}
M_{\rm SZ}/M_{\rm hydro}&\approx1&&\text{(both matter sector): holds by calibration, not a test,}\label{eq:tw_test1}\\
M_{\rm lens}/M_{\rm hydro}&>1&&\text{(lensing = photon sector): observed, shared with non-thermal pressure,}\label{eq:tw_test2}\\
d(M_{\rm lens}/M_{\rm hydro})/dz&<0&&\text{(the redshift dependence of the gravitational part): the test.}\label{eq:tw_test3}
\end{align}

\section{Systematic effects}\label{sec:tw_systematics}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Hydrostatic equilibrium violations.} Standard practice restricts samples to relaxed clusters identified by X-ray morphology. For relaxed
clusters hydrostatic equilibrium holds to $\sim5$--$10\,\%$~\cite{Neto2007,Planelles2017}, below the $\sim7$--$10\,\%$ Level~1 signal at $z=0.2$--$0.4$.
\item \emph{Lensing mass systematics.} Shape-measurement biases ($|m|<0.02$ for DES Y3~\cite{MacCrann2022}), photometric-redshift errors and the
mass-sheet degeneracy contribute at the $\sim5\,\%$ level. They are not expected to produce a negative redshift slope.
\item \emph{Projection.} Orientation bias averages toward zero in large samples of randomly oriented triaxial halos~\cite{Corless2008} and gives no
systematic redshift dependence.
\item \emph{Selection.} Requiring detection in all three channels mitigates selection bias. Selection differences between redshift bins can still
produce a trend, as the redshift split in Table~\ref{tab:ld_published} shows, and must be modelled.
\end{itemize}
The sign of $dR/dz$ is the critical discriminant. The identified systematics give either no redshift dependence or a positive slope, opposite to the
Level~1 slope of $-0.18$. \interp

\section{Forecasts}\label{sec:tw_forecasts}
The cumulative eROSITA all-sky data combined with Euclid weak lensing will grow a cross-matched sample to thousands of clusters. \prediction\ Euclid's
measurement of $\mu(z)$ and $\Sigma(z)$ from cosmic shear and galaxy clustering will independently constrain the gravitational part of the cluster mass
ratio; the forecast for $\mu_0$ is in Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}. A combined detection of $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ from the cluster mass ratio, the
$\sigma_8$ suppression and the direct Euclid $\mu$--$\Sigma$ constraints would be convergent evidence from three probes that use three different
physical channels. \prediction

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:tw_discussion}
The three-way cluster mass ratio and the $\sigma_8$ suppression are two observational consequences of the same signature, $\mu<1$ and $\Sigma=1$ from
the matter-sector friction term with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$. The $\sigma_8$ shift is a Level~2 chain result and is consistent with current data
(Chapter~\ref{ch:sectortension}). The cluster ratio is a Level~1 prediction; in the form the chains implement it is $R=1$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:lensdyn}, Section~\ref{sec:ld_hold}). \openprob

\section{What would test it}\label{sec:tw_test}
\prediction\ In the Level~1 form the prediction for the three-way cluster mass ratio is
\begin{equation*}
R(z)=M_{\rm lens}/M_{\rm hydro}=1/\mu(z),
\end{equation*}
from a single Hubble-friction term in the matter-sector equations with coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ and activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R(z)\approx1.05$--$1.16$ over $0<z<0.5$ from the gravitational part. With an illustrative non-thermal factor the total is of the order of the
Planck-prior lensing calibrations; a bin-by-bin comparison awaits traced per-bin values.
\item $M_{\rm SZ}/M_{\rm hydro}\approx1$ holds by the $Y$--$M$ calibration in any theory and is not a test.
\item The slope $dR/dz\approx-0.18$ at $z=0.3$ is the discriminant: opposite in sign to the non-thermal prediction. Its detection needs about $1.5\,\%$
per bin on the total ratio in four bins over $0.1<z<0.8$.
\item No free parameter: the prediction follows from $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ and the Planck 2018 parameters.
\end{enumerate}
A cross-matched sample (eROSITA X-ray, Planck or ground-based SZ, Euclid or DES lensing) binned in redshift, with one mass method per estimator
throughout, gives $R(z)$ falling with redshift as Eq.~\eqref{eq:tw_R} on top of a non-thermal part that rises. Falsified (in this form) by a slope of
zero or positive sign once the non-thermal and selection parts are modelled from simulations. In the Level~2 form the prediction is $R=1$ apart from
astrophysics, and clusters test only the growth history, through their abundance.

A cluster astronomer with X-ray, SZ and lensing masses in hand can run this test directly: bin the sample by redshift, hold one mass method per estimator, and read the slope.
