\chapter{Missing satellites and growth suppression from $\mu<1$}\label{ch:satellites}

\section*{The place}
The halo of the Milky Way and the small halos inside it. Each satellite galaxy is a bound system whose stars and dark matter wrote their records as they
collapsed. In IAM the cosmic horizon is always available as the surface for those records (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}). This chapter asks how far the
cost of writing, through the weaker matter coupling $\mu<1$ that it implies at late times, changes the number of small dark-matter halos, and gives
that change at its actual size.

\section*{Summary}
The missing satellites problem, a factor of about 8 between the $\sim500$ dark-matter subhalos predicted by $\Lambda$CDM N-body simulations and the
$\sim60$ satellite galaxies then known around the Milky Way, has resisted a single physical explanation for over two decades. \observed\ The virial
partition fixes the cosmological coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ with no free parameter, and through it a matter-sector gravitational coupling $\mu(a)<1$:
$\mu=0.864$ today, a coupling $13.62\,\%$ below unity, with $\mu\to1$ as $E(a)\to0$ at high redshift. \calc\ That coupling slows late-time growth and acts
on the abundance of dark-matter halos directly, not on the baryons inside them. Its size is set by the growth equation: the linear growth factor today is
lower by $0.78\,\%$ in the effective-coupling form ($0.67\,\%$ and $1.87\,\%$ in the two other implementations), $\sigma_8$ is lower by $1.1$--$1.6\,\%$ in
the chains, and the Press--Schechter abundance of halos at satellite masses changes by under one percent. \calc\ Against an order-of-magnitude deficit
the growth mechanism is a small correction; its growth signature is a live prediction that the coming surveys test directly.

\section{The problem}\label{sec:ms_problem}
The missing satellites problem was identified independently by Klypin et al.~\cite{Klypin1999} and Moore et al.~\cite{Moore1999}. High-resolution
N-body simulations of $\Lambda$CDM predict of order 500 dark-matter subhalos with circular velocities $v_c>10$~km\,s$^{-1}$ within the virial radius of
a Milky Way-mass host. The satellites then known numbered about 60, including the classical dwarfs found before SDSS and the faint
dwarfs found in modern photometric surveys~\cite{Koposov2008,Tollerud2008}; the Local Volume Database counts at least 65, and its Milky Way dwarf catalog now has 68 entries~\cite{Pace2025LVDB}. \observed\
Proposed explanations within $\Lambda$CDM include reionization suppressing gas cooling in low-mass halos~\cite{Bullock2000,Benson2002b}, ram-pressure
and tidal stripping removing baryons from satellites~\cite{Mayer2006}, and baryonic feedback ejecting gas and lowering the central dark-matter
density~\cite{ReadGilmore2005,PontzenGovernato2012}. These act on baryons; they do not address whether the abundance of dark-matter subhalos itself is suppressed.

IAM proposes a further, gravitational contribution. The same decoherence process that supplies the late-time informational term at cosmological
scales (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}) modifies the effective gravitational coupling of matter at perturbation scales, and so acts on the abundance of dark-matter
subhalos directly, not on the baryons inside them. \conjecture\ The chapter follows the order of the argument: the virial coupling
(Section~\ref{sec:ms_closure}), the growth suppression and its size (Section~\ref{sec:ms_A}), the chain results (Section~\ref{sec:ms_chains}), how the
mechanism sits beside the baryonic explanations (Section~\ref{sec:ms_unified}), and the tests (Section~\ref{sec:ms_tests}).

\section{The virial partition and the coupling}\label{sec:ms_closure}
For a bound system in a $1/r$ potential, the virial theorem (Part~\ref{part:1}, Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}) gives
\begin{equation}
2K+V=0,\qquad K=\tfrac12|V|.
\label{eq:ms_virial}\end{equation}
It is an exact theorem for time-averaged bound motion, valid for gravitational and Coulomb potentials at every scale from hydrogen atoms to galaxy
clusters. \derived

In IAM's Law the virial partition sets how much of a bound system's energy is written as irreversible records, each at the Landauer cost $k_BT\ln2$
per bit~\cite{Landauer1961}, to the nearest available surface (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}); the thermodynamics of the horizon surface follows Jacobson
and Cai--Kim~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005}. For the cosmological matter sector the partition fixes the coupling
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.15765,
\label{eq:ms_beta}\end{equation}
the share of the matter energy density that takes part in decoherence per Hubble time, with no free parameter (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}). \prediction\
In the chains $\beta_m$ is fixed, not fitted; the posterior value $0.1583\pm0.0032$ is $\Omega_m/2$ evaluated on the Level~2 posterior
$\Omega_m=0.3166\pm0.0065$. \calc

\section{Growth suppression from $\mu<1$}\label{sec:ms_A}
\subsection{Derivation}
The accumulated record of gravitational decoherence since the electroweak transition is carried by the activation function
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right),
\label{eq:ms_E}\end{equation}
with $a$ the scale factor, unity today, obtained from horizon thermodynamics in the Jacobson--Cai--Kim formalism~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). It satisfies $E(a)\to0$ as $a\to0$ and $dE/da>0$ always: the ledger only accumulates. \interp

Photons travel on null geodesics with $d\tau=0$; they accumulate no proper time and take no part in gravitational decoherence. The informational
modification applies to the matter sector only. \interp\ The effective matter coupling is
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},
\label{eq:ms_mu}\end{equation}
with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ms_beta} and $\Sigma(a)=1$ for photons; Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth} gives the mapping from the
matter-sector rate. \interp\ At $z=0$, where $E=1$ and $H_{\Lambda\rm CDM}=H_0$,
\begin{equation}
\mu(z=0)=\frac1{1+\beta_m}=\frac1{1+\Omega_m/2}=0.864,
\label{eq:ms_mu0}\end{equation}
a coupling $13.62\,\%$ below unity; in the notation $\mu_0\equiv\mu(z=0)-1$, $\mu_0=-0.136$. \calc\ That number is the coupling, not the growth
suppression. At high redshift $E(a)\to0$ and $\mu\to1$: at $z=2$, $E=0.135$ and $\mu=0.998$, so the model behaves as $\Lambda$CDM for $z\gtrsim2$ and
the suppression is a late-time effect. \calc

In the effective-coupling form the linear matter perturbation equation reads
\begin{equation}
\ddot\delta_m+2H\dot\delta_m-\tfrac32\,\Omega_m(a)H^2\,\mu(a)\,\delta_m=0,
\label{eq:ms_growth}\end{equation}
with $\Omega_m(a)H^2=H_0^2\Omega_ma^{-3}$ (form (i) of Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}). In the Level~2 form of the Boltzmann code the term enters instead
as extra Hubble friction on the matter perturbations (form (ii); Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}). \derived\ (definitions) A lower $\delta_m$ at late times lowers
the abundance of virialized dark-matter halos, most where the abundance is most sensitive to the growth rate.

\subsection{The size of the growth change}
\calc\ With the same early amplitude, the linear growth factor today changes by
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{\Delta D}{D}\right|_{z=0}=\frac{D_{\rm IAM}(0)-D_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(0)}{D_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(0)}=-0.78\,\%
\label{eq:ms_dD}\end{equation}
in form (i), $-0.67\,\%$ in the Level~2 matter-rate form (ii) and $-1.87\,\%$ if the whole growth equation is put on the matter-sector rate (form iii). The change
grows from zero at $z\gtrsim2$ to these values today (Fig.~\ref{fig:sat_mechanisms}b). Because $\mu$ does not depend on scale, the same fractional change
applies at every linear scale, including the scales of satellite halos: the linear power spectrum today is lower by $1.55\,\%$ (form i). In the chains
$\sigma_8$ falls by $1.57\,\%$ (Level~1, $0.8143\to0.8015$) and $1.11\,\%$ (Level~2, $0.8087\to0.7998$).

\subsection{The halo mass function}
In the Press--Schechter formalism~\cite{PressSchechter1974} the abundance of halos of mass $M$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{dn}{dM}=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\bar\rho}{M^2}\,\nu\,\left|\frac{d\ln\sigma_M}{d\ln M}\right|\,e^{-\nu^2/2},\qquad\nu=\frac{\delta_c}{\sigma_M},
\label{eq:ms_psmf}\end{equation}
with $\sigma_M$ the rms linear density fluctuation on mass scale $M$ and $\delta_c\simeq1.686$ the collapse threshold. \derived\ (standard)
\textbf{Step 1.} A scale-independent fractional change $\epsilon$ in $\sigma_M$, $\sigma_M\to\sigma_M(1+\epsilon)$, sends $\nu\to\nu(1-\epsilon)$ to first
order and leaves $d\ln\sigma_M/d\ln M$ unchanged. \textbf{Step 2.} At fixed mass only $\ln\nu-\nu^2/2$ changes, by $(-1+\nu^2)\epsilon$. So
\begin{equation}
\Delta\ln n=(\nu^2-1)\,\epsilon .
\label{eq:ms_ps}\end{equation}
\derived\ (sympy) The exponential factor dominates only for rare halos, $\nu>1$, where a lower $\sigma_M$ lowers the abundance; for $\nu<1$ the factor $\nu$
dominates and a lower $\sigma_M$ slightly raises it; at $\nu=1$ the two cancel.

\textbf{Step 3 (satellite masses).} For halos of $10^7$--$10^9\,M_\odot$ today the Planck 2018 linear spectrum (Eisenstein--Hu shape,
$\sigma_8=0.811$) gives $\sigma_M=7.0$--$4.8$ and $\nu=0.24$--$0.35$. With $\epsilon=-0.78\,\%$ (form i), Eq.~\eqref{eq:ms_ps} gives
$\Delta\ln n=+0.68$ to $+0.73\,\%$ at fixed mass; for any $\nu<\sqrt2$ the change is below $0.78\,\%$ in size. \calc\ Press--Schechter counts field
halos; subhalo counts also depend on the host and on accretion, which share the same growth history, so the field estimate gives the order of the effect.
\interp\ The growth mechanism therefore changes the count of satellite-mass halos by under one percent, against a deficit of about an order of magnitude
($\ln10=2.30$). \calc\ Its signature is in the growth history itself, tested in Chapters~\ref{ch:latetime} and \ref{ch:s8trend} and in
Section~\ref{sec:ms_tests}.

\section{Beside the baryonic explanations}\label{sec:ms_unified}
The growth mechanism lowers the growth of all matter perturbations at late times. It is a continuous effect, operating from $z\sim2$ to the present and
controlled by one coupling, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$, and it acts on the abundance of dark-matter subhalos itself rather than on the baryons of halos assumed to
have virialized already. Reionization, tidal stripping and feedback act on those baryons. The two classes are not exclusive: a change in the subhalo
abundance does not prevent baryonic processes from acting as well. At the sizes found above, the baryonic processes carry the satellite deficit, and the
informational term contributes a sub-percent correction to the dark-matter halo count. \interp\ The coupling it shares with the growth and Hubble-rate
results of Chapters~\ref{ch:latetime} and \ref{ch:level2} is what makes it testable.

\section{Observational tests}\label{sec:ms_tests}
\paragraph{The coupling today.} \prediction\ The weak-lensing and clustering surveys measure $\mu_0$ directly; IAM predicts
$\mu_0=\mu(z=0)-1=-0.136$. The forecast is in Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}. A measurement of $\mu(z=0)$ above $0.90$ at $2\sigma$, once
translated from the survey's template, would be in tension with the prediction.

\paragraph{The growth history.} \prediction\ The same coupling fixes $f\sigma_8$ lower by $4.25\,\%$, $2.17\,\%$, $1.35\,\%$ and $0.41\,\%$ at $z=0$,
$0.3$, $0.5$ and $1$ (form i; Appendix~\ref{app:der:growth}), and $\sigma_8=0.7998\pm0.0058$ at Level~2 against $0.8087$ for $\Lambda$CDM. Redshift-space
distortion and weak-lensing surveys test these directly (Chapters~\ref{ch:latetime} and \ref{ch:s8trend}).

\paragraph{The satellite scale.} \prediction\ Because $\mu$ is scale-independent at linear order, the growth deficit at satellite scales equals the one at
large scales: $-0.78\,\%$ in $D$ and $-1.55\,\%$ in the linear power today (form i). A measurement of small-scale power that departs from the large-scale
deficit would separate the informational term from any scale-dependent effect. A joint measurement of $\mu_0$ from the surveys and of small-scale power,
with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ fixed, tests the coupling at cosmological and galactic scales together.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:ms_discussion}
The missing satellites problem has mostly been approached through baryonic physics: which processes remove gas from satellites, suppress their star
formation, or disrupt their halos after they form. The informational term adds a question about the dark matter itself: how many halos grow at all,
given the late-time coupling $\mu<1$. The answer, from the growth equation and the halo mass function, is that the coupling changes the count of
satellite-mass halos by under one percent, so the satellite deficit remains a question for baryonic physics, while the coupling remains a live
prediction through the growth history it fixes. \interp\ Whether satellite halos have a floor in velocity dispersion at infall, before tidal stripping lowers it, is the open test. \openprob

The growth deficit is scale-independent and dated. A cosmologist can run the same chains against the next survey release, and a simulator can test the velocity-dispersion floor at infall directly.
