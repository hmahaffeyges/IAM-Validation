\chapter{Growth across redshift: the $S_8$ trend}\label{ch:s8trend}

\section*{The place}
The cosmic horizon, where matter's records are written as it collapses into structure (Chapter~\ref{ch:surfaces}). The cost of writing weakens the
coupling felt by matter, $\mu<1$, and leaves light untouched, $\Sigma=1$. This chapter follows that weakening through the redshifts at which surveys
measure the clustering amplitude.

\section*{Summary}
An analysis of growth-rate data has found that the amplitude parameter $S_8\equiv\sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ inferred from it rises with effective
redshift, from roughly $3\sigma$ below the Planck $\Lambda$CDM expectation at low redshift to agreement within $1\sigma$ at high redshift. The
informational term has the shape this trend requires: its activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ is largest today ($z=0$) and vanishes smoothly at
high redshift. The coupling constant $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ is set by the virial partition and is held fixed in every chain; no free parameter is added to
$\Lambda$CDM. The photon sector is kept exactly ($\Sigma=1$), so BAO positions and lensing geometry are those of $\Lambda$CDM by construction, which
avoids a common difficulty of models that modify both sectors. The size of the effect is calculated here from the linear growth equation: the shape of
the trend is reproduced, and its amplitude is about a tenth of the low-redshift deficit. The chapter states the mechanism and the predictions that
existing compilations can already test.

\section{The observation}
\observed\ An analysis of redshift-space-distortion $f\sigma_8(z)$ measurements (\emph{MNRAS} 528, L20, 2024; arXiv:\allowbreak{}2303.06928) holds
$\Omega_m$ to a Planck + BAO prior, $\Omega_m=0.3111\pm0.0056$, so that the only parameter fitted is $\sigma_8$, the scale of the $f\sigma_8(z)$
curve, whose shape is then fixed. Removing the data below a minimum redshift $z_{\rm min}$ and refitting, it finds that the inferred $S_8$ rises with
the effective redshift of what remains: a $\sim3\sigma$ tension with the Planck value at lower redshifts becomes consistent within $1\sigma$ at high
redshifts. With the 20 points of its main sample taken as independent the shift is $1.6\sigma$; the same trend appears in a larger sample of 66
points, where survey overlap overstates it ($2.8\sigma$). The flat $\Lambda$CDM model is still preferred over an ad hoc model with a jump in $S_8$ at
one redshift, and without the $\Omega_m$ prior the high-redshift data are the more consistent with Planck. The analysis reads redshift-dependent
fitting parameters as a hallmark of model breakdown.

\observed\ The result is among the most carefully argued observational cases for redshift-dependent growth suppression: the apparent $S_8$ tension is
not a fixed offset but a \emph{trend}, in which low-redshift data prefer a low $S_8$ and the preference weakens monotonically toward the Planck value
of $0.832$~\cite{Planck2018VI} at high redshift. This behaviour is difficult to accommodate within $\Lambda$CDM, in which $S_8$ is one number at every
redshift. It is also difficult to accommodate in modified-gravity models that rescale the growth rate by a scale-dependent or epoch-independent factor
rather than with a specific redshift shape. \interp

\observed\ The growth-index description $f=\Omega_m(a)^\gamma$, fitted to the same kind of data with Planck, gives
$\gamma=0.633^{+0.025}_{-0.024}$ against $0.55$ in general relativity, excluding $0.55$ at $3.7\sigma$; with $f\sigma_8$ and Planck alone,
$\gamma=0.639^{+0.024}_{-0.025}$ ($4.2\sigma$) (Nguyen, Huterer and Wen 2023~\cite{Nguyen2023}).

\section{The coupling}\label{sec:s8_coupling}
The informational term extends the thermodynamic route to gravity (Jacobson 1995~\cite{Jacobson1995}) by the Landauer cost~\cite{Landauer1961} of the
records written by gravitational decoherence (Chapters~\ref{ch:theory} and \ref{ch:virial}). The change enters only the matter-sector perturbation
equations; the background Friedmann equations and the photon sector are unchanged. The gravitational coupling felt by matter is
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\qquad H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)=H_0^2\left(\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_\Lambda\right),
\label{eq:s8_mu}\end{equation}
with the activation function
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)
\label{eq:s8_Ea}\end{equation}
and the coupling constant
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.15765\qquad(\Omega_m=0.3153),
\label{eq:s8_beta}\end{equation}
fixed by the virial partition of the virialised structures that source the decoherence flux (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}). The photon coupling is
$\Sigma(a)=1$ exactly: decoherence of this kind needs timelike worldlines, and null geodesics are unaffected.

\paragraph{The value today.} The steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At $a=1$ the activation function is $E(1)=e^0=1$.
\item The background rate today is $H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(1)=H_0^2(\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda)$, which is $H_0^2$ for a flat universe
($\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda=1$; radiation is negligible today).
\item Equation~\eqref{eq:s8_mu} then gives $\mu(1)=(\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda)/(\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda+\beta_m)=1/(1+\beta_m)$.
\item So
\begin{equation}
\mu_0\equiv\mu(a{=}1)-1=-\frac{\beta_m}{\Omega_m+\Omega_\Lambda+\beta_m}=-\frac{\beta_m}{1+\beta_m}=-0.1362 .
\label{eq:s8_mu0}\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\derived\ (given Eqs.~\ref{eq:s8_mu}--\ref{eq:s8_beta}; checked with sympy in \texttt{verify\_s8\_trend.py}). The value follows from the
coupling alone; it was not adjusted to fit any $S_8$ data. The value $-0.135$ used in the Level~1 chains is the MGCAMB amplitude of
Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}; it differs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:s8_mu0} by about one per cent of the free-$\mu_0$ posterior width.

\paragraph{The redshift shape.} Equation~\eqref{eq:s8_Ea} is the key object. $E(a)$ reaches its largest value, unity, at $a=1$ (today, $z=0$) and
falls monotonically toward earlier times; it is below $0.1$ for $z>\ln10=2.30$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:s8_trend_iam}a). This is the shape that produces a trend
in which the weakening is strongest at low redshift and vanishes at high redshift. With the Planck 2018 background, $\mu(z{=}0)=0.864$,
$\mu(0.5)=0.948$, $\mu(1)=0.982$ and $\mu(2)=0.998$ \calc. The shape of the trend therefore comes with nothing adjusted.

A survey measures the clustering at its redshift and, analysing with $\Lambda$CDM growth, reports $S_8$. Two different quantities can be meant.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Lensing amplitude.} A lensing survey measures the amount of structure, the integrated growth $D(z)$, not the instantaneous coupling. The
inferred value is $S_8^{\rm Planck}\,D_{\rm IAM}(z)/D_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(z)$, with $S_8^{\rm Planck}=0.832$~\cite{Planck2018VI} and both growth factors from the linear
growth equation (Eq.~\ref{eq:st_ode}, Chapter~\ref{ch:sectortension}) with the same early-time amplitude.
\item \emph{Growth rate.} A redshift-space-distortion measurement is of $f\sigma_8(z)$. A $\Lambda$CDM fit of $\sigma_8$ to it at fixed $\Omega_m$ infers
$S_8^{\rm Planck}\,(f\sigma_8)_{\rm IAM}/(f\sigma_8)_{\Lambda\rm CDM}$ at that redshift; the trend analysis of the previous section is of this kind.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The chain form.} The Level 1 chains used MGCAMB's form~\cite{Zhao2009MGCAMB,Wang2023MGCAMB} $\mu=1+\mu_0\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)/\Omega_{\rm DE,0}$, which equals the exact $\mu$

\section{The perturbation level only}\label{sec:s8_perturbation}
Does the weakening of $\mu(a)$ imply a change in the background expansion $H(z)$? It does not: the informational term acts on the perturbation
equations, and the Friedmann background is that of $\Lambda$CDM. This is a result of the tests, not a modelling choice. Applying the same coupling
$\beta_m$ to the background Friedmann equation gives $H_0\approx61.5\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}), excluded at high
significance by every $H_0$ measurement. \fitted\ The perturbation-only implementation gives $\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ against $\Lambda$CDM under the full
Planck 2018 CamSpec TT, TE, EE likelihood~\cite{Planck2018VI}: IAM reproduces the CMB as well as $\Lambda$CDM with no added parameter \measured.

$\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$ was set before the Planck chains were run and is fixed in every IAM chain of the eighteen converged chains (IAM with $\beta_m$ fixed, $\Lambda$CDM controls and free-$\mu_0$ tests). It is not returned by
them. What the chains test is consistency: the Level~2 posterior $\Omega_m=0.3166\pm0.0065$ gives $\Omega_m/2=0.1583\pm0.0032$, $0.2\sigma$ from the
fixed value \calc. The test of the coupling's size is the free-amplitude run (Table~\ref{tab:lt_free}).

\section{Light is unchanged}
With $\Sigma=1$ photons follow standard null geodesics. BAO angular positions, set by the ratio of the sound horizon to the angular-diameter distance
along photon paths, the CMB acoustic scale and the lensing geometry are therefore those of $\Lambda$CDM. This removes what could otherwise look like a
conflict between a weaker growth and the well-measured BAO scale. \prediction

Lensing and dynamics measure different things in a cluster. With $\Sigma=1$ the relation between the lensing potential and the density is that of
general relativity, so lensing measures the mass $M_{\rm lens}$. Dynamics measures the response of matter, $\mu$ times the Newtonian pull. The
observable difference from $\Lambda$CDM is therefore the ratio $M_{\rm lens}/M_{\rm dyn}=1/\mu(z)>1$, which depends on redshift: $1.16$ today, $1.05$ at
$z=0.5$, $1.02$ at $z=1$ in the linear coupling \calc. It is a weaker dynamical response, not a change of lensing. Whether the linear $\mu$ applies
inside virialised clusters is open (Chapter~\ref{ch:threeway}) \openprob.

\section{Scale independence}
The Landauer cost in the informational term is paid at the Hubble horizon, orders of magnitude larger than any survey scale. The change to the growth
rate is therefore independent of $k$ at every observationally accessible scale: $\mu$ depends on $a$ only. \interp\ This discriminates between
models. $f(R)$ gravity gives scale-dependent growth above a transition scale~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2008}. The informational term predicts a flat ratio
$P_{\rm IAM}(k)/P_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(k)$, constant across the survey range, which sufficiently precise power-spectrum measurements can test. \prediction

\section{Other models of the same trend}
Several models have been proposed for the $S_8$ trend, among them a sign-switching cosmological constant ($\Lambda_s$CDM), interacting dark energy
and modified-gravity families; the case for redshift-dependent $\Lambda$CDM parameters is reviewed in \emph{Universe} 10, 305 (2024;
arXiv:\allowbreak{}2402.04767). \observed\ Locating the informational term among them sharpens the predictions.

\paragraph{The growth index.} The most widely used empirical description of late-time growth suppression is $\gamma$CDM, $f(a)\approx\Omega_m(a)^\gamma$,
with $\gamma=0.55$ in general relativity, and the data prefer $\gamma=0.633$ (above). It describes the redshift shape of the suppression well but
carries no mechanism; it is an effective description. \interp\ The informational term supplies a mechanism for the same qualitative behaviour, the
greatest weakening today and recovery toward high redshift, and its shape comes from horizon thermodynamics rather than from a fitted exponent.
\interp\ The two are not the same curve. The informational term behaves as $\gamma_{\rm eff}=\ln f/\ln\Omega_m(a)=0.585$ today, $0.572$ at $z=0.5$ and
$0.562$ at $z=1$, against $0.554$, $0.549$ and $0.547$ in $\Lambda$CDM: about $40\,\%$ of the measured departure today. With the same early
amplitude, $\gamma=0.633$ lowers $f\sigma_8$ by $11.4\,\%$ today, $5.4\,\%$ at $z=0.5$, $2.7\,\%$ at $z=1$ and $1.5\,\%$ at $z=1.5$; the informational
term by $4.25$, $1.35$, $0.41$ and $0.13\,\%$. \calc\ Data at the per-cent level can separate them at every redshift below $z\approx1$.

\paragraph{Structural differences.} The informational term makes a set of predictions that differ structurally from the other proposals:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{BAO.} BAO positions identical to $\Lambda$CDM ($\Sigma=1$, background unchanged). $\Lambda_s$CDM changes the background and moves the BAO
near its transition redshift, $z_\dagger\sim1.7$--$2.4$. \prediction
\item \emph{ISW.} With $\Sigma=1$ the lensing potential follows $\delta_m$, which grows more slowly, so the potentials decay faster: the ISW source
$(1-f)D/a$ is $3.1$--$3.5\,\%$ larger at $z\le0.5$, and the ISW--galaxy cross-correlation about $3\,\%$ larger (uniform weight over $0.05<z<1.5$) \calc.
$f(R)$ gravity, which enhances growth, predicts the opposite sign, a clean discriminant \interp.
\item \emph{Cluster mass ratio.} $M_{\rm lens}/M_{\rm dyn}=1/\mu(z)>1$ with that redshift dependence; the ratio is $1$ in $\Lambda$CDM and is not
produced in this form by models that change lensing ($\Sigma\neq1$). \prediction
\item \emph{The $\mu$--$\Sigma$ plane.} $\mu_0=-0.136$, $\Sigma_0=0$ exactly. Most modified-gravity models give $\Sigma\neq1$ or move $\mu$ and $\Sigma$
together; the informational term sits at a distinct point of the plane. \prediction
\item \emph{Scale dependence.} Unlike $f(R)$ or Galileon gravity, a $k$-independent growth change, because the Landauer cost is paid at the Hubble
horizon. \prediction
\end{itemize}

\section{Falsifiable predictions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Euclid.} The predicted coupling is $\mu_0=-0.136$ with $\Sigma_0=0$. The forecast with the informational $\mu(z)$ is in
Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}. A measurement of $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$ and the redshift
dependence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:s8_Ea} would be a detection; $\mu_0$ consistent with zero at the precision that separates the two would falsify the term.
\prediction
\item \emph{The growth-rate shape.} The term predicts a specific curved $f\sigma_8(z)$ deficit, not a parallel rescaling: $3.1\,\%$ at $z=0.15$, $1.9\,\%$
at $0.35$, $0.9\,\%$ at $0.65$, $0.4\,\%$ at $1.05$ and $0.1\,\%$ at $1.55$, vanishing above $z\sim2$ \calc. Measured to $1\,\%$ in each of fifteen bins
of width $0.1$ over $0.1<z<1.6$, this shape differs from $\Lambda$CDM by $5.0\sigma$ \calc.
\end{itemize}

This chapter trades a free parameter for a shape. A cosmologist can take the same compilations, run the comparison, and check whether the trend follows the activation function; the surveys now under way will measure that curve directly.

\section{Status}
Stands: the redshift shape of the weakening, the sector split, scale independence, the dated $\mu_0$. Calculated here \calc: the amplitude deficit the
informational term produces is $0.8\,\%$ today, about a tenth of the low-redshift deficit; the growth-rate deficit is $4.25\,\%$; the growth index moves
$40\,\%$ of the way to the measured value; and the trend analysis applied to the term's own growth gives a rise of $S_8$ with $z_{\rm min}$ of $0.3\sigma$.
The $S_8$ trend is consistent in shape and smaller in size. Eighteen converged chains (IAM with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ fixed, $\Lambda$CDM controls and free-$\mu_0$ tests) find the term consistent with the
CMB ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$, Level~2). Code, chains and the modified CAMB source are in the repository (\texttt{camb\_validation/}, \texttt{docs/verification/}).
