\chapter{Dark energy or two rulers?}\label{ch:sectortension}

\section*{The place}
The cosmic horizon (Chapter~\ref{ch:surfaces}). Matter writes the record and feels $\mu<1$; light writes nothing and keeps $\Sigma=1$. Any analysis that
puts observables from the two sectors into one fluid fit can read the difference as something else. This chapter and the next ask whether DESI's
evidence for evolving dark energy is that.

\section*{Summary}
The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) reports a preference for dynamical dark energy with $w_0>-1$, $w_a<0$ and a phantom crossing of the
equation of state at $z\approx0.35$--$0.50$. The question tested here and in Chapter~\ref{ch:phantom} is whether that preference can be a
sector-assignment artifact of the informational term, a perturbation-only extension of $\Lambda$CDM from horizon thermodynamics with no free parameter
beyond the standard model. The term gives $\mu(a)<1$ and $\Sigma(a)=1$. Its physical origin is gravitational decoherence: timelike (matter)
worldlines decohere as structure forms, null (photon) worldlines do not. The matter coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$ is set by the virial
partition and fixed in every chain; the Planck posterior is consistent with it ($0.2\sigma$).

The observational consequence is a sector split. BAO distances and photon propagation are those of $\Lambda$CDM ($\Sigma=1$ exactly); the growth
rate of galaxies is weakened by $\mu(a)<1$, and the lensing amplitude follows the lower matter density. A single-fluid $w_0w_a$CDM fit that takes
$\Lambda$CDM distances together with sub-$\Lambda$CDM growth must infer $w_{\rm eff}(z)\neq-1$ to reconcile them.

The term is confronted with the DESI DR1 full-shape growth rates in six redshift bins ($0.295\le z_{\rm eff}\le1.491$)~\cite{DESI2024V}, the DESI DR1
distances, weak-lensing $S_8$ from KiDS~\cite{Heymans2021,Asgari2021,Wright2025,Stolzner2025}, DES Y3~\cite{DESY3} and HSC Y3~\cite{HSCY3Dalal}, and the
DESI DR2 $w_0w_a$ fits~\cite{DESI2025}. Under the full Planck 2018 likelihood the term reproduces the CMB as well as $\Lambda$CDM with no added parameter
($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ at the best points, eighteen converged chains, $R-1\le0.010$) \measured. Its $\sigma_8=0.7998\pm0.0058$ agrees with the 2025 joint
lensing value $\sigma_8=0.802^{+0.022}_{-0.018}$~\cite{Stolzner2025} at $0.1\sigma$ \calc. The growth deficits at the DESI redshifts are $0.1$--$2.2\,\%$,
below the present errors \calc. The DESI DR2 fits use distances only, and the term predicts $\Lambda$CDM distances; Chapter~\ref{ch:phantom} shows
that it does not produce DESI's preference \calc. Euclid and the completed DESI survey test the growth shape set by $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$
(Section~\ref{sec:lt_euclid}; Chapter~\ref{ch:phantom}).

\section{Three tensions}
$\Lambda$CDM describes the CMB anisotropies, baryon acoustic oscillations, type Ia supernovae and large-scale structure over many decades of
scale~\cite{Planck2018VI}. Yet at least two classes of inter-dataset tension have persisted through extensive scrutiny and cross-checks, and neither
has a clear systematic explanation within the standard model. \interp

\paragraph{The Hubble tension.} \observed\ The Planck 2018 CMB analysis under $\Lambda$CDM gives $H_0=67.4\pm0.5\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$~\cite{Planck2018VI}. The
Cepheid-calibrated supernova ladder of SH0ES gives $73.04\pm1.04$~\cite{Riess2022}, $4.9\sigma$ apart \calc. The tip of the red giant branch gives
$70.39\pm1.94$~\cite{Freedman2025}, between the two, and time-delay lensing gives a similarly high value~\cite{Wong2020}. The tension is not confined to
one calibration method. \interp

\paragraph{The $S_8$ tension.} \observed\ Weak-lensing surveys measuring the matter power spectrum amplitude at low redshift have reported
$S_8\equiv\sigma_8(\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5}$ below the $\Lambda$CDM CMB value $0.832$~\cite{Planck2018VI}: KiDS-1000 $0.766^{+0.020}_{-0.014}$ (lensing with
galaxy clustering)~\cite{Heymans2021} and $0.759^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$ (cosmic shear)~\cite{Asgari2021}; DES Y3 $0.776\pm0.017$ (lensing with galaxy
clustering)~\cite{DESY3}; HSC Y3 $0.776^{+0.032}_{-0.033}$ (cosmic-shear power spectra)~\cite{HSCY3Dalal}; a 2--3$\sigma$ tension. The completed KiDS
survey finds $S_8=0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$ from cosmic shear~\cite{Wright2025}. Combined with DES Y3 cosmic shear, Pantheon+ supernovae and DESI DR1 BAO it
gives $S_8=0.814^{+0.011}_{-0.012}$ and $\sigma_8=0.802^{+0.022}_{-0.018}$, consistent with Planck~\cite{Stolzner2025}.

depending on the supernova compilation~\cite{DESI2025}:

\paragraph{The question.} The informational term gives matter and photon observables different effective expansion rates, because gravitational
decoherence changes only the matter perturbation equations. It therefore makes three statements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Photon-sector observables} (BAO angular positions, comoving distances, the CMB acoustic scale) are those of $\Lambda$CDM. The
potential that deflects light obeys the general-relativistic relation to the density ($\Sigma=1$ exactly). \prediction
\item \emph{Matter-sector observables} (the growth rate $f\sigma_8$, peculiar velocities) are lowered through $\mu(a)<1$. The coupling deficit $1-\mu$ is
$13.6\,\%$ today and vanishes above $z\approx2$; the growth-rate deficit it produces is $4.25\,\%$ today, $2.17\,\%$ at $z=0.3$ and $0.41\,\%$ at $z=1$.
\prediction\ \calc
\item \emph{A single-fluid $w_0w_a$CDM fit} to observables of both sectors at once would read the difference as $w\neq-1$. \conjecture\ Whether this
produces the DESI crossing is tested in Chapter~\ref{ch:phantom}.
\end{enumerate}
All three follow from the one coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$, with no further amplitude.

\section{The informational term in brief}\label{sec:st_model}
\paragraph{Thermodynamic origin.} The term comes from an extension of the Jacobson--Cai--Kim thermodynamic route to gravity (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}).
Jacobson showed that the Einstein equations are an equation of state: they follow from $\delta Q=T\,\delta S$ applied to local Rindler horizons, with $S$
the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy proportional to area~\cite{Jacobson1995}. Cai and Kim extended this to the apparent horizon of an expanding universe
and obtained the Friedmann equations from the first law $dE=T\,dS+W\,dV$~\cite{CaiKim2005}. Both derivations assume that the entropy contains only the
geometric term, $S=k_BA/(4\ell_P^2)$. \derived\ (in those references)

The informational term adds an entropy that becomes significant at late times. Gravitational structure formation, the hierarchical collapse of matter
into bound objects, is the dominant source of irreversible classical information in the late universe. Each virialised structure reduces the
phase-space volume available to its matter and so writes classical information in Shannon's sense~\cite{Shannon1948}. By Landauer's
principle~\cite{Landauer1961}, verified at the mesoscale~\cite{Berut2012}, creating or erasing one bit costs at least $k_BT\ln2$. By the holographic
principle~\cite{Bousso2002} the entropy is carried on the cosmic apparent horizon. The horizon's entropy budget therefore has two parts,
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm total}=S_{\rm geometric}+S_{\rm informational},\qquad S_{\rm geometric}=\frac{k_BA}{4\ell_P^2},
\label{eq:st_entropy}\end{equation}
where $S_{\rm informational}$ accumulates from structure formation. \interp\ Inserted into the Cai--Kim first law, the extra term enters the matter
perturbation equations as a modified effective expansion rate. It does \emph{not} change the background Friedmann equations: the background is
$\Lambda$CDM throughout, as the Level~2 chains require (Section~\ref{sec:s8_perturbation}).

\paragraph{The sector split.} The causal structure of spacetime decides which species couple to the informational entropy. Timelike worldlines (cold
dark matter and baryons) interact with collapsed structures and feel the decoherence-induced friction. Null worldlines (photons) travel on the light
cone and do not couple to the matter density fluctuations through this channel. \interp\ The split is therefore at the level of the perturbations:
the cold-dark-matter and baryon equations see the effective rate
\begin{equation}
\tilde H_m^2(a)=H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2,\qquad H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)=H_0^2\left(\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_ra^{-4}+\Omega_\Lambda\right),
\label{eq:st_hm}\end{equation}
with $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}) and $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}); the photon equations are not changed. In the
$\mu$--$\Sigma$ parametrisation of modified gravity~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016} this is
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2}<1,\qquad \Sigma(a)=1\ \text{(exact)},
\label{eq:st_mu}\end{equation}
the coupling of Eq.~\eqref{eq:s8_mu}, whose value today, $\mu(1)=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.864$, is derived step by step in Section~\ref{sec:s8_coupling}.
$\mu<1$ weakens the gravitational source of structure growth. $\Sigma=1$ follows from the null-worldline argument: the relation between the
potential that deflects light and the density is unchanged, so photon propagation (BAO angles, the geometry of lensing) is that of $\Lambda$CDM. The
lensing \emph{amplitude} still follows the matter density, which grows more slowly (Section~\ref{sec:st_lensing}).

\paragraph{Coupling and activation function.} At virialisation the gravitational energy of a collapsed structure divides equally between the kinetic
energy of the virialised particles (the geometric channel) and the information of the collapsed state (the informational channel); this one-to-one
partition, an instance of IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}), gives $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.3153/2=0.15765$. \interp\ The activation function comes from
the Gibbons--Hawking temperature of the de Sitter horizon, which sets the thermal cost of a Landauer erasure, combined with the cumulative decoherence
over cosmic history (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). It has $E(a\to0)\to0$ and $E(1)=1$, so the change vanishes in the early universe and is largest today.

\section{Growth at the DESI redshifts}\label{sec:st_growth}
\paragraph{Data.} \observed\ The DESI DR1 full-shape analysis~\cite{DESI2024V} gives, in its Appendix~A, compressed ShapeFit-only data vectors (the
power-spectrum fit alone, without the post-reconstruction BAO) for six tracer samples, $0.295\le z_{\rm eff}\le1.491$, each with a Gaussian covariance of
$D_V/r_d$, $D_H/D_M$, $f\sigma_{s8}$ and the shape parameter. These are appropriate for a comparison at fixed cosmology with the BAO constraint applied
separately. The growth entry is $f\sigma_{s8}$, the growth rate times $\sigma_8$ in $8\,h^{-1}$Mpc of the template cosmology; DESI converts it to
$f\sigma_8$ only for display, assuming the template's sound horizon, and uses $f\sigma_{s8}$ for inference. For legacy context at low redshift and as a
check of the overall normalisation the comparison adds the 6dF Galaxy Survey at $z=0.067$~\cite{Beutler2012} and the SDSS redshift-space-distortion
measurements (Main Galaxy Sample at $z=0.15$; BOSS DR12 at $0.38$ and $0.51$; eBOSS DR16 luminous red galaxies, emission-line galaxies and
quasars)~\cite{Alam2021}.

\paragraph{The growth equation.} For pressureless matter on sub-horizon scales the density contrast obeys
$\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta=4\pi G\mu\bar\rho_m\delta$. The steps to the form used here are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item With $x=\ln a$, $d/dt=H\,d/dx$, so $\dot\delta=H\delta'$ and $\ddot\delta=H^2\delta''+HH'\delta'$, where a prime is $d/dx$.
\item Dividing by $H^2$: $\delta''+(2+H'/H)\delta'=4\pi G\mu\bar\rho_m\delta/H^2$.
\item With $4\pi G\bar\rho_m=\tfrac32H_0^2\Omega_ma^{-3}$ the right side is $\tfrac32\mu\,\Omega_m(a)\delta$, where $\Omega_m(a)=\Omega_ma^{-3}/(H/H_0)^2$.
\end{enumerate}
So the linear growth factor $D(a)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{d^2D}{dx^2}+\left(2+\frac{d\ln H}{dx}\right)\frac{dD}{dx}-\frac32\,\mu(a)\,\Omega_m(a)\,D=0,
\label{eq:st_ode}\end{equation}
with $H(a)$ the $\Lambda$CDM background and $\mu(a)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:st_mu}. \derived\ Initial conditions are set deep in matter domination on the
growing mode, $D(a_{\rm ini})=a_{\rm ini}$ and $dD/dx=a_{\rm ini}$ at $a_{\rm ini}=10^{-4}$, where $\mu=1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:st_ode} is integrated with
the DOP853 Runge--Kutta method in $x\in[\ln10^{-4},0]$, tolerances $10^{-11}$ (relative) and $10^{-14}$ (absolute). The growth rate is $f=d\ln D/d\ln a$,
and the prediction is $f\sigma_8(z)=f(z)\,\sigma_8\,D(z)/D(0)$ with $\sigma_8=0.7998$ (Level~2 Run~A) for the informational term and $0.8087$ (Run~C) for
$\Lambda$CDM.

The equation is a reduced treatment of the full Boltzmann calculation of the Level~2 code. With the same early amplitude it lowers $\sigma_8$ by
$0.78\,\%$, against $1.10\,\%$ in the Level~2 chains, so that $0.8087\times D_{\rm IAM}(1)/D_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(1)=0.8024$ lies $0.3\,\%$ above the Boltzmann
value $0.7998$ \calc. The predictions in this section use the equation with the chain values of $\sigma_8$; the difference is small against the present
errors.

The physical point is the \emph{redshift dependence}. The term predicts a curved deficit, largest at low redshift and falling monotonically, set by

\section{The clustering amplitude}\label{sec:st_lensing}
\paragraph{Survey data.} \observed\ Weak lensing gives the most direct low-redshift test of the clustering amplitude. The published values:
\begin{itemize}
\item KiDS-1000: $S_8=0.759^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$ from cosmic shear over $1000\,{\rm deg}^2$~\cite{Asgari2021}; $0.766^{+0.020}_{-0.014}$ with galaxy
clustering~\cite{Heymans2021}.
\item KiDS-Legacy, the completed survey of about $1350\,{\rm deg}^2$: $S_8=0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$ from cosmic shear~\cite{Wright2025}. Joined with DES Y3 cosmic shear, Pantheon+ and DESI DR1 BAO:
$S_8=0.814^{+0.011}_{-0.012}$, $\sigma_8=0.802^{+0.022}_{-0.018}$~\cite{Stolzner2025}.
\item DES Y3: $S_8=0.776\pm0.017$ from cosmic shear and galaxy clustering over $\sim4100\,{\rm deg}^2$~\cite{DESY3}.
\item HSC Y3: $S_8=0.776^{+0.032}_{-0.033}$ from cosmic-shear power spectra over $416\,{\rm deg}^2$~\cite{HSCY3Dalal}.
\item Planck CMB lensing: $\sigma_8\Omega_m^{0.25}=0.589\pm0.020$~\cite{Planck2018VIII}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The prediction.} The term lowers the amplitude of the matter power spectrum relative to $\Lambda$CDM. The Level~2 posteriors give \fitted
\begin{equation}
\sigma_8^{\rm IAM}=0.7998\pm0.0058\quad\text{against}\quad\sigma_8^{\Lambda\rm CDM}=0.8087\pm0.0059,
\label{eq:st_sigma8}\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
S_8^{\rm IAM}=0.822\pm0.011\quad\text{against}\quad S_8^{\Lambda\rm CDM}=0.830\pm0.011,
\label{eq:st_S8}\end{equation}
with $S_8$ evaluated at each chain's own $\Omega_m$ ($0.7998\sqrt{0.3166/0.3}=0.822$) \calc. The change comes only from the $\mu<1$ friction term; nothing
is changed in the primordial spectrum, the baryon-to-dark-matter ratio or any geometric parameter. The Level~2 posteriors confirm it: $\ln10^{10}A_s$
moves by $+0.09\sigma$ and $\Omega_m$ by $+0.05\sigma$ against $\Lambda$CDM. With $\Sigma=1$ the lensing potential follows $\delta_m$, so CMB
lensing power is lower by $0.08\,\%$ (Limber estimate) \calc, below any current measurement. The term's $\sigma_8\Omega_m^{0.25}=0.600$ lies $0.55\sigma$
from the Planck lensing value \calc.

What separates the term from dynamical dark energy is that its $\sigma_8$ shift is produced with $\Sigma=1$. A model that lowers $S_8$ by changing the

combination $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ is the diagnostic signature (Chapter~\ref{ch:phantom}). \prediction
