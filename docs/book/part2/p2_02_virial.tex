\chapter{The cosmological coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$}\label{ch:virial}

\section*{Overview}
IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}) prices every classical record at $k_BT\ln2$ per bit on the nearest encoding surface. For matter distributed through
the universe the place is the cosmic horizon at the Gibbons--Hawking temperature. The law fixes the price; the virial partition fixes how much bound matter
writes. Part~\ref{part:1} closed with the virial partition: a stable $1/r$ system holds exactly half its binding energy as motion, and that half is the
irreversible cost of the binding transition (Chapters~\ref{ch:virial_law} and~\ref{ch:virial_identity}). Applied to the universe, the same half fixes the
coupling of the record term in the horizon first law, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$, with no free parameter.

The core IAM mechanism is a single term, $\beta_mE(a)$, added to the matter-sector perturbation equations only, with activation function
$E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ derived from horizon thermodynamics. No background quantities are modified. The $1/2$ partition at the heart of the coupling is not a
numerological coincidence but a mathematical necessity verified across a wide range of physical scales: exact for $1/r$ potentials, proven analytically for
both the Coulomb and gravitational potentials, verified computationally for 20 atoms (H through Xe) and 10 molecules at machine precision, confirmed by the
Chandrasekhar limit ($1.456\,M_\odot$), and validated at galaxy cluster scales ($\sim$20\,\% precision). The same mathematical theorem governs electrons in
hydrogen atoms and the coupling of matter to the horizon at the Hubble scale.

This chapter derives the coupling and what follows from it --- the coupling in a real ensemble of halos, the exponent of the record rate, the activation
function, the growth coupling, the amplitude of structure, the two expansion rates, the two values of $\Omega_m$, and the history of the ratio of matter to
the record term. Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_tests} sets out the tests that will decide it. Every later chapter in Part~\ref{part:2} uses this number.

\section{From the theorem to the cosmological coupling}\label{sec:vc_coupling}
IAM is a physically motivated extension of $\Lambda$CDM in which gravity acts as a decoherence mechanism that partitions
gravitational energy into geometric entropy (spacetime curvature) and informational entropy (quantum decoherence). The single observational consequence is a
modified growth of matter perturbations; all other physics, including the background expansion history, is identical to $\Lambda$CDM.

IAM adds to the horizon first law of Jacobson and Cai--Kim~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005} the entropy of the classical records produced as matter decoheres
gravitationally,
\begin{equation}
-dE=T_H\,d\!\left(S_{\rm geo}+S_{\rm info}\right).\label{eq:vc_firstlaw}
\end{equation}
In the IAM framework, gravitational structure formation partitions gravitational energy into two channels. The first is the geometric channel: collapsing
matter curves spacetime, depositing energy into the geometric entropy $S_{\rm geo}$ of the system's gravitational configuration. The second is the
informational channel: the same collapse decoheres quantum superpositions into classical states, producing irreversible information at a rate governed by
Landauer's principle~\cite{Landauer1961}. This information accumulates on the cosmological apparent horizon as informational entropy $S_{\rm info}$~\cite{Jacobson1995,CaiKim2005}.

The virial theorem determines the partition. At virialisation, the kinetic energy of collapsing matter --- which drives decoherence --- equals half the
magnitude of the potential energy of the gravitational configuration. The share of the matter density that enters the record term is that kinetic half:
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}=0.15765\qquad(\Omega_m=0.3153,\ \text{Planck 2018}).\label{eq:vc_beta}
\end{equation}
\prediction\ This value is not fitted to any cosmological observation. It is derived from the theorem and the measured matter density, enters every chain at
this value and is never sampled. The coupling constant $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ is derived from the virial theorem applied to cosmological structure formation,
not fitted to data. The claim is simple: the $1/2$ at the heart of that derivation is not a free choice. It is a mathematical theorem that holds for all $1/r$
potentials, and the span of its verification (Table~\ref{tab:virial_domains}) provides the physical basis for $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$.

Within IAM the interpretation is precise. The potential half $|V|/2$ is held in spacetime curvature --- the geometric channel that general relativity
describes. The kinetic half $K=|V|/2$ is the decoherence energy: the Landauer cost~\cite{Landauer1961} of converting quantum superpositions to classical outcomes at each
virialization event. Every time a dark matter halo virializes, half its gravitational binding energy goes into irreversible information production, written
on the nearest available encoding surface at thermodynamic cost $k_BT\ln2$ per bit. \interp\ This identification gives the cosmological coupling constant
immediately: $\beta_m$ is the fraction of the matter energy density that takes part in decoherence per Hubble time.

With the coupling fixed, the Planck data are a consistency test, not a measurement of $\beta_m$. In the Level~2 chains (Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}) the
posterior $\Omega_m=0.3166\pm0.0065$ gives $\beta_m/\Omega_m=0.498\pm0.010$ for the fixed $\beta_m$, and the modified growth has a minimum $\chi^2$ higher
than $\Lambda$CDM's by $0.54$ with the same number of parameters: consistent with Planck. \measured

\section{The half in a real ensemble of halos}\label{sec:vc_population}
The virial theorem is exact for systems in perfect equilibrium; cosmological halos are not perfectly equilibrated. The virial argument for
$\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ asks therefore how the equilibrium value is approached in a real ensemble of halos, and the coupling can be written as
\begin{equation}
\beta_m=\Omega_m\times f_{\rm coll}\times\eta_{\rm vir},\label{eq:vc_decompose}
\end{equation} \derived{}
where $f_{\rm coll}$ is the collapsed fraction from mass functions~\cite{Tinker2008} and $\eta_{\rm vir}$ is the virial efficiency --- the fraction of collapsed matter's
gravitational potential energy that is converted into irreversible kinetic (decoherence-producing) degrees of freedom. The virial theorem requires the
product $f_{\rm coll}\times\eta_{\rm vir}=1/2$. For a collapsed fraction $f_{\rm coll}=0.62$,
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm vir}=\frac{1}{2f_{\rm coll}}=\frac{1}{2\times0.62}=0.81 .\label{eq:vc_eta}
\end{equation} \calc{}
Equation~\eqref{eq:vc_eta} is a definition of $\eta_{\rm vir}$, not a measurement: it makes $f_{\rm coll}\eta_{\rm vir}=1/2$ hold by construction, so the
product $\Omega_mf_{\rm coll}\eta_{\rm vir}$ returns $\Omega_m/2$ whatever $f_{\rm coll}$ is. The decomposition describes how the equilibrium value is
approached in an ensemble of halos; $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ rests on the theorem and on the cross-domain record of Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}.

\paragraph{What the simulations measure.} The observable is the virial ratio of the simulated halos within the virial radius, with $K$ the total kinetic
energy and $U$ (or $W$) the gravitational potential energy. The published simulations report it as $2T/|U|$, at or above 1 and rising with mass
(Figure~\ref{fig:vc_halo}a, Table~\ref{tab:vc_halo}). \observed\ The excess over 1 is the pressure of matter still falling through the boundary; with the
surface term the ratio comes within a few to 17\,\% of equilibrium. In terms of the efficiency of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vc_eta}, the reciprocal $|U|/2T$ of these
values lies between 0.77 and 0.89 within the virial radius and between 0.85 and 0.98 with Klypin et al.'s surface-pressure correction (Power et al.'s
surface-corrected $\eta'$, centred near 0.9, gives about 1.1); it depends on mass and on how the surface term is taken, so it is a property of the
halo sample chosen, not a constant. \calc

\paragraph{Physical interpretation of the departures.} Real halos are never perfectly relaxed, and three well-understood physical contributions move the
measured ratio away from the equilibrium value:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ongoing mergers and infall:} halos at any snapshot contain material not yet at equilibrium; unrelaxed halos sit further from equilibrium than
relaxed ones (Neto et al.\ 2007).
\item \emph{Ordered angular momentum:} bulk rotation contributes kinetic energy that does not produce gravitational decoherence (Bett et al.\ 2007 quantify the
spin).
\item \emph{Non-thermal pressure support:} AGN feedback, cosmic rays, and magnetic fields contribute sub-dominant kinetic energy not sourced by gravitational
collapse.
\end{itemize}
Each moves the measured ratio away from the equilibrium value, and the surface-pressure correction of Klypin et al.\ shows that most of the excess is the
first. \interp\ The theorem holds across the simulated halos to within the infall still under way.

\paragraph{The collapsed fraction.} The collapsed fraction is not a fixed number: it depends on the smallest halo counted. Integrating the Tinker et al.\
(2008)~\cite{Tinker2008} $\Delta=200m$ mass function at $z=0$ with Planck 2018 gives $F(>10^{10.5}h^{-1}M_\odot)=0.486$ at the lower edge of its calibrated
range, $0.447$ above $10^{11}$ and $0.348$ above $10^{12}$; $f_{\rm coll}=0.62$ is reached at $M_{\rm min}\approx10^{8.2}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$, below that
range. \calc\ Because $\eta_{\rm vir}=1/(2f_{\rm coll})$ inherits this choice, so does any number assigned to the efficiency.

\paragraph{What this means.} The virial theorem prediction $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ is not a fit to cosmological data. It is a prediction from a theorem more
than 150 years old applied to gravitational energy partitioning. The published simulations show the halos at the equilibrium the theorem
requires, to within the infall still under way. A rigorous calculation of the cosmological gravitational energy partition --- the kinetic energy written
per unit time by all halos together, as the sum over the mass function of the kinetic half of each halo --- is the next formal step.
\openprob\ Chapter~\ref{ch:quantumrecords} gives the bottom-up version of that sum.

\section{The exponent of the record rate}\label{sec:vc_exponent}
The activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ is derived from the cumulative integral of the record rate on the horizon (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). Write the
rate at which matter writes records as $\dot I\propto\rho_mD^nfH$, accumulated on the horizon as $dS_{\rm info}/dt\propto\dot I/(T_HA_H)$. In matter domination
this gives $S_{\rm info}\propto a^{\,n-9/2}$; for the integral to yield the $1/a$ in the exponent, the instantaneous rate must scale as $D(a)^n$ with
\begin{equation}
n-\tfrac92=-1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n=\tfrac72\qquad\text{(analytical, pure matter domination).}\label{eq:vc_n}
\end{equation}
\derived\ The effective exponent is derived analytically in the matter-dominated limit; the real universe has radiation domination at early times and
$\Lambda$ domination at late times. The full $\Lambda$CDM integration finds the closest fit for $D^{7/2}$, with the coefficient of $1/a$ within 2\,\% and the constant within 7\,\% (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}).
\calc\ $E(a)$ itself does not depend on $n$.

The observable is the logarithmic slope of the halo collapse rate with respect to the linear growth factor, and it should be verified by measuring the
actual rate of structure formation in simulations at each redshift. Computed from the published mass functions, the slope of the collapsed fraction,
$d\ln F(>M)/d\ln D=f/F$, is strongly mass-dependent: about $0.5$ above $10^{10}$, about $1$ above $10^{12}$ and about $3$ above
$10^{14}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$ at $z=0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:vc_halo}b). \calc\ A value near $3$--$4$ is reached only for cluster-mass halos, so a slope of the collapsed
fraction is not by itself the exponent. Summing the kinetic half of every halo instead --- the energy the law prices --- gives a bottom-up exponent that
passes through $7/2$ at $z\approx3$--$4$ and averages near 4 over the matter era (Chapter~\ref{ch:quantumrecords}, Figure~\ref{fig:neff_bottomup}). \calc

\section{The activation function and the growth coupling}\label{sec:vc_activation}
The record accumulates as structure forms. Integrating the record rate through the horizon first law, from the Cai--Kim formalism~\cite{CaiKim2005} applied
to the cosmic apparent horizon, gives the activation function
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)=e^{-z}.\label{eq:vc_E}
\end{equation}
\derived\ Its properties follow directly from the physical identification: $E\to0$ as $a\to0$ (negligible at early times; $E(z{=}10)=4.5\times10^{-5}$); $E(1)=1$
today; $dE/da>0$ always (monotonically increasing, since each decoherence event is irreversible); $E(\infty)=e$, the asymptotic limit, never reached. It
reaches 10, 50 and 90\,\% of today's value at $z=2.30$, $0.69$ and $0.11$, naturally restricting the modification to late-time structure growth. The rate at
which the record is written, $dE/d\ln a=E/a$, peaks exactly at $a=1$; $E$ itself has its inflection at $a=1/2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:vc_muE}a). \calc

Matter perturbations grow with the effective coupling
\begin{equation}
\ddot\delta_m+2H\dot\delta_m-\tfrac32\Omega_mH_0^2a^{-3}\mu(a)\,\delta_m=0,\qquad
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\label{part2:eq:mu_virial}
\end{equation}
with $\mu(a)\to1$ at high redshift and $\mu(1)=1/(1+\beta_m)=0.864$ today ($\mu_0\equiv\mu(1)-1=-0.136$, from $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ with no fitting).
\derived\ The photon sector is unmodified: $\Sigma=1$ exactly, because photons travel on null geodesics ($\Delta\tau=0$) and produce no irreversible
gravitational information. Here $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ are the standard parameters of modified growth and lensing~\cite{BertschingerZukin2008}. The photon sector
is geometrically standard; the matter sector carries the accumulated thermodynamic history of the decoherence transactions.

Equation~\eqref{part2:eq:mu_virial} is the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ form, in which $\mu$ multiplies the source term; it is the form used in the Level~1 chains. The
Level~2 chains put the same term into the expansion rate felt by matter perturbations, $H_m^2=H^2+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2$, so that it enters as an additional
Hubble friction (Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}); the two give the same growth to within a few tenths of a percent \calc. Every growth number below states which form it
uses.

\paragraph{The growth deficit.} The coupling deficit $1-\mu$ is not the growth deficit. Solving the linear growth equation with the same early amplitude, the
$\mu$--$\Sigma$ form gives an $f\sigma_8$ lower than $\Lambda$CDM's by $4.25\,\%$ today, $2.19\,\%$ at $z=0.295$, $2.17\,\%$ at $z=0.3$, $1.35\,\%$ at $z=0.5$,
$0.41\,\%$ at $z=1$ and $0.13\,\%$ at $z=1.491$; the Level~2 (matter-rate) form agrees within $0.4\,\%$. \calc\ The predicted shape is a curved ramp,
\begin{equation}
f\sigma_8^{\rm IAM}(z)=f_{\rm IAM}(z)\,\sigma_8\,\frac{D_{\rm IAM}(z)}{D_{\rm IAM}(0)},\label{eq:vc_fs8}

largest today and decreasing monotonically with redshift, following the activation of $E(a)$, not a constant rescaling. \prediction\ No measurement has yet
reached the precision to confirm or exclude it (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_tests}).

\section{Two expansion rates}\label{sec:vc_h0}
Matter-sector distance indicators probe the effective expansion rate experienced by matter, which is enhanced by the $\beta_mE(a)$ term. The matter-sector
$H_0$ is
\begin{equation}
H_0^{(m)}=H_0^{(\gamma)}\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=67.16\times1.0759=72.26\ \text{km\,s}^{-1}\text{Mpc}^{-1}\label{eq:vc_h0}
\end{equation}
(Level~2 chain; with Planck's $67.36$, $72.48$). \derived\ Photon-sector probes (the CMB acoustic scale, BAO angles, lensing time delays) measure
$H_0^{(\gamma)}$; matter-sector probes (Cepheids, megamasers, surface brightness fluctuations, and the supernova ladder they calibrate) measure $H_0^{(m)}$.
The assignment follows the worldline rule: a probe sits on the matter ruler when its calibration rests on matter on timelike worldlines, and on the photon
ruler when the measured quantity is a property of light paths (Chapter~\ref{ch:sectortension}). No single $H_0$ fits both sectors in $\Lambda$CDM; IAM
predicts both endpoints from the same $\beta_m$. The two groups of measurements are not in tension with each other; they are measuring different physical
quantities, the photon-sector and the matter-sector expansion rates, predicted to differ by $\sqrt{1+\beta_m}-1=7.6\,\%$. \interp

Define the cosmological virial ratio
\begin{equation}
R(a)=\frac{\Omega_ma^{-3}}{\beta_mE(a)},\label{eq:vc_R}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
R(1)=\frac{\Omega_m}{\beta_m\,E(1)}=\frac{\Omega_m}{(\Omega_m/2)\cdot1}=2 .\label{eq:vc_R1}
\end{equation}
