\chapter{Late-time growth suppression: confrontation with Planck and large-scale structure}\label{ch:latetime}

\section*{Summary}
This chapter tests a scale-independent, late-time suppression of linear structure growth within the standard $\mu$--$\Sigma$ framework of
modified gravity. The informational term imposes $\mu(a)<1$ for matter perturbations while preserving $\Sigma(a)=1$, which leaves photon geodesics
and the CMB acoustic structure unmodified. The time dependence is governed by the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$, which vanishes at early
times and reaches unity at $a=1$, so recombination-era physics is preserved by construction. For the coupling $\beta=\Omega_m/2$ the present-day
gravitational coupling is $\mu(z{=}0)=0.864$, a $13.6\,\%$ reduction of the effective Newton constant that governs linear growth.

The parameterisation is implemented in MGCAMB~\cite{Zhao2009MGCAMB,Wang2023MGCAMB} and tested in a suite of twelve Markov chain Monte Carlo
analyses run with Cobaya~\cite{Torrado2021}: the Planck 2018 likelihoods (TT, TE, EE and lensing)~\cite{Planck2018V,Planck2018VIII} alone and
combined in turn with growth-rate data from redshift-space distortions (RSD), with baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), and with the Pantheon+ type Ia
supernovae~\cite{Scolnic2022,Brout2022}. With the amplitude fixed at the prediction, IAM fits every combination as well as
$\Lambda$CDM, $\Delta\chi^2$ between $+0.56$ and $+1.73$ for the same number of parameters, and every standard cosmological parameter is
unchanged within its uncertainty. When growth-rate data are included (Planck + RSD) the fixed prediction gives $\Delta\chi^2=+0.56$. When the
amplitude is allowed to float, the posterior of $\mu_0$ rises to the upper edge of its prior ($+0.2$) in every combination; the prediction lies
inside the central $90\,\%$ credible interval in all four, with $10$--$21\,\%$ of the posterior below it. The model shifts $\sigma_8$ downward,
from $0.813$ to $0.800$, without degrading the CMB fit. BAO angles and supernova distances are unaffected, as expected for a change that
leaves the background expansion and $\Sigma=1$ untouched. The survey forecast for this amplitude is in Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}. All chains, configuration files and analysis scripts are in the repository
(\texttt{mgcamb\_validation/}).

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:lt_intro}
The $\Lambda$CDM model provides an excellent fit to the CMB anisotropies~\cite{Planck2018VI} and to large-scale structure. Nevertheless, persistent
discrepancies between early- and late-time determinations of the matter clustering amplitude, usually expressed through $\sigma_8$ and
$S_8\equiv\sigma_8(\Omega_m/0.3)^{0.5}$, have motivated controlled late-time modifications of cosmic growth that preserve early-universe consistency
\cite{DESY3,Heymans2021,HSCY3}. Weak-lensing surveys have reported $\sigma_8$ values lower than the Planck $\Lambda$CDM prediction
$\sigma_8\approx0.81$~\cite{Planck2018VI}, a difference at the 2--3$\sigma$ level that persisted across independent experiments and analysis
pipelines (DES Y3, KiDS-1000 and HSC Y3~\cite{DESY3,Asgari2021,Heymans2021,HSCY3}) \observed. The completed KiDS survey (KiDS-Legacy) now finds
$S_8=0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$, in agreement with Planck~\cite{Wright2025} \observed.

A general phenomenological approach encodes departures from general relativity (GR) through the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ parameterisation (Bean and
Tangmatitham 2010~\cite{BeanTangmatitham2010}; Daniel et al.\ 2010~\cite{Daniel2010}; Pogosian and Silvestri 2016~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016}), in
which $\mu(a)$ multiplies the source of $\Psi$, the potential that matter falls in, and $\Sigma(a)$ modifies the lensing potential $(\Phi+\Psi)/2$. The framework is
used by the major surveys, among them DES~\cite{DESY3Ext}, KiDS~\cite{Heymans2021} and Euclid~\cite{Frusciante2025}. Keeping $\Sigma(a)=1$
preserves photon geodesics and the CMB acoustic structure while allowing growth to change.

This chapter examines one realisation within that framework: a scale-independent, late-time suppression with $\mu(a)<1$. The functional form is
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta\,E(a)\,H_0^2},
\label{eq:lt_mu}\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
E(a)=\exp\!\left(1-\frac1a\right)
\label{eq:lt_activation}\end{equation}
is an activation function that vanishes as $a\to0$ and reaches unity at $a=1$. This ensures a negligible modification during radiation domination
and at recombination. Because $H_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a{=}1)=H_0$, the present-day value is
\begin{equation}
\mu(z{=}0)=\frac{1}{1+\beta}=\frac{1}{1.15765}=0.864\qquad(\beta=\Omega_m/2=0.15765,\ \Omega_m=0.3153),
\label{eq:lt_mu0}\end{equation}
a $13.6\,\%$ suppression of the effective gravitational coupling for matter perturbations \calc. (The factor $H_0^2$ in the denominator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_mu} makes the two terms dimensionally alike; $\beta E(a)$ is the extra term in units of today's critical density.)

\paragraph{Where the form comes from.} The functional form of $E(a)$ follows from the hypothesis that late-time structure formation adds an
informational entropy contribution to the matter sector. The activation function arises from integrating the cumulative decoherence rate weighted by
the thermodynamics of the cosmic horizon (Bekenstein--Hawking entropy~\cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}, Gibbons--Hawking
temperature~\cite{GibbonsHawking1977} and Landauer's principle~\cite{Landauer1961}); the full derivation is in Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} and
Appendix~\ref{app:der:activation}. The step that fixes the exponent is short. During matter domination the record surface density on the apparent
horizon scales as $a^{-2}$, so the logarithm of the accumulated record fraction, counted back from today, is
\begin{equation}
\ln E(a)=-\int_a^1\frac{da'}{a'^2}=1-\frac1a,\qquad E(1)=1,\qquad \lim_{a\to0}E(a)=0,\qquad E=e^{-z}.
\label{eq:lt_lnE}\end{equation}
\derived\ (given the $a^{-2}$ scaling of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). The coupling $\beta=\Omega_m/2$ follows from the virial theorem applied to the
partition of gravitational energy between geometric curvature and informational entropy (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}); the factor $1/2$ is
\derived\ there and $\Omega_m$ is \measured\ by Planck. The rational form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_mu} then follows from adding the informational entropy to
the Cai--Kim horizon first law~\cite{CaiKim2005} (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). None of these ingredients is fitted to growth data.

\interp\ Independently of that derivation, the rational form is the minimal single-parameter realisation among monotonic activation functions that
vanish at early times and saturate at $a=1$: it preserves $\mu\to1$ at high redshift by construction while fixing $\mu(z{=}0)$ through the single
amplitude $\beta$. This chapter is concerned with the phenomenology within the standard $\mu$--$\Sigma$ framework; no covariant action is assumed. The
parameterisation is implemented with the public MGCAMB code (Wang et al.\ 2023~\cite{Wang2023MGCAMB}), which provides native $\mu$--$\Sigma$ support
inside the CAMB Boltzmann solver (Lewis, Challinor and Lasenby 2000~\cite{Lewis2000CAMB}).

\paragraph{Where it sits among modified-gravity models.} The signature $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$ has received less attention than $\mu>1$. $f(R)$
gravity~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2008} and the normal branch of the DGP braneworld~\cite{KoyamaMaartens2006} predict enhanced growth, $\mu>1$, and in
Horndeski scalar--tensor theories $\mu-1$ and $\Sigma-1$ of opposite sign are strongly disfavoured~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016}. The
self-accelerating branch of DGP does give $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$, but it carries a ghost~\cite{Koyama2007} and is in strong tension with growth and
distance data~\cite{Fang2008} \observed. A suppression shifts $\sigma_8$ downward, in contrast to most scenarios in the current literature, and
places the model in a less explored region of the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ plane.

Section~\ref{sec:lt_model} defines the model and its implementation; Section~\ref{sec:lt_data} describes the data and the method;
Section~\ref{sec:lt_results} presents the results of all twelve chains; Section~\ref{sec:lt_discussion} discusses them, including what the next
surveys can do; Section~\ref{sec:lt_conclusions} concludes.

\section{Model definition and implementation}\label{sec:lt_model}
\subsection{Modified Poisson equations}
In the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ framework the modified Poisson equations in Fourier space are
\begin{align}
k^2\Psi&=-4\pi G\,\mu(a)\,a^2\bar\rho_m\delta_m,\label{eq:lt_poisson}\\
k^2(\Phi+\Psi)&=-8\pi G\,\Sigma(a)\,a^2\bar\rho_m\delta_m,\label{eq:lt_lensing}
\end{align}
where $\Psi$ (time--time) and $\Phi$ (space--space) are the scalar metric potentials in conformal Newtonian gauge, $ds^2=a^2[-(1+2\Psi)d\tau^2+(1-2\Phi)d\mathbf x^2]$, $\bar\rho_m$ is the mean matter density and $\delta_m$ the
matter density contrast. The function $\mu(a)$ acts on $\Psi$, the potential that matter falls in, and so modifies the growth of density perturbations, while $\Sigma(a)$ modifies the lensing potential
$(\Phi+\Psi)/2$. General relativity is $\mu=\Sigma=1$.

$\Sigma(a)=1$ is imposed identically, so photon geodesics, gravitational lensing and the CMB acoustic structure remain standard. At fixed primordial
amplitude the CMB lensing power changes by less than $0.5\,\%$: a Limber estimate gives $0.05$--$0.3\,\%$ for $30\le L\le1000$ \calc. The ISW
contribution at $\ell<30$ changes only through the late-time decay of the potentials, which is modified only at $z\lesssim2$; it remains small
against cosmic variance, which is at least $\sqrt{2/(2\ell+1)}=18\,\%$ for $\ell\le30$ \calc. The function $\mu(a)$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_mu}.

\subsection{MGCAMB implementation}
The model is implemented with MGCAMB v1.5.2 (Wang et al.\ 2023~\cite{Wang2023MGCAMB}), which provides a native $\mu$--$\Sigma$ parameterisation
inside CAMB. MGCAMB's pure-MG mode implements the time dependence used by DES~\cite{DESY3Ext},
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=1+\mu_0\,\frac{\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)}{\Omega_\Lambda},\qquad\Sigma(a)=1+\Sigma_0\,\frac{\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)}{\Omega_\Lambda},
\label{eq:lt_mgcamb}\end{equation}
where $\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)=\Omega_\Lambda/[\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_\Lambda]$ is the dark-energy density fraction (radiation neglected at late times). The
division by $\Omega_\Lambda$ (MGCAMB \texttt{mgcamb.f90}, \texttt{MGCAMB\_Mu = 1 + mu0*omegaDE\_t/omegav}) makes $\Omega_{\rm DE}(1)/\Omega_\Lambda=1$,
so that $\mu_0$ is the deviation from GR at $z=0$: $\mu(z{=}0)=1+\mu_0$ \derived.

To match the model at $z=0$ the runs set $\mu_0=-0.13495$ and $\Sigma_0=0$. The exact form gives $\mu_0=-\beta/(1+\beta)=-0.13618$; the value used
in the runs corresponds to $\beta=0.1560$. The difference, $0.0012$, is about one per cent of the width of the free-$\mu_0$ posterior
($\sigma=0.125$, Planck + RSD) and changes no result \calc. Equation~\eqref{eq:lt_mgcamb} reproduces the exact form at $z=0$ ($0.865$ against $0.864$)
and at $z\gg1$. In between it suppresses slightly more: the difference in $\mu$ reaches $2.8\,\%$ near $z\approx0.65$, is $2.5\,\%$ at $z=1$ and
$1.2\,\%$ at $z=2$, and falls below $1\,\%$ above $z\approx2.3$ (Table~\ref{tab:lt_mu_compare}) \calc. The difference arises because $E(a)$ and
$\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)$ have different transition profiles; both vanish at early times and reach their maximum at $z=0$.

\section{Data and methodology}\label{sec:lt_data}
\subsection{Data sets}
\paragraph{Planck 2018 CMB.} The Planck 2018 likelihoods~\cite{Planck2018V,Planck2018VIII}: low-$\ell$ temperature and polarisation
(\texttt{lowl.TT}, \texttt{lowl.EE}), high-$\ell$ temperature and polarisation (\texttt{plik\_lite} TTTEEE) and CMB lensing. The variants differ
between combinations: the Planck-only and Planck + RSD chains use \texttt{plik\_lite\_native} with \texttt{lensing.CMBMarged}; the Planck + BAO and
Planck + Pantheon+ chains use \texttt{plik\_lite} with \texttt{lensing.native}. Each $\Delta\chi^2$ below is taken against the $\Lambda$CDM chain with the
same likelihoods, so each comparison stands; the four $\Delta\chi^2$ values are not on one common likelihood.

\paragraph{Redshift-space distortions (RSD).} The growth-rate measurements in the ``Planck + RSD'' chains come from the BOSS DR12 final consensus
likelihood (Alam et al.\ 2017~\cite{Alam2017}): BAO distances together with $f\sigma_8$ at $z=0.38$, $0.51$ and $0.61$, with their joint covariance.
These directly probe the combination of growth rate and clustering amplitude that $\mu(a)$ changes. The same chains add the eBOSS DR16 ELG, quasar
and LRG likelihoods~\cite{Alam2021}, which are BAO distances only.

\paragraph{Baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO).} The ``Planck + BAO'' chains use the 6dFGS BAO measurement (Beutler et al.\ 2011~\cite{Beutler2011BAO}),
the SDSS DR7 main galaxy sample (Ross et al.\ 2015~\cite{Ross2015MGS}) and the BOSS DR12 consensus BAO distances~\cite{Alam2017}. The sector
assignment matters here. The BAO feature is imprinted in the matter distribution, but its angular position is measured with photons travelling on
null geodesics. With $\Sigma=1$ and an unmodified background, the angular-diameter distance $D_A(z)$, and hence the BAO angular scale
$\theta_{\rm BAO}$, are those of $\Lambda$CDM. The amplitude of the BAO feature may be affected by the modified growth rate, but its geometric position,
the primary BAO observable, is not.

\paragraph{Type Ia supernovae.} The Pantheon+ sample of 1701 light curves in $0.001<z<2.26$ (Scolnic et al.\ 2022~\cite{Scolnic2022}; Brout et al.\
2022~\cite{Brout2022}) \observed. Supernova hosts are matter-sector objects (Chapter~\ref{ch:dsvalidation}); in this perturbation-level model, which leaves
the background expansion unchanged, their luminosity distances are those of $\Lambda$CDM.

\subsection{Analysis strategy}
Twelve chains are run, organised into four data combinations, each with three model configurations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Fixed $\mu_0$:} $\mu_0=-0.135$, the predicted value, with no free parameter beyond $\Lambda$CDM.
\item \emph{Floating $\mu_0$:} $\mu_0$ sampled with a flat prior $\mu_0\in[-0.5,+0.2]$ (one additional parameter); the chain starts at the prediction
(reference $-0.135$, proposal width $0.05$).
\item \emph{$\Lambda$CDM baseline:} $\mu_0=0$, $\Sigma_0=0$ (standard GR, the same MGCAMB code).
\end{enumerate}
The four combinations are Planck alone (Runs A, B, C), Planck + RSD (D, E, F), Planck + BAO (G, H, I) and Planck + Pantheon+ (J, K, L). The six
standard parameters $\{\Omega_bh^2,\Omega_ch^2,H_0,\tau,\ln(10^{10}A_s),n_s\}$ are sampled in every run with flat priors $\Omega_bh^2\in[0.020,0.025]$,
$\Omega_ch^2\in[0.10,0.14]$, $H_0\in[60,80]$, $\tau\in[0.01,0.12]$, $\ln(10^{10}A_s)\in[2.6,3.5]$ and $n_s\in[0.92,1.0]$; $\sigma_8$, $\Omega_\Lambda$
and $S_8$ are derived. All chains use the Cobaya sampler (Torrado and Lewis 2021~\cite{Torrado2021}) with the Gelman--Rubin criterion
$R-1<0.01$~\cite{Gelman1992} (\texttt{Rminus1\_stop = 0.01}, \texttt{Rminus1\_cl\_stop = 0.15}); every final chain stops at $R-1\le0.0099$
(Table~\ref{tab:lt_chains}). The $\Lambda$CDM baselines use the same MGCAMB code with $\mu_0=\Sigma_0=0$, so any difference arises from the gravity
parameter alone and not from code-level systematics. All statistics below are from the final chain files with the first $30\,\%$ of each file
removed as burn-in, weighted.

\section{Results}\label{sec:lt_results}
$\Delta\chi^2$ is the lowest $\chi^2$ among the samples kept after the $30\,\%$ burn-in of the fixed-$\mu_0$ chain minus that of its $\Lambda$CDM chain; a positive value means the informational
term fits slightly worse. The two models have the same number of free parameters, so the comparison is a likelihood ratio,
$\mathcal L_{\rm IAM}/\mathcal L_{\Lambda\rm CDM}=e^{-\Delta\chi^2/2}$, not a $\chi^2_1$ test. For the free-$\mu_0$ chains the lowest $\chi^2$ depends on
how deeply the chain sampled its minimum and is not used as a test; the free amplitude is summarised by its posterior
(Section~\ref{sec:lt_free}).

\subsection{Planck alone (Runs A, B, C)}
Table~\ref{tab:lt_planck} gives the marginalised constraints from Planck alone. The fixed prediction $\mu_0=-0.135$ gives $\Delta\chi^2=+0.96$
relative to $\Lambda$CDM, a likelihood ratio of $0.62$ \measured. With the same number of free parameters, IAM's own equations reproduce the Planck data with no added parameter.
When $\mu_0$ floats (Run B) the posterior is consistent with GR and with the prediction (Table~\ref{tab:lt_free}); it is broad, reflecting the limited
sensitivity of the CMB alone to late-time growth when $\Sigma=1$.

\subsection{Planck + RSD (Runs D, E, F)}
Planck with growth-rate data gives a direct test of the modified coupling, since $f\sigma_8(z)$ is sensitive to $\mu(a)$. Table~\ref{tab:lt_rsd}
gives the results. The fixed prediction gives $\Delta\chi^2=+0.56$ (likelihood ratio $0.75$), lower than with Planck alone: the growth-rate data do
not penalise the suppression \measured.

The principal shift is in $\sigma_8$: $0.8004\pm0.0060$ with the informational term against $0.8133\pm0.0060$ for $\Lambda$CDM, lower by $0.013$
($-1.6\,\%$) \measured. Every other cosmological parameter is unchanged within its uncertainty, so the change in $\mu$ acts on growth without opening
parameter degeneracies (Fig.~\ref{fig:lt_post}). Figure~\ref{fig:lt_fs8} shows the growth rate: with the term, $f\sigma_8$ is lower than in
$\Lambda$CDM at every redshift.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:lt_discussion}
\subsection{Compatibility with current data}
The principal result is that a zero-parameter prediction, a $13.6\,\%$ suppression of the effective gravitational coupling at $z=0$ from
$\beta=\Omega_m/2$, passes the full Planck 2018 likelihood without tension. The values $\Delta\chi^2=+0.96$ (Planck) and $+0.56$ (Planck + RSD)
correspond to likelihood ratios of $0.62$ and $0.75$ for a model with the same number of free parameters: IAM's own equations reproduce the data with no added parameter.
Most proposed late-time modifications of gravity are excluded or tightly constrained by Planck; the compatibility of this prediction with current
data was not guaranteed in advance and is a meaningful consistency check \interp.

The result is stable across data combinations. Adding growth-rate data, which probe the modification directly, lowers $\Delta\chi^2$ rather than
raising it: the predicted growth suppression is compatible with the measured $f\sigma_8(z)$ \measured.

\subsection{The $\sigma_8$ shift}
The model shifts $\sigma_8$ downward from $\approx0.813$ to $\approx0.800$ in every combination. This $1.6\,\%$ reduction is a direct consequence of
$\mu<1$: the weaker source term in the Poisson equation reduces the growth rate and hence $\sigma_8$, while the primary CMB spectrum is unchanged
because $\Sigma=1$. Its size is set entirely by the predicted $\mu_0=-0.135$; it is not fitted to any late-time clustering datum, and its
direction, toward the values reported by weak-lensing surveys, was not an input \prediction.

For comparison, KiDS-1000 cosmic shear reports $S_8=0.759^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$ (Asgari et al.\ 2021~\cite{Asgari2021}), the KiDS-1000 multi-probe
analysis $S_8=0.766^{+0.020}_{-0.014}$ (Heymans et al.\ 2021~\cite{Heymans2021}) and DES Y3 $3\times2$pt $S_8=0.776\pm0.017$ (Abbott et al.\
2022~\cite{DESY3}), all below the Planck $\Lambda$CDM value; KiDS-Legacy gives $0.815^{+0.016}_{-0.021}$~\cite{Wright2025} \observed. Whether the
shift produced by $\mu_0=-0.135$ quantitatively reduces the difference between CMB and weak-lensing constraints requires a joint analysis with the
KiDS and DES likelihoods \openprob.

\subsection{Comparison with other modified-gravity results}
The combination $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ has received less observational attention than $\mu>1$. The most studied theories, $f(R)$ gravity, the normal
branch of DGP and Horndeski scalar--tensor theories, generically produce $\mu\ge1$~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016}; the self-accelerating DGP branch,
which gives $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$, is excluded~\cite{Koyama2007,Fang2008}. A suppression with $\Sigma$ unmodified is observationally distinguishable
from all of them.

\observed\ Current constraints in the same parameterisation are already informative. DES Y3 alone does not constrain $\mu_0$; DES Y3 with external
data gives $\mu_0=0.08^{+0.21}_{-0.19}$ (Abbott et al.\ 2023~\cite{DESY3Ext}). ACT + WMAP + SDSS + supernovae give $\mu_0=0.02\pm0.19$ (Andrade et al.\
2024~\cite{Andrade2024}). The DESI 2024 full-shape analysis gives $\mu_0=0.11^{+0.45}_{-0.54}$ with BAO and BBN, and $0.04\pm0.22$ with CMB and DES Y3
(DESI Collaboration 2025~\cite{DESI2024VII}). The predicted $\mu_0=-0.136$ lies inside the allowed region of every one.

\subsection{What next-generation surveys can do}
With $\mu_0$ free, Planck + RSD gives $\sigma(\mu_0)=0.125$ (chain E), so at present precision the prediction is $1.1\sigma$ from general relativity
\calc. The forecast for Euclid, Planck lensing and DESI, made with the IAM $\mu(z)$ itself, is in Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}. The MGCAMB form
used in this chapter is the $\Omega_{\rm DE}$-tracking form in which Euclid's own forecasts are made.

\subsection{Limitations and future directions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Phenomenological framework.} Only a $\mu$--$\Sigma$ parameterisation is tested here. The activation function $E(a)$ and the coupling
$\beta=\Omega_m/2$ come from horizon thermodynamics and the virial theorem (Chapters~\ref{ch:theory} and \ref{ch:virial}); no covariant action is
assumed. The results stand independently of that derivation: any model with $\mu_0\approx-0.135$ and $\Sigma=1$ faces the same tests.
\item \emph{Perturbation level only.} The background (the Friedmann equation) is $\Lambda$CDM throughout. The background-level alternative is
tested with the coded mechanism in Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}.
\item \emph{MGCAMB approximation.} The form $\mu(a)=1+\mu_0\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)/\Omega_\Lambda$ approximates but does not reproduce
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lt_mu}. The difference, up to $2.8\,\%$ near $z\approx0.65$, is below present uncertainties and becomes relevant at Euclid precision.
\item \emph{Linear scales only.} Nonlinear structure formation (cluster counts, small-scale lensing) is not included; extending $\mu$ to the
nonlinear regime requires N-body simulations or validated fitting functions.
\item \emph{Weak-lensing likelihoods.} The $\sigma_8$ shift is compared with the reported CMB--lensing difference but not tested directly against
the KiDS, DES or HSC likelihoods; a joint analysis is the next step.
\item \emph{Prior range of the free amplitude.} The free-$\mu_0$ posteriors reach the upper prior edge; a wider prior is needed to bound them from
above.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:lt_conclusions}
A comprehensive perturbation-level test of a scale-independent, late-time growth suppression, $\mu_0=-0.135$ within the standard $\mu$--$\Sigma$
framework, gives these findings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A fixed $13.6\,\%$ suppression of the gravitational coupling at $z=0$, with no free parameter, fits Planck 2018 as well as $\Lambda$CDM
($\Delta\chi^2=+0.96$) and remains so with growth-rate data added ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.56$).
\item $\sigma_8$ moves from $\approx0.813$ to $\approx0.800$ without degrading any other cosmological parameter.
\item With $\mu_0$ free, the prediction lies inside the central $90\,\%$ credible interval in every combination; the posterior leans toward positive
$\mu_0$ and reaches the prior edge.
\item BAO and supernova distances are unaffected, as a perturbation-level change with $\Sigma=1$ and an unmodified background requires.
\item The survey forecast with the IAM $\mu(z)$ itself is in Section~\ref{sec:sp_euclid}.
\end{enumerate}
The parameterisation $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ lies in a less explored region of the modified-gravity parameter space than $f(R)$, DGP and most
scalar--tensor theories. Current data do not disfavour it, and it makes specific predictions for the next surveys. The mechanism itself, coded
directly into the Boltzmann solver rather than imposed as a function, is the subject of Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}.

The data are public and the test is sharp. A cosmologist can rerun these chains, and a relativist can take the mechanism beyond the parameterisation, which is exactly what Chapter~\ref{ch:level2} begins.
