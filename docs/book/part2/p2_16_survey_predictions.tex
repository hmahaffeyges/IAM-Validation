\chapter{What the surveys will measure}\label{ch:surveys}

\section*{The place}
The cosmic horizon, where matter's records are written as it collapses into structure (Chapter~\ref{ch:surfaces}). The cost of writing weakens the
coupling felt by matter, $\mu<1$, and leaves light untouched, $\Sigma=1$. This chapter writes down, before the data arrive, the numbers that Euclid,
DESI and the next surveys will measure, and the precision each test needs.

\section{The coupling at survey redshifts}
Everything below follows from one line with no free parameter (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}):
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\qquad E(a)=e^{1-1/a},\qquad\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2},\qquad\Sigma(a)=1,
\label{eq:sp_mu}\end{equation}
with $\Omega_m=0.3153$, so $\beta_m=0.1577$ and $\mu_0\equiv\mu(z{=}0)-1=-0.136$. \prediction The coupling is not the growth. A survey measures the integrated
growth $D$ and the growth rate $f\sigma_8$, which follow from the linear growth equation with the same early-time amplitude as $\Lambda$CDM.

The growth rate carries the signal: $f\sigma_8$ is $4.25\,\%$ low today and $2.17\,\%$ low at $z=0.3$, while the amplitude $D$ is lower by under
$1\,\%$. With $\Sigma=1$ the lensing potential follows the matter density, so $\Delta\Phi/\Phi=\Delta D/D$. The ramp is curved, not a constant
rescaling, and it is gone by $z\approx2$.

\section{What light sees}
$\Sigma=1$ fixes the relation between the lensing potential and the matter density; it does not make lensing observables identical to $\Lambda$CDM,
because the density itself grows more slowly.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{CMB lensing.} A Limber estimate of $C_L^{\phi\phi}$ is lower by $0.08\,\%$ at fixed primordial amplitude, below any current or planned
measurement (Chapter~\ref{ch:sectortension}). \calc
\item \emph{$E_G$.} $E_G=\Omega_{m0}\Sigma/f$ rises because $f$ falls while lensing does not: $+1.8\,\%$ at $z=0.3$, $+3.6\,\%$ today. \calc
\item \emph{The integrated Sachs--Wolfe effect.} Slower growth makes the potentials decay faster. The ISW source $(1-f)D/a$ is larger by $3.4\,\%$ at
$z\le0.5$ and $2.4\,\%$ at $z=1$; weighted uniformly over $0.05<z<1.5$, the ISW--galaxy cross-correlation amplitude is
\begin{equation}A_{\rm ISW}^{\rm IAM}/A_{\rm ISW}^{\Lambda\rm CDM}\approx1.03.\label{eq:sp_isw}\end{equation}
\calc\ The sign is opposite to that of $f(R)$ gravity, whose enhanced growth slows the decay of the potentials. Current cross-correlations detect the
ISW signal at about the $4\sigma$ level~\cite{Giannantonio2008,Planck2015ISW}, an amplitude error near $25\,\%$; a $3\,\%$ enhancement is far below
it. The ISW test is a sign test for the future, not a near-term discriminator. \observed
\end{itemize}

\section{Current constraints on $\mu_0$}
\observed\ In the same parameterisation (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}): DES Y3 with external data, $\mu_0=0.08^{+0.21}_{-0.19}$~\cite{DESY3Ext} (DES Y3
alone does not constrain $\mu_0$); DESI DR1 full-shape clustering alone, $0.11^{+0.45}_{-0.54}$~\cite{DESI2024VII}; ACT + WMAP + SDSS + supernovae,
$0.02\pm0.19$~\cite{Andrade2024}. Our free-$\mu_0$ chains reach the upper prior edge ($+0.2$), so they are quoted as a median and a one-sided $90\,\%$ bound: Planck, median $+0.059$, lower
bound $-0.305$; Planck + RSD, median $+0.064$, lower bound $-0.204$. The prediction $\mu_0=-0.136$ lies inside every one of these, and so does general
relativity. \fitted\ No present measurement discriminates.

\section{The timeline}
Euclid's complete first data release, with the clustering and lensing products needed for cosmology, is scheduled for mid-2027; no forecast of
$\sigma(\mu_0)$ for it has been published, so none is quoted here. For the full survey: Euclid's own forecasts are made for $\mu(z)=1+\mu_0\,\Omega_{\rm DE}(z)/\Omega_{\rm DE}(0)$~\cite{Albuquerque2025,Frusciante2025}. The 68\,\% error
on $1+\mu_0$ is $23.3\,\%$ with conservative scale cuts and all primary probes, $4\,\%$ for weak lensing and photometric clustering alone taken to
$k\approx4\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, where the nonlinear modelling has been validated to 1\,\%, and of order $1\,\%$ for all probes with those scales.
\observed{} The IAM $\mu(z)$ falls off with redshift faster than that template: for the same $\mu_0=-0.136$ it gives $\mu=0.982$ at $z=1$ against
the template's $0.958$, so the significance is not $|\mu_0|/\sigma$. Matching the linear-growth deficit over $0<z<2$ gives a template-equivalent
$\mu_0\approx-0.07$ ($-0.03$ over the spectroscopic bins alone): about $0.3\sigma$ with conservative cuts, $1.8\sigma$ at $\sigma(\mu_0)=0.04$ and about
$7\sigma$ at one per cent \calc{} (\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}verification/\allowbreak{}scripts/\allowbreak{}verify\_euclid\_template.py}). A forecast made
with the IAM $\mu(z)$ itself, and for Euclid with CMB lensing and spectroscopic growth, is the first calculation still to be done. \openprob

Redshift-space distortions test the shape directly: the deficit falls from $4.25\,\%$ at $z=0$ to $1.35\,\%$ at $z=0.5$ and $0.41\,\%$ at $z=1$
(Section~\ref{sec:sp_falsify}).

\section{Standard sirens}
Gravitational waves are emitted by matter on timelike worldlines, and the host galaxies trace the matter flow. The matter-sector rate is
\begin{equation}H_0^{\rm m}=H_0^{\gamma}\sqrt{1+\beta_m}=67.16\times\sqrt{1.15765}=72.26~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},\label{eq:sp_siren}\end{equation}
against $H_0^{\gamma}=67.16$ for light (Level~2 chains, Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}). \calc\ GW170817 alone gives
$70.0^{+12.0}_{-8.0}$~\cite{Abbott2017Siren}, consistent with both. \observed\ Separating the two values at $3\sigma$ needs
$\sigma(H_0)\le1.70~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $2.4\,\%$ of $72.26$, from a siren population. \calc\ SH0ES
($73.04\pm1.04$~\cite{Riess2022}) is $0.75\sigma$ from $72.26$.

\section{What would falsify it}\label{sec:sp_falsify}
\prediction\ Any of the following falsifies the prediction:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mu_0$ measured consistent with $0$ and excluding $-0.136$ (for example $\mu_0>-0.05$ at $95\,\%$).
\item $\Sigma\neq1$ detected at any redshift.
\item $f\sigma_8(z)$ following $\Lambda$CDM to better than the predicted $2$--$4\,\%$ at $z<0.3$, or a deficit that is a constant factor rather than the ramp
of the first table.
\item Any scale dependence of $\mu$ or $\Sigma$: Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_mu} depends on $a$ only.
\item $A_{\rm ISW}<1$ at significance, or $E_G(z)$ at or below $\Lambda$CDM at sufficient precision.
\item A siren population precise enough to separate the two rates that excludes $72.26$.
\end{itemize}
