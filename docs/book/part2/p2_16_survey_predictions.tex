\chapter{What the surveys will measure}\label{ch:surveys}

\section*{The place}
The cosmic horizon, where matter's records are written as it collapses into structure (Chapter~\ref{ch:surfaces}). The cost of writing weakens the
coupling felt by matter, $\mu<1$, and leaves light untouched, $\Sigma=1$. This chapter writes down, before the data arrive, the numbers that Euclid,
DESI and the next surveys will measure, and the precision each test needs. IAM produces specific, parameter-free predictions for forthcoming
cosmological surveys that differ from both $\Lambda$CDM and generic modified gravity theories. These are forecasts, not guarantees. Survey
results may confirm, partially confirm, or challenge these predictions. Any outcome provides useful information about the model. The purpose of
this chapter is to make the predictions explicit before the data arrive so that the comparison is unambiguous.

\section{The dual-sector signature in the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ language}\label{sec:sp_mapping}
IAM adds a single Hubble friction term to the matter-sector perturbation equations (Chapters~\ref{ch:theory} and~\ref{ch:level2}), producing a
dual-sector structure in which the effective matter coupling $\mu(z)<1$ while the lensing coupling $\Sigma=1$ exactly. In the standard $\mu$--$\Sigma$
modified gravity parametrization~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2016}, with the metric
$ds^2=-a^2(1+2\Psi)d\tau^2+a^2(1-2\Phi)d\mathbf{x}^2$ and the comoving density contrast $\Delta$,
\begin{equation}k^2\Psi=-4\pi G\,a^2\,\mu(a,k)\,\bar\rho\,\Delta,\qquad k^2(\Phi+\Psi)=-8\pi G\,a^2\,\Sigma(a,k)\,\bar\rho\,\Delta .\label{eq:sp_poisson}\end{equation}
$\mu$ governs how matter falls; $\Sigma$ governs how light is bent. IAM maps onto this description as
\begin{equation}
\mu(a)=\frac{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)}{H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)+\beta_mE(a)H_0^2},\qquad E(a)=e^{1-1/a},\qquad\beta_m=\frac{\Omega_m}{2},\qquad\Sigma(a)=1,
\label{eq:sp_mu}\end{equation}
where $H^2_{\Lambda\rm CDM}(a)=H_0^2(\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_\Lambda)$, $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (virial theorem), and $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ (horizon
thermodynamics). No parameters are fitted; everything below follows from this line (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}). With $\Omega_m=0.3153$, $\beta_m=0.15765$ and
\begin{equation}\mu_0\equiv\mu(z{=}0)-1=-\frac{\beta_m}{1+\beta_m}=-0.136 .\label{eq:sp_mu0}\end{equation}
\prediction\ $\beta_m$ is fixed at $\Omega_m/2$ by the virial partition (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_law}) and is not fitted in any chain. The MGCAMB runs of
Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime} use the tracking form with $\mu_0=-0.13495$; the exact Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_mu} gives $-0.13618$. \calc

The Level~2 modified-CAMB chains (Chapter~\ref{ch:level2}) are consistent with Planck 2018 CMB data, with $\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$ relative to
$\Lambda$CDM and $\sigma_8=0.800$, suppressed in the direction favored by weak lensing surveys. \measured\ This chapter records the explicit
numerical predictions that forthcoming surveys will test, and the precision each survey needs to test them.

\section{The shape of $\mu(z)$}\label{sec:sp_shape}
Three forms of $\mu(z)$ with the same value today can be told apart only by their redshift dependence. The exact form \eqref{eq:sp_mu} and the
MGCAMB tracking form $\mu=1+\mu_0\,\Omega_{\rm DE}(a)/\Omega_\Lambda$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:latetime}) agree at $z=0$ ($0.864$ and $0.865$) and at high
redshift; in between the tracking form suppresses more, by up to $0.027$ in $\mu$ ($2.8\,\%$) near $z\approx0.65$--$0.7$ (Figure~\ref{fig:survey_transition}f).
A constant $\mu$ and a power law in $a$ give different shapes again. \calc

A tomographic reconstruction of $\mu(z)$ in redshift bins tests whether the data prefer the specific shape of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_mu} over a
constant $\mu$ or a power law. Because the shape is fixed by $\beta_m$ and $E(a)$, such a reconstruction measures $\beta_m$ from the shape alone and
tests the virial value $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ directly. \prediction\ How precisely Euclid's bins can do this needs a forecast made with the exact
$\mu(z)$ and Euclid's published binning and errors; making that forecast is the next step. \openprob

\section{What light sees}\label{sec:sp_light}
$\Sigma=1$ fixes the relation between the lensing potential and the matter density; it does not make lensing observables identical to $\Lambda$CDM,
because the density itself grows more slowly.

\subsection{The integrated Sachs--Wolfe effect}
The integrated Sachs--Wolfe (ISW) effect arises from time evolution of gravitational potentials along the photon path. In the metric of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_poisson},
\begin{equation}\left.\frac{\Delta T}{T}\right|_{\rm ISW}=\int\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\tau}\right)d\tau ,\label{eq:sp_iswT}\end{equation}
which reduces to $2\int\partial\Phi/\partial\tau\,d\tau$ when $\Phi=\Psi$; in the convention where the spatial potential carries the
opposite sign, $\Phi\to-\Phi$ with $\Phi=-\Psi$, the same result reads $-2\int\partial\Phi/\partial\tau\,d\tau$. Photons follow the lensing combination $\Phi+\Psi$, which Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_poisson}
ties to $\Sigma$. Since $\bar\rho a^2\propto a^{-1}$ and $\Delta\propto D$, with $\Sigma=1$
\begin{equation}\Phi+\Psi\propto\frac{D}{a},\qquad\frac{\partial(\Phi+\Psi)}{\partial\tau}\propto aH\,\frac{d(D/a)}{d\ln a}=H\,D\,(f-1),\qquad f\equiv\frac{d\ln D}{d\ln a}.\label{eq:sp_iswsrc}\end{equation}
\derived\ The background $H$ is the same in IAM and $\Lambda$CDM, so the ISW source changes only through $D$ and $f$. In IAM, $\mu<1$ causes
gravitational potentials to decay faster than in $\Lambda$CDM ($\Sigma=1$ leaves photon geodesics unchanged). The faster potential decay enhances
the ISW signal. The potential itself follows $D$: normalised at $z=3$, $(\Phi+\Psi)$ is $0.22\,\%$ lower than
in $\Lambda$CDM at $z=0.5$ and $0.78\,\%$ lower today. \calc

The source ratio $(1-f)D|_{\rm IAM}/(1-f)D|_{\Lambda\rm CDM}$ is $1.031$ at $z=0$, $1.035$ at $z=0.3$, $1.034$ at $z=0.5$ and $1.024$ at $z=1$. For
galaxy samples with Gaussian redshift windows ($\sigma_z=0.1$) centred on the DESI bright galaxies ($z\approx0.3$) and luminous red galaxies
($z\approx0.5$ and $0.7$), the ISW--galaxy cross-correlation amplitude relative to $\Lambda$CDM is $1.035$, $1.034$ and $1.031$, with the galaxy bias
calibrated from the galaxy auto-power. Weighted uniformly over $0.05<z<1.5$,
\begin{equation}A_{\rm ISW}^{\rm IAM}/A_{\rm ISW}^{\Lambda\rm CDM}\approx1.03 .\label{eq:sp_isw}\end{equation}
\calc\ The MGCAMB tracking form, which suppresses $\mu$ more at intermediate redshift, gives $1.054$, $1.064$ and $1.072$ for the same three samples;
the shape matters at this level. The sign is opposite to that of $f(R)$ gravity, whose enhanced growth ($\mu>1$) slows the decay of the potentials
and lowers $A_{\rm ISW}$; the size of that suppression depends on the $f(R)$ amplitude. This provides a sign-based discriminant that does not
require precise amplitude measurement. The enhancement is concentrated at $z\lesssim1$ where IAM's modification is largest. Current ISW
measurements from cross-correlation with SDSS, 2MASS, and WISE galaxy catalogs~\cite{Giannantonio2008,Planck2015ISW} are consistent with
$\Lambda$CDM at ${\sim}4\sigma$ significance but with $20$--$30\,\%$ uncertainties; the IAM enhancement of about $3\,\%$ is within current error
bars. \observed\ The ISW test is therefore not a near-term discriminator but provides a consistency check and a sign test.

\begin{itemize}

\item \emph{$E_G$.} $E_G=\Omega_{m0}\Sigma/f$ rises because $f$ falls while lensing does not: $+1.8\,\%$ at $z=0.3$, $+3.6\,\%$ today. \calc

relativity. \fitted\ No present measurement discriminates.

\section{The timeline}\label{sec:sp_timeline}
Euclid's complete first data release, with the clustering and lensing products needed for cosmology, is scheduled for mid-2027; no forecast of

Redshift-space distortions test the shape directly: the deficit falls from $4.25\,\%$ at $z=0$ to $1.35\,\%$ at $z=0.5$ and $0.41\,\%$ at $z=1$

\section{The equation of state seen by surveys}\label{sec:sp_weff}
IAM's $E(a)$ produces an effective dark energy equation of state that evolves with redshift. Combining $\Lambda$ with the informational density,
the vacuum-like total has $w_{\rm eff}=-1-\tfrac{1}{3a}\,\rho_{\rm info}/(\rho_\Lambda+\rho_{\rm info})$: $-1.015$ at $z=3$, $-1.052$ at $z=1$,
lowest at $-1.063$ near $z=0.19$, $-1.062$ today, and back toward $-1$ in the future ($-1.012$ at $a=10$). \calc\ So $w_{\rm eff}<-1$ at recent times,
transitioning to $w_{\rm eff}\to-1$ at high $z$, and this distinguishes IAM from a pure cosmological constant --- on the matter ruler. The informational density $\rho_{\rm info}\propto E(a)$ has the equation of state $w_{\rm info}=-1-1/(3a)$: $-4/3$ today, tending to $-1$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:darkenergy}). It belongs to the matter-sector rate. DESI's preference for $w_0>-1$ comes from distances measured with light, on
which the term predicts $w=-1$, so the two are not compared directly; the test is the two-ruler expansion history (Chapter~\ref{ch:wzfuture},

\section{What would falsify it}\label{sec:sp_falsify}
\prediction\ Any of the following falsifies the prediction:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mu_0$ measured consistent with $0$ and excluding $-0.136$ (for example $\mu_0>-0.05$ at $95\,\%$).
\item $\mu>1$ at late times, the sign of $f(R)$ and normal-branch DGP.
\item $\Sigma\neq1$ detected at any redshift.
\item $f\sigma_8(z)$ following $\Lambda$CDM to better than the predicted $2$--$4\,\%$ at $z<0.3$, or a deficit that is a constant factor rather than the ramp
of the first table.
\item Any scale dependence of $\mu$ or $\Sigma$: Eq.~\eqref{eq:sp_mu} depends on $a$ only.
\item $A_{\rm ISW}<1$ at significance, or $E_G(z)$ at or below $\Lambda$CDM at sufficient precision.
\item A siren population precise enough to separate the two rates that excludes $72.26$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion: what the signature separates}\label{sec:sp_separates}
IAM makes specific, falsifiable predictions for every major forthcoming cosmological survey. The predictions are parameter-free:
$\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ from the virial theorem and $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ from horizon thermodynamics, with all other quantities following from Planck
2018 cosmological parameters. \prediction

$\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$ is the signature predicted here, and among viable models it is distinctive. $\Lambda$CDM has $\mu=\Sigma=1$. $f(R)$ gravity
has $\mu>1$ with $\Sigma=1$, and normal-branch DGP has $\mu>1$; the self-accelerating DGP branch gives $\mu<1$ with $\Sigma=1$ but carries a ghost and
is excluded. Background $w_0$--$w_a$ models change distances and keep $\mu=\Sigma=1$, and modified-gravity models in which $\mu$ and $\Sigma$
depart from one together have $\Sigma\neq1$ whenever $\mu\neq1$~\cite{PogosianSilvestri2008,PogosianSilvestri2016}. \derived\ The sign of the ISW change --- enhanced for IAM, suppressed for
$f(R)$ --- provides a model-discriminating observable independent of amplitude precision. If the data deviate from these predictions, those
deviations are equally informative --- they constrain the parameter space and may point toward refinements or extensions of the model. The
predictions recorded here are explicit, the measurement techniques are established, and the survey schedule is set. The surveys will decide.

Every number here is fixed before the data arrive. A cosmologist with a Boltzmann code and the survey pipelines can run these tests as the releases come in and see whether the two Hubble rates and the ramp in $f\sigma_8$ hold.
