\chapter{The baryon density}\label{ch:baryon}

The matter--antimatter asymmetry of the universe --- one surviving baryon for roughly $1.6\times10^9$ photons --- is conventionally a
free parameter of the standard model, explained qualitatively by the Sakharov conditions but not derived from first principles. If the
cosmological constant accumulates as the Landauer cost of irreversible gravitational decoherence on baryonic timelike worldlines
(Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda}), the question arises whether the baryon asymmetry $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$ is genuinely free, or whether
it is a self-consistency condition of the universe's information-writing budget.

IAM's Law requires that the rate at which baryonic matter writes classical information onto the cosmic horizon remain compatible
with the horizon's capacity over the full cosmic history. This chapter states a self-consistency loop in which the matter content sets
the decoherence rate, which sets the writing rate, which must match the horizon's accommodation rate, which feeds back on the matter
content, and proposes that $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$ is the fixed point of this loop rather than an arbitrary output of the Sakharov
mechanism. \conjecture\ It does not derive $\eta$. It states the physical conditions a derivation would have to satisfy, presents the
loop as a research target, and names the test that was run: letting the baryon density float in a Planck chain and asking what the
posterior returns (Chapter~\ref{ch:baryon_chain}). In this chapter and the next, $\eta$ is the baryon-to-photon ratio, not the horizon
entropy per unit area of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}.

\section{The size of the asymmetry}
The observed universe contains matter and no appreciable antimatter. The baryon-to-photon ratio,
\begin{equation}\eta\equiv\frac{n_b-n_{\bar b}}{n_\gamma}\approx6.1\times10^{-10},\label{eq:bar_eta}\end{equation}
is measured independently from Big Bang nucleosynthesis~\cite{Cyburt2016} and from the cosmic microwave background power
spectrum~\cite{Planck2018VI}, with consistent results: $6.180\pm0.195$ from nucleosynthesis with deuterium and $6.108\pm0.060$ from the
CMB alone in the same analysis~\cite{Cyburt2016}, and $6.127\pm0.041$ from the Planck 2018 baryon density $\Omega_bh^2=0.02237\pm0.00015$
(TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing)~\cite{Planck2018VI}, all in units of $10^{-10}$, with $\eta=273.9\times10^{-10}\,\Omega_bh^2$~\cite{Steigman2006}.
\observed\ The two measurements together show that the asymmetry was in place before nucleosynthesis and is a property of the early
universe, not a local accident.

The Sakharov conditions~\cite{Sakharov1967} name the necessary ingredients for generating a baryon asymmetry from a symmetric initial
state: baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium. The standard model meets all three in
principle, but the CP violation of the CKM matrix~\cite{KobayashiMaskawa1973} appears too small to produce the observed $\eta$ without
extensions~\cite{Gavela1994}. This has motivated a large literature on baryogenesis: electroweak
baryogenesis~\cite{KuzminRubakovShaposhnikov1985,RubakovShaposhnikov1996}, leptogenesis~\cite{FukugitaYanagida1986,Davidson2008} and
Affleck--Dine baryogenesis~\cite{AffleckDine1985}.

What these mechanisms have in common is that they treat $\eta$ as an output of dynamical processes in the early universe, with its value
depending on parameters that are not themselves derived from a deeper principle. In this sense $\eta$ remains a free parameter: the
standard model and its extensions can accommodate a wide range of values, and $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$ is not understood as
necessary rather than contingent.

The question asked here is different. Under IAM's Law the universe has an information-writing budget: baryonic matter generates
irreversible classical information through gravitational decoherence on timelike worldlines, and this information is encoded on the
cosmic horizon at a cost set by Landauer's principle~\cite{Landauer1961}. The total accumulated cost over cosmic history is identified
with the cosmological constant (Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda}). \conjecture\ This writing process has a natural rate and a natural capacity.
The question is whether the two together constrain the fraction of matter that must survive annihilation for the universe to write its
own history coherently.

\section{The frame}\label{sec:bar_frame}
The informational term extends the thermodynamic derivation of the field equations~\cite{Jacobson1995} by an informational contribution

worldlines as the dominant source of irreversible classical information, at a rate set by the virial theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial})
\interp; Landauer's principle, which at the horizon temperature $T_H=\hbar H_0/(2\pi k_B)$~\cite{GibbonsHawking1977} gives a cost per
irreversible bit $E_{\rm bit}=\hbar H_0\ln2/(2\pi)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lam_ebit}) \derived; and the holographic

entropy~\cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}, $S=A_H/(4l_P^2)$ \derived.

The result is a sector-dependent term in the perturbation equations: matter feels an enhanced Hubble friction, $\mu(a)<1$, while
photon-sector observables keep $\Sigma(a)=1$ exactly (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}). The coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ is derived from the
virial theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}) \derived; it is held at that value in every chain, so the chains test the term with $\beta_m$
fixed rather than measure it.

In the virial reading of Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_partners}, what is observed as dark matter is the accumulated geometric half of prior
gravitational decoherence events, and dark energy is the kinetic, informational half. These are then not two independent phenomena but
two expressions of the virial equilibrium $2K+V=0$ at cosmological scales. \interp\ The cosmological virial ratio
$\Omega_m/[\beta_mE(a)]$ equals 2 at $a=1$ by the definition $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ with $E(1)=1$. \derived\ (definition) That the
present epoch is where the ratio reaches 2 is how this reading addresses the coincidence problem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_partners});
it is a consequence of the normalisation, not a measurement. \interp

\section{The information-writing constraint}\label{sec:bar_constraint}
The cosmological constant at any epoch $a$ accumulates as the history integral Eq.~\eqref{eq:lh_integral} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda_history}, in which $\Omega_b(a')/\Omega_{\rm tot}(a')$ is the baryonic fraction of the total energy density at
scale factor $a'$ and $l_H(a')=c/H(a')$ the Hubble length at that epoch, integrated from the electroweak transition. \conjecture\ The
integrand is the writing rate per unit scale factor: the fraction of the horizon's Planck-area bits being written by baryonic
decoherence at each epoch, weighted by the baryonic fraction of the energy and the inverse square of the Hubble length. As written the
integral is dominated by its lower limit and gives a coefficient of $3.1\times10^{30}$ where $0.523$ is required (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lh_K});
its normalization is open. \calc\ \openprob\ The writing rate has two natural limits.

\paragraph{Upper limit.} The writing rate cannot exceed the horizon's accommodation rate. The horizon can encode at most
$N_H=A_H/(4l_P^2)$ nats at any epoch. If baryonic decoherence tries to write information faster than the horizon can accommodate it,
the excess cannot be encoded. Physically this is a universe with too much matter: structure collapses before the horizon has grown
enough to accommodate the encoded history. \conjecture

\paragraph{Lower limit.} The writing rate must be enough to accumulate the observed cosmological constant over 13.8 billion years. If
baryonic matter is too dilute, too few decoherence events occur per unit time and the accumulated Landauer cost falls below the observed
$\Lambda$. Physically this is a universe with too little matter: the writing rate is insufficient and the dark sector does not
accumulate the observed energy density. \conjecture

Together the two limits constrain the baryonic matter density. In the cosmological-constant expression the baryonic fraction enters
as the measured $\Omega_b/\Omega_m=0.1564$~\cite{Planck2018VI}, with the physical argument that only baryons decohere
(Section~\ref{sec:lam_dm}); it entered after comparison with the data, so it is an input to that expression, not a derived constraint.
\observed

\section{The self-consistency loop}\label{sec:bar_loop}
The connection between the writing constraint and the baryon asymmetry runs in five steps.

\paragraph{Step 1.} The matter content of the universe is set by the baryon asymmetry $\eta$ before nucleosynthesis; at the QCD
transition ($T\approx150$\,MeV, $a_{\rm QCD}\approx9.5\times10^{-13}$ with entropy conservation and $g_{*s}=17.25$ \calc) the surviving
quarks are confined into baryons. For every $10^{9}$ photons in the thermal bath about $0.6$ baryons survive annihilation, and the
surviving baryon density is
\begin{equation}n_b=\eta\,n_\gamma .\label{eq:bar_nb}\end{equation}
\derived\ (definition)

\paragraph{Step 2.} The surviving baryonic matter density sets the decoherence rate. Baryons on timelike worldlines generate
irreversible classical information through gravitational interactions at a rate proportional to their number density and the local
gravitational potential, set by the virial theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}). \conjecture

\paragraph{Step 3.} The decoherence rate sets the writing rate onto the cosmic horizon. The accumulated writing over cosmic history is
the integral Eq.~\eqref{eq:lh_integral}, with $\Omega_b$ fixed by $\eta$ through Eq.~\eqref{eq:bar_nb}. \conjecture

\paragraph{Step 4.} The writing rate must stay compatible with the horizon's accommodation rate at every epoch. The accommodation rate is
set by the Hubble expansion, which depends on the total matter--energy content including the dark sector; in the virial reading the
dark sector is the accumulated record of prior decoherence (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial_partners}). \conjecture

\paragraph{Step 5.} The dark sector feeds back on the matter content through the virial partition: the geometric half of each
decoherence event contributes to what is observed as dark matter, the kinetic, informational half to dark energy, so the total energy
budget depends on the full history of baryonic decoherence, which depends on $\eta$ through Steps 1--4. \conjecture\ Dark matter is
present at its measured density at recombination ($z\approx1100$), before structure forms; any feedback through the dark sector must
therefore act through the present-epoch partition, not through the matter content set before nucleosynthesis. \observed

This closes the loop:
\begin{equation}
\eta\;\longrightarrow\;n_b\;\longrightarrow\;\dot W_{\rm info}\;\longrightarrow\;\Lambda_{\rm acc}\;\longrightarrow\;
\{\rho_{\rm dm},\rho_{\rm de}\}\;\longrightarrow\;H(a)\;\longrightarrow\;\eta .\label{eq:bar_loop}
\end{equation}
The proposal is that the observed $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$ is the fixed point of this loop: the value of the baryon asymmetry
consistent with a universe that can write its own decoherence history onto a horizon of finite capacity over a finite cosmic time.
\conjecture

This is not a claim that the Sakharov conditions are unnecessary. They provide the dynamical mechanism by which an asymmetry is
generated. The proposal is that among the values the Sakharov mechanism could produce, the universe takes the one consistent with the
loop~\eqref{eq:bar_loop}. \conjecture

\section{What a derivation would require}\label{sec:bar_derivation}
The loop is a physical argument, not a completed derivation. A derivation of $\eta$ from IAM's Law would need three elements, which
make the research target precise.

\paragraph{Element 1: a writing rate at the QCD transition.} The writing rate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lh_integral} is formulated with the
post-recombination universe in mind, where baryons dominate the matter sector. A derivation of $\eta$ needs it at the QCD epoch
($a\approx9.5\times10^{-13}$), where the universe is radiation dominated and the baryons are a small perturbation on the thermal bath. The
form of the writing-rate integrand there is not established, and is the main technical obstacle. \openprob

\paragraph{Element 2: the horizon's capacity at the QCD transition.} The fixed-point condition compares the writing rate with the
horizon's capacity at the epoch when $\eta$ is set. At $T=150$\,MeV the Hubble rate is $H=1.93\times10^4$\,s$^{-1}$, the Hubble length
$l_H\approx15.5$\,km $=5.0\times10^{-13}$\,pc, and the horizon holds $A_H/4l_P^2=2.9\times10^{78}$ nats ($4.2\times10^{78}$ bits).
\calc\ Whether the writing rate at that epoch saturates, undershoots or overshoots this capacity is the quantitative question that
would determine $\eta$. \openprob

\paragraph{Element 3: a link between the writing constraint and the Sakharov mechanism.} The loop constrains the result of baryogenesis,
not its mechanism. A complete derivation would show how the writing constraint meets the dynamical Sakharov conditions to select the
fixed point $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$. This probably needs a treatment of the electroweak transition (Chapter~\ref{ch:electroweak})
beyond what is developed. \openprob

These three elements are the research programme the loop implies. The loop~\eqref{eq:bar_loop} is the structural claim; the
derivation of its fixed point is the open problem. \openprob

\section{One relation, read as $\eta$}\label{sec:bar_onerel}
The cosmological-constant expression contains $\Omega_b$ explicitly, so it can be inverted for the baryon density the observed
$\Lambda$ would require. With $\eta=273.9\times10^{-10}\,\Omega_bh^2$~\cite{Steigman2006} and the Planck
$\Omega_mh^2=0.3153\times0.6736^2=0.1431$~\cite{Planck2018VI}: the expression with $2/\pi$ and without $\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:base}, which is $\Omega_b/\Omega_m=3\Omega_\Lambda/16$, gives $\Omega_bh^2=0.01837$ and $\eta=5.03\times10^{-10}$; with
$\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr}, which is $\Omega_b/\Omega_m=\tfrac3{16}\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$, it gives $0.02220$ and
$\eta=6.08\times10^{-10}$, $0.5$--$1.0\,\%$ below the CMB values of Chapter~\ref{ch:baryon_chain} ($6.113$--$6.137$) and $0.8\,\%$ below
Planck's $6.127$. \calc\ This is the relation $\Omega_b/\Omega_m=(3/16)\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda} in another
variable. The cosmological constant and the baryon density are one measured relation, not two. \observed\ The derivation is written
out step by step, with the status of each premise, in Appendix~\ref{app:der:lambda}.

\section{Relation to other approaches}\label{sec:bar_prior}
Baryogenesis has been approached from many directions. Electroweak baryogenesis~\cite{RubakovShaposhnikov1996} generates $\eta$ through
sphaleron processes at the electroweak phase transition. Leptogenesis~\cite{Davidson2008} produces a lepton asymmetry through heavy
neutrino decay, which is then converted into a baryon asymmetry. Affleck--Dine baryogenesis~\cite{AffleckDine1985} uses flat directions
of supersymmetric scalar fields. In each case $\eta$ is an output of specific dynamics whose parameters are not themselves derived.

The reading carried here does not propose a new dynamical mechanism. The Sakharov conditions and their realisations in the standard
model and its extensions are accepted as the correct description of how the asymmetry was generated. The proposal is that the size of
the asymmetry is not set by the dynamics alone but is further constrained by the requirement that the resulting baryon density be
consistent with the universe's information-writing capacity. \conjecture

The closest conceptual parallel is Weinberg's anthropic reasoning~\cite{Weinberg1989}, which bounds the cosmological constant by
requiring structure to form. The present reading differs in that it invokes no observer selection: the writing constraint is a
physical requirement of IAM's Law, not a selection criterion imposed from outside. The universe is not required to permit observers;
it is required to write its own history coherently. \interp

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:bar_concl}
A self-consistency loop connects the baryon asymmetry $\eta$ to the universe's information-writing constraint. \conjecture\ A Planck 2018
chain with $\Omega_bh^2$ sampled over a flat range six times wider than the other runs, and no abundance data, returns
$\eta=(6.113\pm0.037)\times10^{-10}$ from the CMB (Chapter~\ref{ch:baryon_chain}); it converged with $R-1=0.009273$ after 22,400 accepted
steps. \measured\ Every CMB fit returns this value, because the early universe is unchanged by the informational term, so the chain is
a consistency check, not a test of the loop; the loop's constraint is not in its likelihood. The fixed point is not derived. \openprob

The cosmological constant and the baryon asymmetry are not two independent unsolved problems in this reading: through
$\Omega_b/\Omega_m=\tfrac3{16}\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$ they are one measured relation at the present epoch, read in two variables, with
the coefficient still to be derived from IAM's Law, $\Delta E=k_BT_H\ln2$ per bit. \observed\ \openprob

This places the matter--antimatter asymmetry in the same conceptual category as the cosmological constant (Chapter~\ref{ch:lambda}) and
the virial coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:virial}): quantities that appear contingent in the standard framework but
may emerge as necessary conditions within a framework governed by an information-writing constraint. Whether this pattern reflects a
deeper principle is left open. \interp

The broader question is whether the standard model's treatment of $\eta$ as a free parameter is complete. In a framework where the
universe has a finite information-writing budget, connected to both the dark sector and the cosmological constant, the baryon asymmetry
is not obviously free. It may be the value the universe had to have in order to become the universe it is. \conjecture
