\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This book does not propose a new model and it is not modified gravity. It takes Jacobson's derivation of Einstein's equations from horizon
thermodynamics~\cite{Jacobson1995} exactly as written and goes one step further: the horizon entropy is completed with information. The Einstein
equations are untouched. \derived

IAM's Law states one physical cost for every event in which a possibility becomes a fact. When a system
irreversibly commits one bit, at least $\kB T\ln 2$ is paid at the surface where the bit is written, where $T$ is the temperature of that encoding
surface. The cost is Landauer's~\cite{Landauer1961,Bennett1982}, and it has been measured~\cite{Berut2012}. The surface and its temperature are
Bekenstein's and Hawking's~\cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}. Decoherence~\cite{Zurek2003} makes the classical world out of quantum possibility; the
law prices each record it leaves, and the energy a system spends against thermal noise is what keeps those records written.

The book follows that one accounting across thirty-seven orders of magnitude. Part~\ref{part:1} states the law and its local expression, the virial
partition. Part~\ref{part:2} applies it at the largest surface, the cosmological horizon, where it completes the horizon entropy of Jacobson and of
Cai and Kim and gives a matter-sector coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$, weaker growth with unchanged lensing, and the particle scale. Part~\ref{part:3}
reads quantum processors and transistors against their floors. Part~\ref{part:4} reads the living cell: the methylation pattern as a written surface,
its copy error against the healthy cell of its own kind and against the thermal floor, measured cell by cell with cosmology's tools.
Part~\ref{part:5} joins the parts, collects what is predicted, and lists what is still open.

Nothing in the book is a new model to rival general relativity or the standard model. It is the cost that both already imply wherever a record is
made, written out.

\section*{Status labels}
Every result carries a label. A reader who remembers nothing else should remember which numbers are derived and which were set from data.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
\derived & Follows from stated premises by algebra or standard physics, with no parameter adjusted to data. \\
\calc & A number computed from a derived formula and published constants or measurements; recomputed for this edition. \\
\calibrated & A value set once from healthy or reference data for one system and then held fixed. The data are named. \\
\measured & A value read from data by the instruments of this book and frozen in a file in the repository. \\
\observed & A published measurement, quoted with its source. \\
\fitted & A parameter adjusted to make a formula match the quantity it is later compared with. The agreement is then not evidence. \\
\conjecture & A structural or interpretive claim that is not yet a calculation. \\
\analogy & A correspondence between two domains that guides a method but is not a derivation. \\
\interp & How we read a set of results; it adds no result of its own. \\
\prediction & A falsifiable statement about a measurement not yet made, with the test named. \\
\openprob & A known gap or inconsistency, stated openly. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section*{The path through the book}
Part~\ref{part:1} is required reading for everything after it. A physicist interested in cosmology reads Parts~\ref{part:1} and~\ref{part:2}; in
quantum hardware, Parts~\ref{part:1} and~\ref{part:3}; a geneticist or biological physicist, Part~\ref{part:1}, Part~\ref{part:4} and the one-gauge chapter
(Chapter~\ref{ch:onegauge}, Part~\ref{part:5}). Part~\ref{part:5} assumes all of them.

\section*{What this book does not contain}
Instrument software beyond what is needed to reproduce a number, calibration tooling and commercial material are not reproduced. The book gives no
medical, nutritional or treatment advice: the cell instrument measures a state; it does not diagnose, and it is not used on any question until it
has been commissioned for it.
