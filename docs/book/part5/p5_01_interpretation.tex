\section{The nine-step chain}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Gravitational interaction: matter attracts and overdense regions begin to collapse (general relativity).
\item Collapse: matter forms bound systems --- halos, galaxies, stars (structure formation).
\item Decoherence: gravitational interaction turns superpositions into definite classical states (Zurek~\cite{Zurek2003}).
\item Irreversible information: each event writes a classical record that cannot be undone (second law).
\item Landauer cost: each bit costs at least $k_BT\ln2$~\cite{Landauer1961}.
\item Holographic encoding: the records are bounded by, and written on, the horizon area (Bekenstein, 't~Hooft, Susskind~\cite{Bekenstein1973,tHooft1993,Susskind1995}).
\item Information pressure: growing the horizon area to hold the records drives expansion. That horizon thermodynamics can yield gravitational dynamics is Jacobson's result~\cite{Jacobson1995}; its use here to drive expansion is the framework's own step.
\item Modified expansion: matter sees $H^2$ with the extra term $\beta E(a)H_0^2$.
\item Feedback: the extra friction on matter lowers the effective matter density parameter seen by perturbations, weakens collapse, and slows the whole chain.
\end{enumerate}
Step 9 feeds back into step 1 with reduced amplitude. The loop converges, so $E(a)\to e$ at late times. Steps 1--5 are established physics; the
identification in steps 6--7 that the records are written on, and drive, the cosmic horizon, and the feedback of steps 8--9, are the framework's
conjecture. \conjecture

\section{Gravity as cause and product}
Gravity causes the decoherence and is changed by the information it produces: the modified expansion changes the gravitational environment. This fits
the Verlinde and Padmanabhan programmes~\cite{Verlinde2011,Padmanabhan2010}, in which gravity emerges from information and entropy. Here dark energy and gravity are two outputs of the
same process: locally, decoherence concentrates information and attracts; globally, it writes information on the horizon and drives expansion.

\paragraph{Cosmological regime.} Distributed decoherence from large-scale structure is written gradually on the growing apparent horizon at
$T_{GH}=H/2\pi$. Expansion dilutes $\Omega_m(a)$ and the loop settles, giving $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$.
\paragraph{Collapse regime.} Concentrated decoherence in a contracting region produces information faster than local capacity can hold. The loop runs
away and a new local horizon forms: a black hole. The criterion is holographic saturation,
\begin{equation}\frac{S_{\rm info}(R)}{A(R)}\ge\frac{1}{4\ell_P^2}.\end{equation}
With $S_{\rm info}=S_{BH}=4\pi GM^2/\hbar c$ at $R_s=2GM/c^2$, the ratio is $c^3/4\hbar G=1/4\ell_P^2$: saturated exactly at the Schwarzschild radius. This is
Thorne's hoop conjecture~\cite{Thorne1972} stated in information terms.
\paragraph{Two thermal surfaces.} $T_{BH}=\hbar c^3/8\pi GMk_B$ and $T_{GH}=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$ are equal at
\begin{equation}M_{\rm eq}=\frac{c^3}{4GH}\approx2.3\times10^{22}M_\odot\quad(H_0=67.16),\end{equation}
far above the most massive black holes measured (TON~618, $\log M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot=10.82$, about $7\times10^{10}M_\odot$, from its H$\beta$ line
width~\cite{Shemmer2004}). Every astrophysical black hole has $T_{BH}\gg T_{GH}$, so information flows from hot local horizons to the cold cosmic horizon
by Hawking radiation~\cite{Hawking1975}. Pricing the black-body Hawking power $P=\hbar c^6/15360\pi G^2M^2$ at $\kB T_{BH}\ln2$ per bit gives the
rate $\Gamma=P/(\kB T_{BH}\ln2)=c^3/(1920\,GM\ln2)$ bits\,s$^{-1}$. \calc
\paragraph{The encoding transition.} Early on ($E\approx0$) the cosmic horizon is small and hot and writes inefficiently; early records are held on
black-hole horizons. As the universe expands, the cosmic horizon grows and cools and becomes the main encoding surface. $E(a)/e$ is the fraction of the
accumulated information entropy written on the cosmic horizon relative to its asymptotic value. On this reading massive black holes at high redshift are
the expected encoding surfaces of that epoch. \interp

\section{The arrow of time}
The microscopic laws are time-symmetric; the standard account places irreversibility in the low-entropy initial state without a mechanism for the
increase. Here the arrow has a physical carrier, $E(a)$: the direction in which superpositions decohere into classical states, records accumulate, and
the cosmic horizon grows to hold them. $E(a)$ only increases, because each record is irreversible. In redshift, $E(z)=\exp(-z)$: looking back, the record
falls exponentially; looking forward, it approaches saturation. Penrose's low-entropy initial state~\cite{Penrose1989} is, on this reading, a state with almost no classical
records, and the history since --- structure, chemistry, biology --- is the accumulation of those records, measured by $E(a)$. \interp

The chain here is short enough to test link by link. A relativist can take the horizon-saturation argument into other collapse models, and a cosmologist can set the epoch of heaviest record-writing against structure-formation data.
