\chapter{Conclusion: pick it up, and try to break it}\label{ch:conclusion}

The book began with one price. Making a classical record costs at least $\kB T\ln2$ per bit, paid at the surface where the record is written,
at that surface's temperature. Nothing in that statement is new. Landauer stated the price, Bennett showed where it is paid, B\'erut and
colleagues measured it, Bekenstein and Hawking gave surfaces their capacity and temperature, Jacobson showed that gravity follows from the
thermodynamics of surfaces, and Zurek showed how decoherence leaves the records. What this book adds is the step that joins them: these are one
accounting, and it can be followed, with the same formula and the same units, from the cosmic horizon to the strand of DNA in a single cell.

That is a large claim, and it is made in the most useful way a large claim can be made: every piece of it can be checked by someone who already
has the tools. Nothing here asks a reader to accept a new force, a new particle or a new field. It asks only that a price everyone already
agrees on be paid everywhere it is owed. If that is right, a great deal follows. If it is wrong, it is wrong somewhere specific, and finding where
is a result in itself.

\section*{One cost, every scale}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{At the cosmic horizon}, with no free parameter, the price fixes a matter-sector coupling $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2=0.15765$, lensing
      exactly as in general relativity ($\Sigma=1$), a growth signal, $f\sigma_8$, $4.25\,\%$ lower today, and two Hubble rates from one law: $67.16$ for light,
      as the CMB reads it, and $72.26$ for the matter flow, as the local distance ladder reads it. \derived, \calc{} The two rates the
      Hubble tension sets against each other come out of one law. With the coupling fixed, the fit to Planck 2018 is consistent with Planck, as the $\Lambda$CDM fit is ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.54$, no added parameter). \measured
\item \textbf{At a black hole}, the same price gives the Smarr relation as the cost of the horizon's bits and the Bekenstein bound as the full
      surface: a black hole is a record that has filled its page. \derived
\item \textbf{At the particle scale}, the virial partition that fixes $\beta_m$ reappears from the lepton masses to the horizon, and the Koide
      ratio sits at $Q=0.66666446$, $0.43\sigma$ from $2/3$. \observed
\item \textbf{At a qubit and a transistor}, the price is a floor under every device, set by $\kB T\ln2$ at the device's own temperature. A current
      desktop processor runs $576$--$593$ times above it. \calc{} That room is where the next generations of hardware will be won, and the gauge shows
      an engineer exactly where a device is losing what it writes.
\item \textbf{At the cell}, the price becomes an instrument. A cell's methylation pattern is its written record, and it is read against the same
      cell when healthy, with the thermal floor on one side and the full surface on the other, using the tools cosmology built to read a faint
      signal against a calibrated sky. \derived, \measured{} (the floor's height)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Why a cosmologist should pick it up}
The coupled universe makes sharp, near-term predictions with nothing left to tune: $\mu_0=-0.136$, $\Sigma=1$, a growth deficit that ramps from
zero at high redshift to $4.25\,\%$ today, and the two Hubble rates. \prediction{} Euclid, DESI, LSST and the gravitational-wave sirens are
measuring exactly these quantities this decade. A cosmologist with a Boltzmann code can run the chains in an afternoon (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
If the growth follows the ramp and the sirens land on 72.26, the dark sector has a thermodynamic origin. If they do not, the test is clean and decisive.

\section*{Why a relativist or a quantum theorist should pick it up}
Jacobson derived Einstein's equations from $\delta Q=T\,dS$ at every local horizon. IAM keeps his derivation exactly and completes the entropy
with the records the universe has written. Only one step is taken: the horizon entropy carries information. That step connects decoherence,
measurement, the arrow of time and black-hole information to a single quantity that can be counted. A coherence time that grows with temperature
is a prediction that sets IAM apart, and levitated-mass experiments are closing in on the regime where it can be seen. \prediction

\section*{Why a device physicist should pick it up}
Every platform, from transmons at millikelvin to trapped ions, neutral atoms, spins and CMOS at a hot junction, sits on the same gauge, each read
against itself as built. The floor is not a fitting parameter; it is $\kB T\ln2$ at the temperature the record sees. That gives hardware a
physical target, and a way to ask of any loss channel how close it is to the floor and what it would take to move it. \derived

\section*{Why a geneticist should pick it up}
Cancer, ageing and inflammation are, at bottom, failures to keep a written pattern. IAM gives that loss a physical scale: a cell's copy error,
measured against the same cell when healthy, in units that mean the same thing in every tissue and every person. No group of people enters and no
classifier is trained; a reading needs only the cell, its own healthy reference and the floor. The same mathematics that maps the cosmic
microwave background maps the methylome: power spectra, residual maps, masks, component separation. A geneticist who has ever wanted a reading
that is about this person, today, and not a distance from a group of people, has one to test here. \interp

\section*{Try to break it}
The best thing a reader can do with this book is to try to break it. Every number can be recomputed from the repository; every prediction is
dated and states what would falsify it (Chapter~\ref{ch:predictions}); the next steps are laid out with a plan for each (Chapter~\ref{ch:open}).
Every result carries a label saying whether it is derived, calculated, measured, calibrated, fitted, predicted or conjectured
(Figure~\ref{fig:status_summary}), so a reader always knows exactly what is being claimed and how strongly.

The order in the title is the half of the accounting that is easiest to see and hardest to measure: every system that persists spends energy
to hold what it has written against the thermal noise of its own surface. A cell that stops holding its pattern stops being that cell. \interp{}
If that is right, the physics that reads the sky can read the health of a cell long before a disease shows itself, and can tell an engineer where
a qubit or a chip is losing what it writes. One price, paid from the horizon to the genome. Pick it up, take it further, and try to break it.
