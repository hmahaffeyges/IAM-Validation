\chapter{Measurement as the writing of a record}\label{ch:measurement}

\section{The problem}\label{sec:mp_problem}
The measurement problem --- what constitutes a measurement, why measurement appears to cause collapse, and what role the observer plays --- has been
debated for a century. Quantum mechanics is the most precisely tested theory in physics; its predictions have been confirmed to great accuracy in every
experimental domain. Yet its standard formulation posits two processes. Between measurements, states evolve unitarily under the Schr\"odinger equation.
At measurements, states undergo a non-unitary collapse to an eigenstate of the measured observable. What constitutes a ``measurement'' is not defined
within the theory; whether the observer plays a special role remains contested; the randomness of outcomes is taken as fundamental.

Decoherence explains why interference between macroscopically distinct branches becomes unobservable~\cite{Zurek2003,Schlosshauer2005,JoosZeh1985}. It
does not by itself select one outcome, and it does not say where the quantum-to-classical line lies. Collapse models~\cite{GRW1986,Bassi2013},
many-worlds~\cite{Everett1957}, Bohmian mechanics~\cite{Bohm1952} and QBism~\cite{Fuchs2014} each give a different answer.

This chapter applies IAM's Law to the question. It does not modify the quantum formalism, and it does not choose among the interpretations on the
question of single outcomes. What it supplies is a physical definition of the event that standard quantum mechanics leaves unnamed: a measurement is
complete when a record has been written irreversibly into matter, at a cost of at least $\kB T\ln2$ per bit at the surface where it is written. No
observer and no consciousness enter. \interp{} The same definition is then applied to the double slit, delayed choice, the quantum eraser, Schr\"odinger's
cat, entanglement and Bell tests, Wigner's friend and the quantum Zeno effect.

\section{The two sectors, and where a record is written}\label{sec:mp_sectors}
At cosmological scale, matter and light feel different effective couplings. The modification is governed by the activation function $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$
of the horizon thermodynamics (Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}), with $\beta_m=\Omega_m/2$ fixed by the virial partition. The signature at the level of
perturbations is $\mu<1$ (suppressed growth of matter perturbations) with $\Sigma=1$ (lensing by light unchanged), Eq.~\eqref{part2:eq:mu}; it has been
confronted with Planck 2018 and large-scale structure in eighteen chains, with $\sigma_8=0.800$ and $H_0^{(\rm matter)}=72.26$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$
(Chapters~\ref{ch:dual} and~\ref{ch:level2}). \measured{} The reason is that records are written only on timelike worldlines: a photon on a null geodesic
accumulates no proper time and writes no gravitational record in flight, while matter does (Chapter~\ref{ch:quantumrecords}). \derived

In the laboratory the same distinction becomes a statement about where a record is written. A photon in flight, a superposition, an entangled pair in
transit and a reversible which-path marker hold no irreversible record yet: their information can still be erased. A record becomes irreversible when it
is written into matter --- the photon absorbed in a detector, the signal amplified and dissipated. \interp{} Figure~\ref{fig:mp_record}a shows the division.
$\mu$ and $\Sigma$ remain the names of the cosmological perturbation functions; they are not used here as labels for the state of a laboratory system.

\section{The criterion}\label{sec:mp_criterion}
Let $S$ be a quantum system and $D$ a material detector (any system on a timelike worldline). Let $Q(S,D)$ be the energy irreversibly dissipated in their
interaction, and
\begin{equation}
Q_L\equiv\kB T_D\ln2\label{eq:mp_QL}
\end{equation}
the Landauer threshold at the detector temperature $T_D$~\cite{Landauer1961}. At room temperature $Q_L=0.0179$\,eV. \calc{} The criterion is:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $Q(S,D)<Q_L$, no irreversible record is written. The interaction is thermodynamically reversible and quantum erasure is possible.
\item If $Q(S,D)\ge Q_L$, a record is written irreversibly; Landauer entropy has been produced in the detector.
\end{itemize}

\input{tables/part5/tab_eraser}

The criterion has to be read with Bennett's result~\cite{Bennett1982}: acquiring a record has no minimum dissipation; the $\kB T\ln2$ per bit is paid when
a record is erased or the apparatus is reset. What $Q\ge Q_L$ marks is therefore where a record becomes irreversible in practice --- at absorption in a
dissipative detector --- not a cost that the act of measurement itself must pay. \derived{} (Landauer--Bennett). Read this way, the criterion places
the record in the detector or the amplifier chain, and erasure remains possible until then. Every experiment discussed below agrees with that reading.
\observed{} The same criterion distinguishes a CCD pixel ($Q\approx3$\,eV, $Q/Q_L\approx167$), a retinal rod ($Q\approx2.5$\,eV, $Q/Q_L\approx140$) and a
photographic grain from a beam splitter or a polarisation rotator, which dissipate essentially nothing, $Q/Q_L\ll1$. \calc

\section{The double slit}\label{sec:mp_slit}
In the double slit a photon passes two slits and builds an interference pattern on a screen. A which-path detector at the slits destroys the pattern;
the textbook account attributes this to observation collapsing the wave function. With the criterion, the photon travels from source to slits with
$E_G=0$ and no record written; it is coherent throughout its flight. The record is written at the screen, which is matter: the photon is absorbed, an
electron is excited, and $Q\approx2$\,eV is dissipated, $Q/Q_L\approx112$ at room temperature. That absorption is where the outcome becomes a record.
\interp{} A which-path detector introduces matter before the screen. If its interaction is irreversible ($Q>Q_L$), the record is written at the slit and
interference is lost before the screen. If it is reversible ($Q<Q_L$) --- a polarisation rotation, for example --- no record is written and interference
can be restored. A retina ($Q\approx2.5$\,eV, $Q/Q_L\approx140$) writes the same kind of record as a CCD pixel ($Q\approx3$\,eV, $Q/Q_L\approx167$).
Consciousness plays no role. \calc

\section{Wheeler's delayed choice}\label{sec:mp_delayed}
In Wheeler's delayed-choice experiment~\cite{Wheeler1978,Jacques2007} the decision to reveal wave or particle behaviour is made after the photon has
passed the slits, and the photon seems to decide retroactively which it was. With the criterion no retrocausality is needed. The photon has $E_G=0$
throughout its flight; through a 5\,m apparatus the flight takes 16.7\,ns, and the gravitational record written in that time is exactly zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:mp_record}b). \calc{} The experimenter's choice of configuration decides where the record is written, not whether the photon was coherent
in transit: nothing irreversible happened to the photon until absorption. Standard quantum mechanics already gives this account~\cite{Ma2016}. The
contribution of IAM's Law is the thermodynamic criterion for \emph{where} the absorption becomes irreversible. \interp

\section{The quantum eraser}\label{sec:mp_eraser}
In quantum-eraser experiments~\cite{ScullyDruhl1982,Kim2000} which-path information is obtained and then ``erased'', and the interference pattern is
restored in the sorted coincidences. Standard quantum mechanics attributes this to the reversibility of the entanglement between the photon and the
which-path marker. The criterion draws one distinction. If the which-path interaction was reversible ($Q<Q_L$), no record was written; the eraser reveals
coherence that was always present. If an irreversible record has been written ($Q\ge Q_L$, dissipated in matter), the information it holds cannot be
recovered by any operation on the marker alone. \interp

What counts is energy \emph{dissipated} into a record, not the energy of the quantum that carries the which-path information. A which-path marker
stored in a photon's polarisation carries 2\,eV of photon energy but dissipates nothing, and it is erasable. The same holds for a photon spontaneously
emitted by an atomic which-path marker: emission into free space is not by itself a record. A trapped ion has been entangled with its own
spontaneously emitted photon and the entanglement measured~\cite{Blinov2004}; remote entanglement of two ions~\cite{Moehring2007}, and of two electron
spins 1.3\,km apart~\cite{Hensen2015}, has been built by interfering spontaneously emitted photons, which requires that their which-path information be
erased by measuring them in a conjugate basis. \observed{} Emission alone writes no irreversible record; the record becomes irreversible when the photon
is absorbed in a dissipative detector, which is where the criterion places it. Emission into a high-$Q$ cavity, where vacuum Rabi oscillation makes the
exchange reversible~\cite{Haroche2006,Blais2021}, is the controlled setting in which the dissipated energy per cycle can be varied. \interp

\paragraph{An erasure fidelity.} The criterion has been given a quantitative form for erasers:
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{IAM}}(Q,T)=1-\frac{1}{e}\exp\!\left(1-\frac{\kB T\ln2}{Q}\right)=1-\exp\!\left(-\frac{Q_L}{Q}\right),
\label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is the energy dissipated into the which-path record. The two forms are identical (sympy, \texttt{verify\_records\_measurement\_time.py} B3).
\calc{} The form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F} is taken as given here, and its derivation is open: it carries over the activation ramp, while the rate integral it is built from gives a plain exponential (Chapter~\ref{ch:gravdec}). \conjecture\ Taking the form as given, reversible markers ($Q/Q_L<0.04$) give $F>1-e^{-25}\approx1$, and
irreversible ones ($Q/Q_L>100$) give $F<0.01$. \calc{} At the crossover $Q=Q_L$, $F=1-e^{-1}=0.632$.
The derivation is written out step by step, with the status of each premise, in Appendix~\ref{app:der:quantum}.

\section{Schr\"odinger's cat}\label{sec:mp_cat}
Schr\"odinger's thought experiment posits a cat alive and dead at once until observed. A 4\,kg cat ($R\approx0.15$\,m) has gravitational self-energy
$E_G=Gm^2/R\approx7.1\times10^{-9}$\,J, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:mp_tauIAM} gives $\tau_{\rm IAM}\approx3.5\times10^{-51}$\,s at 300\,K, $1.6\times10^7$ times
shorter than the Planck time ($5.4\times10^{-44}$\,s). \calc{} A time below the Planck time has no operational meaning, and in any case the gravitational
channel is not the operative one: decoherence of a cat by air molecules and thermal photons is faster than any gravitational channel by many orders of
magnitude~\cite{JoosZeh1985,Zurek2003}. \observed{} A macroscopic superposition of this kind cannot be created or maintained. What IAM's Law adds is the
location and cost of the record: the cat's state is written irreversibly into its environment at $\kB T\ln2$ per bit, long before anyone opens the box.
No observation is needed. \interp

\section{Entanglement and Bell tests}\label{sec:mp_bell}
Entangled particles show correlations that violate Bell inequalities~\cite{Bell1964,Aspect1982,Hensen2015}. IAM's Law is not a local hidden-variable theory, and it changes nothing in quantum
mechanics: an entangled pair is one quantum state, and its correlations are those of that state until an irreversible record is written. Photons in
transit carry no gravitational record ($E_G=0$, $\tau_{\rm IAM}=\infty$); the full state is preserved in flight, and the law specifies only when the
transition from indefinite to recorded occurs. The correlation function is the quantum one, $E(\theta)=-\cos\theta$, and the CHSH value of an ideal
source is $|S|=2\sqrt2$~\cite{Tsirelson1980}, above the local bound of 2. \derived

For matter-based pairs, a loss of coherence $D$ in the pointer basis leaves the coherence $c=1-D$, and the largest CHSH value is
\begin{equation}
S_{\max}=2\sqrt{1+c^2}\label{eq:mp_smax}
\end{equation}
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ent:smax}; Horodecki criterion~\cite{Horodecki1995}), above 2 for any $c>0$. \derived{} At fixed settings optimised for the pure state,
$S=\sqrt2(1+c)$, below 2 for $D>0.586$; only for isotropic noise, $S_{\max}=2\sqrt2(1-D)$, is the threshold $D>0.293$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:entanglement}). \calc{} The experimental records are consistent with standard decoherence: photon pairs have been distributed over
1,200\,km by satellite~\cite{Yin2017}; electron spins 1.3\,km apart have violated a Bell inequality without loopholes, $S=2.42\pm0.20$~\cite{Hensen2015}.
\observed{} The ratio of the two distances is about 900. Matter entanglement is shorter-ranged because matter qubits are harder to isolate from their
environments, not because of a gravitational channel.

\section{Wigner's friend}\label{sec:mp_wigner}
In the Wigner's-friend scenario a friend measures a quantum system inside a sealed laboratory. Standard quantum mechanics allows Wigner, outside, to
describe the laboratory including the friend as a superposition; extended scenarios~\cite{FrauchigerRenner2018} show that this leads to contradictions
among the agents' statements. With IAM's Law the record is objective once it is written. A Stern--Gerlach measurement read on a fluorescent screen
produces about 1,000 photons of 2\,eV, which dissipate $2\times10^3$\,eV, or $1.1\times10^5$ times $Q_L$ at room temperature. \calc{} The probability
that the entropy so produced spontaneously reverses is of order $2^{-111\,612}\approx10^{-33\,599}$. \calc{} This is not a practical difficulty; it is
thermodynamically excluded. Wigner cannot treat the laboratory as a coherent superposition once that entropy has been produced, because the record is a
physical fact in matter, not a state of anyone's knowledge. In the textbook reading collapse is observer-relative; here the record is thermodynamic.
\interp

\section{The quantum Zeno effect}\label{sec:mp_zeno}
The quantum Zeno effect --- frequent measurement freezing a system's evolution~\cite{Itano1990} --- is confirmed in many systems. Standard quantum
mechanics explains it by repeated projection. It has been observed with a linear, dispersive readout of a superconducting qubit through a
near-quantum-limited amplifier, in the strong-measurement regime~\cite{Slichter2016}, a measurement in which the qubit itself dissipates almost nothing. \observed{} In the reading of
Section~\ref{sec:mp_criterion} this is expected: the record is written where the signal is amplified and absorbed, in the amplifier chain, and the
dissipation there is far above $Q_L$. The criterion locates the record in the readout chain; it does not require the coupling to the system to be
dissipative. \interp

\section{Connection to the cosmology}\label{sec:mp_cosmo}
The writing of records that governs the laboratory is the same process that, summed over about $10^{11}$ galaxies and 13.8\,Gyr, produces the
information term that modifies the growth of matter perturbations. At cosmological scale the accumulated record gives $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$ and the
signature $\mu<1$, $\Sigma=1$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:quantumrecords}). \interp{} At the quantum scale the same law gives the gravitational time of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mp_tauIAM}; the time profile $E_q(\eta)=\exp(1-1/\eta)$ is carried over from the cosmology by analogy, since the rate integral gives an
exponential (Chapter~\ref{ch:gravdec}). \conjecture{} The laboratory measures the rate per record; cosmology measures the sum. Consistency between the
two --- surveys preferring $\mu<1$ with the predicted redshift dependence, and laboratory data showing $\tau_{\rm IAM}\propto T^2m^{-5}$ at the predicted
scale --- would join quantum mechanics to the growth of structure through one law. \prediction

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:mp_discussion}
\paragraph{Relation to the interpretations.} The reading shares elements with several approaches and differs from each. Like decoherence
theory~\cite{Zurek2003}, it takes environmental interaction as the mechanism of apparent collapse. Unlike decoherence theory, it names a threshold,
$Q_L=\kB T\ln2$, and a place, the surface where the record is written in matter. Like objective-collapse models~\cite{Penrose1996,Diosi1987}, it treats
the record as a physical event independent of observers. Unlike them, it introduces no new stochastic field and does not modify the Schr\"odinger
equation. \interp

\paragraph{What it does not change.} IAM's Law does not modify the predictions of quantum mechanics for any experiment performed to date. Erasers with
reversible markers restore interference; erasure after spontaneous emission works when the emitted photon is measured in a conjugate
basis~\cite{Blinov2004,Moehring2007,Hensen2015}; photonic and matter Bell tests give the quantum values; the Zeno effect appears with projective and with
dispersive readout~\cite{Itano1990,Slichter2016}. All agree with the law read as above: the record, and its cost, are where the information is absorbed and
dissipated in matter. \observed

\paragraph{What would falsify the reading.} Three outcomes would. (i) Successful erasure of which-path information after it has been absorbed and
dissipated in a detector, $Q\gg Q_L$, with no coherent copy left. (ii) A dependence of erasure on dissipated energy and detector temperature different
from what standard decoherence theory gives, if Eq.~\eqref{eq:F} is offered as the law; a measurement of $F(Q/Q_L)$ with a controlled dissipation tests
it (box above). (iii) For the gravitational channel, the absence of the $\tau_{\rm IAM}\propto T^2m^{-5}$ scaling in massive superpositions once
environmental channels are excluded (Chapter~\ref{ch:gravdec}). \prediction

A quantum-device physicist already has what is needed to probe this: a which-path eraser with controllable dissipation, read as a function of energy and temperature. Each measurement of that curve tells us whether erasure reads out a record.
