\chapter{Notation and units}\label{app:notation}
This appendix fixes the units, sign conventions and symbols used in Parts~\ref{part:1}--\ref{part:5}, and lists the physical constants with their
CODATA~2018 values~\cite{CODATA2018}. Values that belong to the framework itself (the cell constants, the coupling $\beta_m$, the gauge points) are
those of the canon file \texttt{CANON/\allowbreak{}iam\_canon.json}, which is also the source of Appendix~\ref{app:canon}. Each field of physics keeps
its own conventions where they differ, so several letters carry more than one meaning; Section~\ref{app:notation:clash} lists them.

\section{Units and general conventions}
\begin{itemize}
\item SI units throughout. Cosmological rates are quoted in km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$; masses of stars and black holes in solar masses $M_\odot$; energies of
particles and gaps in eV, meV or $\mu$eV ($1~\mathrm{eV}=1.602176634\times10^{-19}$~J, exact).
\item $\ln$ is the natural logarithm and $\log_2$ the logarithm to base 2. One bit is $\ln2=0.693147$ nats. A Shannon entropy written $H$ is in bits; a
thermodynamic entropy written $S$ is in J\,K$^{-1}$, that is in units of $\kB$ (nats times $\kB$). A horizon entropy in bits is $S/(\kB\ln2)$.
\item $\kB T$ is the thermal energy quantum; ``$3.41\,\kB T$'' means $3.41$ times $\kB T$ at the stated temperature. \emph{Landauer units} measure an energy in units
of $\kB T\ln2$, the Landauer cost of one bit: an energy $x\,\kB T$ is $x/\ln2$ Landauer units.
\item The Mahaffey number $\mathcal M=E_{\rm drive}/\kB T$ (also written $M$) is defined without a factor $\ln2$; $\mathcal M/\ln2$ is the same quantity in Landauer units. For
the cell $\mathcal M=\dGATP/(R\Tb)=20.94$, which is $30.21$ in Landauer units (canon value 30.2).
\item The gate error of a qubit is expressed in nats, $\varepsilon=-\ln(1-p_{2Q})$; for $p_{2Q}<10^{-2}$ it equals $p_{2Q}$ to better than $0.5\,\%$.
\item Temperatures: $\Tb=T_{\rm cell}=310.15$~K (37\,\textdegree C) for human cells; $T_j$ is a chip's junction temperature (nominal 348.15~K, 75\,\textdegree C); a
qubit's temperature is its refrigerator stage or, for ions and atoms, the motional temperature; a horizon's temperature is its Hawking or
Gibbons--Hawking temperature.
\item Natural units ($\hbar=c=\kB=1$, $G$ kept) are used only where a chapter says so: the Cai--Kim derivation of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory}, where
$T_H=H/2\pi$ and $S_{\rm geo}=A_H/4G$, and the restatements of it in Chapters~\ref{ch:dsnote} and~\ref{ch:theoryinterp}; and the radiation-era time $t=0.301\,g_*^{-1/2}\mP c^2/T^2$ (with $\hbar=c=\kB=1$) of Chapter~\ref{ch:electroweak}. Everywhere else the constants are written out.
\item Cosmological parameters are Planck~2018 TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing~\cite{Planck2018VI} unless a chapter names a chain. Uncertainties are $1\sigma$ unless
stated; ``95\,\%'' and ``90\,\%'' are credible or confidence levels as the source states them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sign and normalisation conventions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Metric.} Signature $(-,+,+,+)$: a timelike worldline has $g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu/d\tau^2=-1$ and a null one $g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu=0$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:dsnote}). Proper time is $\tau=\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu}/c$.
\item \textbf{Scale factor and redshift.} $a=1/(1+z)$, $a=1$ today. The activation function is normalised to $E(1)=1$ and in redshift is $E=e^{-z}$.
\item \textbf{Growth and lensing couplings.} $\mu$ multiplies $G$ in the Poisson equation for matter and $\Sigma$ multiplies it in the lensing potential
$(\Phi+\Psi)/2$; general relativity is $\mu=\Sigma=1$. $\mu_0\equiv\mu(z{=}0)-1$ and $\Sigma_0\equiv\Sigma(z{=}0)-1$; a negative $\mu_0$ is suppressed growth. The
prediction is $\mu_0=-\beta_m/(1+\beta_m)=-0.136$ (quoted as $-0.135$ where the MGCAMB input $-0.13495$ is meant) with $\Sigma_0=0$.
\item \textbf{Potentials.} Conformal Newtonian gauge; with no anisotropic stress $\Psi=\Phi$, and $k^2\Psi=-4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_m\delta_m$, so the potentials are
negative in an overdensity. $\delta_m$ is the matter density contrast; $D(a)$ the linear growth factor with $D(1)=1$; $f=d\ln D/d\ln a$.
\item \textbf{Equation of state.} $w=p/\rho c^2$; $w<-1$ is called phantom. The CPL form is $w(a)=w_0+w_a(1-a)$.
\item \textbf{Two Hubble rates.} $H_0^{(\gamma)}$ (photon sector, the background) and $H_0^{(m)}=H_0^{(\gamma)}\sqrt{1+\beta_m}$ (matter sector). The book's values are
$67.16$ (photon sector, Level~2 chain) and $72.26$ (matter sector)~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$; $67.4$ and $67.36$ are Planck~2018 values and are labelled as such where used.
\item \textbf{Fit statistics.} $\Delta\chi^2$ is IAM minus $\Lambda$CDM on the same data and code, from the lowest $\chi^2$ in each chain; positive means IAM fits
slightly worse. With equal parameter counts the likelihood ratio is $e^{-\Delta\chi^2/2}$. Chains are converged when the Gelman--Rubin $R-1<0.01$.
\item \textbf{The one gauge.} Every score $A$ is a reading divided by the same system's healthy or as-built reading on the same instrument: $A=1$ is healthy, in
the middle; $A>1$ is more error than healthy; the floor $\Hmin$ lies below 1 and nothing reads below it. Normal is $0.95\le A\le1.05$. $A$ is never the
temperature exponent $n$, and $\Hmin$ is not the denominator of $A$.
\item \textbf{Methylation.} $\beta\in[0,1]$ is the methylated fraction of the copies at one CpG. On the methylated channel an error lowers $\beta$
($\beta=1-\varepsilon$), on the unmethylated channel it raises it ($\beta=\varepsilon$); $H(\beta)=H(1-\beta)$ makes both read $H(\varepsilon)$. Residuals are
specimen minus reference: $z_i>0$ is more entropy than healthy at site $i$; in serial mode $\Delta\beta=\beta^{\rm now}-\beta^{\rm prior}$.
\item \textbf{Virial theorem.} For a potential homogeneous of degree $k$, $2\langle K\rangle=k\langle V\rangle$; gravity and electrostatics have $k=-1$.
Simulation papers write the virial ratio as $2T/|U|$ or $2K/|W|$, with $T$ or $K$ the kinetic energy.
\end{itemize}
