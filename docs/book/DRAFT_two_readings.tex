\section{Two readings of one record}\label{sec:two_readings}

A cell holds its identity as a methylation pattern. The pattern is a record, and the cell copies it at every division. The record can be
read at two scales: on single molecules, and across the many cells of a specimen. The instrument reads it both ways.

\paragraph{IAM-A: copy errors on single molecules.} Take one molecule from a long read. Where the molecule is methylated along its length,
an unmethylated call standing alone between methylated neighbours is a copy error: the copy did not carry the mark across. Count the errors
and the places where one could have happened,
\begin{equation*}
\varepsilon=\frac{\text{errors}}{\text{opportunities}} .
\end{equation*}
\measured{} The floor is $\varepsilon_0=0.032$, set by the energy that holds a mark at 310\,K (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps0}). \derived{} The reading is
\begin{equation*}
\text{IAM-A}=\frac{H(\varepsilon)}{P_{\rm cell}\,H(\varepsilon_0)}
\end{equation*}
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:iama}), where $H$ is the binary entropy and $P_{\rm cell}$ is the healthy position of that cell type on one read-level
pipeline. \calibrated{} This is the cell's error per gate, read against a thermal floor.

\paragraph{Met-A: the pattern across the ensemble.} An array does not see molecules. It sees $\beta$, the fraction of copies methylated at
each site, over all the cells in the specimen. Met-A is the Shannon entropy of $\beta$ at the cell's identity sites, over the healthy floor of
that cell type on the same platform (Eq.~\eqref{eq:meta}). The floor comes from purified healthy cells. \calibrated{} This is the cell's state
fidelity, read across the ensemble.

\paragraph{One record, two scales.} The two readings are of one record. Copy errors are what blur the pattern: an error moves the $\beta$ of
its site toward one half, where $H(\beta)$ is largest. \derived{} So IAM-A reads the cause on single molecules and Met-A reads its result
across the specimen. Each A score carries one C-score beside it, computed from the same sites, which says where along the genome the
departures sit (Chapter~\ref{ch:cscore}). \calc

\paragraph{The efficiency side.} The Mahaffey number is the drive over the thermal quantum, $\mathcal M=\Delta G_{\rm ATP}/\kB T$. For one
ATP at 310\,K it is about 21 ($\mathcal M=20.94$). \calc{} It is the efficiency side of the same cell, and it sets $\varepsilon_0$ through
the holding energy (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps0}). \derived

\paragraph{Two kinds of method.} Most methods that read methylation for disease are classifiers: machine-learning models trained on large
groups of people with and without disease. Ageing clocks are regressions trained on large groups of people of known age. Both ask which group a
specimen looks like. The readings here ask a different question: how far is this cell from its own healthy record, measured against a physical
floor? One compares a specimen with other people; the other measures one cell. They are different methods, and nothing here says which detects
disease better. Detection is not tested. \openprob

Both readings are open to anyone with a long-read run or an array. Take a healthy cell, read it both ways, and try to break it.
