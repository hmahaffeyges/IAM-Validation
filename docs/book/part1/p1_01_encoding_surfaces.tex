\section*{The statement}
Entropy is the same whether it is driving cosmic expansion or a malignant cell. That sentence requires justification. This chapter provides it.

Black hole formation and the loss of a cell's identity are both instances of the same thermodynamic event: a physical system exceeding its
information maintenance budget at its nearest encoding surface. The key quantity at both scales is identical: the Landauer cost $k_BT\ln2$ per bit
of irreversible information processing at the local encoding surface. At stellar scale the encoding surface is the Bekenstein--Hawking horizon; at
cellular scale it is the methylation record of the chromatin, maintained by DNMT1 across the genome's CpG sites. Both transitions are
irreversible, both leave a signal before the event, and both are instances of the same equation at different values of $T$ and $N$. This is not a
metaphor. It is a structural identity.

\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{\textbf{Floor breach condition (both scales).} A physical system undergoes floor breach when its local
rate of irreversible information production saturates the encoding capacity of its nearest causal surface. The system can no longer pay the
thermodynamic cost of remaining itself. At stellar scale: a black hole. At cellular scale: malignant transformation.}}\end{center}

The derivation has six steps. Each step is established physics or a direct extension of it. The single new physical identification is in Step~5.
This is IAM's Law (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}): every irreversible transition from quantum superposition to classical record pays $k_BT\ln2$ per bit to the
nearest encoding surface, at that surface's temperature, and the record is written on that two-dimensional surface. IAM is not found in a cell, a
device, a black hole or the cosmos: these are places found in IAM, each a surface where records are written at a temperature.

\paragraph{Why matter and not light.} Why should the record cost act on matter but not on light? The answer is proper time. Massive particles
travel on timelike worldlines, $\Delta\tau>0$. Duration permits a sequence of gravitational interactions: overdensities attract matter, matter
collapses, and quantum superpositions resolve into classical outcomes. Time is required for decoherence, and only matter experiences time.
Photons travel on null geodesics, $ds^2=0$, $\Delta\tau=0$: a photon experiences no proper time, does not cluster, does not decohere, and writes no
record. Given the identification of Step~5 below, this is not a further assumption but a consequence of the causal structure that general
relativity already describes: the metric signature distinguishes timelike from null, and the distinction has a thermodynamic consequence. Matter
pays the cost of becoming classical; light does not. \derived{} (given Step~5) In the $\mu$--$\Sigma$ language matter feels a weakened effective
coupling, $\mu(a)<1$, and light feels standard gravity, $\Sigma(a)=1$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:iams_law}). On our reading the weakening is the part of the
energy budget that matter spends writing its records to the horizon. \interp{} The history follows from $E(a)=\exp(1-1/a)$: at early times
$E\to0$, with no structure, no decoherence and no cost; today $E=1$; in the far future $E\to e$, and the feedback regulates itself \derived. Why

\subsection*{Step 1. Bekenstein (1973): horizon entropy}
Bekenstein showed that a black hole of horizon area $A$ carries entropy proportional to that area~\cite{Bekenstein1973}. The Bekenstein--Hawking
entropy is
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm BH}=\frac{k_B\,A}{4\ell_P^2},\qquad \ell_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}.
\end{equation}
One unit of $k_B$ is held per four Planck areas; one bit, $k_B\ln2$, needs $4\ln2\,\ell_P^2$, so a solar-mass black hole holds
$1.51\times10^{77}$ bits \calc. This is not a coincidence: the coefficient $\tfrac14$ is the ratio $2\pi/8\pi$, where $2\pi$ is the period of the Euclidean
Rindler cone and $8\pi$ is the normalisation of the Einstein equations (Chapter~\ref{ch:blackholes}). \derived{} The encoding surface has a definite
capacity. When that capacity is saturated, the stellar system has crossed its floor.

\subsection*{Step 2. Hawking (1975): the encoding surface has a temperature}
Hawking showed that black holes radiate thermally~\cite{Hawking1975},
\begin{equation}
  T_H=\frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi GMk_B},
\end{equation}
$6.17\times10^{-8}$\,K for one solar mass \calc; a cosmological horizon radiates at $T_{GH}=\hbar H/2\pi k_B$~\cite{GibbonsHawking1977},
$2.65\times10^{-30}$\,K today \calc. This is the temperature at which the encoding surface operates. Landauer's principle now applies: each irreversible
bit written to this surface costs $k_BT_H\ln2$ in free energy. The encoding surface is not passive storage. It is an active thermodynamic boundary
with a cost per operation.

\subsection*{Step 3. Landauer (1961): the cost per bit is universal}
Landauer's principle states that the irreversible erasure of one bit of information in a physical system at temperature $T$ dissipates a minimum
energy~\cite{Landauer1961,Bennett1982}
\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm bit}=k_BT\ln2 ,
\end{equation}
approached in measured erasures~\cite{Berut2012}: $2.87\times10^{-21}$\,J (17.9\,meV) at 300\,K. \observed{} This is scale-independent. $T$ is the local
temperature of the encoding surface and $N$ the number of bits being written; the total Landauer cost is $N$ times $E_{\rm bit}$. At the black hole
horizon $T=T_H$ and $N=\delta A/(4\ln2\,\ell_P^2)$. At the cell nucleus $T=T_{\rm body}=310.15$\,K and $N$ is the number of CpG sites. The equation
is the same.

\subsection*{Step 4. Jacobson (1995): gravity is the thermodynamics of encoding surfaces}
Jacobson's result is the pivot of the entire chain. He showed that Einstein's field equations are not a fundamental law: they are the
thermodynamic equation of state of spacetime, derivable from the Clausius relation applied to local Rindler horizons~\cite{Jacobson1995},
\begin{equation}
  \delta Q=T\,dS\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
\derived{} The entropy functional $S$ determines the theory of gravity. Jacobson used the geometric entropy $S_{\rm geo}=k_BA/4\ell_P^2$ alone, and the
result is general relativity. This means gravity is not a force that exists alongside thermodynamics. Gravity is the thermodynamic response of
spacetime to information being written to encoding surfaces. The encoding surface is not a consequence of gravity; gravity is a consequence of the
encoding surface. \interp

\subsection*{Step 6. The same floor at cellular scale}
The same Landauer cost applies to DNA methylation maintenance. DNMT1-mediated CpG methylation maintenance is an irreversible binary information

site. \calc{} Over the $2.82\times10^{7}$ CpG sites of the human genome,
\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm floor}=N_{\rm CpG}\,k_B\,T_{\rm body}\ln2=8.37\times10^{-14}\ {\rm J\ per\ copy}\approx9.3\times10^{5}\ {\rm ATP}
\end{equation}
at 54\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$~\cite{Nelson2017}, with no free parameter \calc; for all $28{,}217{,}448$ CpGs of the genome, each a binary decision, it is
$8.37\times10^{-14}$\,J (Chapter~\ref{ch:landauer}).

The floor $H_{\min}$ is the methylation entropy expression of this floor: the absolute lowest a cell can operate at while remaining
thermodynamically itself. It lies to the left of healthy. On single molecules it is set by the thermal copy error

reads $1/P=0.910$ on the gauge (Chapter~\ref{ch:iama}). On arrays it is still to be derived. \openprob{} The A-score reads where the cell sits:

\section{What this is and what it is not}
This derivation establishes a structural identity: the same thermodynamic equation governs floor breach at stellar and at cellular scale. It does
not establish a numerical unification between the two scales. For a solar-mass black hole the temperatures differ by a factor $5.0\times10^{9}$ and
the bit counts by $5.4\times10^{69}$. \calc{} The equation is the same; the numbers are not.

The open problem is the quantitative bridge: showing that the floor $H_{\min}$ of a given cell type can be derived from first principles through
the full Landauer--Jacobson--IAM chain without reference to measurement. The cell's floor now rests on a measured holding energy. Its full
first-principles derivation, from the Bekenstein--Hawking coefficient through the cellular scale, is the remaining open problem. \openprob
\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{\textbf{Honest caveat.} The structural derivation is complete. The full numerical unification, deriving
the floor from first principles through the complete chain without measurement, is the open problem that would make this a complete theory rather
than a structural result. The structural result is what is demonstrated here.}}\end{center}

\paragraph{Why the bridge is hard.} In the superconducting qubit one energy does double duty. The gap $\Delta_{\rm Al}=182\,\mu$eV sets the
temperature of the encoding surface, $\Delta_{\rm Al}/k_B=2.112$\,K \calc, and it is also the energy quantum of the observable: one quasiparticle
costs at least $\Delta_{\rm Al}$. Dividing one by the other leaves a count, and the quasiparticle fraction is then fixed by quantities measured on the
device (Chapter~\ref{ch:xqp}) \interp. In the cell no single energy plays both roles. The body temperature fixes the cost of a bit,
$k_BT\ln2=2.968\times10^{-21}$\,J at 310.15\,K \calc, while the floor is set by a separate holding energy, $E_{\rm hold}=3.41\,k_BT$, through
$\varepsilon_0=1/(1+e^{E_{\rm hold}/k_BT})$; $E_{\rm hold}$ is read from single molecules, not derived \measured. The chain from the horizon to the
nucleus therefore has no surface quantum to divide by. Two entries are also missing from the cell's column of the structural table
(Table~\ref{tab:synth_slots}): an explicit event rate, the analogue of the fluctuator rate $1/\tau_{\rm TLS}$, which is at present only implicit in
the maintenance cycle; and a participation volume, the analogue of the volume over which quasiparticles are counted. Supplying both, and deriving
$E_{\rm hold}$, would close the bridge. \openprob
