  Physics:      Photons deflected by gravitational potential → Σ parameter
  Sector:       PHOTON (null geodesics, ds² = 0)

  If Σ = 1:       Lensing gives the CORRECT total mass everywhere

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PROBE 2: GALAXY CLUSTERING (spectroscopic survey)  
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Instrument:  NISP spectrograph (near-infrared, Hα emission)
  What it does: Measures redshifts of 30+ MILLION galaxies
  Physics:      Galaxy positions + velocities → growth rate → μ parameter
  Sector:       MATTER (timelike worldlines, ds² > 0)
  
  What Euclid measures: Galaxy power spectrum P(k,z) + RSD
  This depends on:      μ(z) × growth rate f(z) × galaxy bias b(z)
  
  IAM prediction: μ < 1 → galaxies cluster LESS than ΛCDM expects
  If μ = 0.864:   Growth rate suppressed by ~14% → fewer clusters, lower σ₈
  
  Think of it as: Euclid's SPECTROGRAPH watches how MATTER moves,
  and matter moves on timelike worldlines (μ < 1, modified)

THE MAGIC: CROSS-CORRELATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Euclid measures BOTH probes over the SAME volume of universe.
  The cross-correlation between lensing and clustering is the 
  "3×2pt" analysis: galaxy-galaxy, galaxy-shear, shear-shear.
  
  This cross-correlation directly separates μ from Σ:
  
  • Shear-shear (WL×WL):    Sensitive to Σ² × P(k)
  • Galaxy-galaxy (GC×GC):   Sensitive to μ × b² × P(k)  
  • Galaxy-shear (GC×WL):    Sensitive to μ × Σ × b × P(k)
  
  By combining all three, Euclid can solve for μ and Σ INDEPENDENTLY.
  
  This is why Euclid is the decisive test:
  It doesn't just measure one number. It measures BOTH sectors
  simultaneously over the SAME patch of sky and SEPARATES them.
